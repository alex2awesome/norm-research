ELEGANCE AND INSIGHT RUBRIC — Math.SE Answers (v3)

An answer is ELEGANT and INSIGHTFUL when it reveals WHY a result is true through a minimal, well-chosen idea — not merely when it is correct, confident, or uses advanced mathematical language.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 1 — RATE EACH SUB-CRITERION (H=high, M=mid, L=low/zero)

SC-A  CONCEPTUAL CORE — Does the answer name the structural reason why the result holds?
  H: Identifies a key structural fact, invariant, symmetry, or contradiction that immediately entails the result.
  M: Identifies an approach but the core reason is buried in algebra or stated unclearly.
  L: Applies a standard algorithm or formula without naming the underlying mechanism.
  ZERO: No explanation; just a result, a link, or a label.

SC-B  ECONOMY OF MEANS — Is the insight delivered with minimal scaffolding?
  H: The insight could be stated in 1–3 sentences; all subsequent steps are clearly subordinate. One cannot shorten the answer without losing correctness.
  M: Reasonably concise but contains some redundant or over-explained steps.
  L: A long chain of symbolic manipulation, repeated substitutions, or case-splits where a shorter path demonstrably exists.

SC-C  EXPLANATORY FORCE — Does it explain WHY, not just HOW?
  H: After reading, a student understands the mechanism and could apply the principle to a new problem.
  M: Correct and somewhat explanatory, but primarily shows HOW to perform a computation.
  L: Purely procedural: each step is justified locally but no higher-level understanding is transmitted.

SC-D  AVOIDANCE OF MECHANICAL GRINDING — Does it avoid or critique brute force?
  H: Explicitly takes the principled path over case enumeration, computer assistance, or symbol-grinding.
  M: Mechanical but the problem genuinely offers no elegant shortcut (not penalized).
  L: Uses Wolfram Alpha, extensive computer output, or brute-force case-splits when a conceptual route is available.

SC-E  GENERATIVITY — Does the insight extend beyond this specific problem?
  H: The approach or principle is clearly generalizable; the answer implicitly or explicitly names the general pattern.
  M: The approach works for this problem but is not obviously general.
  L: The answer is tailored so specifically to this instance that it would not suggest any broader technique.

SC-F  NON-OBVIOUSNESS — Does the answer reveal something a skilled student would NOT immediately know?
  H: The key move is surprising: a non-trivial reframing, an unexpected connection, or a shortcut that bypasses an expected route.
  M: The approach is somewhat expected but cleanly executed.
  L: The answer applies the most obvious available technique (e.g., directly expanding, directly substituting, directly applying a named theorem the question itself invoked).

─────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 2 — ASSIGN THE FINAL SCORE

Count H-rated sub-criteria:
  6H → 1.0  |  5H → 1.0  |  4H → 0.75  |  3H → 0.5  |  2H → 0.25  |  0–1H → 0.0

HARD CAPS:
  • SC-A = L or ZERO → score CANNOT exceed 0.25.
  • SC-C = L → score CANNOT exceed 0.5.
  • If the answer uses a computer/CAS as the primary solver → SC-D = L, SC-B = L automatically.

WHY: Well-organized but mechanical; does not explain why Inclusion-Exclusion works.

--- SCORE 0.5 ---
A: [Clever substitution for a limit without L'Hôpital]
"Set u = x − π/3. The denominator 1−2cos(u+π/3) expands to √3·u + O(u²), giving limit 1/√3."
SC-A=H (reframe via substitution), SC-B=H (compact), SC-C=M (shows HOW the substitution works, not deeply WHY), SC-D=M, SC-E=M, SC-F=M → 2H → 0.5

--- SCORE 0.75 ---
A: [Proving |√x − √y| ≤ √|x−y|]
"Rationalize: |√x−√y| = |x−y|/(√x+√y). Since √|x−y| ≤ √x+√y (triangle inequality on square roots), dividing gives the result."
SC-A=H (rationalization is the structural move), SC-B=H (2-step), SC-C=H (reveals the mechanism), SC-D=H (avoids squaring both sides mechanically), SC-E=M (rationalization technique is general but not stated), SC-F=M (expected move for this type) → 4H → 0.75
WHY: Elegant and explanatory but does not explicitly generalize or surprise.

--- SCORE 1.0 ---
A: [Is A=[[6,0],[-2,2]] diagonalizable?]
"Nothing to calculate: A is lower-triangular, so eigenvalues = diagonal entries = 2 and 6. Distinct real eigenvalues ⟹ diagonalizable over ℝ."
SC-A=H, SC-B=H, SC-C=H, SC-D=H, SC-E=H (lower-triangular principle applies broadly), SC-F=H (question invited computation; this avoids it entirely) → 6H → 1.0
WHY: Structurally complete: avoids all computation, names a general principle, surprises by short-circuiting the expected approach.

--- SCORE 1.0 ALTERNATIVE ---
A: [Solve 2^m − 2^n = 112]
"Factor: 2^n·(2^(m−n)−1) = 2^4·7. Left side is (even)×(odd); the factorization into even and odd parts is unique, so 2^n=2^4 and 2^(m−n)−1=7."
SC-A=H (uniqueness of even/odd factorization), SC-B=H (3 lines), SC-C=H (WHY: parity uniqueness of factorization), SC-D=H, SC-E=H (parity argument generalizes), SC-F=H (surprising reframe of the problem) → 6H → 1.0

ANTI-PATTERNS
• SHORT ≠ ELEGANT. A one-line answer that states the result without mechanism → 0.0 or 0.25.
• CORRECT MATHEMATICAL VOCABULARY ≠ INSIGHT. "By Inclusion-Exclusion..." names a tool, not an insight; still 0.25.
• LONG ≠ INELEGANT. A long proof can score 0.75 if the core idea is clearly named and all steps follow from it.
• ADVANCED TOPIC ≠ INSIGHTFUL. Using Hamel bases, measure theory, or algebraic topology in a routine way is not elegant.
• COMPUTER-ASSISTED answers that pivot to explain the mathematical WHY behind the output may partially recover SC-C; but SC-B and SC-D remain L.
