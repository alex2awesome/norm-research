ELEGANCE AND INSIGHT RUBRIC — Math.SE Answers (v2)

An answer is ELEGANT and INSIGHTFUL when it reveals WHY a result is true through a minimal, well-chosen idea — NOT merely when it is correct, pedagogically organized, or uses correct mathematical language.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 1 — SCORE EACH SUB-CRITERION (H=high, M=mid, L=low)

SC-A  CONCEPTUAL CORE: Does the answer name a single structural or conceptual reason why the result holds?
  H: Points to a key structural fact (e.g., symmetry, contradiction, invariant, pigeonhole) that immediately entails the result.
  M: Identifies an approach but buries the core reason in algebra or steps.
  L / ZERO: Works through an algorithm, applies a standard formula, or merely states facts without naming the underlying mechanism.

SC-B  ECONOMY OF MEANS: Is the key idea delivered compactly, without unnecessary scaffolding?
  H: The insight could be stated in 1–3 sentences; any extra steps are clearly subordinate illustration.
  M: Reasonably concise but includes some redundant steps.
  L: Long chains of algebraic manipulation, case splits, or formula-substitution where a shorter path exists.

SC-C  EXPLANATORY FORCE (WHY not HOW): After reading, does a student understand the mechanism — not just how to replicate the calculation?
  H: Reveals a principle or perspective that transfers beyond this problem.
  M: Correct and somewhat explanatory, but primarily shows HOW to compute rather than WHY the computation works.
  L: Purely procedural: each step is justified locally but no higher-level understanding is transmitted.

SC-D  AVOIDANCE OF MECHANICAL GRINDING: Does the answer deliberately avoid exhaustive case-bashing, computer enumeration, or brute-force symbol-manipulation when a principled shortcut exists?
  H: Explicitly avoids or critiques a brute-force approach; takes the principled path.
  M: Mechanical but the problem genuinely offers no elegant shortcut.
  L: Uses Wolfram Alpha / computer output, case enumeration, or plug-and-grind when a conceptual route is available.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 2 — ASSIGN THE FINAL SCORE

Count H scores: 4H → 1.0 | 3H → 0.75 | 2H → 0.5 | 1H → 0.25 | 0H → 0.0

If SC-A is L or ZERO, the answer CANNOT score above 0.25, regardless of other sub-criteria.

WORKED EXAMPLES

--- SCORE 0.0 ---
Q: [Request for reference books on homological algebra]
A: "Here's link to a textbook on local cohomology: link."
WHY 0.0: No mathematical explanation at all — bare hyperlink. SC-A=L, SC-B=L, SC-C=L, SC-D=M → 0H → 0.0

--- SCORE 0.25 ---
Q: How many 6-letter strings contain both 'a' and 'b'?
A: "You subtracted strings without 'a' and strings without 'b'. But you double-counted strings with neither. By Inclusion-Exclusion: 26^6 − 2·25^6 + 24^6. For part (c), treat 'ab' as a block: choose 4 letters from 24, arrange 5 objects → C(24,4)·5!. For part (d): same selection, half of 6! orderings have a before b → C(24,4)·6!/2."
WHY 0.25: Correct and well-organized, but purely procedural — applies Inclusion-Exclusion as a formula and block-counting as a technique. Does not explain WHY Inclusion-Exclusion corrects double-counting beyond stating it. SC-A=M (names I-E but doesn't illuminate it), SC-B=M (organized but multi-step), SC-C=L (HOW not WHY), SC-D=M → 1H → 0.25

--- SCORE 0.5 ---
Q: Compute lim(x→π/3) sin(x-π/3)/(1-2cos x) without L'Hôpital.
A: "Substitute u = x − π/3. The limit becomes lim(u→0) sin(u)/(1−2cos(u+π/3)). Expand cos(u+π/3) = cos(u)·(1/2)−sin(u)·(√3/2). Then 1−2cos(u+π/3) = 1−cos(u)+√3 sin(u) → √3 u as u→0, so the limit is 1/√3."
WHY 0.5: The substitution u = x−π/3 is a clever pivot (SC-A=H), making the singularity transparent. But the rest is algebraic expansion (SC-C=M, SC-D=M). Two highs → 0.5.

--- SCORE 0.75 ---
Q: Prove that |√x − √y| ≤ √|x−y| for x,y ≥ 0.
A: "Multiply both sides by (√x+√y): |x−y| ≤ √|x−y|·(√x+√y). Divide by √|x−y|: √|x−y| ≤ √x+√y, which holds since √|x−y| ≤ √x + √y by triangle inequality on square roots."
WHY 0.75: Identifies the key move (rationalize by conjugate) and the structural reason (triangle inequality on square roots). SC-A=H, SC-C=H, SC-B=H. But includes some algebraic bookkeeping. 3H → 0.75.

--- SCORE 1.0 ---
Q: Is A = [[6,0],[-2,2]] diagonalizable over ℝ?
A: "Nothing to calculate: A is lower triangular, so the eigenvalues are the diagonal entries 2 and 6. These are distinct and real, so A is diagonalizable over ℝ."
WHY 1.0: Single structural observation (lower-triangular ⟹ eigenvalues on diagonal) immediately resolves the problem. No computation needed. SC-A=H, SC-B=H, SC-C=H, SC-D=H → 1.0.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
ANTI-PATTERNS (common judge errors)
• Do NOT reward an answer just because it is SHORT — a one-line answer that merely states the answer without reasoning is 0.0.
• Do NOT reward an answer for correct mathematical vocabulary — "by Inclusion-Exclusion" names a tool, not an insight.
• Do NOT penalize LENGTH if the extra content is genuine illustration — a long proof can score 0.75 if the core idea is clearly named.
• A pedagogically organized step-by-step solution to a routine problem (counting, simple algebra, standard calculus) scores 0.25 at most.
