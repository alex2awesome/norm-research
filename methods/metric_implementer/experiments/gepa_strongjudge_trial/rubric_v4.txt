ELEGANCE AND INSIGHT RUBRIC — Math.SE Answers (v4 — Final)

Score a math answer on how elegantly and insightfully it reveals WHY a result is true through a minimal, well-chosen idea. This is DIFFERENT from being correct, well-explained, or using advanced mathematics.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
FOUR SUB-CRITERIA (rate each: H=high, M=mid, L=low)

SC-A  CONCEPTUAL CORE — Does the answer name the structural reason WHY the result holds?
  H: Points to a key structural fact (e.g., parity uniqueness, lower-triangular form, diagonal argument, pigeonhole) that immediately entails the result.
  M: Identifies an approach by name (e.g., "Inclusion-Exclusion") but does not explain why that approach delivers the answer.
  L or ZERO: Applies an algorithm step-by-step without naming any underlying mechanism. Or gives only a result/link with no reasoning.

SC-B  ECONOMY OF MEANS — Is the insight delivered compactly?
  H: The core idea fits in 1–3 key sentences. Any additional steps are subordinate illustration, not the main argument.
  M: Reasonably concise but contains some redundant or over-explained steps.
  L: A long chain of algebraic manipulation, multi-case analysis, or formula-substitution where a shorter principled path is available.

SC-C  EXPLANATORY FORCE — Does it explain WHY, transferably?
  H: After reading, a student understands a principle that would help them solve a similar problem without re-deriving from scratch.
  M: Mostly shows HOW to perform the computation correctly; some insight is present but not prominently stated.
  L: Purely procedural. Each step is locally justified but the overall mechanism is never named.

SC-D  AVOIDANCE OF BRUTE FORCE — Does the answer avoid mechanical grinding when a principled shortcut exists?
  H: Explicitly takes the principled route and avoids case enumeration, computer search, or symbol-grinding.
  M: Method is somewhat mechanical but the problem genuinely offers no obvious elegant shortcut.
  L: Uses Wolfram Alpha, computer code output, or exhaustive case analysis as the primary solver when a conceptual path was available.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 2 — ASSIGN FINAL SCORE

Apply HARD CAPS first:
  • SC-A = L or ZERO → score ≤ 0.25 (no matter how good the other sub-criteria).
  • SC-C = L → score ≤ 0.5.

Then count H-rated sub-criteria:
  4H → 1.0  |  3H → 0.75  |  2H → 0.5  |  1H → 0.25  |  0H → 0.0

CALIBRATION EXAMPLES (five score levels)

▶ SCORE 0.0
Q: Recommend resources for homological algebra.
A: "Here's a link to a textbook on local cohomology: link."
SUB-CRITERIA: SC-A=ZERO, SC-B=L, SC-C=L, SC-D=M → 0H, SC-A cap → 0.0
LESSON: No mathematical content means 0.0 regardless of length.

▶ SCORE 0.25
Q: How many 6-letter strings over the alphabet contain both 'a' and 'b'?
A: "Subtract strings without 'a' and without 'b', add back strings with neither: 26^6−2·25^6+24^6. For consecutive 'ab': treat 'ab' as a block, pick 4 more from 24, arrange 5 objects: C(24,4)·5!. For 'a' before 'b': C(24,4)·6!/2."
SUB-CRITERIA: SC-A=M (names Inclusion-Exclusion but explains why only vaguely), SC-B=M, SC-C=L (purely shows HOW to count; does not explain the general set-theoretic reason), SC-D=M → SC-C=L cap → ≤ 0.5; only 0H anyway → 0.25
LESSON: Correct and pedagogically organized ≠ elegant. Naming a technique is not the same as explaining the structural reason it works.

▶ SCORE 0.5
Q: Compute lim(x→π/3) sin(x−π/3)/(1−2cos x) without L'Hôpital.
A: "Substitute u = x − π/3. Denominator becomes 1−2cos(u+π/3). Expanding, this is approximately √3·u near u=0. So the limit is sin(u)/(√3·u) → 1/√3."
SUB-CRITERIA: SC-A=H (substitution makes the singularity transparent), SC-B=H (compact), SC-C=M (partially explains WHY the substitution works, but the final step is algebraic), SC-D=M → 2H → 0.5
LESSON: A clever first move (good SC-A) that resolves into algebra rather than structural insight caps at 0.5.

▶ SCORE 0.75
Q: Prove |√x − √y| ≤ √|x−y| for x,y ≥ 0.
A: "Multiply: |√x−√y|·(√x+√y) = |x−y|. Since √x+√y ≥ √|x−y| (triangle inequality on square roots), dividing gives |√x−√y| ≤ √|x−y|."
SUB-CRITERIA: SC-A=H (conjugate rationalization is the structural move), SC-B=H (2-step), SC-C=H (explains the mechanism; student can reuse the conjugate trick), SC-D=H (avoids squaring both sides and tracking cases) → 4H → but wait: this is already 4H so it should be 1.0.
CORRECTION: Assign 0.75 here because the triangle-inequality step is invoked but not fully explained. A truly 1.0 answer would either (a) name a deeper reason or (b) avoid invoking another non-trivial inequality.

▶ SCORE 0.75 (second example, for clarity)
Q: [Bijection/composition proof]
A: "If g∘f is bijective, note: for any c∈C, (g∘f)(a)=c has a unique solution a, so g(f(a))=c shows g is surjective. For injectivity of f: if f(a₁)=f(a₂) then g(f(a₁))=g(f(a₂)), so a₁=a₂ by injectivity of g∘f."
SUB-CRITERIA: SC-A=H, SC-B=H, SC-C=H (explains the chain-of-deduction), SC-D=M → 3H → 0.75
LESSON: 3H maps to 0.75; a very clean structural argument that still uses M on one sub-criterion.

▶ SCORE 1.0
Q: Is A=[[6,0],[-2,2]] diagonalizable over ℝ?
A: "Nothing to calculate: A is lower-triangular, so eigenvalues = diagonal entries = 2 and 6. Distinct real eigenvalues ⟹ diagonalizable over ℝ."
SUB-CRITERIA: SC-A=H (lower-triangular structure), SC-B=H (one observation resolves everything), SC-C=H (names a general principle about triangular matrices), SC-D=H (explicitly avoids computing the characteristic polynomial) → 4H → 1.0

▶ SCORE 1.0 (second example)
Q: Find natural numbers m,n with 2^m − 2^n = 112.
A: "Factor: 2^n·(2^(m−n)−1) = 2^4·7. Left factor is even, right is odd. By uniqueness of even/odd factorization: 2^n=2^4 and 2^(m−n)−1=7, giving n=4, m=7."
SUB-CRITERIA: SC-A=H (parity uniqueness of factorization), SC-B=H (3 lines), SC-C=H (WHY: parity forces unique split), SC-D=H (no trial-and-error) → 4H → 1.0

─────────────────────────────────────────────
CRITICAL ANTI-PATTERNS
• CORRECT + ORGANIZED ≠ ELEGANT. A well-structured walkthrough of Inclusion-Exclusion, substitution, or matrix row-reduction is ≤ 0.25 if it never names WHY the method works.
• SHORT ≠ INSIGHTFUL. A one-line answer that just states the result → 0.0.
• ADVANCED TOPIC ≠ INSIGHTFUL. Using Hamel bases, Zorn's lemma, or Fourier analysis mechanically is not elegant — it must reveal WHY.
• NAMING A THEOREM ≠ INSIGHT. Saying "by Inclusion-Exclusion" or "by the Pigeonhole Principle" names the tool; insight explains WHY that tool delivers this result here.
