You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about research quality. It is about the comic idea, the
      craft, the wording choices, the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself research quality: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: abstracts of machine-learning conference paper submissions. Each item is a abstract.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
research quality too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Actionable interpretability
Give 10 when analysis exposes internal structure that supports prediction, intervention, control, or a substantive scientific explanation and validates that use; give 0 when interpretability is only visualization or post hoc labeling.

[X02] Counter-Intuitive Scientific Finding
Score 10 if the abstract highlights a surprising or paradoxical phenomenon that challenges conventional wisdom (e.g., 'removing clean images boosts robustness'). Score 0 if the results are exactly what one would expect from standard methods.

[X03] Operational specificity of what was built and evaluated
Score 0-10 for how precisely the abstract pins down the object of the work and the conditions under which it was examined, so that an independent reader could say exactly what exists and exactly what was done to it. Look for: what precisely was constructed or proposed (its components, inputs and outputs, or the procedure); the data or environment it was applied to, named rather than gestured at; scale or size where scale is part of the claim; what it was compared against; and the evaluation conditions, protocol or measure by which the claim is supported. 10 = the object and the conditions are stated with enough precision that a reader could restate the setup without guessing, and any quantitative claim is attached to a named measure and a named comparison; a short, plainly written, unfashionable abstract that does this MUST be able to score 10. 0 = the abstract leaves the object generic ('a new framework', 'a large-scale study', 'a comprehensive benchmark') with no named data, no named comparison, and no stated conditions, so the reader cannot tell what was actually built or what was actually run. CRITICAL: award NO points for any statement that code, data, models, checkpoints, demos or project pages are available or will be released, for URLs, repository names, licences, or release promises, and deduct nothing for their absence. Announcing an artifact is not specifying one. An abstract that announces several released artifacts but describes its object only in generic terms must score 0; an abstract that announces no release at all but specifies its object exactly must score 10.

[X04] Depth of empirical and engineering execution actually described
Score 0-10 for how much executed work the abstract describes, weighed against the breadth of what it claims. Evidence of execution includes: the number and diversity of settings, domains, datasets, tasks or model families actually examined; controlled comparisons, ablations or component-wise analyses; examination of failure cases, negative results, robustness or stress conditions; system-level or scale-level implementation where the claim needs it; and explicit statement of the conditions and limits under which the reported result holds. Judge in context: a narrow, carefully scoped claim supported by one clean, well-specified study is fully executed and can score 10; a sweeping claim supported by one demonstration is not. 10 = the described work clearly covers the ground its own claim requires, with comparison or ablation and with stated scope conditions; a short, plainly written, unfashionable abstract MUST be able to reach 10. 0 = a broad claim with no described comparison, no conditions, and no indication that anything beyond a single unexamined demonstration was carried out. CRITICAL: award NO points for statements that code, data, checkpoints or project pages are or will be available, for links or repository names, for reproducibility or compliance boilerplate, for named infrastructure, tooling, hardware or compute, or for any mention of who did the work or with what resources; these are not execution. An abstract loaded with release announcements and infrastructure vocabulary but describing no comparison, no ablation and no conditions must score 0.

[X05] Branded system or acronym naming
Score the extent to which the abstract coins or foregrounds a catchy system name or acronym (e.g., “ImpScore”, “Point-SAM”, “VariBAD”, “ProtoSnap”). Score 0 for no coined name, 10 for multiple emphasized branded names. Do not judge whether naming is effective; score only how much branding is present.

[X06] Surface polish and typographic perfection
Score the extent to which the abstract is free of typos, grammatical errors, and formatting quirks, exhibiting a uniformly professional copyedited style. Score 0 for many errors, 10 for flawless polish. Do not judge whether polish makes the paper better; score only how clean the text is.

[X07] Abstract Word Count
Score the extent to which the abstract is exceptionally long or wordy, from 0 (very short/concise) to 10 (excessively long/verbose). Do not judge the quality of the writing, only the sheer volume of text.

[X08] Proportion of sentences written in the passive voice
Score 0-10 for what fraction of the sentences are in the passive voice. 10 = essentially every sentence is passive; 0 = every sentence is active. Count the grammatical construction only; do not judge whether passive voice is good or bad here.

[X09] Public-release and link boilerplate (code, data, project page)
Rate 0-10 the extent to which the text contains explicit availability or release statements: full URLs (GitHub repositories, project pages, university or lab sites, shortened links), and phrases such as 'code is available', 'publicly available', 'we release', 'our data and code can be found at', 'model weights are public', 'see our project page'. 0 = no availability statement and no URL anywhere; 5 = a bare availability phrase with no location; 10 = multiple release statements and one or more full URLs, prominently placed. Score only the presence, count and prominence of release/availability language. You must NOT judge whether releasing artifacts is good practice, whether the release seems credible or complete, whether the work looks reproducible, or whether the hosting location seems reputable.

[X10] Explanatory mechanism depth
Give 10 when the work explains why a phenomenon occurs using a coherent mechanism, theory, or diagnostic analysis; give 0 when it mainly reports a method and aggregate empirical gains without explanation.

[X11] Defensive Consensus Challenging
Score the extent to which the abstract uses explicit phrasing to claim it is challenging a consensus, debunking an open secret, or resolving a widespread misconception, from 0 to 10. Do not judge if the consensus is real.

[X12] Something survives when all benchmark numbers are deleted
Mentally strike every leaderboard number, percentage and speedup from the abstract and score what remains. 10: a substantive claim remains standing -- a mechanism, a limit, a phenomenon, a design principle -- that a reader could carry into other work; the numbers were evidence for it rather than the content of it. 5: a modest transferable claim remains alongside numbers that do most of the work. 0: what remains is only 'we propose X and it performs better than baselines'; the numbers were the claim.

[X13] Specific checkable content about its own contribution, length-independent
Score 0-10 for how much specific, checkable research content the abstract actually conveys about its own contribution, judged relative to what it claims to have done -- NOT for how much text there is. Ask four questions: (a) is the problem stated concretely enough that one could say what would and would not count as an instance of it; (b) is the proposed idea described by its mechanism, so a knowledgeable reader could paraphrase how it works, rather than only by a name or a slogan; (c) is the setting in which it was examined named (data, domain, task, scale, comparison points, or conditions); (d) is the stated finding specific rather than a generic assertion of effectiveness. 10 = every claim the abstract makes is pinned to concrete, non-interchangeable content on all four questions; a plainly written three-or-four-sentence abstract with no hedging, no elaborate qualification, and no fashionable vocabulary MUST score 10 if those few sentences are fully specific. 0 = the text consists of statements that could be transplanted unchanged into almost any paper in the field ('we propose a novel framework', 'extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach') with no mechanism, no named setting, and no specific claim; a very long, heavily qualified, elaborately written abstract MUST score 0 if that is all it contains. Explicitly do NOT reward or penalise word count, sentence count, density of qualifying or elaborating clauses, hedging, polish, or vocabulary fashion in either direction. Length is neither evidence for nor against content. Where length and content point in different directions, score content only.

[X14] Novel Core Architectural Primitive
Score 10 if the abstract introduces a completely new mathematical operation or topological structure at the core of a neural network (e.g., higher-dimensional attention, novel tensor products). Score 0 if it uses standard architectures with only external modules or hyperparameter tweaks.

[X15] Count and prominence of artifact-release advertising
Score 0-10 for EXTENT ONLY. Count how much the abstract advertises artifacts, using four observable quantities: (a) how many DISTINCT artifact types are mentioned or promised (code, data or datasets, models or checkpoints or weights, project or demo or leaderboard pages, annotations, benchmarks); (b) whether a concrete link, URL, repository name or hosting site is given; (c) how prominently the advertising sits (a trailing final clause is less prominent than a standalone sentence, which is less prominent than placement in the opening or as part of the headline contribution); (d) how many separate times artifact availability is referred to. 0 = no mention of any artifact being available or promised. 3 = one artifact type mentioned in passing, no link. 5 = one or two artifact types clearly promised. 8 = several artifact types promised, or one promised together with a concrete link. 10 = multiple artifact types plus a concrete link or repository, advertised prominently and referred to more than once. You are explicitly NOT deciding whether releasing artifacts is good, whether the promise is likely to be kept, whether the artifacts would be useful, complete, usable or reproducible, and you are not judging the research in any way. An abstract with no substance at all can and should score 10 here if it carries the markers.

[X16] Explains an existing phenomenon vs. names a new system
Score 0-10 on whether the abstract's primary contribution is characterizing/explaining an already-observed phenomenon versus introducing a new named architecture, model, or system as the headline deliverable. A 10 is a pure analysis/characterization paper with no new named system. A 0 leads with a new named model/system as the headline contribution.

[X17] Rigorous Bounds vs Heuristic Trade-offs
0 = discovers an empirical, ad-hoc trade-off between two conflicting metrics. 10 = mathematically establishes rigorous bounds, optimal trade-offs, or fundamental optimality conditions.

[X18] Math Notation Density
Score the extent to which the abstract contains dense mathematical notation, LaTeX formatting, variables, and formal definitions, from 0 (none) to 10 (very dense). Do not judge the correctness of the math, only its visible presence.

[X19] Use of the Oxford (serial) comma in lists
Score 0-10 for how consistently the text places a comma before the final conjunction in a list. 10 = used in every list; 0 = never used, or there are no lists. Judge the punctuation habit only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X20] States a concrete limitation or scope condition of its own claim
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the abstract names a condition under which its own claim would not hold, or a case it does not cover. 10 = a specific, checkable limitation or scope condition is stated; 0 = no limitation or scope condition is acknowledged anywhere.

[X21] Structure-matched inductive bias
Give 10 when the representation or architecture encodes a real structural property of the domain and the abstract explains why that structure matters; give 0 when the architecture is generic or structurally unmotivated.

[X22] Prominence of explicit availability and release announcements
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of explicit release and availability announcement language in the abstract, counting only the announcement itself. Count as announcements: a code URL; a GitHub, project-page, website or hosting link; phrases such as 'code is available', 'our data and code will be released', 'code has been released', 'publicly available at'; the naming of released checkpoints, weights, benchmarks, annotations, leaderboards or datasets as deliverables to be handed over; and explicit reproducibility or compliance statements ('we release everything needed to reproduce our results'). 0 = no availability statement of any kind. 3 = a bare promise of future release with no link. 6 = one concrete link, or one clearly named released artifact. 8 = two or three artifacts announced together, or one artifact plus a link plus a reproducibility statement. 10 = several artifacts announced together (for example code plus data plus model weights plus a project page), or announcements repeated in more than one place in the abstract. You must NOT assess whether releasing is good practice, whether the promise is credible, whether the link looks maintained, or whether the artifact is valuable, and you must not judge the research. Count only how much availability announcement text is present and how prominently it is placed. An item whose entire announcement is boilerplate attached to empty content still scores 10 if the boilerplate is complete.

[X23] Gives a mechanism for why the proposed approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the abstract supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X24] Failure-mode-to-mechanism fit
Score 10 when the abstract identifies a specific bottleneck and gives an intervention whose mechanism directly addresses it; score 0 when the method is generic or disconnected from the stated problem.

[X25] Sheer text volume and density of qualifying clauses
Score 0-10 for EXTENT ONLY: how much text there is and how elaborated it is. Estimate three things and combine them: (a) approximate word count; (b) number of sentences; (c) density of qualifying, hedging, appositive or elaborating clauses per sentence (subordinate clauses, parentheticals, 'which', 'while', 'although', 'notably', 'in particular', strings of adjectives, enumerated sub-claims). 0 = extremely terse, roughly two or three short simple sentences, well under about 60 words, essentially no subordinate clauses. 3 = brief, about 60-100 words, mostly simple sentences. 5 = mid-length, about 100-160 words, occasional subordinate clauses. 8 = long, about 160-230 words, or shorter but with heavy multi-clause qualification in most sentences. 10 = very long and heavily elaborated, roughly 230 words or more, many sentences, most of them carrying multiple qualifying or elaborating clauses. You are explicitly NOT deciding whether the length is appropriate, whether the qualification is warranted, whether the writing is clear or bloated, or whether the research is any good. Do not let informativeness raise or lower this score: a long abstract that is packed with substance and a long abstract that is empty receive the same score if they are the same length and elaboration.

[X26] Simplifying conceptual payoff
Score 10 when the work removes unnecessary components, stages, or learned machinery while preserving the intended function and explaining why; score 0 when it only adds complexity.

[X27] Causal intervention quality
Score 10 when the study changes targeted factors or uses counterfactual controls to distinguish competing mechanisms; score 0 when it merely correlates observations with outcomes or compares opaque systems.

[X28] Contemporary LLM/agent-era vocabulary density
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much of the abstract's vocabulary belongs to the current large-language-model/agent wave (terms like 'large language models', 'hallucination', 'context-faithfulness', 'agents', 'in-context', 'proactive', 'self-updatable') as opposed to classical statistical/ML-theory vocabulary. 0 = no such terms present; 10 = the abstract is saturated with this vocabulary. Score only the density of this topical vocabulary, not whether the topic choice itself is a good or important one.

[X29] Recasts the problem in a formal frame that buys leverage
Score whether a known problem is restated inside a different, precisely named formal frame, and whether the reframing purchases something concrete. 10: the abstract names the reformulation (an optimisation problem, transport between distributions, an extension of a classical theorem, an information-theoretic quantity) AND names what it yields -- a derivation, an explicit formula, a near-optimality statement, a bound, a new estimator. 5: a reformulation is announced but nothing specific follows from it. 0: the problem is stated in its standard form and attacked with the standard toolkit for that form.
