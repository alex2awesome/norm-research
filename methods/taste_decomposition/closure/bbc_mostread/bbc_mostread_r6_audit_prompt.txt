[X02] Practical service promise
High when the headline promises advice, instructions, or help with a decision; low when it offers no usable guidance; it does not apply without a service frame

[X03] Mystery-death hook
Score high when an unexplained death or unexplained cause is the core hook ('something in room', serial killings, radiation spike). Score low when cause of harm is stated.

[X04] Ideological Polarization

[X07] Social-Identity Resonance
Scores high when the headline invokes a shared group identity, belonging, or identity-related value; low when no group identity is implicated. It does not apply when relevance requires personal details absent from the headline.

[X08] Withholds exactly the detail the headline makes the reader want
Score 0-10 for whether the headline sets up a specific, legitimate question in the reader's mind whose answer is in the story -- who, how much, what happened next -- without being deceptive about what the story contains. 10 = a precise curiosity gap a reader can only close by reading; 0 = either everything is already in the headline or the gap is vague clickbait that the story cannot pay off.

[X11] Concrete Scene Vividness

[X12] In-quote ordinary voice

[X13] Numerical specificity
Mark digits, percentages, ages, counts, or precise quantities. This may fingerprint data-led, health, or breaking-news treatments.

[X14] First-person voice
Score high when the headline contains a first-person pronoun or quoted speech ('I', 'we', 'our', 'litres and litres'). Score low when it is entirely third-person institutional reporting.

[X15] Source Transparency
Scores high when the headline identifies a source, evidence, or attribution that clarifies claim provenance; low when it presents an unsupported assertion. It does not apply to plainly observable events without contested claims.

[X16] Follow-up headline ellipticalness
Headline omits full names/events and relies on assumed prior knowledge ("Why Donald Trump lost", "The biggest news wasn't about Manafort"), signalling day 2+ of a running story.

[X18] Explicit question-mark headline

[X19] Explicit emotion word

[X20] Signals direct consequence for the reader's own life

[X21] Explanation or analysis marker
Score high when the headline signals explainer or analytical framing ('Why...', 'Explaining...', 'The rise and fall'). Score low for breaking-event headlines.

[X22] Curiosity gap
Score high when the headline withholds key information the reader must click to resolve ('this photo', 'wasn't about Manafort', 'something in room'). Score low when the headline states its own conclusion.

[X23] Personal welfare stakes
High when it signals direct effects on people’s health, safety, rights, or daily routines; low when consequences remain remote or institutional; it does not apply without individual consequences

Score 0-10 for occurrences of the literal tokens 'you', 'your' or 'yours' in the headline. 10 = several occurrences or one in the opening two words; 0 = none. Count the tokens only; do not judge whether the story actually concerns the reader.

[X25] Headline word count

[X26] Scare-quoted scepticism marker
Single quoted word casting doubt ('deceptive', 'unidentified', 'no value'). Scores low-to-moderate; hedge weakens urgency

[X27] POLARIZATION
Scores high when the headline presents opposing camps or a divisive proposition; low when it describes consensus or a technical matter. It does not apply when disagreement is not explicitly stated

[X28] Mundane-relatable action
Score high when the headline centres on an everyday, ordinary activity framed as remarkable (cutting a carrot, losing a teddy bear). Score low when actions are institutional or geopolitical.

[X29] Reader-addressing question
