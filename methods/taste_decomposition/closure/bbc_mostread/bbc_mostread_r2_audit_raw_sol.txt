CRITERIA:

[X01] Humor or playfulness
Scores high when puns, irony, comic phrasing, or whimsical premises are explicit; low when wording is sober and literal. Does not apply when no playful cue is present

[X02] List or Ranking Frame
High when the headline uses an explicit list, count, ranking, comparison, or superlative; low when it presents one undifferentiated item. Does not apply when none of these structures appears.

[X03] House-style punctuation pattern
DESCRIPTION: Mark distinctive use of quotation marks, apostrophes, colons, dashes, ellipses, or sentence fragments.

[X04] Truncated final phrase

[X05] Scene Imaginability
<=8 words | DESCRIPTION: High when the wording evokes a specific, observable scene or action a reader can readily picture; low when it remains abstract or bureaucratic. It does not apply to purely conceptual claims.

[X06] Conversational discussability
Scores high when the headline states a contestable claim or explicit reaction prompt; low when it offers no apparent basis for discussion. Does not apply without a social or evaluative angle

[X07] NON-WESTERN ELITE OR STRONGMAN ACTION
DESCRIPTION: Headlines focusing on political elites in Africa, Asia, or South America score 1. Western elite actions score 0.

[X08] Health bodily function or taboo
Headlines using explicit biological or sexual terms score high; headlines about general wellness score low.

[X09] First-Person Testimony
High when the headline foregrounds quoted words, confession, testimony, or a personal account; low when reporting impersonal facts. Does not apply when no personal voice or account is signaled.

[X10] Signals direct consequence for the reader's own life
Score 0-10 for whether the headline makes clear that the story affects the reader personally -- their money, health, commute, bills, weather, rights -- and roughly how. 10 = the personal stake is explicit and concrete; 0 = no implication that the reader's own circumstances are touched.

[X11] UNRESOLVED MYSTERY HOOKS
Poses unresolved question | DESCRIPTION: Headline ends with a question mark or uses phrases like 'what happened after' without resolving the outcome in the text.

[X12] Contains the word 'you' or 'your'
Score 0-10 for occurrences of the literal tokens 'you', 'your' or 'yours' in the headline. 10 = several occurrences or one in the opening two words; 0 = none. Count the tokens only; do not judge whether the story actually concerns the reader.

[X13] First-person or quoted voice
DESCRIPTION: Mark headlines containing first-person language or a quotation attributed to a speaker.

[X14] Norm Violation
High when the headline describes conduct likely to trigger moral judgment, embarrassment, taboo, or scandal; low when conduct is ordinary or administrative. Does not apply when no behavior or statement can be evaluated against a social norm.

[X15] Personal Consequence
High when the headline signals an immediate consequence for ordinary readers’ health, plans, safety, possessions, or choices; low when effects are abstract or remote. Does not apply without a plausible personal consequence.

[X16] SCARE-QUOTED FIRST-PERSON HOOK
DESCRIPTION: Headline opens with or prominently features a first-person perspective enclosed in single or double quotation marks.

[X17] Clarity Of Main Event
<=8 words | DESCRIPTION: High when a reader can immediately identify who did what, where, and with what result; low when the event is vague or syntactically unclear. It does not apply when the headline intentionally withholds the event.

[X18] Withholds exactly the detail the headline makes the reader want
Score 0-10 for whether the headline sets up a specific, legitimate question in the reader's mind whose answer is in the story -- who, how much, what happened next -- without being deceptive about what the story contains. 10 = a precise curiosity gap a reader can only close by reading; 0 = either everything is already in the headline or the gap is vague clickbait that the story cannot pay off.

[X19] Proper-name density
Headline contains at least two capitalized proper-name tokens, excluding sentence-initial capitalization.

[X20] Visual or tabloid curiosity juxtaposition
Headline pairs unusual, dramatic, or juxtaposed imagery (e.g. 'nun and monk', 'taxi and boat', 'jogger trashed') that appeals directly to visual or voyeuristic curiosity.

[X21] Actionability
<=8 words | DESCRIPTION: Scores high when it gives advice, instructions, or a clear behavior-relevant takeaway; low when no reader action is implied. Do not apply when the headline makes no practical promise.

[X22] Curiosity Gap
<=8 words | DESCRIPTION: Scores high when a consequential fact is withheld while an answer is promised; low when the headline states the complete point. Do not apply when no key information is withheld.

[X23] Headline brevity
Headline has six or fewer words or visibly compressed syntax.

[X24] RESOLUTION UPDATES
Phrase uses non-final verbs | DESCRIPTION: Headline indicates an ongoing event has a new update via words like 'first time', 'new', 're-arrested', or 'resumes'.

[X25] High-Arousal Emotion
Headlines evoking intense anger, outrage, or tragedy ("misery", "trashed", "dead") score high. Neutral diplomacy or analysis score low.

[X26] Narrative trajectory
Scores high when the headline signals progression, aftermath, return, reversal, or a before-and-after relation; low when it describes a static condition. Does not apply without a temporal or sequential relationship

[X27] Listicle or service format
DESCRIPTION: Mark numbered headlines and explicit formats such as “reasons,” “guide,” or “what to know.”

[X28] CLICKABLE WORDPLAY OR PUNS
DESCRIPTION: Headlines using idioms, puns, or clever wording to describe an event score 1. Dry literal headlines score 0.

[X29] Headline word count
Score 0-10 for how long the headline is in words. 10 = very long (13+ words); 5 = around 8-9 words; 0 = 4 words or fewer. Judge extent only -- do not consider whether the length serves the story.
