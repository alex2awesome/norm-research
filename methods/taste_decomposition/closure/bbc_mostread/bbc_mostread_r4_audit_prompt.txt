Score high when the headline implies usable consequences for personal safety, rights, health, travel, or finances; score low when no personal decision is suggested. Do not apply when reader action is not inferable.

[X02] Signals direct consequence for the reader's own life
Score 0-10 for whether the headline makes clear that the story affects the reader personally -- their money, health, commute, bills, weather, rights -- and roughly how. 10 = the personal stake is explicit and concrete; 0 = no implication that the reader's own circumstances are touched.

[X03] Withholds exactly the detail the headline makes the reader want

[X04] Emotional arousal

[X05] Cultural touchstone

[X06] Personal consequence
DESCRIPTION: Scores high when the subject plausibly affects an ordinary reader’s own health, safety, money, rights, or family. Scores low when consequences are remote or abstract; does not apply without a personal implication.

[X08] Goal Conflict

[X09] Expectation Reversal

[X10] Punchy single quote marker

[X11] CURIOSITY GAP

[X12] International place-name density

[X13] Bizarre Novelty Contrast

[X14] Direct Pronoun Posessive Hook
Headlines leading with a first-person quote ("I can tell", "my family") score high. Third-person institutional actions score low.

[X15] Contains the word 'you' or 'your'
Score 0-10 for occurrences of the literal tokens 'you', 'your' or 'yours' in the headline. 10 = several occurrences or one in the opening two words; 0 = none. Count the tokens only; do not judge whether the story actually concerns the reader.

[X16] Evidence or reveal cue

[X17] Ominous but vague future threat

[X18] Headline word count

[X19] Plain-language accessibility
Score high when wording is familiar, direct, and syntactically simple; score low when it relies on jargon, convoluted syntax, or obscure references. Do not apply when specialized terminology is unavoidable and clearly explained.

[X21] Headline length
DESCRIPTION: Count the headline’s words; unusually short or long headlines form a measurable format proxy.

[X22] Explanatory promise
DESCRIPTION: Scores high when the wording promises to explain why, how, or what happened. Scores low when it merely announces an event; does not apply without an implied explanatory question.

[X23] Analytical interrogative format
Opens with a question word (e.g., "How," "Why") implying an explanatory or analytical article.

[X24] Human-interest vocabulary

[X25] VISUAL SCENEABILITY

[X27] Casual or sensational colloquialism

[X28] Playful or quirky phrasing
