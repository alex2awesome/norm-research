You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the caption is as a contest entry. It is about the substance, the
      reasoning, the evidence, the craft, or the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the caption is as a contest entry: length or verbosity, formatting or layout,
      boilerplate or template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter
      or topic markers, temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of
      some upstream circumstance of the item's production (who made it, with what
      resources, when, with what help).

The corpus is: reader-submitted captions for a weekly cartoon caption contest. Each item is a caption.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
well-resourced producer may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually do
better work). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces
real how good the caption is as a contest entry too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you
label "quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Staccato repetition or fragmenting punctuation
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the caption uses repeated short clauses, sentence fragments, or unusual punctuation (ellipses, an isolated single word set off by a period, trailing dashes) purely for rhythmic emphasis. 0 = normal single-clause punctuation; 10 = heavy fragmenting/repetition for emphasis. Score only the presence of this typographic/rhythmic device, not whether the emphasis is effective.

[X02] Freshness over stockness
Score 10 when the premise and wording take an uncommon route and avoid familiar joke templates; score 0 when the line is canned banter, a predictable invitation, or a stock pun.

[X03] Stock dialogue scaffolding
On a 0-10 scale, score only how much the caption uses familiar conversational setups, such as greetings, invitations, disclaimers, or routine dialogue openers; do not judge whether the setup works.

[X04] Vivid scene specificity
Score 10 when the caption evokes a concrete, unusual scene with identifiable roles, objects, and actions; score 0 when it could be pasted into many unrelated scenes without change.

[X05] No self-commentary on its own funniness
Score whether the caption stays inside its situation or steps out to appraise the joke. 0 = the caption labels its own material as amusing, ironic, thematic, or notable ('it's hysterical', 'that must be exciting', 'is a really cool band name', 'i think there's a theme'), or otherwise tells the reader how to react. 5 = mild evaluative language that could plausibly be in-character. 10 = the speaker is entirely absorbed in the situation and never comments on the humor; the line would be said the same way if it were not funny.

[X06] Compactness with payload
Score 10 when every word advances the setup, reinterpretation, or punchline and nothing feels expendable; score 0 when padding, repetition, or excess wording weakens the joke.

[X07] Use of the Oxford (serial) comma
Score 0-10 for how consistently the text uses a comma before the final conjunction in a list. 10 = used in every list; 0 = never used or no lists present. Judge punctuation habit only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X08] Copyediting normalization
Score 0-10 for the extent of standardized punctuation, spacing, capitalization, apostrophe, and sentence-format conventions. Judge only visible editing conventions; do not judge whether the result is good.

[X09] Reasoning given for why the approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the item supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X10] Sustained double meaning across the whole line
Score whether the caption's key words operate on two simultaneous levels of meaning across the entire line, versus containing only one clever word embedded in an otherwise single-register sentence. 10 = the whole caption sustains a coherent double reading start to finish; 0 = purely literal, single-register meaning throughout.

[X11] Phonetic wordplay compressed into a single word
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of sound-based wordplay in the caption: homophones, near-homophones, portmanteaus, respellings, or an idiom whose ordinary meaning is displaced by a same-sounding alternative. 0 = no sound play, all words used in their plain sense; 5 = a mild double sense that does not depend on pronunciation; 10 = the caption's whole content is a phonetic substitution or coinage. Score only the presence and density of sound play as an observable linguistic operation. Do NOT judge whether the pun works, is groan-worthy, is original, or is good.

[X12] Pandemic or geopolitical event-pinning vocabulary
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the caption references a specific, dateable real-world event or era outside party politics — such as vaccination status, a named ongoing war or invasion, or other period-specific current-events shorthand. 0 = no such reference; 10 = the caption's premise depends entirely on that event being current. Score only the presence of such era-pinning content, not whether the reference is used cleverly.

[X13] Identity-role markers
Score 0-10 for the extent of explicit family, dating, occupational, religious, age, or other social-role labels. Judge only amount and specificity; do not judge whether the portrayal is good.

[X14] Throat-clearing before the joke
Score the amount of conversational preamble that precedes any comic content. 0 = the caption opens with discourse filler -- 'i don't know about you, but', 'believe it or not', 'on the plus side', 'enough about me', 'of course,', 'actually', 'i don't know.' -- that adds naturalness but no joke. 5 = a short functional lead-in that also sets up the payoff. 10 = the caption starts inside the joke with no runway at all. Score only the opening; a caption may be long and still open cold.

[X15] Clash between elevated/formal diction and mundane scene
Score the degree of register mismatch between the caption's vocabulary and the ordinary or absurd situation it describes. 10 = formal, archaic, legalistic, or technical diction is applied to a mundane/absurd scene for comic contrast; 0 = the diction is uniformly casual/plain throughout, matching the scene register.

[X16] Count of numerals and digit characters in the text
Score 0-10 for how many numerals and digit characters the text contains relative to its length. 10 = digits everywhere; 0 = no digits at all. Count characters only; do not judge whether the numbers are meaningful or whether their presence is good.

[X17] Exact wordplay fit
Score 10 when a homophone, near-homophone, spelling shift, or lexical substitution is precise and semantically apt; score 0 when there is no wordplay or the resemblance feels strained.

[X18] Quantification density
Score 0-10 for the extent of explicit numerals, counts, weights, durations, distances, or other measurements. Judge only quantity and salience; do not judge whether specificity is good.

[X19] Cultural specificity
On a 0-10 scale, score only the extent of named people, brands, places, groups, media, or other culturally specific references; do not judge whether the references are apt or funny.

[X20] Speaker asserts and acts, rather than asking and hedging
Score the speaker's stance toward the person addressed. 10 = a confident declarative that does something in the scene -- issues a threat, delivers a verdict, refuses, insults, or reframes the other party's position -- so the line changes the situation. 5 = a neutral observation or a question that carries real edge. 0 = a hedged question, a request for reassurance, or an agreeable pleasantry that leaves everything as it was. Score stance and force, not politeness.

[X21] Coherent causal twist
Score 10 when the punch follows from a surprising but inferable chain of implications; score 0 when it is only a topical association, arbitrary juxtaposition, or non sequitur.

[X22] Distinctive speaker voice
Score 10 when diction reveals a particular speaker’s attitude, role, or personality and would not sound interchangeable; score 0 when the voice is generic and characterless.

[X23] Late twist or escalation in the final clause
Score whether the caption sets up an expectation early and then overturns or sharpens it in its closing words, producing a distinct turn. 10 = a clear rug-pull or sharpening twist lands specifically at the end of the line; 0 = the caption proceeds as one flat, predictable statement with no turn.

[X24] Explicit statement of the limitation or failure regime
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the item names a condition under which its own claim would not hold, or a case it does not cover. 10 = a specific, checkable limitation is stated; 0 = no limitation or scope condition is acknowledged anywhere.

[X25] Off-colour, bodily and dark-subject content
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of transgressive content in the caption: sexual or anatomical reference, consent or harassment themes, bodily-function or scatological words, bodily harm, death, cannibalism, or cruelty toward a named party. 0 = wholly innocuous content; 5 = one mild edgy or bodily word; 10 = the caption's central content is sexual, scatological, violent or otherwise taboo. Score only the observable presence and intensity of such content. Do NOT judge whether the transgression is funny, gratuitous, offensive, well-handled, or good, and do NOT reward or penalise edginess; you are counting content type only.

[X26] Idiomatic frame transformation
Score 10 when a familiar idiom, title, proverb, or lyric is recognizable but altered into a fresh, situation-specific meaning; score 0 when no such frame exists or it is merely repeated.

[X27] Meaningful numerical specificity
Score 10 when an exact number, count, or measurement makes the premise vivid and intensifies the joke; score 0 when numerical detail is absent, arbitrary, or irrelevant.

[X28] Genre-pastiche of a non-cartoon written register
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the caption mimics the fixed phrasing conventions of a specific, unrelated written genre — e.g. a restaurant review, a real-estate listing, or a dating-app message — rather than reading as spontaneous spoken dialogue. 0 = no genre mimicry; 10 = the caption reads as if lifted directly from that other genre. Score only the presence of the borrowed register, not whether the pastiche is executed well.

[X29] Directness on taboo or transgressive subject matter
Score how directly the caption confronts dark, risque, or uncomfortable content rather than softening or avoiding it. 10 = the caption states taboo or edgy content plainly, with no cushioning; 0 = the content is entirely safe, warm, or family-friendly with no edge.
