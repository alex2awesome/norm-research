You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the caption is as a contest entry for THAT cartoon. It is about the substance of
      what is being communicated, the evidence behind it, and the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the caption is as a contest entry for THAT cartoon: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: reader-submitted entries to a weekly cartoon caption contest, each one written for a specific published cartoon (the cartoon is described alongside every entry below). Each item is a caption.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
how good the caption is as a contest entry for THAT cartoon too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Live-news political reference (news-cycle fingerprint)
MODE 2 fingerprint. Score 0-10 for how much the caption depends on a REFERENCE TO CURRENT-EVENTS POLITICS or public-affairs news: named or implied parties, primaries, slates, tickets, candidates, votes and ballots, indictments, the White House, officeholders, campaigns, scandals. 0 = no political or civic content whatever; 3 = one glancing civic word that is not load-bearing; 7 = the joke's second meaning requires the reader to hold a political story in mind; 10 = the caption is nothing but the political reference. Judge only whether the political frame is present and how load-bearing it is. You must NOT judge whether the politics is timely, accurate, agreeable, tired or funny, and you must not form any view of the caption's merit.

[X02] Obvious first-thought pun
Score 0-10 the extent to which the caption relies on the single most obvious wordplay or literal interpretation directly invited by the cartoon's main visual element (e.g., 'wool' for a wolf in sheep's clothing). Explicitly do not judge whether the pun is funny.

[X03] Number of words in the caption
Score 0-10 inversely with the caption's raw length in words: 10 = five words or fewer, 0 = more than twenty-five words, scaling in between. Count words only; do not judge whether the length suits the joke.

[X04] House-style colloquialism
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the caption uses plain, clean, everyday spoken language and an ordinary conversational register. Do not judge whether that register suits the cartoon.

[X05] Contains a proper noun or brand name
Score 0-10 for how prominently the caption uses a proper noun -- a person's name, a place, a company, a product, a title. 10 = a named entity is the subject of the line; 0 = no proper noun appears. Count the presence of the name only; do not judge whether it is apt.

[X06] Topical news and era reference
Score from 0 to 10 the extent to which the caption explicitly references a specific contemporary political event, public figure, or cultural news item (e.g., elections, pandemics, indictments) that dates it to a particular week's news cycle. Do not judge the funniness of the reference.

[X07] Meta-humor about the joke/genre itself
Score how much the caption comments on the act of joke-telling, the contest genre, or the comedic convention of noticing absurdity, rather than staying purely inside the depicted scene. 10 = the caption knowingly winks at its own status as a joke or at the reader's/judge's expectations; 5 = a mild self-aware aside; 0 = the caption is entirely diegetic with no self-reference.

[X08] Social-norm inversion
Give 10 when a recognizable social rule or institution becomes the mechanism that explains the cartoon’s absurdity; give 0 when no social norm is involved or the topical reference is merely attached.

[X09] Transcription and typography conventions of the compiled list
MODE 4 fingerprint. Score 0-10 for how much NON-DEFAULT TYPOGRAPHY the caption carries relative to the plain lowercase unpunctuated norm of this list. Add extent for: terminal punctuation present (a final period, question mark or exclamation); internal sentence-final periods splitting the line; quotation marks around an embedded phrase; double-hyphen or dash constructions; ellipses; digits written as numerals; symbols that appear stripped or mangled (a percentage or currency sign missing beside a number); any capitalisation at all. 0 = all lowercase, no terminal punctuation, no marks beyond a single comma; 10 = several of these conventions at once. You must NOT judge whether the punctuation is correct, careful or sloppy; count only how much marked typography is visible.

[X10] Metaphorical or Satirical Mapping
Score 10 if the caption brilliantly maps the surreal visual elements to a recognizable real-world concept (e.g., a political process, social dysfunction, or bureaucracy) without over-explaining the connection; score 0 if the caption is entirely literal or misses the chance for metaphor.

[X11] Generic well-formed complete-sentence 'caption-shaped' joke
0-10: how much does the caption read as a fully-formed, grammatically complete, self-contained joke sentence with a clear setup-and-punchline shape that would be recognizable as 'a good caption' even to someone who has never seen this particular drawing, versus a fragment, an oblique aside, or a line that only makes sense once you already know the specific scene? 0 = incomprehensible or meaningless without the specific drawing; 10 = polished, complete, self-contained joke sentence that reads as generically caption-shaped. Score only this generic well-formedness; do not judge whether it is actually the best caption for this drawing.

[X12] Salient-detail integration
Score 10 when the caption makes two or more conspicuous visual details interact in one joke; score 0 when it could fit a generic version of the scene and ignores its specifics.

[X13] Uniform lowercase/no terminal punctuation transcription style
0-10: how fully does the caption follow a flattened transcription style — entirely lowercase (no capital letters anywhere, including at the start of the sentence or for proper nouns) and no terminal punctuation mark (no period, question mark, or exclamation point at the very end)? 0 = caption uses standard capitalization and/or ends with punctuation; 10 = caption is fully lowercase throughout with no closing punctuation mark. Score only this transcription pattern; do not judge whether the styling is appropriate.

[X14] Phonetic pun precision
Score 10 when a sound-alike or word-form substitution is exact, immediately decipherable, and image-specific; score 0 when the pun is strained, obscure, or detachable from the cartoon.

[X15] Adapted familiar phrase
Score 10 when a recognizable idiom, lyric, or saying is minimally altered so that its new wording fits the depicted situation; score 0 when a familiar phrase is merely pasted onto the scene.

[X16] Register of the words clashes with the situation drawn
Score 0-10 for how far the vocabulary and tone of the caption belong to a different world from the scene in the drawing -- corporate jargon in a jungle, therapeutic language at a crime scene, domestic small talk in a catastrophe. 10 = a sustained clash of register runs through the line; 0 = the words match the setting.

[X17] Two-reading semantic fit
Score 10 when one phrase supports both a literal reading and an idiomatic or conceptual reading, with both fitting the cartoon; score 0 when only one unforced meaning is present.

[X18] Off-screen narrative
Give 10 when the caption implies a specific unseen event, history, or consequence that makes the pictured moment newly funny; give 0 when it adds no temporal or narrative information.

[X19] Terse fragment/staccato delivery
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the caption is delivered as short clipped fragments (very few words, punctuated as separate short bursts rather than one flowing sentence) rather than as a longer flowing sentence. 0 = long flowing single sentence with no internal breaks; 10 = extremely short, broken into staccato fragments/repeated short bursts. Score only the extent of this fragment/staccato quality present in the wording and punctuation; do not judge whether the caption is funny or good.

[X20] Macabre or Edgy Subtext
Score 10 if the caption confidently extracts a dark, morbid, or highly inappropriate implication from an otherwise mundane or silly visual; score 0 if the caption is sanitized, overly safe, or points out the obvious absurdity instead of the dark implication.

[X21] Dark or transgressive content delivered deadpan
Score for content that is morbid, bodily, sexual, or ethically uncomfortable, delivered in a flat conversational register with no apology or wink. 10 = genuinely edgy material said as though it were small talk. 5 = mildly impolite. 0 = entirely wholesome content, or edgy content that is signposted, leering, or delivered with visible relish. Score the presence and deadpan handling of the transgression; do not penalize the caption for being impolite and do not reward mere shock without the flat delivery.

[X22] Comic economy: punch lands with minimal, load-bearing words
Score how tightly compressed the caption is in achieving its comedic effect, with essentially no throat-clearing, hedging, or explanatory padding, ending on the load-bearing punch word. 10 = extremely compressed, every word necessary, caption ends on the punch; 0 = padded or explanatory phrasing dilutes or delays the punch.

[X23] Novel interpretive angle
Give 10 when the caption finds a surprising but defensible angle instead of the most obvious observation or stock pun; give 0 when it is predictable, generic, or simply restates the premise.

[X24] Datable era idiom fixing the entry to a period
MODE 3 fingerprint. Score 0-10 for how strongly the caption's wording DATES it to a specific stretch of years, independent of any political content. Look for: pandemic-era idiom (distancing measures such as six feet, herd immunity, closures and shutdowns of restaurants or venues, masking, remote work); named consumer technology and platforms of a given moment (iphone, streaming, apps, texting); slang and address terms whose currency rises and falls (dude, hysterical); and conversely idiom that reads as older-register (telegram, party line). 0 = wording that would read the same in any decade; 5 = one mildly period-flavoured word; 10 = the caption cannot be understood without knowing what was happening in one particular year or two. You must NOT judge whether the dated reference is stale, fresh, or well used; score only how tightly the wording pins a period.

[X25] Canonical first-thought angle
Score 0-10 for how much the caption uses the most immediate, conventional interpretation or pun invited by the drawing; do not judge whether that interpretation is good, funny, or apt.

[X26] Conversational Deadpan Delivery
Score 10 if the caption mimics a completely mundane, casual remark or complaint that sharply contrasts with the absolute chaos or surrealism of the drawing; score 0 if the tone is melodramatic, exclamatory, or explicitly acknowledges the weirdness.

[X27] Idiom-to-Visual Binding
Score how perfectly a common idiom, saying, or stock phrase is rendered literal or physical by the specific content of the drawing. A 10 means a familiar dead phrase springs back to life because the drawing makes it literally true in a new way. A 0 means no idiom is present, or the idiom is used figuratively with no visual reinforcement.

[X28] Withholds the explanation the drawing seems to demand
Score 0-10 for how far the caption declines to explain the odd thing in the drawing, and instead speaks as though it needed no explanation, leaving the reader to supply the logic. 10 = the strangeness is treated as entirely unremarkable; 0 = the caption spells out why the scene is the way it is.

[X29] Punchline Unpredictability
Score how surprising the caption's meaning or ending is given the visual setup. A 10 means the caption arrives at a destination no reasonable person would predict from the drawing, yet it makes perfect sense in hindsight. A 0 means the punchline is the most obvious or expected first-thought response.
