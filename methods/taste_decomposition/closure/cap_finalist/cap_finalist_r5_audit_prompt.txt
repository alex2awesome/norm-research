You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the caption is as a contest entry for THAT cartoon. It is about the substance of
      what is being communicated, the evidence behind it, and the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the caption is as a contest entry for THAT cartoon: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: reader-submitted entries to a weekly cartoon caption contest, each one written for a specific published cartoon (the cartoon is described alongside every entry below). Each item is a caption.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
how good the caption is as a contest entry for THAT cartoon too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Time-bound political topicality
Score the extent to which the caption references specific, contemporaneous political events, figures, or election cycles. 0 = no political reference, 10 = highly specific political reference. Do not judge whether the political joke is good or bad.

[X02] Perspective Ventriloquism
Score 0 if the implied speaker or addressee is generic or ambiguous. Score 10 if humor arises from a specific, surprising speaker identity whose perspective creates a productive mismatch with the visual.

[X03] Familiar-phrase transformation
Score 10 when a recognizable idiom, lyric, title, or saying is altered so its new wording precisely fits the image; score 0 when the phrase is used unchanged or the reference is arbitrary.

[X04] Concise completeness
Score 10 when a compact line is grammatically complete, self-contained, and delivers its setup and payoff; score 0 when it is bloated, fragmentary, or requires missing context.

[X05] Caption reframes what the drawing is a picture of
Score 0-10 for how far the caption makes the reader see the whole drawing as a picture of a different situation from the one it obviously depicts. 10 = the scene is completely relocated by the line; 0 = the caption accepts the obvious reading of the scene and comments inside it.

[X06] Direct pun or wordplay reliance
Score the extent to which the caption's humor relies entirely on a literal pun, homophone, or direct wordplay matching an element in the drawing. 0 = no wordplay, 10 = the entire joke is a simple pun. Do not judge whether puns are good or bad.

[X07] Direct Idiom Literalization
Score the extent to which the punchline relies solely on taking a common idiom and applying it literally to the exact visual elements present in the drawing. 0 = no idiom used; 10 = the entire joke is just the literal application of a common phrase. Do not judge if the idiom use is clever.

[X08] Generic conversational sitcom quip
Score the extent to which the caption reads like a standard, safe situational-comedy line (e.g., using everyday domestic complaints or mundane observations like eating takeout or doing chores) rather than a unique twist. 0 = highly unique/absurd premise, 10 = extremely standard sitcom-style domestic complaint. Do not judge whether this makes the caption good or bad.

[X09] Stakes Incongruity
Score 0 if the caption operates in a single conceptual register (e.g., purely domestic or purely existential). Score 10 if the caption productively juxtaposes mundane/domestic language with existential, cosmic, or bodily stakes, creating humor from the mismatch.

[X10] Line is assigned to the character it is least expected from
Score 0-10 for how far the caption puts the line in the mouth of the figure in the drawing from whom it is least expected, so that the surprise comes from the pairing of speaker and speech. 10 = the speaker attribution is itself the joke; 0 = the line could be said by any figure in the drawing, or by no one in particular. Judge the speaker choice, not whether the caption amused you.

[X11] Idiom-to-Visual Subversion
Score 10 if the caption takes a well-known idiom, phrase, or cliché and brilliantly applies its literal meaning to the exact absurd scenario depicted in the cartoon. Score 0 if there is no idiom used, or if it is used purely in its figurative sense without interacting with the visual.

[X12] Conceptual Recontextualization
Score 10 if the caption completely redefines the literal shapes or objects in the drawing as a totally different concept or metaphor (e.g., piano keys as house keys). Score 0 if the objects retain their literal meaning from the cartoon.

[X13] Era-coded idiom
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the caption uses period-marked slang, archaic wording, song or title allusions, or culturally dated phrasing; do not judge whether the wording is amusing or appropriate.

[X14] Strictly Lowercase Orthography
Score the extent to which the text entirely lacks standard capitalization, ignoring standard rules even for the first word or proper nouns. 0 = perfect standard capitalization; 10 = entirely lowercase. Do not judge if this formatting is good or bad.

[X15] Generic caption closure
Score 0-10 for how much the line has a generic, self-contained setup-to-punch structure with an obvious semantic mapping that a general-purpose writer could readily produce; do not judge whether the joke is good.

[X16] Extreme Brevity Word-Count
Score the extent to which the caption is a very short, standalone fragment (typically 1-3 words, such as a single pun or name) lacking a full sentence structure. 0 = a full grammatical sentence; 10 = a single word or fragment. Do not judge if this brevity is good or bad.

[X17] Explicitly names an object visible in the drawing
Score 0-10 for how many of the concrete objects, animals or settings visible in the cartoon are named in so many words in the caption. 10 = the caption names several of them explicitly; 0 = it names none. Count the naming only; do not judge whether naming them helps the joke.

[X18] Cultural-frame mapping
Score 10 when the caption maps the whole image onto a recognizable cultural, political, or social frame with clear visual correspondences; score 0 when it is an arbitrary name-drop.

[X19] Dual-Code Wordplay Activation
Score 0 if no word or phrase carries simultaneous literal-visual and figurative-idiomatic meanings. Score 10 if a key word/phrase is literally anchored in a visible element AND figuratively creates an additional comic layer, where removing either reading collapses the joke.

[X20] Surprise reinterpretation
Score 10 when the ending forces a sharp, understandable second reading of the setup; score 0 when the punchline is predictable description without a turn.

[X21] Punctuated as a completed sentence of direct speech
Score 0-10 for how far the caption is typed as a single complete sentence of direct speech with standard terminal punctuation. 10 = one full sentence, capitalised, ending in a period or question mark; 0 = a fragment, a list, or no terminal punctuation. Count the typography only; do not judge whether the caption works.

[X22] Consensus-angle pun
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the caption uses the most immediate conventional word association invited by the drawing, such as wolf/sheep-wool, piano-keys, or ride-quarter; do not judge whether that angle is funny or publishable.

[X23] Pop-Culture Prop Reliance Penalty
Score 0 if the joke relies entirely on the specific interactions within the cartoon. Score 10 if the joke fails without the recognition of a specific real-world brand, pop-culture franchise, or celebrity name.

[X24] List-normalization typography
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the text shows raw-entry or transcription conventions such as lowercase starts, omitted terminal punctuation, doubled hyphens, repeated punctuation, or dialect apostrophes; do not judge whether the typography improves the caption.

[X25] Socially Plausible Impossible Character
Score 10 when an impossible participant is given a specific social role, motive, or etiquette that makes the absurd interaction feel internally natural; score 0 when the caption only notes that the participant is unusual.

[X26] Deadpan Tone Contrast
Score 10 if the caption's delivery is completely emotionless, dry, and understated, creating a sharp contrast with the visual's extreme chaos or absurdity. Score 0 if the caption is highly emotional, exclamatory, or acknowledges the absurdity directly.

[X27] Layered Double Entendre
Score 10 if the caption uses a word or phrase that has a completely separate, often adult or edgy meaning that fits perfectly with the visual's hidden implications. Score 0 if the wording is straightforward and single-meaning.

[X28] Character-voiced perspective
Score 10 when the line clearly sounds like something the depicted character would say or think; score 0 when no speaker or viewpoint is plausibly established.

[X29] Exact pun construction
Score 10 when a sound, spelling, name, or word substitution is precise, recognizable, and tightly image-linked; score 0 when the pun is forced, vague, or absent.
