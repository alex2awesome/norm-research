You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the caption is as a contest entry. It is about the substance, the
      reasoning, the evidence, the craft, or the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the caption is as a contest entry: length or verbosity, formatting or layout,
      boilerplate or template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter
      or topic markers, temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of
      some upstream circumstance of the item's production (who made it, with what
      resources, when, with what help).

The corpus is: reader-submitted captions for a weekly cartoon caption contest. Each item is a caption.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
well-resourced producer may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually do
better work). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces
real how good the caption is as a contest entry too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you
label "quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Dark or taboo content delivered flatly
Score 10 if the caption references injury, death, crime, or otherwise taboo material and states it in a flat, unemotional, matter-of-fact tone with no softening. Score 0 if the caption is wholly anodyne and family-safe, or if dark content is present but delivered with visible emotional flourish rather than deadpan understatement.

[X02] Explicit statement of the limitation or failure regime
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the item names a condition under which its own claim would not hold, or a case it does not cover. 10 = a specific, checkable limitation is stated; 0 = no limitation or scope condition is acknowledged anywhere.

[X03] Phonetic pun or wordplay density
Score 0-10 the extent to which the caption's core joke relies on a phonetic pun, near-homophone, or double meaning of a word, where 10 = the entire joke is built on such wordplay and 0 = no wordplay is present. Do not judge whether the pun is clever or funny -- only whether wordplay is present.

[X04] Electoral and news-cycle political referents
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the caption refers to electoral politics, government institutions, or legal-political events: parties, primaries, slates, tickets, ballots, votes, indictments, offices of government, named political figures. 0 = no political or governmental referent; 3 = a word with a faint political shading used non-politically; 6 = one clear political referent; 10 = the caption is built entirely around a political or electoral referent and would be unintelligible without it. Report only how much such reference is present. You must NOT judge whether the political material is timely, pointed, tired, funny, partisan, or well used, and you must not favour or disfavour any political position.

[X05] Named external media or cultural-property reference
Score 0-10 how much the caption explicitly names a specific media franchise, fictional character, celebrity, TV/book property, or branded cultural reference as part of the joke, where 10 = the joke depends entirely on recognizing a specific external cultural reference and 0 = no such reference appears. Do not judge whether the reference is obscure, dated, or well-chosen.

[X06] Count of numerals and digit characters in the text
Score 0-10 for how many numerals and digit characters the text contains relative to its length. 10 = digits everywhere; 0 = no digits at all. Count characters only; do not judge whether the numbers are meaningful or whether their presence is good.

[X07] Sustained character voice appropriate to a reimagined role
Score 10 if the caption fully commits to and sustains a specific persona or role (e.g. pirate captain, drill sergeant, folk narrator) that recasts the pictured figure, without breaking voice to explain the joke. Score 0 if the caption is delivered in a generic, unmarked contemporary speaking voice with no adopted persona.

[X08] Caption brevity / word count
Score 0-10 how short and terse the caption is (word count / clause count), where 10 = an extremely short single clause (roughly <=5 words) and 0 = a long multi-clause caption (roughly >=15 words). Score only the LENGTH of the text; do not judge whether the caption's length helps or hurts it as a joke.

[X09] Cultural allusion repurposing
Score 10 when a recognizable cultural, political, literary, or musical reference is substantially reworked for a new comic context; score 0 when it is merely named or quoted.

[X10] Deadpan audacity
Score 10 when an absurd, dark, or outrageous premise is delivered in calm matter-of-fact language; score 0 when the line overexplains, emotionally signals, or announces its joke.

[X11] Specific numeric or quantitative detail
Score 0-10 the extent to which the caption's joke is built around a specific, concrete number or quantity (a day count, weight, measurement, dollar amount, etc.) stated in the text, where 10 = a specific number is the central device of the joke and 0 = no numbers appear. Do not judge whether the particular number chosen is funny.

[X12] Reasoning given for why the approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the item supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X13] Named cultural allusions
On a 0-10 scale, how much does the caption rely on recognizable names, cultural references, places, works, genres, or domain-specific allusions? Score only the amount of reference material; do not judge whether the allusion is apt.

[X14] Distinctive speaker voice
Score 10 when diction reveals a specific persona, attitude, or relationship that could not be voiced neutrally; score 0 when the line could be spoken by anyone without characterization.

[X15] Ethical or social reversal
Score 10 when the caption exposes a contradiction, hypocrisy, or power assumption through a concise reversal; score 0 when it offers only a generic opinion.

[X16] Joke requires an ironic/sarcastic reversal
Score 10 if the caption's literal words must be read as the opposite of, or as ironically undercutting, the sincere sentiment they appear to express, and this reversal carries the entire joke. Score 0 if the caption is sincere and literal throughout, with no ironic inversion required to get the point.

[X17] Punctuation-driven timing
Score 10 when punctuation, spacing, or repetition creates a materially different reading or comic pause; score 0 when punctuation has no effect beyond ordinary grammar.

[X18] Institutional register
On a 0-10 scale, how much does the caption use legal, bureaucratic, organizational, evaluative, or formal public-institution vocabulary? Score only the extent of that register; do not judge whether the wording is sophisticated or effective.

[X19] Idiom literalization
Score 10 when a familiar idiom, lyric, or saying is repurposed through a fresh literal or situational reading; score 0 when it is used conventionally with no reinterpretation.

[X20] Era-dating situational idiom and euphemism
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the caption relies on a situational idiom that dates it to a particular period: physical-distancing language, delivery and takeout as a default, coy euphemism for a shared ongoing situation ('the you know what'), household paperwork drives such as a census. 0 = the situation described could belong to any decade; 5 = one dating idiom present but incidental; 10 = the caption's whole premise is a period-specific situation and it would read as a non-sequitur outside that period. Report only how strongly the wording dates the caption. You must NOT judge whether the period reference is fresh, stale, over-used, or funny -- topicality is neither a credit nor a penalty here.

[X21] Elliptical inference
Score 10 when omitted explanation invites one strong, recoverable inference; score 0 when omission makes the caption incoherent or directionless.

[X22] Use of the Oxford (serial) comma
Score 0-10 for how consistently the text uses a comma before the final conjunction in a list. 10 = used in every list; 0 = never used or no lists present. Judge punctuation habit only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X23] Clashing interpretive frames
Score 10 when two incompatible domains or interpretations collide coherently in one line; score 0 when the associations are routine or the juxtaposition is incoherent.

[X24] Taboo/body vocabulary
How much sexual, bodily, consent-related, or otherwise taboo vocabulary is present? Score only the amount of this subject matter; do not judge whether it improves the caption.

[X25] Conversational plausibility
Score 10 when the line sounds like a naturally motivated spoken exchange with clear pragmatic intent; score 0 when it sounds forced, contextless, or syntactically unnatural.

[X26] Oddly precise number anchors the joke
Score 10 if the caption includes a specific, unusually precise number or quantity (not a round or generic figure) that intensifies or anchors the comic point. Score 0 if the caption contains no numeric or quantitative detail at all.

[X27] Temporal anchoring
On a 0-10 scale, how much does the caption explicitly anchor itself to time through words such as today, now, this morning, days, duration, sequence, or deadlines? Score only temporal reference; do not judge whether the timing makes the caption effective.

[X28] Punchline compression
Score 10 when every word contributes setup, turn, or attitude with no filler; score 0 when the line is padded, diffuse, or generic.

[X29] Clash of incongruous registers in one line
Score 10 if the caption yokes two clearly mismatched registers (e.g., epic/historical/violent/legalistic language set directly against mundane domestic content) inside a single short line. Score 0 if the caption maintains one consistent, unremarkable register throughout.
