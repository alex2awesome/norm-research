You are an independent auditor. Below are candidate criteria for scoring
PUBLIC COMMENTS submitted to United States federal agencies on proposed rules. Each
would be scored 0-10 by a judge reading only one comment's text.

Classify EACH criterion as exactly one of:

  "quality_relevant" -- it measures something a careful regulatory analyst would call
      substantive merit of the comment as regulatory input: evidence, analysis,
      specificity about the rule, feasibility, actionable alternatives, first-hand
      knowledge, engagement with the agency's reasoning.

  "incidental" -- it is predictive-but-not-merit: a surface or shape feature, a
      formatting or length proxy, boilerplate or template similarity, a topic or
      agency-style marker, a submitter-identity or professional-drafting fingerprint,
      a tone or register marker, or the bare presence of a word or phrase.

DECISION AIDS.
  * If the criterion could be scored high by a comment that says nothing substantive,
    it is incidental.
  * If the instruction tells the judge to score the SHAPE of the document rather than
    what the document establishes, it is incidental -- even when the shape usually
    co-occurs with merit. Say so in your justification if you see this.
  * "Related to quality" is not enough. The question is what the JUDGE would be
    scoring when following this exact instruction.

Emit exactly one JSON object and nothing else:

{"verdicts": [
  {"id": "P01", "label": "quality_relevant" or "incidental",
   "confidence": "high"/"medium"/"low",
   "justification": "<one or two sentences>"},
  ... one entry for EVERY criterion ...
]}


--- CRITERION P01 ---
NAME: Prominence of organizational size and prestige claims
INSTRUCTION: Score how prominently the comment foregrounds its own organisational scale and standing. Score 10 when quantified membership, customer counts, assets under management, market share, national or multi-state reach, founding date, or prestige superlatives ('the largest', 'the nation's leading') are placed front and centre, typically in the opening paragraph or a credentialing footnote, and repeated. Score 5 when organisational size is mentioned once, in passing. Score 0 when no organisational-scale or prestige claim appears at all, including comments from individuals. Score these claims as text, never whether the claimed scale makes the argument stronger or the author better informed.

--- CRITERION P02 ---
NAME: Filing apparatus density
INSTRUCTION: Score the density of formal filing conventions visible in the document, a formatting feature. Count: an addressee block naming an official or office and address; a 'Re:' or 'Subject:' caption carrying a docket, RIN, or file number; numbered footnotes, especially ones describing the organisation's membership, assets, or history; a formal salutation such as 'Dear Sir or Madam'; and a closing signature giving a name with a professional title. Score 10 when the full apparatus is present, 5 when one or two conventions appear, and 0 for plain conversational text carrying none. Never score what the document argues.

--- CRITERION P03 ---
NAME: Specialist insider shorthand assuming a small expert readership
INSTRUCTION: Score how strongly the text presumes a small, already-expert readership. Markers: program acronyms and technical shorthand unexpanded at first use; bare citation of sections, paragraphs, tables, or preamble page numbers with no explanation; direct address to a named office, division, docket manager, or staff contact; casual cross-reference to other dockets, prior rulemakings, meetings, or ex parte contacts as shared history, including assumed familiarity with the commenter and their role; jargon-dense sentences with no lay gloss. Score 10 when this is pervasive and a general reader could not follow without a glossary; 5 for a mixed register with key terms explained; 0 when everything is spelled out for a lay reader. Score the assumed audience only, not whether the technical content is correct.

--- CRITERION P04 ---
NAME: Identified accountable author with stated standing
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the text names the author or organisation and states a role, occupation, expertise, or regulated relationship to the rule's subject that lets a reader weigh its claims. Score 10 when both are present (for example, 'I have operated a licensed facility of this type for eleven years'). Score 5 when standing is asserted vaguely, naming no role, activity, or relationship. Score 0 when nothing establishes the writer's relation to the rule; salutations, signature blocks, titles, and letterhead never count.

--- CRITERION P05 ---
NAME: Reconciles conflicting evidence rather than citing one side
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment engages evidence pointing against its own position. Score 10 when it states what the contrary evidence is and explains how to reconcile it with the position the comment takes. Score 5 when contrary evidence is acknowledged but left unreconciled. Score 0 when only supporting evidence is presented. Judge the engagement with conflicting evidence, not which side of the question is right.

--- CRITERION P06 ---
NAME: Aggregated first-hand evidence from the represented population
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment reports what is actually observed across the population it speaks for and applies it to the rule. Score 10 for pooled or patterned evidence, such as survey results, recurring case types and how often they arise, ranges of practice across members, documented incidents, or variation between large and small participants, stated to imply a specific effect of the proposal. Score 5 for one or two concrete instances from its constituency, with no pattern established and no provision attached. Score 0 when breadth of members, customers, or dollars is claimed but nothing observed across them is reported.

--- CRITERION P07 ---
NAME: Addressee-directed numbered items with rule-citation tags
INSTRUCTION: Score the layout of the document, a surface feature. Score 10 when the comment is addressed to a named official or office and its body is broken into discrete numbered or lettered items, each anchored to a citation tag such as a rule paragraph, CFR subsection, Airworthiness Directive number, docket number, or service-bulletin reference, with little connective persuasive narrative between items. Score 5 when it is partly itemised, itemised without citation tags, or tagged without itemisation. Score 0 for continuous persuasive prose with no addressee-directed itemisation keyed to rule sections. Items that say nothing substantive still score 10.

--- CRITERION P08 ---
NAME: Letter furniture: salutation and signature block
INSTRUCTION: Score only the presence of formal letter furniture, a surface feature of the document. Score 10 when the comment both opens with a formal salutation ('Dear Administrator', 'To Whom It May Concern') and closes with a signature block naming a person or organisation, usually with a complimentary close ('Sincerely,'). Score 5 when exactly one of the two is present, and 0 when neither is. Do not read the body: a blank form letter with both scores 10, and a comment that begins mid-argument and ends without a sign-off scores 0.

--- CRITERION P09 ---
NAME: Explicit disclosure of past dealings with agency
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the writer explicitly discloses a prior or ongoing relationship with the agency, counting the disclosure as a textual feature. Score 10 when the comment states outright that it holds or has held permits, licences, contracts, or authorisations from the agency, or describes recurring meetings, ongoing communications, or past dealings with it. Score 5 for an oblique or single-clause reference to earlier contact. Score 0 when no prior agency relationship is mentioned anywhere in the text. Whether the disclosed relationship lends authority, and whether anything follows from it, are outside this criterion.

--- CRITERION P10 ---
NAME: Density of figures, citations and named sources
INSTRUCTION: Score the amount of quantified and attributed material visible in the text, a surface feature scored without regard to accuracy, relevance, or use. Score 10 when precise statistics, dollar amounts, percentages, numerical forecasts, named studies or reports, cited authorities, and links or footnotes appear repeatedly throughout. Score 5 for a few scattered figures or one or two named sources. Score 0 when the text contains no numbers and names no external source. Count what appears on the page: numbers that go nowhere still count, and figure-free qualitative reasoning scores 0.

--- CRITERION P11 ---
NAME: Provision-by-provision engagement with the proposed text
INSTRUCTION: Score how precisely the comment engages the content of particular proposed provisions. Score 10 when it identifies the specific provisions it addresses, states what each would require, and gives a reason directed at that requirement, so a reader can tell which parts it is about and what it says about each. Score 5 when it engages the proposal's substance only at the level of the rulemaking as a whole, without separating provisions. Score 0 when it expresses support, opposition, or general subject-area discussion without engaging what any particular provision would require; a docket or RIN citation in a caption is not engagement.

--- CRITERION P12 ---
NAME: De novo issue scaffolding versus incorporation by reference
INSTRUCTION: Score how much the comment builds its issue from the ground up, as though nothing has been said yet, rather than attaching itself to another party's submission. Score 10 for fully de novo scaffolding: it restates what the rule proposes, supplies its own framing and definitions of key terms, introduces the commenter and their stake at length, walks through background the agency already knows, and adopts, endorses, echoes, or incorporates no other filing. Score 5 for mostly standalone framing with a brief nod to another filing or one 'we agree with' aside. Score 0 for a follower move such as 'we join and incorporate by reference the comments of X', with little or no standalone framing. Judge framing posture only, not correctness or writing quality.

--- CRITERION P13 ---
NAME: Comment is shorter than three sentences
INSTRUCTION: Score only the comment's sentence count. Score 10 when the comment consists of fewer than three sentences, that is one or two. Score 0 when it consists of three or more. Count sentences as written, regardless of their length, content, or quality.

--- CRITERION P14 ---
NAME: First-hand operational and engineering particulars
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment supplies concrete technical or operational particulars drawn from hands-on work with the equipment, process, or facility the rule governs, tied to the requirement. Score 10 for specifics only such involvement yields: part or configuration numbers and what differs about them, observed failure or inspection findings and their frequency, actual labour hours or downtime, a conflict with an existing manual or procedure, an interval unmeetable for a stated physical reason. Score 5 when particulars are generic to the industry rather than the writer's own operation. Score 0 when no observed particular appears, however precisely rule paragraphs are cited.

--- CRITERION P15 ---
NAME: Specifies how compliance would be demonstrated in practice
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment says concretely what a regulated party would have to record, measure, or show to demonstrate compliance, either under the proposal or under the comment's own alternative. Score 10 when the records, measurements, or showings are named specifically enough that a reader could carry them out. Score 5 when demonstration is mentioned only in general terms. Score 0 when how compliance would be demonstrated is not addressed. Judge the concreteness of the demonstration described, not whether it would be workable.

--- CRITERION P16 ---
NAME: Deadline, extension, and re-opened-period timing language
INSTRUCTION: Score how much the text explicitly orients itself to the clock of the comment period, counting only language about the filing window and never compliance deadlines proposed inside the rule. Markers: naming the closing date; asserting timeliness ('submitted prior to the close of the comment period'); noting the hour or channel of filing; requesting an extension; identifying the document as supplemental, amended, corrected, or a re-submission; invoking a re-opened or extended window. Score 10 for several such moves, prominently placed; 5 for exactly one passing reference; 0 when nothing anywhere addresses dates, deadlines, timeliness, extensions, or an earlier filing by this same commenter. Ignore references to what other parties have filed.

--- CRITERION P17 ---
NAME: Documented experience complying with the existing requirements
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment reports concrete experience of operating under the agency's current requirements and uses it to say how the proposal would work. Score 10 when it describes what compliance actually involved, such as steps performed, time or cost incurred, review timelines encountered, obstacles or workarounds, and connects that record to a specific effect of the proposed change. Score 5 when compliance experience is described but tied to no provision. Score 0 when nothing about what complying entailed is reported, or the proposal is discussed purely in the abstract; a relationship with the agency is not experience.

--- CRITERION P18 ---
NAME: Mentions of prior contact with the agency
INSTRUCTION: Score how much the text signals an existing relationship with the agency, counting the references themselves. Score 10 when it refers explicitly and repeatedly to prior meetings, ongoing collaboration, earlier submissions, established partnerships, working groups, or direct exchanges with named agency personnel. Score 5 for a single passing reference to previous contact. Score 0 when the comment carries only a generic address to the agency and mentions no relationship history. Do not consider whether the relationship described adds anything to the comment.

--- CRITERION P19 ---
NAME: Mass-campaign petition register
INSTRUCTION: Score how strongly the text reads as one voice in an organised mass-participation drive rather than an individually composed submission to a decisionmaker. Markers: template or boilerplate phrasing with a small personalised insert; crowd framing ('I join the thousands of Americans', 'add my name'); calls for the reader or others to act, sign, or share; head-count or vote-count framing; slogan or campaign-title language; a single short paragraph of sentiment with no rule-specific detail, citation, or factual claim; a salutation addressed to a cause or the public rather than the agency. Score 10 for several such markers, unmistakably a campaign artifact; 5 for individually written but clearly campaign-prompted text; 0 for rule-specific, individualised text addressed to the agency. Do not score the position taken.

--- CRITERION P20 ---
NAME: Numbers tied to a stated consequence for this rule
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment's figures are attached to an identified provision with a stated step from figure to conclusion. Score 10 when a figure's source is shown and the comment states what it implies for that provision ('the proposed 30-day window covers 4,200 filings at 6 staff-hours each, 25,200 hours the rule does not account for'). Score 5 when a sourced, relevant figure's inference to the rule stays implicit. Score 0 when no figure is linked by stated reasoning to a provision, however dense the statistics and cited reports.

--- CRITERION P21 ---
NAME: Adversarial legal register and violation language
INSTRUCTION: Score how strongly the text adopts the vocabulary and posture of an adversarial legal filing, scored as register and independent of any argument. Score 10 when it is saturated with legal-conflict language: 'arbitrary and capricious', 'unlawful', 'exceeds statutory authority', 'violates the plain text', 'fails to satisfy notice-and-comment requirements', 'we reserve all rights', demands that the agency withdraw or rescind, warnings of legal jeopardy or challenge. Score 5 when one such formulation appears inside otherwise ordinary prose. Score 0 when no legal-violation or legal-remedy language appears; do not check whether any cited provision exists or whether the characterisation is apt.

--- CRITERION P22 ---
NAME: Substance carried over from the prior interaction
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment reports actual content from earlier dealings and applies it to this proposal. Score 10 when it states what was specifically learned, tested, agreed, required, or observed earlier and uses it as evidence about this rule ('the weekly sampling required by our 2022 authorisation produced no exceedances over 18 months, which bears on the frequency proposed here'). Score 5 when earlier work is described generally and only loosely tied to a provision. Score 0 when prior meetings, submissions, or collaboration are referenced but nothing from them is reported or brought to bear.

--- CRITERION P23 ---
NAME: Awareness of the accumulated docket record
INSTRUCTION: Score how much the comment shows that a record already existed when it was written. Markers: naming, quoting, characterizing, or rebutting other commenters; responding to arguments raised by others in the docket; citing a hearing, listening session, or roundtable already held; referring to a coalition, sign-on, or joint letter already submitted; citing the agency's own post-proposal actions; citing outside developments that post-date the proposal. Score 10 when the comment is built substantially around such material, with two or more distinct references to things that happened after publication; 5 for one clear reference in an otherwise self-contained comment; 0 when only the proposed rule and the commenter's own material are engaged. Citations to prior rulemakings, statutes, case law, or academic literature predating the proposal do not count.

--- CRITERION P24 ---
NAME: Specific legal defect identified with authority and cure
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment identifies a legal defect specifically enough to be acted on. Score 10 when it names the particular statute, regulation, or procedural requirement, states what it requires, explains how the proposal departs from it, and states the change to the proposed text that would cure the departure. Score 5 when an authority is named and a conflict described but the mechanism or the cure is missing. Score 0 when unlawfulness or procedural defect is asserted without identifying which authority conflicts and how. Judge specificity only, not whether the legal argument is correct.

--- CRITERION P25 ---
NAME: Attachment or exhibit is referenced
INSTRUCTION: Score only whether the text refers to an attached document, exhibit, appendix, or enclosure. Score 10 when at least one such reference appears anywhere in the comment. Score 0 when it refers to none. Score the reference itself as a surface feature, never the content of the attachment.
