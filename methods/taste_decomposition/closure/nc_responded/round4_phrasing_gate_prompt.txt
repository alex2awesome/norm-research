You are auditing edits to a bank of scoring criteria for PUBLIC COMMENTS
submitted to United States federal agencies on proposed rules.

For each PAIR below, decide ONE question:

  Would an independent judge, scoring public comments against criterion X and against
  criterion Y, be measuring THE SAME UNDERLYING CONCEPT?

Answer SAME only if the two would produce essentially interchangeable scores. Answer
DIFFERENT if a comment could plausibly score high on one and low on the other --
including when one is a broadened, narrowed, or shifted version of the other. A
sharper wording of the same property is SAME; a different property is DIFFERENT.

Emit exactly one JSON object and nothing else:

{"verdicts": [
  {"pair_id": "GP001", "verdict": "SAME" or "DIFFERENT", "confidence": "high"/"medium"/"low",
   "reason": "<one sentence>"},
  ... one entry for EVERY pair ...
]}


--- PAIR GP001 ---
X NAME: Docket-lineage familiarity
X INSTRUCTION: Score how much the comment narrates its own prior involvement in this regulatory process. A 10 recounts specific earlier filings, prior amendments, pilot participation, waivers, or agency precedents it took part in. A 5 alludes to past involvement without specifics. A 0 treats the docket as entirely new and references no prior history.
Y NAME: Docket-lineage familiarity
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment narrates prior filings, earlier amendments, pilot participation, waivers, agency precedents, or repeated involvement in the same regulatory process; score 0 when it treats the docket as entirely new.

--- PAIR GP002 ---
X NAME: Quantifies the rule's cost using the commenter's own data
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment supplies its own numeric estimate of compliance cost with the basis stated; 0 when no numbers appear.
Y NAME: Supplies own dollar estimates of compliance burden
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the submission puts forward concrete monetary burden figures it derived itself and says where they come from; 0 when it offers no figures.

--- PAIR GP003 ---
X NAME: Names a specific implementable alternative
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment sets out a concrete different design the agency could adopt, specific enough to write into the rule; 0 when it only objects.
Y NAME: Offers an actionable substitute regulatory design
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when an alternative approach is described concretely enough to be implemented; 0 when the comment proposes nothing.

--- PAIR GP004 ---
X NAME: Emotional-hook opening sentence before substantive content
X INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment opens with a short, punchy, emotionally-loaded sentence or fragment functioning as a headline/attention-grabber (a declarative claim of personal harm, moral outrage, or a rhetorical jab) before any substantive policy content appears. 10 = a clear stand-alone emotional hook line opens the comment, disconnected from any immediately following factual support. 0 = the comment opens directly with a substantive claim, fact, or procedural statement, with no rhetorical hook.
Y NAME: Emotional-hook opening sentence before substantive content
Y INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment opens with a short, emotionally loaded sentence or fragment working as an attention-grabbing headline, such as a declarative claim of personal harm, moral outrage, or a rhetorical jab, before any substantive policy content. A 10 opens with a clear stand-alone hook line, disconnected from any factual support that immediately follows. A 5 opens with a substantive claim carrying noticeable emotional charge. A 0 opens directly with a substantive claim, fact, or procedural statement.

--- PAIR GP005 ---
X NAME: Reasoning-based persuasion vs rhetorical amplification
X INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 whether the comment's persuasive force comes from developed reasoning and evidence, versus relying on repetition, capitalization, insults, or hyperbole in place of an argument. A 10: persuasive force rests entirely on developed reasoning/evidence delivered in a measured tone, even when the position is strongly held. A 0: the comment relies on capitalized/repeated demands, insults, or hyperbolic rhetoric as a substitute for developing any argument.
Y NAME: Reasoning-based persuasion vs rhetorical amplification
Y INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment's persuasive force comes from developed reasoning and evidence rather than from rhetorical amplification. A 10 rests entirely on developed reasoning or evidence delivered in a measured tone, even when the position is strongly held. A 5 mixes real argument with heated rhetoric. A 0 substitutes capitalized or repeated demands, insults, or hyperbole for any developed argument.

--- PAIR GP006 ---
X NAME: Submission-production residue

--- PAIR GP007 ---

A 10 describes such an alternative in enough detail that how it would work is clear. A 5 names an alternative direction without specifying any design. --- PAIR GP009 ---

Y NAME: Field observation that disconfirms a factual premise of the rule
Y INSTRUCTION: Score whether firsthand experience is aimed at a factual premise rather than at a preference. A 10 reports something the commenter directly observed, operated, or measured that contradicts a specific factual assumption the agency relies on (a stock estimate, an occupancy map, an achievability or exposure estimate), and names the assumption it contradicts. A 5 offers relevant firsthand experience as grounds for a preference, confronting no stated premise. A 0 reports no firsthand observation bearing on any premise.

--- PAIR GP010 ---
X NAME: Tests whether the chosen instrument will actually bind
X INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment interrogates the mechanism, not just the policy direction. 10 = it asks or shows whether the instrument will change behavior at all - whether a numeric limit sits above observed practice, whether a threshold will ever be triggered, whether the voluntary approach has already been tried and failed, whether the model or measurement underneath the standard can resolve the effect being regulated - and supports the answer. 5 = it questions stringency without examining whether the instrument operates. 0 = it argues only that the policy should be stronger or weaker.
Y NAME: Tests whether the chosen instrument will actually bind
Y INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment examines whether the proposed instrument would change behavior at all, not just its direction. A 10 asks or shows whether a numeric limit sits above observed practice, whether a threshold would ever be triggered, whether a voluntary approach has already been tried and failed, or whether the underlying model can resolve the regulated effect, and supports the answer. A 5 questions stringency without examining whether the instrument operates. A 0 argues only that the policy should be stronger or weaker.

--- PAIR GP012 ---
X NAME: Unsolicited commendation of agency staff and process
X INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 on how much the comment praises the agency itself - its staff, its effort, its priorities, or the rulemaking process - beyond the pro-forma 'we appreciate the opportunity to comment' line, which does not count. Score 10 for explicit commendation of the work or the workers ('kudos to the staff who undertook such a massive job', 'Service staff are to be commended', 'thanks to the Department for balancing the mandate with industry concerns', 'thank you for your dedication and drive', 'Great initiative'). Score 5 for a single warm sentence about the proposal being a step in the right direction. Score 0 for no praise of the agency anywhere. Do not count praise of the underlying policy goal by third parties, and do not count the ritual opening thanks.
Y NAME: Unsolicited commendation of agency staff and process
Y INSTRUCTION: Score how much the comment praises the agency itself, its staff, its effort, its priorities, or the rulemaking process, beyond the pro-forma 'we appreciate the opportunity to comment', which does not count. A 10 explicitly commends the work or the workers ('kudos to the staff', 'thank you for your dedication'). A 5 offers one warm sentence calling the proposal a step in the right direction. A 0 praises the agency nowhere. Do not count praise of the policy goal by third parties.

--- PAIR GP013 ---

Y NAME: Reports first-hand operational experience

--- PAIR GP015 ---
X NAME: Litigation-brief posture
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment adopts an adversarial legal-brief structure with claims of statutory conflict, plain-text mandates, legal jeopardy, formal violations, and requested legal remedies; score 0 when it lacks that posture.
Y NAME: Litigation-brief posture
Y INSTRUCTION: Score how far the comment adopts an adversarial legal-brief posture. --- PAIR GP016 ---
X NAME: Distinguishes what the rule requires from what it merely permits
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment correctly separates mandatory from discretionary elements of the proposal and reasons about the difference. Score 0 when it treats the proposal undifferentiated. Judge whether the distinction is drawn and used.
Y NAME: Distinguishes what the rule requires from what it merely permits
Y INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment separates what the proposal would require from what it would merely allow. A 10 correctly identifies the mandatory and the discretionary elements and reasons about the difference between them. A 5 notes the distinction in passing without using it in any reasoning. A 0 treats the proposal as undifferentiated, drawing no line between mandates and options.

--- PAIR GP017 ---
X NAME: Traceable Evidence Use
X INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment's factual claims rest on support a reader could locate and check. A 10 identifies specific studies, datasets, statutes, official analyses, or clearly described observations, and explains their relevance and limitations. A 5 points to real sources but leaves their bearing or limits unstated. A 0 leaves claims unsupported or rests them on vague references such as 'studies show'.
Y NAME: Traceable Evidence Use

--- PAIR GP018 ---

--- PAIR GP020 ---
X NAME: Traceable causal chain from the rule to the claimed effect
X INSTRUCTION: Score whether claimed consequences are connected to the rule by a mechanism a reader can follow and check. A 10 names the intermediate steps, making the claim falsifiable: the rule permits A, which changes B, which produces C. A 5 states one link and assumes the rest. A 0 asserts bare consequences with no pathway ('this will destroy the program'). Score the chain's presence and checkability, not agreement with its conclusion.
Y NAME: Traceable causal chain from the rule to the claimed effect
Y INSTRUCTION: Score whether consequences are connected to the rule by a mechanism a reader can follow and check. 10 = intermediate steps are named, so the claim is falsifiable (the rule permits A, which changes B, which produces C, which matters because D). 5 = one link is stated and the rest assumed. 0 = consequences are asserted as bare outcomes with no pathway ('this will destroy the program', 'this will endanger everyone'). Judge the presence and checkability of the chain, not whether you agree with its conclusion.

Y NAME: Ties a requested change to a specific paragraph of regulatory text

A 0: the comment states only a general 'for' or 'against' position with no specific alternative.
