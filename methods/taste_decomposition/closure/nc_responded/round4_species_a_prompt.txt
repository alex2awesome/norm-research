You are auditing a pool of candidate scoring criteria written by several
independent analysts for judging PUBLIC COMMENTS submitted to United States federal
agencies on proposed rules.

For each PAIR below, decide ONE question:

  Would an independent judge, scoring public comments against criterion X and against
  criterion Y, be measuring THE SAME UNDERLYING CONCEPT?

Answer SAME only if the two would produce essentially interchangeable scores because
they name one concept in different words. Answer DIFFERENT if a comment could plausibly
score high on one and low on the other -- including when the two are closely related,
overlapping, or members of the same family. Related is not the same.

Emit exactly one JSON object and nothing else:

{"verdicts": [
  {"pair_id": "FP001", "verdict": "SAME" or "DIFFERENT", "confidence": "high"/"medium"/"low",
   "reason": "<one sentence>"},
  ... one entry for EVERY pair ...
]}


--- PAIR FP001 ---
X NAME: Responsiveness to what the rule changes
X INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 whether the comment addresses the specific delta the rule introduces relative to current practice, versus discussing the general subject-matter area without engaging what is actually changing. A 10: the comment precisely identifies and responds to the specific change(s) being proposed. A 0: the comment discusses the general policy area in terms that would apply regardless of what the specific rule change actually is.
Y NAME: Specific, precise proposed regulatory fix
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 how concretely the comment specifies what should change: exact replacement text, a specific numeric threshold, or a precise procedural modification, versus a general expression of support or opposition. A 10: the comment proposes exact substitute wording, a specific number, or an unambiguous procedural change the agency could adopt directly. A 0: the comment states only a general 'for' or 'against' position with no specific alternative.

--- PAIR FP002 ---
X NAME: Quantitative Magnitude And Context
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment uses relevant numbers, denominators, comparisons, or calculations to show the scale and practical significance of an effect; score 0 when numbers are absent, unexplained, or merely rhetorical.
Y NAME: Measurement and evaluation design
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment specifies what should be measured, compared, monitored, or studied, including useful metrics or comparison groups; score 0 when it calls for more research or transparency without saying what information would resolve the issue.

--- PAIR FP003 ---

--- PAIR FP004 ---

--- PAIR FP005 ---
X NAME: Omission detection
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment identifies a material population, condition, data source, activity, or risk omitted from the proposal and explains how that omission changes the analysis; score 0 when it merely lists additional topics without showing their relevance.
Y NAME: Names a specific omission in the agency's supporting analysis
Y INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment engages the agency's own record rather than only its own position. 10 = it points to a particular thing the notice, preamble, or impact analysis failed to address - an unexamined failure mode, an uncosted burden, a population or entity class not analyzed, a required analysis not performed, a study that does not exist - and says why that omission bears on the decision. 5 = it gestures at inadequate analysis without locating the gap. 0 = the agency's record never enters; the comment argues only for its preferred outcome.

--- PAIR FP009 ---
X NAME: Feasibility-aware constructive alternative
X INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 whether the comment offers a path forward that accounts for the agency's likely practical constraints (cost, timing, administrability), versus only stating a problem or demand with no attention to what the agency could feasibly do. A 0: the comment only opposes, complains, or demands, with no attention to feasibility.
Y NAME: Workable alternative with feasibility and transition reasoning

--- PAIR FP010 ---
X NAME: Corrective specificity
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment identifies precise text, threshold, definition, exemption, boundary, or procedure to change and states the requested replacement; score 0 when the requested action is merely support or opposition.
Y NAME: Provision-level remedy: names the text and the replacement
Y INSTRUCTION: Score how far the comment converts its position into something a drafter could act on. 10 = it identifies the specific provision, paragraph, threshold, table entry, tariff code, or definition at issue AND states what it should say instead (a number to change, a category to delete, a phrase to substitute, a standard to adopt). 5 = it names a specific requirement but leaves the fix implicit ('this should be reconsidered', 'we have concerns'). 0 = it expresses only a direction of preference ('do more', 'protect us', 'act quickly') with nothing operable behind it. Score the operability of the ask itself; a two-sentence comment that says 'change 25 percent to 100 percent in paragraph (c)(2)' scores high, and an elaborate letter that never states a requested change scores low.
