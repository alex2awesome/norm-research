You are auditing a pool of candidate scoring criteria written by several
independent analysts for judging PUBLIC COMMENTS submitted to United States federal
agencies on proposed rules.

For each PAIR below, decide ONE question:

  Would an independent judge, scoring public comments against criterion X and against
  criterion Y, be measuring THE SAME UNDERLYING CONCEPT?

Answer SAME only if the two would produce essentially interchangeable scores because
they name one concept in different words. Answer DIFFERENT if a comment could plausibly
score high on one and low on the other -- including when the two are closely related,
overlapping, or members of the same family. Related is not the same.

Emit exactly one JSON object and nothing else:

{"verdicts": [
  {"pair_id": "FP001", "verdict": "SAME" or "DIFFERENT", "confidence": "high"/"medium"/"low",
   "reason": "<one sentence>"},
  ... one entry for EVERY pair ...
]}

X NAME: Constructive Counterargument
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment acknowledges a plausible opposing consideration or preserves a useful part of the proposal, then explains a focused modification; score 0 when it offers only unconditional praise, rejection, or attack.
Y NAME: Explicit engagement with tradeoffs or counterarguments
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 on whether the comment acknowledges a real cost, competing consideration, or counterargument to its own position and explains how it weighs against the recommendation made. X INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment demonstrates that the writer actually read and correctly understood the proposed action before responding to it. A 10 characterizes the proposal correctly (quoting or paraphrasing it faithfully) and the response is aimed at what the agency actually proposed. A 5 is broadly on target but garbles a material aspect of the proposal or the posture of the proceeding. A 0 misdescribes the proposal, responds to a different action, or contains no engagement with it at all. Text copied from the agency's own document without any responsive content scores 0, since nothing has been characterized.
Y NAME: Proposal-Specific Engagement
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment accurately addresses particular provisions, definitions, thresholds, or alternatives in the proposal and gives targeted recommendations; score 0 when it could apply unchanged to almost any rule.

--- PAIR FP049 ---
X NAME: Checkable sources that actually support the claim made
X INSTRUCTION: Score the evidentiary backing: are the claims sourced, are the sources the kind that can bear the weight, and does what is cited actually support the proposition it is attached to? A 10 cites identifiable evidence (studies, agency data, statutes, published analyses) that genuinely establishes the point being argued. A 5 cites something real but weak, dated, or only loosely connected to the claim. A 0 makes empirical assertions with no support, or attaches a citation to a proposition it does not establish. Penalize a mismatch between source and claim as heavily as an absent source.
Y NAME: Verifiable, attributable evidentiary sourcing
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 on whether factual claims used to support the comment's position are attached to identifiable, checkable sources (named studies, agency data, statutes, official reports) as opposed to vague, unattributed, or dubious sourcing (unnamed blogs, hearsay, 'as I understand it'). A 10: specific citations to checkable sources that actually support the claim made. A 0: confident factual claims with no source, or sourced to non-authoritative/unrelated material.

--- PAIR FP050 ---
X NAME: Identifies a false or outdated premise in the proposal
X INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment locates a specific assumption the proposal rests on and shows why it is wrong, no longer true, or does not hold for some class of cases. A 10 names the premise, shows it is mistaken, and draws the consequence for the rule (the requirement is misdirected, the scope is wrong, the effect will invert). A 5 asserts that the agency has misunderstood something without demonstrating it. A 0 registers disagreement with the outcome while accepting or ignoring the reasoning behind it. Reward attacks on the reasoning, not the intensity of the objection.
Y NAME: Engages a named provision, alternative, or solicited question
Y INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment fastens onto something specific in the proposal (a section, a defined alternative, a numeric threshold, a phrase, a question the agency expressly asked) AND says what is wrong with it or answers it. Both halves are required: a 10 both identifies the exact target and gives a reason or an answer, so the agency could act on the comment without guessing which provision is at issue. A 5 identifies a target with only a bare preference attached, or gives reasoning that never lands on a locatable part of the proposal. A 0 addresses only the general subject matter of the rulemaking. Pinpointing alone, with no stated reason, does not earn above a 4.

--- PAIR FP051 ---
X NAME: Compliance feasibility grounded in how the work is done
X INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment demonstrates concrete knowledge of what complying would actually require, and uses it to evaluate the proposal. A 10 walks through the real sequence of work, access constraints, staffing, or timing and shows why the proposed requirement is or is not achievable as written, ideally proposing the achievable version. A 5 asserts that something is burdensome or unrealistic without describing the work. A 0 makes a demand with no account of how anyone would carry it out. Detailed knowledge of the operation, not the strength of the complaint, is what is being scored.
Y NAME: Feasible Implementation Design
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment identifies who would implement the change, the practical constraints, and a workable adjustment that preserves the regulatory objective; score 0 when it demands action without implementation analysis.

--- PAIR FP052 ---
X NAME: Long-Term Risk Scope
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment identifies delayed, cumulative, geographically connected, or persistent effects and specifies the relevant time or spatial horizon; score 0 when risks are treated as immediate, local, or indefinite without explanation.
Y NAME: Temporal and cumulative effects
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment analyzes delayed, repeated, cumulative, or long-term effects rather than only immediate impacts; score 0 when its reasoning is confined to a short-term assertion.

--- PAIR FP053 ---
X NAME: Regulatory Coherence
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment identifies a concrete conflict, gap, overlap, or interaction with existing law, standards, programs, or guidance and proposes a coherent resolution; score 0 when outside authorities are merely mentioned.
Y NAME: Legal-technical coherence
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment identifies a specific conflict, assumption, statutory boundary, or inconsistency and reasons through its practical significance; score 0 when it relies on legal or technical language without applying it.

--- PAIR FP054 ---
X NAME: Decision-Relevant Novelty
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment contributes material information, context, or an affected operating environment not apparent from the proposal and explains why it changes the analysis; score 0 when it repeats the proposal, supplies boilerplate, or merely endorses another submission.
Y NAME: Constructive Counterargument
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment acknowledges a plausible opposing consideration or preserves a useful part of the proposal, then explains a focused modification; score 0 when it offers only unconditional praise, rejection, or attack.

--- PAIR FP055 ---
X NAME: Mechanistic causal reasoning
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment traces a specific proposal or requirement through a plausible mechanism to concrete consequences; score 0 when it only asserts that something is good or harmful without explaining how.
Y NAME: Traces a causal chain from provision to effect
Y INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment connects the specific regulatory text to a concrete outcome through stated intermediate steps, rather than asserting a consequence. A 10 lays out the chain (this requirement causes this behavior by this actor, which produces this result) with each link plausible and stated. A 5 names a cause and an effect with the mechanism left implicit. A 0 asserts harm or benefit with no mechanism, or names only a mood or a value. The chain need not be quantified; it must be articulated.

--- PAIR FP056 ---
X NAME: Comparative tradeoff analysis
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment compares plausible alternatives, states their advantages and disadvantages, and explains why the preferred option performs better; score 0 when it presents only a categorical conclusion.
Y NAME: Explicit engagement with tradeoffs or counterarguments
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 on whether the comment acknowledges a real cost, competing consideration, or counterargument to its own position and explains how it weighs against the recommendation made. A 10: names a specific tradeoff (cost, burden, competing value) and explains why the recommendation still holds, or proposes a compromise. A 0: pure one-sided advocacy with no acknowledgment that any cost or competing interest exists.

--- PAIR FP057 ---
X NAME: Monitoring and Verification Design
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment proposes measurable monitoring, testing, reporting, feedback, or verification that would reveal whether the regulatory approach is functioning; score 0 when it asks for protection or compliance without a way to assess it.
Y NAME: Monitoring and corrective feedback
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment specifies what should be monitored, reported, tested, or reviewed and how the information would guide correction; score 0 when it recommends action without a way to evaluate performance.

--- PAIR FP058 ---
X NAME: Traceable evidentiary support
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when important factual claims are supported by identifiable data, studies, records, or authoritative sources used in the reasoning; score 0 when claims are unsupported, vague, or presented as self-evident.
Y NAME: Verifiable, attributable evidentiary sourcing
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 on whether factual claims used to support the comment's position are attached to identifiable, checkable sources (named studies, agency data, statutes, official reports) as opposed to vague, unattributed, or dubious sourcing (unnamed blogs, hearsay, 'as I understand it'). A 10: specific citations to checkable sources that actually support the claim made. A 0: confident factual claims with no source, or sourced to non-authoritative/unrelated material.

--- PAIR FP059 ---
X NAME: Alternative Tradeoff Analysis
X INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment compares plausible policy options, explicitly weighs their benefits, costs, and risks, and explains why one option is preferable; score 0 when it simply endorses or rejects one option.
Y NAME: Explicit engagement with tradeoffs or counterarguments
Y INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 on whether the comment acknowledges a real cost, competing consideration, or counterargument to its own position and explains how it weighs against the recommendation made. A 10: names a specific tradeoff (cost, burden, competing value) and explains why the recommendation still holds, or proposes a compromise. A 0: pure one-sided advocacy with no acknowledgment that any cost or competing interest exists.

--- PAIR FP060 ---
X NAME: First-hand experiential grounding with checkable specifics
X INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 on whether the commenter grounds their position in their own direct, checkable stake or observation (a named location, date, incident, or role they personally experienced) rather than a generic expression of concern. A 10: specific personal facts (address, date, named event) that tie the commenter's position to something verifiable. A 0: comment expresses concern or support with no personal, checkable detail -- it could have been written by anyone about anything similar.
Y NAME: First-hand situated account of how the rule lands
Y INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment supplies a specific, located account of the rule's real-world contact surface: an identified place, incident, caseload, transaction, or population the writer has direct contact with, described in enough particularity to be checked. A 10 gives dated or located specifics that the agency could verify and that bear on the provision at issue. A 5 gestures at experience ('in my work I see...') without any particular instance. A 0 speaks only in generalities about people in the abstract. Do not score sympathy or urgency; score the particularity of the situation described.
