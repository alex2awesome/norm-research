You are an editor for a bank of scoring criteria used to judge PUBLIC
COMMENTS submitted to United States federal agencies on proposed rules. Each criterion
is scored 0-10 by an independent judge who sees ONLY one comment's text and ONE
criterion.

Rewrite EVERY criterion below into the bank's house style. House style is:

  * ONE scorable property per criterion, stated in the first sentence;
  * an explicit description of what a 10 looks like and what a 0 looks like;
  * judgeable from the comment text alone, by a reader with no outside information;
  * plain declarative sentences, 2-4 sentences, no more than ~80 words;
  * NO reference to any outcome (agency response, rule change, acceptance, approval);
  * NO instruction that tells the judge to score the SHAPE of the document in place of
    its substance (e.g. "score the question-and-answer structure, not the importance of
    the question"). If the underlying property is substantive, the instruction must
    score the substance; if the underlying property is a surface feature, say so
    plainly and score the surface feature, without dressing it as merit.

HARD CONSTRAINT: preserve the CONCEPT exactly. You may sharpen, disambiguate and
anchor the wording. You may NOT broaden it, narrow it, or replace it with a nearby
idea. An independent judge must agree that the rewritten criterion measures the same
thing as the original.

Emit exactly one JSON object and nothing else:

{"rewrites": [
  {"id": "<the id given below, unchanged>",
   "name": "<short name, <= 12 words; keep the original unless it is unclear>",
   "instruction": "<the rewritten 0-10 scoring instruction>"},
  ... one entry for EVERY criterion below ...
]}


--- CRITERION A01 ---
NAME: Causal mechanism from provision to consequence
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment traces a credible chain from the proposed provision through behavior or implementation to a safety, health, environmental, or administrative consequence; score 0 when it merely asserts that harm or benefit will occur.

--- CRITERION A02 ---
NAME: Marks the limits of its evidence and reasons from them
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment distinguishes what it knows from what it does not, and whether the recommendation follows from that distinction. 10 = the comment states where the evidence runs out, identifies the specific unknowns, and derives a recommendation that is responsive to the uncertainty (precaution, a specified disclosure, a question the agency must answer before proceeding). 5 = uncertainty is acknowledged in passing and does not affect the recommendation. 0 = uniform certainty regardless of basis, or hedging with no consequence attached.

--- CRITERION A03 ---
NAME: Credible alternative meeting the regulatory objective
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment proposes a specific alternative, exception, phase-in, or substitute and explains why it can achieve the same objective with relevant tradeoffs; score 0 when it only supports or opposes the proposal.

--- CRITERION A04 ---
NAME: Implementation realism
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment maps the practical steps, resources, timing, personnel, or supply-chain conditions needed to implement the proposal; score 0 when it ignores implementation realities.

--- CRITERION A05 ---
NAME: Tradeoff and side-effect analysis
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment weighs the proposal’s benefits against costs, risks, displaced protections, or unintended effects and explains the relevant tradeoff; score 0 when it presents only one-sided approval or opposition.

--- CRITERION A06 ---
NAME: Cross-rule and system interaction
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment identifies overlap, contradiction, gap, or sequencing with another rule, program, document, or process and specifies how to resolve it; score 0 when it analyzes the proposal in isolation.

--- CRITERION A07 ---
NAME: Quantified supporting evidence
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment uses relevant numerical data, calculations, comparisons, or rates to support its position; score 0 when it provides no verifiable quantitative support.

--- CRITERION A08 ---
NAME: Distributional consequence analysis
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment identifies which vulnerable, dependent, underserved, or differently situated groups bear distinct effects and explains the mechanism; score 0 when it invokes broad public interest without identifying affected groups.

--- CRITERION A09 ---
NAME: Ask serves more than the commenter's own burden or exposure
INSTRUCTION: Score the beneficiary of the requested change as the comment itself frames it. 10 = the change would improve the rule's functioning for third parties, the protected resource, or the public, including where it plainly costs the commenter something. 5 = mixed: the ask helps the commenter but the stated reasons are about workability for everyone. 0 = the entire ask is relief for the submitter -- delay, exemption, a higher threshold, more time -- with the public-interest framing supplying no independent content.

--- CRITERION A10 ---
NAME: Explicit evidence-to-requested-remedy linkage
INSTRUCTION: Score 0-10 on whether the comment draws an explicit, traceable line from the evidence or reasoning it presents to the exact provision, number, or action it asks the agency to change. A 10 shows the requested remedy derived transparently, step by step, from the stated evidence. A 0 presents evidence/complaint and a specific ask as disconnected pieces, with no traceable link between them.

--- CRITERION A11 ---
NAME: Audits the agency's own figures, record, or arithmetic
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment tests the agency's stated justification against numbers or statements the agency itself placed in the record. 10 = the comment reworks the agency's arithmetic, or juxtaposes the proposal with the agency's own admission or its own prior rule, and shows the figures so the check is reproducible. 5 = the comment disputes a number but supplies no computation or source. 0 = the agency's figures are accepted or ignored entirely; the comment engages only at the level of preference.

--- CRITERION A12 ---
NAME: Applies a controlling standard to the facts rather than citing it
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment takes a governing legal, scientific, or professional standard and works the proposal's facts through it. 10 = the standard is named, its elements are identified, and the proposal's facts are matched against those elements to show fit or misfit, with the mismatch driving the recommendation. 5 = a standard is invoked and the conclusion asserted, with the application left implicit. 0 = authority or the notice is quoted or summarized at length in place of analysis, or no standard is engaged.

--- CRITERION A13 ---
NAME: Surfaces an affected class or case the proposal overlooked
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment identifies a specific category of person, entity, or operating situation that the proposal's text does not appear to contemplate, or contemplates wrongly, and shows the consequence of that gap. 10 = a concrete class or edge case is named, its exclusion or misclassification is tied to specific proposal language, and the resulting harm or absurdity is stated. 5 = a gap is gestured at but neither the class nor the consequence is made concrete. 0 = the comment discusses only the population the notice already addresses.

--- CRITERION A14 ---
NAME: Identifies an internal inconsistency between parts of the proposal
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment points to two parts of the proposed rule or its preamble that cannot both hold, and says why. Score 0 when no inconsistency is identified. Judge whether a specific conflict is shown.

--- CRITERION A15 ---
NAME: States the conditions under which its own recommendation would fail
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment names the circumstances in which its own proposed course of action would not work, or what evidence would change its recommendation. Score 0 when the comment offers no such limits. Judge whether limits on its own claim are stated.

--- CRITERION B01 ---
NAME: Agency-relationship cues
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment mentions prior meetings, ongoing collaboration, earlier submissions, established partnerships, or direct exchanges with agency personnel; score 0 when it has only a generic salutation and no relationship history.

--- CRITERION B02 ---
NAME: Credential and status parade
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the opening prominently lists multiple degrees, certifications, fellowships, offices, titles, or years of experience; score 0 when no status or expertise signals appear.

--- CRITERION B03 ---
NAME: Professional document apparatus
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the submission contains elaborate exhibits, master indexes, slide decks, service-bulletin reproductions, revision histories, or attachment-heavy packaging; score 0 for a plain standalone comment with no document-production signals.

--- CRITERION B04 ---
NAME: First-person credential or stake self-disclosure
INSTRUCTION: Score how explicitly and prominently the comment establishes the writer's personal identity, credentials, professional role, or stake in the issue (e.g. 'I am a Professor...', 'I have X years of experience...', 'I represent...', naming a degree, certification, or affiliation) as a rhetorical move, independent of whether that identity is actually load-bearing for the argument's substance. 10 = the comment opens with or centers on an extended personal/professional credential or identity statement. 0 = the comment contains no self-identifying credential or stake language at all.

--- CRITERION B05 ---
NAME: Emotional advocacy intensity
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 for repeated alarm, outrage, moral condemnation, emphatic praise, all-caps, exclamation marks, or vivid metaphors; score 0 for consistently restrained, neutral prose.

--- CRITERION B06 ---
NAME: NGO action-alert templated phrasing
INSTRUCTION: Score the presence of stock activist/action-alert phrasing typical of NGO mass-comment campaign tools: formulaic gratitude closings ('THANK YOU for your commitment to...'), superlative-modified statements of position ('I HIGHLY support...', 'I strongly urge...'), and emphatic capitalized keywords, especially combined with a short, largely interchangeable one-fact-plus-a-plea structure. 10 = the comment strongly exhibits this templated activist register. 0 = the comment shows no such stock phrasing.

--- CRITERION B07 ---
NAME: Embedded formal citation within personal-voice prose
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment juxtaposes an otherwise personal, informal, first-person narrative voice with one or more embedded formal legal or scholarly citations (e.g. a full case citation, a parenthetical citation to a named organization, a footnoted source) that read as though inserted by someone other than the narrating voice. 10 = a clear formal citation is embedded within personal/informal prose, creating a register shift. 0 = no such embedded citation is present, or the whole comment is uniformly formal or uniformly informal with no citations at all.

--- CRITERION B08 ---
NAME: Formal itemized technical/regulatory rebuttal structure
INSTRUCTION: Score the degree to which the comment is organized as a formal point-by-point regulatory rebuttal: addressed to a named official/office, broken into discrete numbered or lettered items, each citing a specific rule paragraph, CFR subsection, AD/docket number, or service-bulletin reference, with little connective persuasive narrative between points. 10 = the entire comment is such a structured technical-correction list (e.g. 'Comment No1... Page/Paragraph/Section... What is the FAA's proposed requirement...'). 0 = the comment is continuous persuasive prose with no addressee-directed itemized rule-section corrections.

--- CRITERION B09 ---
NAME: Docket number or RIN quoted verbatim
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when a docket identifier or RIN appears verbatim in the comment. Score 0 when neither appears. Score only whether the identifier string is present, never whether the comment addresses the right rule.

--- CRITERION B10 ---
NAME: Text is entirely upper case
INSTRUCTION: Score 10 when the comment is written wholly or almost wholly in capital letters. Score 0 when it uses ordinary sentence casing. Score only the letter casing.
