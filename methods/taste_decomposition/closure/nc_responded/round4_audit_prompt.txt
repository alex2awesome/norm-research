You are an independent auditor. Below are candidate criteria for scoring
PUBLIC COMMENTS submitted to United States federal agencies on proposed rules. Each
would be scored 0-10 by a judge reading only one comment's text.

Classify EACH criterion as exactly one of:

  "quality_relevant" -- it measures something a careful regulatory analyst would call
      substantive merit of the comment as regulatory input: evidence, analysis,
      specificity about the rule, feasibility, actionable alternatives, first-hand
      knowledge, engagement with the agency's reasoning.

  "incidental" -- it is predictive-but-not-merit: a surface or shape feature, a
      formatting or length proxy, boilerplate or template similarity, a topic or
      agency-style marker, a submitter-identity or professional-drafting fingerprint,
      a tone or register marker, or the bare presence of a word or phrase.

DECISION AIDS.
  * If the criterion could be scored high by a comment that says nothing substantive,
    it is incidental.
  * If the instruction tells the judge to score the SHAPE of the document rather than
    what the document establishes, it is incidental -- even when the shape usually
    co-occurs with merit. Say so in your justification if you see this.
  * "Related to quality" is not enough. The question is what the JUDGE would be
    scoring when following this exact instruction.

Emit exactly one JSON object and nothing else:

{"verdicts": [
  {"id": "P01", "label": "quality_relevant" or "incidental",
   "confidence": "high"/"medium"/"low",
   "justification": "<one or two sentences>"},
  ... one entry for EVERY criterion ...
]}


--- CRITERION P01 ---
NAME: Emotional-hook opening sentence before substantive content
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment opens with a short, emotionally loaded sentence or fragment working as an attention-grabbing headline, such as a declarative claim of personal harm, moral outrage, or a rhetorical jab, before any substantive policy content. A 10 opens with a clear stand-alone hook line, disconnected from any factual support that immediately follows. A 5 opens with a substantive claim carrying noticeable emotional charge. A 0 opens directly with a substantive claim, fact, or procedural statement.

--- CRITERION P02 ---
NAME: Acknowledgment of tradeoffs and counterarguments
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment engages costs, competing interests, or plausible reasons for the opposite position. A 10 explicitly identifies and weighs a genuine tradeoff or counter-consideration while still holding its own position. A 5 mentions an opposing consideration but dismisses it without weighing it. A 0 is purely one-sided and shows no awareness that any opposing consideration exists.

--- CRITERION P03 ---
NAME: Challenge to agency assumptions
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment targets a specific premise, dataset, model, or inference in the proposal. A 10 identifies that particular element and presents concrete evidence or reasoning that could disprove or materially revise it. A 5 identifies the element but offers only assertion against it. A 0 merely disagrees with the proposal's conclusion.

--- CRITERION P04 ---
NAME: Reasoning-based persuasion vs rhetorical amplification
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment's persuasive force comes from developed reasoning and evidence rather than from rhetorical amplification. A 10 rests entirely on developed reasoning or evidence delivered in a measured tone, even when the position is strongly held. A 5 mixes real argument with heated rhetoric. A 0 substitutes capitalized or repeated demands, insults, or hyperbole for any developed argument.

--- CRITERION P05 ---
NAME: Litigation-brief posture
INSTRUCTION: Score how far the comment adopts an adversarial legal-brief posture. A 10 reads like a brief: claims of statutory conflict, plain-text mandates, legal jeopardy or formal violations, and explicitly requested legal remedies. A 5 makes one such legal-conflict claim inside otherwise ordinary prose. A 0 has none of that posture. Score the posture itself, not whether the legal claims are correct.

--- CRITERION P06 ---
NAME: Identifies a conflict with operative statutory text
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment demonstrates a conflict between the proposal and controlling legal text. A 10 quotes or precisely cites the operative provision and shows the proposal cannot be squared with it, or that it triggers a procedure the statute requires and the notice omitted. A 5 cites a relevant statute or standard but asserts the tension rather than showing it. A 0 has no legal grounding, or cites authorities decoratively without showing any conflict.

--- CRITERION P07 ---
NAME: Docket-lineage familiarity
INSTRUCTION: Score how much the comment narrates its own prior involvement in this regulatory process. A 10 recounts specific earlier filings, prior amendments, pilot participation, waivers, or agency precedents it took part in. A 5 alludes to past involvement without specifics. A 0 treats the docket as entirely new and references no prior history.

--- CRITERION P08 ---
NAME: Names a specific omission in the agency's supporting analysis
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment locates a specific gap in the agency's own supporting analysis. A 10 points to a particular matter the notice, preamble, or impact analysis failed to address (an unexamined failure mode, an uncosted burden, an unanalyzed population, a required analysis not performed) and says why that omission bears on the decision. A 5 alleges inadequate analysis without locating the gap. A 0 never engages the agency's record.

--- CRITERION P09 ---
NAME: Ties a requested change to a specific paragraph of regulatory text
INSTRUCTION: Score whether a requested change is anchored to identified regulatory text. A 10 states what the comment wants changed and ties that request to a named paragraph, section, or table of the proposed text. A 5 requests a change and identifies only the broad part or topic it falls under. A 0 requests no change, or makes a request that names no provision. Score the anchoring, not the merit of the change requested.

--- CRITERION P10 ---
NAME: Tests whether the chosen instrument will actually bind
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment examines whether the proposed instrument would change behavior at all, not just its direction. A 10 asks or shows whether a numeric limit sits above observed practice, whether a threshold would ever be triggered, whether a voluntary approach has already been tried and failed, or whether the underlying model can resolve the regulated effect, and supports the answer. A 5 questions stringency without examining whether the instrument operates. A 0 argues only that the policy should be stronger or weaker.

--- CRITERION P11 ---
NAME: Traceable Evidence Use
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment's factual claims rest on support a reader could locate and check. A 10 identifies specific studies, datasets, statutes, official analyses, or clearly described observations, and explains their relevance and limitations. A 5 points to real sources but leaves their bearing or limits unstated. A 0 leaves claims unsupported or rests them on vague references such as 'studies show'.

--- CRITERION P12 ---
NAME: Unintended-consequence safeguards
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment pairs a plausible secondary harm with a concrete way to contain it. A 10 identifies a specific secondary failure or side effect and proposes a definite safeguard, monitoring step, exception, or contingency. A 5 identifies the secondary harm and its pathway but proposes no remedy. A 0 predicts harm without explaining the pathway or offering any mitigation.

--- CRITERION P13 ---
NAME: Unsolicited commendation of agency staff and process
INSTRUCTION: Score how much the comment praises the agency itself, its staff, its effort, its priorities, or the rulemaking process, beyond the pro-forma 'we appreciate the opportunity to comment', which does not count. A 10 explicitly commends the work or the workers ('kudos to the staff', 'thank you for your dedication'). A 5 offers one warm sentence calling the proposal a step in the right direction. A 0 praises the agency nowhere. Do not count praise of the policy goal by third parties.

--- CRITERION P14 ---
NAME: Formal institutional filing apparatus / letterhead conventions
INSTRUCTION: Score the density of formal filing conventions in the text: an addressee block, a 'Re:' caption with docket or RIN number, numbered footnotes describing membership or assets, a formal salutation such as 'Dear Sir or Madam', and a closing signature with a professional title. A 10 has the full apparatus. A 5 has one or two of these conventions. A 0 is plain conversational text with none. These are formatting conventions; score their presence only.

--- CRITERION P15 ---
NAME: Viable alternative design
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment offers a practicable alternative regulatory design that still advances the proposal's stated objective while addressing the defect it identifies. A 10 describes such an alternative in enough detail that how it would work is clear. A 5 names an alternative direction without specifying any design. A 0 offers nothing beyond withdrawing the proposal or enforcing it as written.

--- CRITERION P16 ---
NAME: Organizational scale self-credentialing
INSTRUCTION: Score how much the comment foregrounds its own organizational scale: quantified membership, customers, assets, market share, national reach, or institutional prestige. A 10 puts such claims front and center, with figures or superlatives. A 5 mentions organizational size once, in passing. A 0 makes no organizational-scale claim. Score the presence and prominence of these claims, never whether they make the argument stronger.

--- CRITERION P17 ---
NAME: Distinguishes what the rule requires from what it merely permits
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment separates what the proposal would require from what it would merely allow. A 10 correctly identifies the mandatory and the discretionary elements and reasons about the difference between them. A 5 notes the distinction in passing without using it in any reasoning. A 0 treats the proposal as undifferentiated, drawing no line between mandates and options.

--- CRITERION P18 ---
NAME: Repeats the same sentence or phrase multiple times
INSTRUCTION: Score verbatim repetition of a sentence or phrase within the comment. A 10 repeats the same sentence or phrase word for word three or more times. A 5 repeats it word for word exactly twice. A 0 contains no verbatim repetition. Score the repetition itself as a surface property of the text.

--- CRITERION P19 ---
NAME: Operational Feasibility
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment explains how affected parties would actually implement, administer, document, or comply with the proposed approach. A 10 walks through the concrete steps such compliance would take and names specific operational obstacles. A 5 raises implementation concerns only in general terms. A 0 treats implementation abstractly or not at all.

--- CRITERION P20 ---
NAME: Traceable causal chain from the rule to the claimed effect
INSTRUCTION: Score whether claimed consequences are connected to the rule by a mechanism a reader can follow and check. A 10 names the intermediate steps, making the claim falsifiable: the rule permits A, which changes B, which produces C. A 5 states one link and assumes the rest. A 0 asserts bare consequences with no pathway ('this will destroy the program'). Score the chain's presence and checkability, not agreement with its conclusion.

--- CRITERION P21 ---
NAME: Field observation that disconfirms a factual premise of the rule
INSTRUCTION: Score whether firsthand experience is aimed at a factual premise rather than at a preference. A 10 reports something the commenter directly observed, operated, or measured that contradicts a specific factual assumption the agency relies on (a stock estimate, an occupancy map, an achievability or exposure estimate), and names the assumption it contradicts. A 5 offers relevant firsthand experience as grounds for a preference, confronting no stated premise. A 0 reports no firsthand observation bearing on any premise.

--- CRITERION P22 ---
NAME: Mentions the number of members or constituents represented
INSTRUCTION: Score whether the comment states how many members, employees, customers, or constituents the submitter speaks for. A 10 states such a count explicitly. A 0 contains no such count anywhere. Score only the presence of the stated constituency count, never whether it makes the argument stronger.

--- CRITERION P23 ---
NAME: Submission-production residue
INSTRUCTION: Score how much production and ingest residue is visible in the comment text: OCR corruption, duplicated headers, raw email routing lines, attachment placeholders, odd spacing, or extraction errors. A 10 has such artifacts running through the visible text. A 5 has one or two isolated artifacts. A 0 is cleanly presented prose with none. This is a surface property of the text; score the artifacts only.

--- CRITERION P24 ---
NAME: Operational implementation microdetail
INSTRUCTION: Score how granular the comment's inside-the-operation detail is. A 10 supplies specific workflow, equipment, filing, validation, staffing, transition, or compliance-process particulars drawn from inside an affected operation. A 5 refers to operations in general terms without particulars. A 0 offers only broad policy preferences or values and no operational detail.

--- CRITERION P25 ---
NAME: Specific, precise proposed regulatory fix
INSTRUCTION: Score how specifically the comment states the change it wants made to the proposal. A 10 supplies exact replacement wording, a specific numeric threshold, or an unambiguous procedural modification. A 5 names the direction of the change but leaves its substance unspecified. A 0 states only a general 'for' or 'against' position and offers no specific alternative.
