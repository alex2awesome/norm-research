You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about the substantive quality of the comment. It is about the substance, the
      reasoning, the evidence, the craft, or the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself the substantive quality of the comment: length or verbosity, formatting or layout,
      boilerplate or template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter
      or topic markers, temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of
      some upstream circumstance of the item's production (who made it, with what
      resources, when, with what help).

The corpus is: public comments submitted to United States federal agency rulemaking dockets. Each item is a public comment.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
well-resourced producer may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually do
better work). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces
real the substantive quality of the comment too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you
label "quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Reasoning given for why the approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the item supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X02] Recitation of the submitter organization's size or market share
On a scale of 0 (no mention of the submitting organization's membership count, revenue, or market share) to 10 (the text prominently recites such statistics, e.g. member counts, dollar figures, or percentage market share), rate how much of the text is devoted to this kind of self-credentialing. Do not judge whether the organization's scale is actually relevant to its argument.

[X03] Decisive, unambiguous stated position
Score 0-10 on whether the comment takes a clear, unambiguous stance (support, oppose, or a specific requested change) rather than only posing open questions or expressing unresolved uncertainty. 10 = an unambiguous position is stated. 0 = the comment consists entirely of questions or non-committal musing with no stated position.

[X04] Mechanistic causal reasoning
Score 10 when the comment clearly explains how the proposed policy would produce specific intermediate effects and consequences; score 0 when it states only approval, opposition, or an unelaborated prediction.

[X05] Signatory's seniority title paired with a direct personal contact line
On a scale of 0 (no named signatory, or a signatory with no title/direct contact info given) to 10 (the letter closes with a named signatory carrying a senior title -- e.g. Executive Director, Vice President, Senior Manager -- plus a direct personal phone/fax/email), rate how much of this signature-block signaling is present. Do not judge whether the signatory's seniority makes their argument more correct.

[X06] Engages the merits versus requesting only a schedule change
10 = the comment takes a position on the substance of what the agency proposed. 5 = a procedural request accompanied by real substantive comment. 0 = the comment's entire content is a request for more time or another procedural accommodation, with no position on the rule itself, however thoroughly the request is documented and footnoted. Rate the presence of merits content only; do not rate writing quality, formatting, or how well the request is justified.

[X07] Distributional impact analysis
Score 10 when the comment connects the proposal to concrete effects on identifiable communities, users, regions, or vulnerable groups and explains the distribution of those effects; score 0 when affected parties are mentioned only abstractly.

[X08] Information Gain
Score 10 when the comment contributes new facts, analysis, examples, or implications beyond stating a position; score 0 when it merely expresses support, opposition, concurrence, or preference without adding usable substance.

[X09] Overall length and verbosity of the submission
On a scale of 0 (extremely terse, a sentence or two) to 10 (very long, multi-page with extensive elaboration), rate how much text volume this comment contains. Score the sheer AMOUNT of text present, not whether the length is appropriate or whether the content in it is good.

[X10] Timing and urgency cues
How much does the text explicitly mention deadlines, extensions, recent events, production or submission dates, delay, urgency, or requests for swift action? Score only the extent from 0–10; do not judge whether the timing or urgency is justified or good.

[X11] Personal and local grounding
How much does the text contain first-person experience, personal role descriptions, local residence, place-specific activity, or direct involvement with the affected subject? Score only the extent from 0–10; do not judge whether the experience makes the comment good.

[X12] Count of numerals and digit characters in the text
Score 0-10 for how many numerals and digit characters the text contains relative to its length. 10 = digits everywhere; 0 = no digits at all. Count characters only; do not judge whether the numbers are meaningful or whether their presence is good.

[X13] Brevity that resolves to a clear disposition from a positioned party
10 = a short comment states an unambiguous disposition (concur, oppose, withdraw, support the adjustment, take no action) from someone whose position on that question is itself information the agency needs. 5 = a clear disposition from a party with no particular standing on it. 0 = short and non-committal, or long with the disposition never stated or buried past the point of recovery. Never penalize brevity as such; penalize only the absence of a recoverable position.

[X14] Explicit statement of the limitation or failure regime
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the item names a condition under which its own claim would not hold, or a case it does not cover. 10 = a specific, checkable limitation is stated; 0 = no limitation or scope condition is acknowledged anywhere.

[X15] Traceable evidence use
Score 10 when evidence is tied to identifiable studies, agencies, records, standards, or cited source material that directly supports the argument; score 0 when sources are absent, vague, or merely invoked rhetorically.

[X16] Interest disclosed, and the argument survives the interest
10 = the commenter's own benefit from the requested outcome is visible in the text, and the argument still rests on reasons that would hold for someone who did not get that benefit. 5 = interest visible, argument partly independent. 0 = the entire content of the argument is that the outcome would be good for the commenter, the commenter's program, or the commenter's accreditation framework, restated in public-interest language. Score disclosure and independence jointly: an undisclosed benefit driving the whole argument scores 0 even when the writing is polished.

[X17] Temporal and comparative context
Score 10 when the comment uses historical developments, prior rules, trends, or comparisons to clarify why the present proposal matters; score 0 when it provides no temporal or comparative context.

[X18] OCR and scan-degradation noise
Score 0-10 for the density of character-level corruption typical of a document that was faxed or paper-scanned and then passed through optical character recognition. Count: lookalike letter substitutions ('VVildlife' for 'Wildlife', 'ltr/ater' for 'Water', 'Ambient' rendered 'Ambient' with stray accents, 'Criterion' fused to the next word); stray spaces inside numbers and words ('(3 17) 592-46 16', 'Page2', 'August18,2017'); runs of punctuation or symbol garbage; mangled email addresses or URLs; and unreadable renderings of signatures, logos or letterhead art. 0 = clean digital text throughout; 5 = scattered corruption, a few lines affected; 10 = corruption in most lines. Judge only how much corruption is visible. Do NOT treat corruption as evidence of carelessness, low resources, age or standing, and do NOT judge whether it is good or bad.

[X19] Concrete regulatory exposure, not generic organizational self-description
Score how concretely the text establishes that the commenter is a party this rule actually operates on. 10 = the comment names the specific assets, products, operations, filings, acreage, catch, or populations the rule reaches (a fleet count, an affected product line, a form the commenter must file, the crop under the restriction), so the exposure is checkable from the text alone. 5 = an affected-industry claim is asserted only in general terms. 0 = identity is conveyed solely by mission statement, membership counts, or a roster of affiliates, with no link drawn between who the commenter is and what the rule does to them. Judge only the linkage between identity and rule; do not reward prestige, org size, or professional letterhead.

[X20] Written for this docket versus repurposed campaign or marketing copy
10 = the prose is addressed to the decisionmaker in this proceeding and could not be lifted intact from another context: it names the rule, addresses the agency, and its register is that of a submission. 5 = docket-aware but partly templated. 0 = the text is evidently written for another audience and pasted in, marked by second-person consumer address, headline-style openers, action-alert urgency in the close, rhetorical questions repeated verbatim, or no addressee at all. Judge the genre of the writing only, never the position taken or the sincerity of the writer.

[X21] Firsthand Operational Knowledge
Score 10 when the author connects direct professional, geographic, cultural, or experiential knowledge to specific policy effects; score 0 when claimed identity or experience is unrelated to the analysis.

[X22] Use of the Oxford (serial) comma
Score 0-10 for how consistently the text uses a comma before the final conjunction in a list. 10 = used in every list; 0 = never used or no lists present. Judge punctuation habit only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X23] Explicit causal chain from fact to recommended consequence
Score 0-10 on whether the comment explicitly connects a specific factual claim to a specific consequence for the decision via a stated causal or logical mechanism, rather than listing facts, questions, or citations without tying them into an argument. 10 = a clear 'because X, therefore Y should happen' chain is spelled out. 0 = facts or questions are listed with no connecting logic to a recommendation.

[X24] Institutional status cues
Score 0-10 for how much the text explicitly signals institutional office, executive or professional title, organizational scale, credentials, or official capacity; do not judge whether any signal is good, relevant, or deserved.

[X25] Boilerplate language
How much of the text consists of stock openings, closings, recurring organizational descriptions, formulaic requests, or repeated passages? Score only the extent from 0–10; do not judge whether the boilerplate is appropriate or good.

[X26] Submission artifact markers
Score 0-10 for how much the text contains transmission or ingestion artifacts such as attachment placeholders, email headers, fax details, file names, upload notices, or archived-message metadata; do not judge whether these artifacts help or hurt the comment.

[X27] Coalition breadth
Score 0-10 for how much the text names multiple joint commenters, member organizations, represented constituencies, or broad organizational networks; do not judge whether the coalition is representative, important, or persuasive.

[X28] Stated expertise is used to generate a specific insight, not just asserted
Score 0-10 on whether the commenter's stated credential, role, or experience is actually put to work to produce a specific, non-obvious technical judgment, rather than the credential being asserted while the rest of the comment stays at the level of generic support or opposition. 10 = credential is stated and clearly generates a specific technical point a lay commenter could not have made. 0 = a credential/affiliation is stated but the substantive content that follows is generic and could have been written by anyone.

[X29] Net information flow: contributes to the record or draws from it
10 = the comment is overwhelmingly a contribution, putting evidence, analysis, or a defensible position into the record for the agency to use. 5 = balanced. 0 = the comment is overwhelmingly a demand on the agency: it asks the agency to explain, list, confirm, clarify, define, or supply things, without supplying anything itself. Count questions and requests for agency-produced material on the 0 side and facts, reasoning, and stated positions on the 10 side. A question that embeds the answer counts as a contribution.

OUTPUT. Emit exactly one JSON object and nothing else:

{"verdicts": [
  {"id": "<the id shown>", "label": "quality_relevant" or "incidental",
    "mixed": true or false, "confidence": "high"/"medium"/"low",
    "justification": "<one sentence>"},
  ... one entry per criterion, same ids ...
]}
