You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about the substantive quality of the comment. It is about the substance, the
      reasoning, the evidence, the craft, or the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself the substantive quality of the comment: length or verbosity, formatting or layout,
      boilerplate or template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter
      or topic markers, temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of
      some upstream circumstance of the item's production (who made it, with what
      resources, when, with what help).

The corpus is: public comments submitted to United States federal agency rulemaking dockets. Each item is a public comment.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
well-resourced producer may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually do
better work). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces
real the substantive quality of the comment too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you
label "quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Legal citation density
Score 0-10 for the extent of statutes, regulations, court cases, Federal Register citations, docket numbers, and formal legal cross-references; do not judge their correctness or usefulness.

[X02] Equity-Specific Impact Analysis
Score 10 when the comment identifies a particular burden or benefit for vulnerable, underserved, or differently situated groups and proposes a concrete way to address it; score 0 when it invokes fairness or public benefit without identifying affected groups or mechanisms.

[X03] Implementation Feasibility
Score 10 when the comment addresses how regulated parties or agencies would administer, comply with, document, monitor, or enforce the proposal in practice; score 0 when practical execution is not discussed.

[X04] Explicit structural organization of distinct points
Score 10 if the comment uses explicit structural devices -- headings, numbered or lettered points, an executive summary, or roman-numeral sections -- to separate and label multiple distinct arguments or requests. Score 0 if the comment is an undifferentiated block of prose with no structural markers separating distinct points.

[X05] Representational-mandate and constituency-size self-description
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the text devotes space to describing the submitter's organisation, its membership and whom it speaks for. Count: explicit member or constituent counts ('more than 620 corporate members', 'over 30,000 professional flight crewmembers', 'more than 13,800 dermatologists', 'approximately 250 U.S. business enterprises'), founding dates and longevity claims, lists of member categories or named member entities, superlatives of scope ('the oldest and largest', 'the world's largest'), and 'on behalf of' framing that enumerates the represented parties. 0 = no self-description of the submitter. 5 = a single sentence or short paragraph of organisational description. 10 = one or more full paragraphs, or an itemised roster of represented entities, before or alongside the substance. Score how much such self-description is present. Do NOT judge whether the organisation is important, credible, or whether representing many people should matter.

[X06] Private interest translated into the agency's public criteria
Score whether the comment connects what the commenter wants to the considerations the agency is actually charged with weighing. 10: a self-interested ask is expressed in terms of the statutory factors, the rule's stated purpose, or effects on parties beyond the commenter -- safety, cost to consumers, supply reliability, program integrity, the interests of the wider regulated class -- and the connection is argued rather than asserted. 6: the public dimension is named but the argument stays about the commenter. 2: the ask is purely about the commenter's own convenience, paperwork, or schedule. 0: the comment asks for an accommodation and offers no reason the agency should care beyond the commenter's preference. Being self-interested is not penalized; failing to translate it is.

[X07] Developed multi-step reasoning chain vs bare assertion
Score 10 if the comment builds an explicit chain of reasoning: a claim followed by supporting premises, evidence, or sub-arguments that connect logically to that claim across multiple sentences or paragraphs. Score 0 if the comment merely asserts a position, preference, or conclusion with no supporting reasoning chain at all. Score in between based on how many linked reasoning steps are present.

[X08] Standing converted into otherwise-unavailable operational facts
Score how far the commenter's stated position in the regulated world is actually cashed out as facts an outsider could not obtain. 10: the comment names a concrete role with respect to the regulated activity (operator, obligated party, service provider to the affected population, sovereign, practitioner) AND then reports position-specific particulars that follow from that role -- counts of facilities, internal procedures, caseload experience, what happens to clients, market share, which equipment is in which fleet, what the commenter's staff must do. 5: a clear and specific stake is identified but everything offered afterward is publicly available background or general advocacy. 0: the writer's relationship to the rule is unstated, or is present only as mission/credential language with no role-specific facts anywhere in the text. Judge the conversion, not the prestige of the affiliation.

[X09] Adds material to the record the agency did not have
Score the extent to which the comment puts new information into the docket. 10: the comment identifies specific evidence, studies, documents, datasets, or historical agency statements that it represents as absent from or unaddressed by the agency's record, and says what they show. 6-7: it supplies real evidence, but evidence the agency plainly already has. 3: it gestures at evidence existing without identifying it. 0: it contains no evidence beyond the commenter's opinion, or its citations point back at the agency's own proposal and guidance documents. Reciting the agency's sources back to the agency scores at the low end no matter how formally the citations are formatted.

[X10] Use of the Oxford (serial) comma
Score 0-10 for how consistently the text uses a comma before the final conjunction in a list. 10 = used in every list; 0 = never used or no lists present. Judge punctuation habit only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X11] Individually composed rather than templated or duplicated
Score whether the text was written for this proposal by this writer. 10: the reasoning is specific to the writer's situation and could not be dropped into another docket -- particular incidents, particular role conflicts, particular operations. 6: individually written but generic in content. 3: recognizable campaign or form language, stock framings, or a signature block indicating a mass-submission channel. 0: the comment duplicates its own text (a 'background' section reproducing the opening verbatim), or consists of standard talking points with only a name attached. Do not reward or punish length or formatting; look for whether the sentences were built for this rule.

[X12] Orthographic noncompliance and unedited-typing residue
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of departures from standard written-English mechanics: misspelled or transposed words ('theri', 'probalby', 'producgts', 'Wom'), missing apostrophes ('cant', 'Its been', 'isnt'), missing or doubled spaces around punctuation, sentences beginning in lower case, run-on sentences joined without punctuation, inconsistent capitalisation of ordinary words (e.g. shouting a common noun mid-sentence), and stray unclosed parentheses or quotation marks. 0 = no such departures. 5 = several per paragraph but the text is still easy to follow. 10 = departures in nearly every sentence. Count occurrences and score the density only. Do NOT judge whether the writer is careless, uneducated, sincere, or whether the mechanics make the comment better or worse.

[X13] Reasoning given for why the approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the item supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X14] Causal Mechanism
Score 10 when the comment clearly traces how a proposed provision would change conduct or conditions and thereby produce specific effects for people, organizations, markets, or ecosystems; score 0 when it merely asserts that the proposal is good or bad.

[X15] Count of numerals and digit characters in the text
Score 0-10 for how many numerals and digit characters the text contains relative to its length. 10 = digits everywhere; 0 = no digits at all. Count characters only; do not judge whether the numbers are meaningful or whether their presence is good.

[X16] Explicit statement of the limitation or failure regime
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the item names a condition under which its own claim would not hold, or a case it does not cover. 10 = a specific, checkable limitation is stated; 0 = no limitation or scope condition is acknowledged anywhere.

[X17] Technical identifier density
Rate 0-10 for how much specialized model numbers, part numbers, docket codes, scientific names, acronyms, units, or alphanumeric identifiers appear; do not judge whether this feature is good, bad, persuasive, or substantively valuable.

[X18] Procedural posture about deadlines, extensions and prior filings
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the text is occupied with the mechanics of the comment process rather than with the content of the rule. Count: requests to extend or reopen the comment period; statements about how long the comment period was or when it closes; complaints that the available time was insufficient; notices that the submitter previously commented, is resubmitting, or fears earlier comments were lost when a docket was renumbered; references to a related or parallel rulemaking whose schedule interacts with this one; and requests that the agency bifurcate, sequence or expedite its rulemakings. 0 = no process content. 5 = a paragraph of process content alongside substantive discussion. 10 = the item is essentially a procedural request with little or no discussion of the rule's merits. Score how much process-mechanics content is present. Do NOT judge whether the procedural request is reasonable or well founded.

[X19] Adoption-ready remedy joined to its justification
Score the pair, not either half. 10: the comment states a change the agency could adopt substantially as written -- specific regulatory text to amend, a number, a threshold, a definition, a severance of the rulemaking -- AND the reason that change follows from the commenter's analysis. 7: a precise remedy with only asserted justification, or a well-justified position whose remedy is left for the agency to construct. 3: a directional preference ('do less', 'act quickly') with neither text nor reasons. 0: no requested action, or only questions posed to the agency.

[X20] Document bulk and verbosity
Rate 0-10 for the visible amount of prose, pages, sections, tables, appendices, references, or repeated elaboration; do not judge whether this feature is good, bad, persuasive, or substantively valuable.

[X21] Deadline and urgency language
Score 0-10 for the extent of explicit references to comment deadlines, short comment periods, expedited action, prompt consideration, or timing pressure; do not judge whether the urgency is justified.

[X22] Adaptive Ongoing Support
Score 10 when the comment identifies changing conditions and specifies recurring training, monitoring, guidance, or revision needed over time; score 0 when it treats a complex issue as requiring only a one-time action.

[X23] OCR and transcription artifacts
Rate 0-10 for how much visible character substitution, broken spacing, scanning noise, malformed symbols, missing letters, or transcription error appears; do not judge whether this feature is good, bad, persuasive, or substantively valuable.

[X24] Evidence Traceability
Score 10 when important factual claims are tied to identifiable studies, datasets, statutes, cases, technical documents, or named sources; score 0 when claims are unsupported or impossible to trace from the text.

[X25] Submission-artifact markers
Score 0-10 for the extent of visible forwarding headers, email metadata, file paths, attachment notices, OCR errors, scan artifacts, or conversion defects; do not judge whether the artifacts help or hurt readability.

[X26] Density of formal legal/regulatory citations
On a scale of 0 (no formal citations) to 10 (frequent precise citations to case law, U.S.C./C.F.R. sections, or Federal Register pin-cites, e.g. '87 Fed. Reg. 16442' or 'Weyerhaeuser v. U.S. Fish and Wildlife Serv., 139 S. Ct. 361, 368'), rate how much formal legal/regulatory citation apparatus is present. Score only the extent of citation apparatus present, not whether the cited authorities are used correctly or persuasively.

[X27] Grounded Affected-Party Knowledge
Score 10 when the comment uses specific firsthand, professional, or organizational experience to illuminate how the proposal operates for affected people or entities; score 0 when it relies only on an affiliation label or generalized identity claim.

[X28] Formal address to decision-maker as submission
Score 10 if the text is explicitly framed and formatted as a formal submission addressed to the named agency or official, with a docket/RIN citation, salutation, and a closing that identifies the submitter and their role. Score 0 if the text reads as an informal, conversational note or internal exchange (e.g., first-name address, casual sign-off) not framed as an address to the decision-maker.

[X29] Contested consequential question versus ministerial housekeeping
Score what kind of question the comment engages. 10: it takes up something genuinely at issue in the rulemaking -- the agency's authority, the scope of a right or obligation, who bears a cost, whether the evidentiary basis supports the action -- where reasonable parties disagree and the agency must choose. 5: a real but bounded implementation question with material consequences. 2: an internally uncontested correction: a typographical error, a figure reference, an applicability list, a compliance-date alignment, a schedule extension. 0: a procedural request needing no substantive content at all. High precision on a small question is not the same as engaging a large one; score the question, not the precision.

{"verdicts": [
  {"id": "<the id shown>", "label": "quality_relevant" or "incidental",
    "mixed": true or false, "confidence": "high"/"medium"/"low",
    "justification": "<one sentence>"},
  ... one entry per criterion, same ids ...
