You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the caption is as a contest entry. It is about the substance, the
      reasoning, the evidence, the craft, or the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the caption is as a contest entry: length or verbosity, formatting or layout,
      boilerplate or template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter
      or topic markers, temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of
      some upstream circumstance of the item's production (who made it, with what
      resources, when, with what help).

The corpus is: reader-submitted captions for a weekly cartoon caption contest. Each item is a caption.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
well-resourced producer may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually do
better work). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces
real how good the caption is as a contest entry too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you
label "quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Conversational scaffolding
Score 0-10 for the extent of formulaic dialogue openings or frames such as questions, refusals, guesses, imperatives, or routine replies; judge only presence, never whether the framing is good.

[X02] Scene-anchored concrete object nouns
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the caption names concrete, physical, picturable objects, settings, or props rather than speaking only in abstractions or pronouns. 0 = no concrete noun at all; the caption is pronouns, generic nouns ('anything', 'it', 'this'), and verbs; 5 = one concrete picturable noun; 8 = two or more concrete picturable nouns; 10 = three or more, or one unusual and highly specific object named as the caption's hinge. Count only the presence and specificity of physical nouns. Do not judge whether the named object fits any drawing, whether the anchoring is skillful, or whether the caption is funny or good.

[X03] Role/status inversion
Score 10 when the caption unexpectedly reverses who has authority, agency, or control; score 0 when relationships remain entirely conventional.

[X04] Payoff-bearing specificity
Score 10 when numbers, objects, names, or technical details materially sharpen the comic premise; score 0 when specificity is decorative, interchangeable, or cluttering.

[X05] Revealing Character Voice
Score 10 when wording distinctly reveals the speaker’s status, attitude, identity, or relationship; score 0 when any anonymous speaker could say it unchanged.

[X06] Caption length
Score 0-10 for the extent of word count, clause count, and elaboration relative to a short one-line caption; judge only textual length, never whether being longer is good.

[X07] Late-Line Pivot
Score 10 when the key reinterpretation or comic reveal arrives at the final phrase after a credible setup; score 0 when the line is flat throughout or reveals its premise immediately.

[X08] Stacked / compound double meanings
Score whether the caption contains two or more distinct double meanings or puns operating simultaneously, as opposed to a single pun. A 10 has at least two independent, load-bearing wordplay mechanisms both present. A 0 has no wordplay, or exactly one single pun/double meaning.

[X09] Reasoning given for why the approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the item supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X10] Vocative first names and named-addressee direct address
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the caption addresses a specific interlocutor by name or by an explicit relational vocative. 0 = no address term at all; 4 = a generic plural or role vocative ('guys', 'folks', 'ladies and gentlemen', 'sir'); 7 = a personal given name or a kinship vocative ('son', 'honey', 'dad') set off in the utterance; 10 = a named addressee plus a second address term, or a name placed as the caption's rhythmic hinge. Score the presence and prominence of the address term only. Do not judge whether the name is well chosen, whether it adds character or texture, and do not judge the caption's quality.

[X11] Borrowed phrase markers
Score 0-10 for the extent of recognizable titles, slogans, idioms, stock lines, or adapted catchphrases; judge only resemblance or allusion, never whether the borrowing is good.

[X12] Verbal Compression
Score 10 when every phrase efficiently advances the premise or punchline and nothing feels expendable; score 0 when the wording is padded, repetitive, or incomplete.

[X13] Ellipsis/dash pause-timing punctuation
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the caption uses an ellipsis ("...") or an em/en-dash ("--" or "-") as a mid-sentence pause or delivery-timing marker, as opposed to using only standard sentence punctuation (periods, commas, question marks). 0 = no ellipsis or dash pause marker anywhere in the text; 10 = the caption's structure is built around one or more ellipsis/dash pauses. Do not judge whether the pause improves the caption's timing -- score only how much this punctuation device is present.

[X14] Natural Spoken Cadence
Score 10 when the line sounds like spontaneous, believable speech with natural rhythm; score 0 when it reads like a slogan, label, or assembled joke fragment.

[X15] News-cycle / dated-event topicality anchor
On a 0-10 scale, rate how strongly the caption text ties itself to a specific, datable real-world news event or policy moment (for example a pandemic-era reference to a stimulus payment or mask shortage, or an election-cycle reference) as opposed to describing a timeless, undated scenario. 0 = nothing in the text dates it to a specific news moment; 10 = the caption is unmistakably anchored to one identifiable real-world news event. Do not judge whether the topical reference is clever or well-executed -- score only how strongly the text is anchored to a specific datable event.

[X16] Specialist abbreviation / acronym shorthand
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the caption uses a technical, medical, or otherwise domain-specific abbreviation or acronym (for example a string of periods-separated initials, or specialist shorthand) as opposed to using only everyday plain-language words. 0 = no specialist abbreviation or acronym appears; 10 = the caption's key content depends on a specialist abbreviation or acronym. Do not judge whether the abbreviation is used cleverly -- score only how much specialist shorthand is present.

[X17] Count of numerals and digit characters in the text
Score 0-10 for how many numerals and digit characters the text contains relative to its length. 10 = digits everywhere; 0 = no digits at all. Count characters only; do not judge whether the numbers are meaningful or whether their presence is good.

[X18] Topical reference integration
Score 10 when a public figure, event, or issue is essential to the joke’s mechanism and is transformed by the wording; score 0 when the reference is detachable name-dropping or a generic opinion.

[X19] Implied conversational premise
Score 10 when the line sounds like a natural reply that lets the reader reconstruct an unstated exchange; score 0 when it lacks a believable conversational situation.

[X20] Freshness beyond stock jokes
Score 10 when the caption combines familiar material in an unusually specific angle or formulation; score 0 when it reproduces a predictable pun, cliché, or standard one-liner template.

[X21] Concrete absurd escalation
Score 10 when an ordinary premise escalates through a specific, disproportionate concrete detail; score 0 when details are random, merely descriptive, or non-escalatory.

[X22] Visualizable physical staging
Score 10 when the wording implies a clear, comic arrangement of bodies, objects, or movement; score 0 when no concrete staging can be inferred from the text.

[X23] Explicit statement of the limitation or failure regime
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the item names a condition under which its own claim would not hold, or a case it does not cover. 10 = a specific, checkable limitation is stated; 0 = no limitation or scope condition is acknowledged anywhere.

[X24] Deadpan Underreaction
Score 10 when bizarre or high-stakes circumstances are treated with calm, matter-of-fact language; score 0 when the tone merely matches the event or announces the joke.

[X25] Editorial elaboration
On a 0-10 scale, how much does the caption show visibly elaborated syntax, multiple linked clauses, explicit temporal or conditional relations, and carefully maintained referents? Judge only textual elaboration, not writing quality.

[X26] Specific numeric quantity citation
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the caption includes one or more explicit numbers or counted quantities (a dollar figure, a count of items, a time span given in numerals or number-words) as opposed to using no numbers at all. 0 = no numbers or quantities appear anywhere; 10 = the caption's content is built around one or more explicit numeric quantities. Do not judge whether the specific number chosen is funny or apt -- score only how much explicit numeric quantity is present.

[X27] Scene-Conscious Meta-Awareness
Score 10 when the caption exploits conventions of conversation, performance, storytelling, or cartoon presentation itself; score 0 when it treats the situation without self-awareness.

[X28] Use of the Oxford (serial) comma
Score 0-10 for how consistently the text uses a comma before the final conjunction in a list. 10 = used in every list; 0 = never used or no lists present. Judge punctuation habit only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X29] Semantic Hinge
Score 10 when a word or phrase supports two coherent readings and the line pivots between them; score 0 when wording has only one literal meaning or is merely ambiguous.
