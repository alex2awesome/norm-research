You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about the substantive quality of the comment. It is about the substance, the
      reasoning, the evidence, the craft, or the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself the substantive quality of the comment: length or verbosity, formatting or layout,
      boilerplate or template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter
      or topic markers, temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of
      some upstream circumstance of the item's production (who made it, with what
      resources, when, with what help).

The corpus is: public comments submitted to United States federal agency rulemaking dockets. Each item is a public comment.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
well-resourced producer may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually do
better work). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces
real the substantive quality of the comment too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you
label "quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Substantive signal density
Score 10 when most of the text contributes new, proposal-specific analysis with little repetition or boilerplate; score 0 when the text is dominated by pleasantries, credentials, generic endorsement, repetition, or irrelevant material.

[X02] Procedural-only request (extension, scheduling, logistics)
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the comment's content is a purely procedural request -- e.g. a request to extend the comment period, a scheduling or logistics question, or a request for clarification about process -- rather than substantive engagement with the rule's provisions. 0 = comment is entirely substantive engagement with the rule's merits, 10 = comment is entirely a procedural request with no substantive engagement. Do NOT judge whether the procedural request is reasonable or well-justified -- score only the extent to which the content is procedural rather than substantive.

[X03] Submission apparatus
On a 0-10 scale, how much formal submission apparatus is visible, including letterhead, postal addresses, docket metadata, named officials, contact details, transmission methods, page headers, and signatures? Score only extent of apparatus, not professionalism or quality.

[X04] Uncertainty calibration
Score 10 when the comment distinguishes established facts, uncertainty, incomplete information, assumptions, and speculation; score 0 when it presents uncertain or unsupported claims as settled facts.

[X05] Use of the Oxford (serial) comma
Score 0-10 for how consistently the text uses a comma before the final conjunction in a list. 10 = used in every list; 0 = never used or no lists present. Judge punctuation habit only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X06] Acknowledges both merits and drawbacks
Score 0-10 on whether the comment explicitly identifies both positive and negative aspects of the proposal (or explicitly frames the regulator's own competing goals) rather than arguing one-sidedly. A 10: comment clearly separates what it supports from what it opposes and explains why for each. A 0: comment is purely one-sided (all praise or all condemnation) with no acknowledgment of any countervailing consideration.

[X07] Explicit statement of the limitation or failure regime
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the item names a condition under which its own claim would not hold, or a case it does not cover. 10 = a specific, checkable limitation is stated; 0 = no limitation or scope condition is acknowledged anywhere.

[X08] Coordinated template language
On a 0-10 scale, how much does the text show stock, coalition, endorsement, or template language, including references to other comments, shared positions, or attached submissions? Score only textual recurrence or coordination signals, not whether the position is good.

[X09] Personal-experience framing
On a 0-10 scale, how much does the text present the writer's personal circumstances, family, occupation, identity, location, or direct lived experience? Score only the extent of self-reference, not whether the experience strengthens the comment.

[X10] Sender authority cues
How much does the text display senior titles, professional credentials, official roles, or named authoritative representatives? Score only their visible extent, not whether the authority makes the comment good.

[X11] Relevant experiential expertise
Score 10 when the commenter’s role or direct experience is explicitly connected to a concrete, docket-specific observation; score 0 when credentials or affiliation appear only as generic self-description.

[X12] Substance-to-boilerplate ratio
Score 0-10 on what fraction of the visible text is actual argument/analysis versus non-substantive administrative content (letterhead, attorney/board rosters, address blocks, signature blocks, 'see attached file' notices). A 10: virtually all text is substantive argument. A 0: the great majority of visible text is letterhead, personnel lists, or contact boilerplate with little or no argument.

[X13] Sheer volume of retained body text
Reading only the comment text, score 0-10 for HOW MUCH body text there is, as a raw quantity. Judge extent by approximate word count and by structural signs of length: numbered or lettered section headings, a table of contents, roman-numeral parts, bulleted lists of asks, and page-continuation markers. Anchors: 0 = under roughly 60 words (one short paragraph); 3 = a few paragraphs; 5 = roughly one page; 8 = several pages with internal sectioning; 10 = a long multi-part document with a contents listing or many numbered sections. Do NOT reward or penalise length, do NOT judge whether the length is warranted by the subject, and do NOT consider how well the space is used. Report volume only.

[X14] Internally consistent, labeled quantitative detail
Score 0-10 on whether numeric figures in the comment (dollar amounts, dates, volumes, percentages, thresholds) are clearly labeled, tied to a specific claim, and internally consistent with each other, versus numbers appearing without clear labels or context. A 10: every number is clearly labeled, its meaning is unambiguous, and figures are internally consistent. A 0: numbers appear disconnected from any clear label or claim, making it impossible to tell what they refer to or whether they are consistent.

[X15] Evidence traceability
Score 10 when claims are tied to identifiable studies, statutes, datasets, reports, or other sources and the sources are meaningfully interpreted; score 0 when citations are absent, vague, or merely decorative.

[X16] States core position in opening sentences
Score 0-10 on whether the comment's central request or position (support/oppose/specific ask) is clearly stated within the first two sentences, versus deferred behind extended preamble or organizational background. A 10: the specific ask or position is unambiguous from the first sentence or two. A 0: the reader must read through substantial preamble before the core position becomes clear.

[X17] Claims calibrated to evidence, no leaps
Score 0-10 on whether the strength of the comment's claims matches the strength of its evidence, avoiding unsupported causal leaps, conspiratorial framing, or sweeping conclusions drawn from a single tangential fact. A 10: every claim is proportionate to and follows logically from the evidence offered. A 0: comment draws sweeping or conspiratorial conclusions (e.g., alleging a coordinated cover-up, or linking an isolated local fact to a global causal claim) without supporting logic.

[X18] Text volume
On a 0-10 scale, how much text volume is present, measured by visible length, pages, paragraphs, subpoints, or extended elaboration? Score only quantity, not whether the additional text is useful or well reasoned.

[X19] Footnote and formal legal-citation density
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the text uses formal legal-citation apparatus -- numbered footnotes, and/or pin-cites to statutes, regulations, or the Federal Register (e.g. 'U.S.C.', 'C.F.R.', 'Fed. Reg.'). 0 = no such citations or footnotes, 10 = dense, extensive footnoting/citation throughout. Do NOT judge whether the citations are accurate or persuasive -- score only the extent of the citation apparatus present.

[X20] OCR and typographical noise
On a 0-10 scale, how much visible OCR corruption, misspelling, character substitution, spacing error, broken formatting, or typographical noise appears? Score only the amount of noise, not the writer's competence or the comment's quality.

[X21] First-hand operational evidence for claims
Score 0-10 on whether the comment's claims are grounded in the filer's own direct first-hand operational, technical, or field experience (their own fleet, lab, cases, data) rather than generic policy assertions. A 10: comment clearly draws on specific first-hand experience the filer possesses and describes it concretely. A 0: comment consists entirely of generic assertions with no indication of direct first-hand experience.

[X22] Alternative comparison
Score 10 when the comment compares the proposal with one or more concrete alternatives using explicit advantages, disadvantages, or applicability conditions; score 0 when it supplies no alternative or merely demands rejection.

[X23] Concrete organizational standing disclosed
Score 0-10 on whether the commenter establishes concrete, verifiable standing relevant to the rule -- membership size, statutory mandate, years of operation, or domain expertise -- beyond a bare assertion of interest. A 10: comment states specific, concrete standing facts directly tied to the rule's subject. A 0: the commenter's relationship to and authority on the subject matter is entirely unstated or purely generic ('as a concerned citizen').

[X24] Causal impact pathway
Score 10 when the comment clearly traces how a proposed provision produces specific operational, economic, safety, health, or environmental effects; score 0 when it asserts harm or benefit without explaining the mechanism.

[X25] Typographic emphasis intensity (all-caps, exclamation marks)
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the text uses typographic emphasis markers -- words in ALL CAPS, exclamation points, or repeated emphatic punctuation -- to convey urgency or emotion. 0 = no such emphasis markers, 10 = frequent and heavy use throughout. Do NOT judge whether the emotional tone is appropriate or persuasive -- score only the extent of the typographic emphasis markers.

[X26] Reasoning given for why the approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the item supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X27] Pinpoint statutory/regulatory citation
Score 0-10 on whether the comment cites specific statutory sections, CFR provisions, or exact regulatory text (by number/subsection) that are relevant and appear accurately applied to the argument. A 10: multiple precise, correctly-used pinpoint citations integrated into the argument. A 0: no statutory/regulatory citations, or only vague references to 'the law' or 'the Act' without section numbers.

[X28] Share of the text that is new information rather than restatement
Estimate what fraction of the comment's substance is content the agency did not already possess. 10 = nearly all of it: argument, data, experience, or drafting the agency could not have written for itself. 5 = roughly half restatement of the proposal, half addition. 0 = a faithful summary of the proposal, or of another commenter's position, followed by agreement or disagreement, adding nothing. Length must not earn credit; a long, accurate, well-organized paraphrase belongs at the bottom of this scale.

[X29] Count of numerals and digit characters in the text
Score 0-10 for how many numerals and digit characters the text contains relative to its length. 10 = digits everywhere; 0 = no digits at all. Count characters only; do not judge whether the numbers are meaningful or whether their presence is good.
