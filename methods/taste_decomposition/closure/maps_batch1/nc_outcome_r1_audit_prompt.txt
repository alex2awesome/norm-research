You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about the substantive quality of the comment. It is about the substance, the
      reasoning, the evidence, the craft, or the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself the substantive quality of the comment: length or verbosity, formatting or layout,
      boilerplate or template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter
      or topic markers, temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of
      some upstream circumstance of the item's production (who made it, with what
      resources, when, with what help).

The corpus is: public comments submitted to United States federal agency rulemaking dockets. Each item is a public comment.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
well-resourced producer may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually do
better work). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces
real the substantive quality of the comment too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you
label "quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Explicit statement of the limitation or failure regime
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the item names a condition under which its own claim would not hold, or a case it does not cover. 10 = a specific, checkable limitation is stated; 0 = no limitation or scope condition is acknowledged anywhere.

[X02] Quantification of real-world stakes or impact
Score the degree to which the comment supplies specific, checkable figures (dollar amounts, populations affected, physical quantities, percentages, casualty/injury counts, historical baselines) that let the reader gauge the magnitude of the rule's consequences. A 10 gives multiple specific, well-attributed figures tied directly to the argument. A 0 offers only vague qualitative claims ('a lot,' 'many,' 'significant harm') with no quantification at all.

[X03] Disclosed firsthand expertise grounding the claims
Score whether the commenter explicitly establishes personal, verifiable firsthand expertise or direct experience (credentials, years of hands-on practice, direct observation) that specifically grounds the technical claims made, as opposed to speaking from unstated or purely institutional authority. A 10 clearly discloses relevant credentials or experience that directly support the specific claims made. A 0 offers no such grounding -- claims stand on their own or on unstated authority.

[X04] Comment-period timing/procedural request share
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much of this comment is a procedural request about timing (e.g., asking to extend the comment period) or an explicit statement about time pressure (e.g., referencing a holiday season or insufficient time to prepare), as opposed to comment on the substance of the proposed rule. 0 = entirely substantive comment on the rule, no timing/procedural content; 10 = the comment is entirely a timing/procedural request. Do not judge whether the timing request is reasonable or justified -- score only how much of the text is devoted to it.

[X05] Alternative comparison
Score 10 when the comment compares the requested approach with feasible alternatives using explicit criteria such as effectiveness, burden, risk, or consistency; score 0 when no alternative is considered.

[X06] Original firsthand text vs. formulaic template language
Score whether the comment's language is specific to this commenter's own situation and this particular docket, versus reading like a generic template that could be dropped into many different comment periods with minor edits. A 10 is clearly composed for this specific submission with situation-specific detail. A 0 reads as boilerplate (e.g., a generic extension-request form) with the docket number as the only customized element.

[X07] Scanned-attachment ingestion signature: stub, OCR garble, page furniture
Score 0-10 for how much of the item bears the marks of having been filed as a scanned or PDF attachment and then machine-transcribed, rather than typed directly into the comment box. Count, as extent: (a) a short transmittal stub at the top such as 'See attached', 'See attached file(s)', 'Please find attached', optionally preceded by a bare digit; (b) character-level transcription corruption -- dropped or doubled letters, letters substituted for each other, mangled signatures, ligature errors; (c) page furniture carried through from the source document -- 'Page 2 of 8', repeated date/addressee headers, fax and phone footers, stray line breaks mid-sentence; (d) duplication, where the same paragraph appears once in the cover note and again in the attachment body. Anchors: 0 = clean typed prose with none of these; 5 = clear stub plus page headers but readable text; 10 = pervasive corruption and page furniture that makes stretches hard to read. Do NOT judge whether the corruption makes the comment worse, and do NOT penalise or reward the author for it -- you are scoring how much of this signature is visible, nothing else.

[X08] Content exceeds its packaging rather than the reverse
Score the balance between presentational apparatus and delivered content. 10 = plain presentation carrying dense, non-redundant content. 5 = form and content matched. 0 = elaborate apparatus - numbered comment-response tables, formal headings, duplicated cover-and-attachment text, checkbox forms - wrapped around thin, purely editorial, or repeated content. Judge the apparatus against what it actually delivers, not against a general standard of tidiness.

[X09] Technical precision achieved concisely (technical economy)
Score the comment for combining (a) concrete, verifiable technical detail -- part numbers, dates, thresholds, section numbers -- with (b) brevity: no unnecessary padding, throat-clearing, or repetition. A 10 conveys precise technical content in the fewest words needed. A 0 either lacks technical precision, or buries whatever precision exists under substantial unrelated or repetitive padding.

[X10] Legal/regulatory citation apparatus density
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the text displays formal legal/regulatory citation apparatus -- numbered footnotes, pincites to the Federal Register/CFR/U.S.C., precise docket/RIN number recitation, or law-firm-style formal phrasing ('the undersigned', 'respectfully submits'). 0 = no such apparatus; 10 = dense, brief-style citation apparatus throughout. Do not judge whether the legal argument itself is correct -- score only the extent of this citation/formality apparatus.

[X11] Reasoning given for why the approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the item supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X12] Technical substantiation vs. ideological/emotional characterization
Score whether the comment's persuasive force rests on specific technical, factual, or legal substantiation, as opposed to resting primarily on ideological or emotionally loaded characterization (e.g., labeling a prior rule an 'attack,' 'illegal,' or a 'War on Coal') without corresponding technical detail to back the characterization. A 10 backs every strong characterization with specific technical or factual support. A 0 relies on charged rhetoric with little or no technical substantiation behind it.

[X13] Organizational scale self-description
On a 0-10 scale, rate how explicitly the text states the submitting organization's scale or resources -- e.g., a specific membership count, revenue figure, market-share percentage, or employee/customer count. 0 = no such scale statement anywhere in the text; 10 = the text prominently and repeatedly quantifies the organization's size. Do not judge whether the organization's size makes its argument more or less correct -- score only whether such a scale statement is present in the text.

[X14] Count of numerals and digit characters in the text
Score 0-10 for how many numerals and digit characters the text contains relative to its length. 10 = digits everywhere; 0 = no digits at all. Count characters only; do not judge whether the numbers are meaningful or whether their presence is good.

[X15] Uncertainty calibration
Score 10 when the comment distinguishes established facts, inferences, unknowns, and needed evidence and proposes how uncertainty could be resolved; score 0 when speculation is presented as certainty.

[X16] Historical or precedential grounding of the request
Score whether the comment situates its position within relevant regulatory history, prior agency actions, or precedent (e.g., citing how the agency handled comparable past dockets, or tracing the issue's history over time), as opposed to treating the current proposal in isolation with no historical context. A 10 explicitly and specifically grounds the argument in comparable precedent or history. A 0 offers no historical or precedential context at all.

[X17] Short decisive comment from a directly exposed party
Judge whether the comment's length is proportionate to what it has to say, given who is saying it. 10 = a short comment that states a definite position on a matter the commenter is unambiguously exposed to, and then stops - no padding, no restatement of the notice. 5 = length roughly matched to content. 0 = a long comment whose length comes from self-description, restatement of the agency's own notice, or hedged generalities, so that very few distinct claims are made per page. Length by itself is neither good nor bad; the ratio is what is scored.

[X18] OCR and transcription artifacts
On a 0-10 scale, how much scanning, OCR, transcription, encoding, character-substitution, spacing, or visible document-conversion error is present? Score only extent; do not judge whether the artifacts are good, bad, or indicative of substantive quality.

[X19] Distributional Impact Analysis
Score 10 when the comment examines how effects differ across multiple affected groups, especially vulnerable, geographically distinct, or less represented groups; score 0 when impacts are described only for the commenter.

[X20] Argument-carried weight vs. appeal to institutional size
Score whether the comment's persuasive force rests on the substance of its reasoning and evidence, as opposed to resting on an implicit appeal to the submitting organization's size, membership count, or market share (e.g., citing '1,500 member companies' or '225,000 employees' as if that were itself the argument). A 10 relies entirely on reasoned, evidence-based argument. A 0 substitutes recitation of organizational scale or authority for actual argument.

[X21] Technical identifier density
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the text uses specialized acronyms, model numbers, part numbers, docket identifiers, standards codes, or regulatory alphanumeric strings; do not judge whether the technical detail is correct.

[X22] Builds a record: exhibits, indexes, citations, companion filings
Score the evidentiary apparatus the comment brings with it. 10 = footnoted sources, an index of attachments, named studies with authors, prior submissions in this or a related docket, or explicit cross-references to companion comments. 5 = one or two named sources. 0 = no sources, attachments or cross-references of any kind.

[X23] Attachment-index / cover-page share of text
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much of the visible text functions as an index, cover page, or file-list pointing to separately attached material (e.g., filenames, 'Batch X of Y', 'See attached file(s)' immediately followed by a list of exhibit titles) rather than being the substantive comment itself. 0 = the visible text is entirely the substantive comment; 10 = the visible text is entirely an index/cover page for unseen attachments. Do not judge the value of the attachments themselves -- score only how much of the visible text is index/cover material.

[X24] Professional production markers
On a 0-10 scale, how much formal letterhead, legal correspondence structure, contact information, document conventions, or polished signatory presentation is present? Score only extent; do not judge whether the production quality is good or whether the arguments are sound.

[X25] Document volume
On a 0-10 scale, how much extended text, page-level elaboration, or multi-section material is present? Score only extent from the item text; do not judge whether the amount is good, persuasive, accurate, or relevant.

[X26] Applicability Boundaries
Score 10 when the comment precisely identifies which products, units, populations, or circumstances are affected and which are not, with a reason for the distinction; score 0 when it treats materially different cases as interchangeable.

[X27] Use of the Oxford (serial) comma
Score 0-10 for how consistently the text uses a comma before the final conjunction in a list. 10 = used in every list; 0 = never used or no lists present. Judge punctuation habit only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X28] Mechanism-to-effect reasoning
Score 10 when the comment explains a plausible chain from the proposed provision through an operational mechanism to a specific consequence; score 0 when it merely asserts that the proposal is good or harmful.

[X29] Professional legal drafting
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the text uses lawyerly drafting conventions, formal legal framing, structured sections, defined terms, or counsel attribution; do not judge whether the drafting is good.
