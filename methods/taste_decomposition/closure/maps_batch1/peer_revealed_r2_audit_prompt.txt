You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about research quality. It is about the substance, the
      reasoning, the evidence, the craft, or the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself research quality: length or verbosity, formatting or layout,
      boilerplate or template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter
      or topic markers, temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of
      some upstream circumstance of the item's production (who made it, with what
      resources, when, with what help).

The corpus is: abstracts of machine-learning conference paper submissions. Each item is a abstract.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
well-resourced producer may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually do
better work). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces
real research quality too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you
label "quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Reasoning given for why the approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the item supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X02] Multiplicity Modeling
Give 10 when the method represents multiple valid solutions, futures, or explanations without collapsing them into one target; give 0 when an inherently ambiguous problem is forced into a single answer.

[X03] Structure-Aligned Inductive Bias
Score 10 when the method encodes a domain structure or invariance that is explicitly connected to the task and empirically examined; score 0 when architectural structure is arbitrary or purely cosmetic.

[X04] Canonical Ecosystem Naming
How much does the abstract name canonical datasets, benchmarks, architectures, standard methods, or well-known prior authors? Score only visible ecosystem references, not the importance of the work.

[X05] Headline claim survives the abstract's own qualifiers
Read the strongest sentence of the abstract, then read the reported details, and score whether the strong sentence still stands. 10 = the lead claim is exactly what the detailed results show; no later sentence narrows it. 5 = the lead claim is broad but the narrowing is stated up front. 0 = a broad superiority claim is made first and later details reduce it to a subset (one dataset out of several, some tasks, comparable or better). Score consistency between claim and detail, not the level of performance.

[X06] Artifact-release signaling
Score 0-10 for the extent of explicit language promising or linking code, models, datasets, configurations, or other research artifacts; judge only the presence of release signaling, not whether the artifacts are useful or real.

[X07] Trend-Aligned Vocabulary
How much does the abstract use recognizable fashionable terminology associated with currently prominent machine-learning themes? Score only the presence of trend vocabulary, not whether the topic is important or good.

[X08] Superlative Claim Rhetoric
How much does the abstract use superlatives, priority claims, novelty claims, or emphatic comparative language such as first, novel, superior, remarkable, or state of the art? Score only rhetorical frequency, not truth or quality.

[X09] Theory is load-bearing: the derivation licenses the method
Score how tightly the stated theory determines the proposed procedure. 10 = a specific proved statement is what makes the algorithm possible or correct, and the abstract shows the path from the statement to the procedure (an identity that yields a tractable objective, a duality that makes a gradient computable, a proof that one objective equals another). 5 = theory that bounds or justifies a method chosen on other grounds. 0 = formal apparatus, background exposition, or a proof of a property nobody doubted, with the method unchanged if the theory were deleted.

[X10] Theory-to-Algorithm Closure
Give 10 when theoretical analysis directly determines an implementable construction, estimator, or guarantee; give 0 when theory is disconnected from the proposed procedure.

[X11] Economy of exposition (information per sentence)
Score how much distinct information each sentence of the abstract carries, independent of topic. A 10 is a short, tight abstract where every sentence adds new, non-redundant content. A 0 is a long abstract that restates the same point multiple ways or pads with generic filler sentences.

[X12] Sheer expository volume of the abstract body
Read only the abstract text and rate 0-10 how much total expository volume is present. Anchors: 0 = title only, or a single sentence of body text; 3 = roughly two to four sentences; 6 = one standard paragraph of about eight to twelve sentences; 10 = a long multi-part abstract of fifteen or more sentences covering several elaborated sub-topics. Count sentences, clauses, enumerated sub-points and number of distinct sub-topics touched. You are measuring QUANTITY OF TEXT ONLY. Do not judge whether the length is appropriate, whether the writing is clear or informative, or whether a short abstract is terse-and-confident versus thin. Longer is not better and shorter is not better; report extent only.

[X13] Explicit code/artifact release mention
Score whether the abstract explicitly states that code, models, or data will be or are publicly released. A 10 states a concrete release (e.g., a GitHub link or explicit 'code is available'). A 0 makes no mention of release or reproducibility artifacts.

[X14] Challenges a widely-held assumption or standard practice
Score whether the abstract explicitly overturns or complicates a piece of received wisdom or standard practice in the field, versus simply extending or applying accepted practice without challenging any assumption. A 10 states a finding that directly contradicts what practitioners would expect (e.g., a standard component turns out to be unnecessary, or a technique backfires). A 0 straightforwardly builds on accepted assumptions without surprising anyone.

[X15] Density of specific quantified result figures
Rate 0-10 the density of explicitly quantified outcome figures in the text. Count numerals attached to results: percentages, absolute metric values (accuracy, AP, mIoU, FID, MAE), speedup or reduction factors (for example five-fold, two to three orders of magnitude), parameter, FLOP or batch-size counts, wall-clock times, and dataset sizes. Anchors: 0 = no such numerals anywhere; 3 = one figure; 5 = two or three; 8 = four or five; 10 = six or more distinct quantified figures, several carried to one or two decimal places. Score PRESENCE AND DENSITY of numbers only. Do not assess whether the numbers are large, impressive, fairly compared, correctly computed, or whether the gains would matter to anyone.

[X16] Extent of adversarial/security-threat subject framing
Read the abstract text only. Rate 0-10 how much the abstract centers on adversarial/security-threat framing -- attacks, adversarial examples, backdoors, poisoning, defenses, robustness certificates against a malicious actor. Score 0 if the topic has nothing to do with adversarial/security framing, and 10 if the entire abstract is organized around an attack-or-defense narrative. Do NOT judge whether this is an important or trendy topic to work on -- score only how much of the text is organized around this specific subject-matter framing.

[X17] Explicit statement of the limitation or failure regime
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the item names a condition under which its own claim would not hold, or a case it does not cover. 10 = a specific, checkable limitation is stated; 0 = no limitation or scope condition is acknowledged anywhere.

[X18] Single-idea compressibility (elegance)
Score whether the paper's core contribution can be stated as one simple, memorable idea versus a bundle of several loosely-related components. A 10 is a paper whose whole contribution reduces to one clean sentence. A 0 requires listing several separate components/mechanisms (i, ii, iii) to describe what was actually done.

[X19] Density of formal mathematical/symbolic notation
Read the abstract text only. Rate 0-10 how much specialized mathematical/symbolic notation appears (inline formulas, Greek letters, subscripts/superscripts, big-O/tilde-O expressions, set/space notation like R^d). Score 0 if the text is plain prose with no symbolic notation, and 10 if the text is dense with symbolic expressions throughout. Do NOT judge whether the mathematics is correct, rigorous, or important -- score only how much symbolic notation is visibly present in the text.

[X20] Use of the Oxford (serial) comma
Score 0-10 for how consistently the text uses a comma before the final conjunction in a list. 10 = used in every list; 0 = never used or no lists present. Judge punctuation habit only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X21] Publication-Polish Fluency
How much does the abstract exhibit highly polished grammar, smooth transitions, standardized abstract structure, and publication-template fluency? Score only surface editing quality, not research quality.

[X22] Concrete numeric result density
Score how many specific, checkable numeric results (percentages, deltas, counts, timings) the abstract reports, independent of how positive or well-supported they are. A 10 packs multiple precise figures (e.g., '10.9% improvement', '5-fold reduction', '76 minutes'). A 0 reports only qualitative adjectives ('significant', 'considerable', 'remarkable') with no numbers at all.

[X23] Boundary and Failure Characterization
Score 10 when the abstract distinguishes regimes where the method helps, does not help, or changes behavior, with explicit assumptions; score 0 when it presents only universal-sounding success claims.

[X24] Resource Accounting
Give 10 when computational, memory, latency, or deployment constraints are quantified and handled by the core design; give 0 when efficiency is asserted only in general terms.

[X25] Explicit first-to-achieve priority claims
Read the abstract text only. Rate 0-10 how strongly the text makes an explicit claim to being first, novel-in-priority, or unprecedented (e.g., 'we are the first to...', 'for the first time', 'to the best of our knowledge, ... is the first'). Score 0 if no such priority claim appears, and 10 if the text makes an emphatic, explicit priority claim. Do NOT judge whether the priority claim is actually true or historically accurate -- score only whether this rhetorical claim is textually present.

[X26] Explanatory Insight Beyond Performance
Score 10 when the work yields a transferable conceptual explanation, diagnostic, or unifying interpretation in addition to a method and measurements; score 0 when it reports improvements without illuminating why they occur.

[X27] Rhetorical framing as adjudicating a field debate
Score whether the abstract explicitly frames itself as resolving an open question or tension in prior literature, using a direct question or an explicit 'prior work suggests X, but...' setup, versus simply stating 'we propose method M for task T' without any such framing device. A 10 opens with an explicit posed question or named tension the paper will resolve. A 0 states its contribution directly with no debate-framing device.

[X28] Positions against a named strongest prior with a stated inability
Score the specificity of the comparison target. 10 = the abstract names the actual leading prior method and states precisely what it cannot do or where it fails, in terms concrete enough that the failure could be checked. 5 = a named family of approaches with a general limitation. 0 = the prior work is referred to only as existing approaches, previous methods, or current techniques, and the limitation is that the paper's own idea has not been tried. Number of baselines counted in the evaluation does not count here; only the named target of the argument does.

[X29] Count of numerals and digit characters in the text
Score 0-10 for how many numerals and digit characters the text contains relative to its length. 10 = digits everywhere; 0 = no digits at all. Count characters only; do not judge whether the numbers are meaningful or whether their presence is good.

OUTPUT. Emit exactly one JSON object and nothing else:

{"verdicts": [
  {"id": "<the id shown>", "label": "quality_relevant" or "incidental",
    "mixed": true or false, "confidence": "high"/"medium"/"low",
    "justification": "<one sentence>"},
  ... one entry per criterion, same ids ...
]}
