You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the caption is as a contest entry. It is about the substance, the
      reasoning, the evidence, the craft, or the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the caption is as a contest entry: length or verbosity, formatting or layout,
      boilerplate or template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter
      or topic markers, temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of
      some upstream circumstance of the item's production (who made it, with what
      resources, when, with what help).

The corpus is: reader-submitted captions for a weekly cartoon caption contest. Each item is a caption.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
well-resourced producer may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually do
better work). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces
real how good the caption is as a contest entry too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you
label "quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Caption length / verbosity (word count, clause count)
Rate 0-10 how long and multi-clause this caption is, purely as a word-count/structure measure (0 = extremely terse, roughly <=6 words with a single clause; 10 = long, multi-clause with an explicit setup plus payoff). Do not judge whether the length helps or hurts the joke -- score only how much text/structure is present.

[X02] Kinship framing and first-name familiar address
Read only the caption text. Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the caption is framed around household or family relationships, or addresses its listener by a personal first name or familiar role term. Count kinship nouns ('mom', 'dad', 'daddy', 'son', 'husband', 'wife'), possessive family references, domestic-curfew or spousal-complaint framing, and first-name vocatives. 0 = no kinship term, no family framing, and no personal name of address. 5 = one kinship term or one first-name vocative. 10 = the caption's whole premise is a family relationship, or it combines a kinship term with a name of address. Judge extent of kinship and familiar-address framing only. Do NOT judge whether the family framing is warm, tired, relatable, or well observed, and do NOT judge whether the caption is funny.

[X03] Density of identifiable wordplay/puns
Score how much of the caption's humor is built on one or more clearly spottable puns or double meanings. 10 = the line's core joke is a clean, easily identified pun or double meaning. 0 = no wordplay of any kind is present.

[X04] Productive ambiguity
Score 10 when the line leaves an intriguing gap that invites a specific comic inference while remaining understandable; score 0 when it is either fully obvious or merely unclear.

[X05] Time-sensitive framing
On a 0-10 scale, how much does the caption contain dates, times, seasons, delays, temporary windows, or other explicitly time-bound cues? Judge only the temporal cues, not whether they are good.

[X06] Reasoning given for why the approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the item supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X07] Numeric logistical specificity
Rate 0-10 for the extent of numerals, times, measurements, counts, locations, or other logistical specifics. Count their presence only; do not judge whether the details improve the caption.

[X08] Named partisan political figures and outlets
Read only the caption text. Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the caption names, or unmistakably designates, a real partisan political figure, party, campaign, or news outlet. 0 = no political person, party, campaign or outlet is referenced. 5 = one such reference appears as a passing modifier. 10 = a named political figure or outlet is the caption's subject, or two or more such references appear. Judge extent of political reference only. Do NOT judge whether the reference is accurate, fair, agreeable, well-aimed, or funny, and do NOT let your own view of the figure or outlet move the score in either direction.

[X09] Payload is not a pun on a depicted object's name
Score whether the line's comic payload rests on a homophone or double meaning of a physically depicted object. 10 = no such pun anywhere; the line would survive intact if the object were renamed. 4 = a pun is present but is decoration on an independent idea. 0 = the entire caption exists to deliver one word that means both a scene object and something else ('circus breaker', 'fixture', 'downsizing', 'liquidations', 'best in show'). Score the mechanism, not the cleverness of the pun.

[X10] Quietly reassigns power, blame, or who the subject is
Score whether the line performs a reversal of standing between the parties or between speaker and their own situation. 10 = the sentence relocates authority, victimhood, or agency in a way the reader must recompute ('you now report directly to me'; 'at least i know i'm a puppet'). 5 = mild status friction. 0 = the expected character says the expected thing from the expected position.

[X11] Explicit lexical naming of the depicted object or setting
Read only the caption text. Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the caption explicitly names, in words, a concrete physical object, fixture, animal, room, or vehicle that is evidently the thing being drawn, rather than referring to it only by a pronoun or leaving it unnamed. 0 = no concrete depicted object is named at all; the caption uses only pronouns, deixis ('this', 'it', 'him') or abstractions. 5 = one concrete object is named once in passing. 10 = the depicted object is named explicitly and is also the grammatical focus, or is named more than once, or is named together with its setting. Count nouns only, and count them whether or not they are used punningly. Judge extent of naming only. Do NOT judge whether naming the object is a good or bad move, whether it spoils or sharpens the joke, or whether the caption is funny.

[X12] Semantic reframe
Score 10 when an ordinary word or phrase naturally supports one reading and then snaps to another; score 0 when wording remains literal or produces a strained, unclear pun.

[X13] Explicit statement of the limitation or failure regime
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the item names a condition under which its own claim would not hold, or a case it does not cover. 10 = a specific, checkable limitation is stated; 0 = no limitation or scope condition is acknowledged anywhere.

[X14] Status reversal
Score 10 when the line sharply flips who has authority, leverage, knowledge, or social standing; score 0 when the interpersonal relationship remains unchanged.

[X15] Use of the Oxford (serial) comma
Score 0-10 for how consistently the text uses a comma before the final conjunction in a list. 10 = used in every list; 0 = never used or no lists present. Judge punctuation habit only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X16] Specific persona markers (named addressee or verbal tic)
Score how strongly the caption signals a particular speaker's distinct voice via a named addressee, in-group term, or idiosyncratic phrasing. 10 = the line names a specific addressee or contains a clearly idiosyncratic verbal habit that only this character would use. 0 = the line is voice-neutral and could be said by anyone in any context.

[X17] Discourse-marker hedge opener (veteran stylistic tic)
Rate 0-10 how much the caption opens with a short discourse-marker or hedge phrase (e.g. 'well,', 'okay,', 'no idea,', 'at least,', 'ladies and gentlemen,') before its main statement. 0 = caption launches directly into its main statement with no such opener; 10 = caption is built entirely around a prominent framing opener. Do not judge whether the opener is skillfully used -- score only its presence.

[X18] Fragmentary minimalism
Rate 0-10 for sentence fragments, very short utterances, ellipses, or highly compressed syntax. Judge only the degree of compression; do not judge whether brevity is good or funny.

[X19] Joke is inert as standalone text; needs the drawing to complete
Score how much of the joke is withheld from the text and left to an unseen picture. 10 = read alone, the line is an unremarkable sentence with no discernible gag; it can only become funny once a drawing supplies the other half. 5 = partially self-explaining. 0 = a fully freestanding gag: the text alone states a complete joke, and an image would add nothing. Do not reward or penalize whether you personally find it funny; score only the degree of dependence on unseen material.

[X20] Pun spelled out vs. left compressed for the reader to complete
Score whether a pun's second meaning is made explicit by surrounding words, versus compressed into a single word/phrase the reader must decode unaided. 10 = the sentence structure explicitly walks the reader to the pun's alternate meaning (e.g., parallel phrasing that names both senses). 0 = the pun is a single compressed word/phrase with no scaffolding, and the reader must supply the second meaning entirely on their own.

[X21] Question-format framing
On a 0-10 scale, how much is the caption phrased as a direct question or question-like exchange? Judge only the visible interrogative format, not whether it is good.

[X22] Institutional vocabulary reframes the stakes rather than supplying a pun
Composite. First ask whether the line imports vocabulary from an institution — leases, billing, HR, litigation, parenting, medicine. If it does not, score 5. If it does: 10 = the vocabulary recasts what the scene is about, importing a whole system of obligations the picture did not have; 0 = the vocabulary is present only because one of its words literally matches something drawn, i.e. it is A03 wearing a suit.

[X23] Topical-reference integration
Score 10 when a public figure, trend, or current topic is essential to the joke’s mechanism; score 0 when it is merely name-dropped or could be removed without changing the line.

[X24] Enters mid-conversation and implies an unheard prior turn
Score whether the line is the second (or later) beat of an exchange rather than an opening statement. 10 = it plainly answers or continues something never shown — it begins with 'and', 'no,', 'that's because', or supplies an answer whose question the reader must reconstruct. 5 = addresses someone but starts the exchange. 0 = a self-contained opening pronouncement with no implied interlocutor turn.

[X25] Morbid/dark content (death, injury, loss) as the comic engine
Score how much the caption's humor is built directly on death, physical injury, loss, or decline. 10 = the central joke turns on dark subject matter (death, injury, loss) stated as the punchline's core content. 0 = the caption's content is entirely benign, with nothing dark or morbid.

[X26] Earned detail
Score 10 when each concrete detail causes or intensifies the comic turn; score 0 when details are ornamental, exhaustive, or interchangeable.

[X27] Count of numerals and digit characters in the text
Score 0-10 for how many numerals and digit characters the text contains relative to its length. 10 = digits everywhere; 0 = no digits at all. Count characters only; do not judge whether the numbers are meaningful or whether their presence is good.

[X28] Institutional register
On a 0-10 scale, how much does the caption use legal, financial, bureaucratic, corporate, or other institutional vocabulary? Judge only the register, not whether it is good.

[X29] Controlled escalation
Score 10 when an ordinary premise escalates to a larger consequence without abandoning the underlying situation; score 0 when the line adds an abrupt shock or unrelated pile-on.
