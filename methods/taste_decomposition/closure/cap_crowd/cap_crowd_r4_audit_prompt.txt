You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the caption is as a contest entry for THAT cartoon. It is about the substance of
      what is being communicated, the evidence behind it, and the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the caption is as a contest entry for THAT cartoon: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: reader-submitted entries to a weekly cartoon caption contest, each one written for a specific published cartoon (the cartoon is described alongside every entry below). Each item is a caption.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
how good the caption is as a contest entry for THAT cartoon too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Practiced caption compression
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the line is compressed caption-native dialogue that assumes the drawing, omits explanation, and delivers one economical beat; do not judge whether the compression is effective.

[X02] Transcription artifacts
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the caption exhibits catalog or transcription conventions such as lowercase starts, omitted terminal punctuation, ellipses, doubled hyphens, or spacing quirks; judge only visible text, not whether the caption is good.

[X03] Withholds the explanation the drawing seems to demand
Score 0-10 for how far the caption declines to explain the odd thing in the drawing, and instead speaks as though it needed no explanation, leaving the reader to supply the logic. 10 = the strangeness is treated as entirely unremarkable; 0 = the caption spells out why the scene is the way it is.

[X04] Verbose conversational filler
Score the extent to which the caption uses spoken-style filler words (e.g., 'wow', 'sure', 'well', 'i guess') or conversational run-on phrasing to set up the punchline, adding words that do not change the core joke. Do not judge if the phrasing is good.

[X05] Topical Reference Self-Sufficiency
Score how well the caption's humor stands on its own without relying on a topical, cultural, or celebrity reference as the primary comedic engine. A 10 means the caption is fully self-sufficient—any reference present is incidental to a joke that works on its own terms, or no reference is needed at all. A 0 means the caption's entire comedic value depends on recognizing a specific topical reference and removing it leaves nothing.

[X06] Secondary-detail leverage
Score 10 when the humor depends on supporting props, background details, setting, or secondary characters as well as the central anomaly; score 0 when all such details are ignored.

[X07] Generic caption polish
Score 0–10 for how much the caption has compact, complete, immediately legible setup-punch packaging familiar from generic caption examples; do not judge whether it is funny or apt.

[X08] Bureaucratic or corporate jargon usage
Score the extent to which the caption uses specific corporate, bureaucratic, or administrative terminology (e.g., 'downsizing', 'litigation', 'human resources'). Do not judge if it fits the drawing well.

[X09] Explicitly names an object visible in the drawing
Score 0-10 for how many of the concrete objects, animals or settings visible in the cartoon are named in so many words in the caption. 10 = the caption names several of them explicitly; 0 = it names none. Count the naming only; do not judge whether naming them helps the joke.

[X10] Layered semantic convergence
Score 10 when two or more meanings converge cleanly on the pictured situation; score 0 when the line has only one thin, literal reading.

[X11] Extreme conversational brevity
Score the extent to which the caption is extremely short, abandoning any narrative setup to deliver a quick, blunt conversational response (e.g., 'chicken or rabbit'). Do not judge if brevity makes it a good caption.

[X12] Caption reframes what the drawing is a picture of
Score 0-10 for how far the caption makes the reader see the whole drawing as a picture of a different situation from the one it obviously depicts. 10 = the scene is completely relocated by the line; 0 = the caption accepts the obvious reading of the scene and comments inside it.

[X13] Payoff surprise
Score 10 when the ending sharply redirects the reader toward an unexpected but coherent interpretation; score 0 when the punchline is an obvious first association or lacks a turn.

[X14] Generic-prior obvious idiom
Score the extent to which the caption relies on the single most obvious idiom, pun, or literal interpretation directly invited by the visual elements of the scene. Do not judge whether the joke is clever or well-executed.

[X15] Speaker-role fidelity
Score 10 when the line sounds natural for a particular depicted speaker given that character’s role and situation; score 0 when no plausible depicted speaker could say it.

[X16] Pun and idiom density
Score 0–10 for how much the caption relies on homophones, double meanings, transformed idioms, or concentrated wordplay; do not judge whether the wordplay is clever.

[X17] Petty-Stakes Emotional Grounding
Score how specifically and relatably a depicted character is invested in a small, human-scale concern—petty grievance, minor inconvenience, domestic anxiety, social embarrassment—rather than the grand absurdity of the scene. A 10 means the character's emotional investment is vivid, relatable, and comically disproportionate to the surreal situation. A 0 means no specific emotional investment is conveyed.

[X18] Conversational punch timing
Score 10 when the wording has a natural setup and lands its strongest turn at the end; score 0 when it is awkwardly phrased, overexplained, or lacks a punch.

[X19] Topical news cycle reference
Score the extent to which the caption explicitly names or directly references a specific contemporary real-world entity, person, product, or event (e.g., 'n95 mask', 'stimulus', 'trump'). Do not judge if the reference is funny or apt, only how specific and time-bound the named entity is.

[X20] Non-template angle
Score 10 when the association is surprising and individualized rather than a stock response; score 0 when it uses the first cliché suggested by the image.

[X21] House-style tonal register
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the caption uses plain, restrained, everyday conversational diction and understated social framing; do not judge whether that register suits the cartoon.

[X22] Freshness over cliché
Score 10 when a familiar phrase, reference, or association is materially twisted into a new comic idea; score 0 when it reproduces a stock expression with minimal alteration.

[X23] Number of words in the caption
Score 0-10 inversely with the caption's raw length in words: 10 = five words or fewer, 0 = more than twenty-five words, scaling in between. Count words only; do not judge whether the length suits the joke.

[X24] Deadpan Underreaction To Surreal Danger
Score 10 if the speaker treats a highly dangerous, bizarre, or impossible situation as a mundane, bureaucratic, or trivial inconvenience. Score 0 if the speaker reacts with shock or directly addresses the impossibility of the situation.

[X25] Setup-to-Payoff Register Shift
Score how effectively the caption's opening words set up one expectation—emotional, tonal, or topical—and then deliver the payoff in a deliberately different register. A 10 means the gap between setup and payoff registers is wide and surprising yet perfectly coherent. A 0 means the setup leads predictably and linearly to its conclusion with no tonal pivot.
