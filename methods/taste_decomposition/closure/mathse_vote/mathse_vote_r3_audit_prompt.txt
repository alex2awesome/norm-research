CRITERIA:

[X01] Intuition tied to formal meaning
Score 10 when an accessible analogy or geometric interpretation is explicitly linked to the relevant definition or formula; score 0 when the analogy is vague, misleading, or never connected back to mathematics.

[X02] Confident, unhedged register
Score 0-10 for how unhedged the prose is: absence of 'I think', 'perhaps', 'it seems', 'I might be wrong', 'someone correct me', question marks addressed to the reader, and apologetic asides. 10 = flatly declarative throughout; 0 = hedged in almost every sentence. Score the register only; do not judge whether the confidence is warranted.

[X03] Answer length
Score 0-10 for how long the answer is. 10 = very long, many paragraphs; 5 = a few paragraphs; 0 = one or two lines. Judge extent only -- do not consider whether the length is warranted, whether the answer is thorough, or whether anything in it is correct.

[X04] Notational Discipline and Consistency
Score 10 if mathematical notation is clean, standard, consistent throughout, and well-formatted. Score 0 if notation is confusing, inconsistent, non-standard, or acknowledged as broken.

[X05] Decisive counterexample construction
Score 10 when the answer gives a simple counterexample, verifies the relevant hypothesis, and demonstrates the failure; score 0 when it merely asserts falsity or gives an unverified example.

[X06] Explicit cross-reference to other answers or comments
Score 0-10 the extent to which the answer text explicitly references, quotes, defers to, corrects, or builds on another specific answer or comment on the same page (e.g. 'as was pointed out in another answer', 'to expand on Stephen's answer', 'I see I've just repeated X's comment'). Do NOT judge whether referencing others is good; score only how much explicit cross-referencing is present.

[X07] Invites reader dialogue or explicitly requests feedback
Score 0-10 on whether the answer directly addresses the reader with a question or request (e.g., asking if the explanation makes sense, or asking for help/correction from others). 10 = the answer ends with or contains an explicit question/request directed at the reader/community. 0 = the answer is a pure monologue with no direct address inviting a response.

[X08] Domain and exception discipline
Score 10 when the answer explicitly tracks domains, denominators, convergence regions, endpoints, or branch restrictions and adjusts its claims accordingly; score 0 when it silently treats a restricted statement as globally valid.

[X09] Revision trace markers
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the answer contains edit labels, postscripts, apologies, correction notes, or explicit repair language; do not judge whether the revisions improved the answer.

[X10] Vintage of notation and markup conventions
Read only the answer text. Score 0-10 the EXTENT to which the mathematical notation and markup look like an older vintage of this site's conventions rather than current practice. Traces to count: mathematics typed as plain ASCII with no math delimiters at all (things like t^(3/2), >=, x^2 written bare in prose); raw Unicode operator and arrow glyphs pasted inline where a markup macro would now be used; abbreviated blackboard-bold and older macro spellings rather than their long modern forms; older combinatorial and number-theoretic shorthands (pre-superscript binomial forms, 'hcf' rather than 'gcd', subfactorial written with a leading bang); a single undifferentiated display block where a modern post would use an aligned multi-line environment; prose typographic habits such as double-dot ellipses, spaces before punctuation, or emoticons. 0 = uniformly current, environment-rich markup; 10 = heavily dated or markup-free throughout. [X11] Dense structured mathematical display

Do NOT judge whether the formatting is good.

[X12] Transparent Limitation Acknowledgment
Score 10 if the answer openly acknowledges that its own method is incomplete, that something is broken, or that the approach has limitations—while still being useful. Score 0 if the answer presents itself as complete and authoritative without acknowledging any gaps or issues.

[X13] Generalizable method extraction
Score 10 when the answer clearly identifies a reusable strategy and demonstrates why it works; score 0 when it gives only an isolated calculation with no transferable method.

[X14] Structure-revealing transformation
Score 10 when a substitution, factorization, invariant, or change of representation makes the underlying structure evident and is explained; score 0 when algebraic transformations remain opaque or merely procedural.

[X15] Cross-Method Verification
Score 10 if the answer confirms its result through two or more genuinely independent mathematical methods or perspectives. Score 0 if only one method or path is used, or if the 'second method' is merely a restatement of the first.

[X16] Sustained second-person address to this asker
Score how consistently the answer is written to the person who asked, using the text alone. 10 = second-person address runs through the answer, the asker's own notation and framing are adopted, and the answer checks comprehension or anticipates the asker's next confusion. 5 = occasional 'you' inside an otherwise impersonal exposition. 0 = fully impersonal exposition in the answer's own notation, indistinguishable from a textbook passage on the topic.

[X17] Names a canonical object as a load-bearing shortcut, not as trivia
When the answer invokes a named theorem or named object, score whether the name does work. 10 = naming it immediately settles or reorganizes the question (invoking the theorem is the answer, or the isomorphism to a known classified object reduces the problem to lookup). 5 = the name is correct and relevant but the answer would be unchanged without it. 0 = the name is decorative -- supplied as interesting background alongside a derivation that never uses it -- or no naming occurs where one would obviously help.

[X18] Formal/technical machinery calibrated to the question's apparent level
Score 0-10 on whether the weight of formalism/machinery in the answer matches the apparent difficulty level signaled by the question. 10 = the level of technical apparatus (notation, cited theorems, generality) is well matched to what the question calls for -- neither overkill for a simple question nor underpowered for a hard one. 0 = a clear mismatch, such as heavy multi-step formal machinery deployed on a question that reads as elementary/homework-level, or a bare hand-wave on a question that clearly demands rigor.

[X19] Display-math volume share

[X21] Answer length/verbosity
