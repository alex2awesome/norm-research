You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how newsworthy the release is -- whether working journalists at independent outlets would judge it worth writing up as news. It is about the substance of
      what is being communicated, the evidence behind it, and the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how newsworthy the release is -- whether working journalists at independent outlets would judge it worth writing up as news: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: corporate and institutional press releases issued by named organisations. Each item is a press release.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
how newsworthy the release is -- whether working journalists at independent outlets would judge it worth writing up as news too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Operational commitment at scale
Score 10 when the text specifies substantial resources, coverage, staffing, rollout, or enforcement that make an initiative materially real; score 0 when it is primarily an aspiration, intention, or symbolic statement.

[X02] Direct incident facts vs. reassurance-letter voice
Score whether a release describing a harm or crisis affecting third parties (customers, the public) states the hard facts of what happened -- scope, timeline, cause -- plainly and early, versus leading with apology, reassurance, and trust-affirming language that delays or dilutes those facts. A 10 = the incident's who/what/when/scope is stated plainly in the opening sentences, with reassurance language subordinate to it. A 0 = the release opens with and is dominated by apology/reassurance/trust language, with hard facts delayed, minimized, or given only in passing.

[X03] Capture integrity: extraction residue and mid-sentence truncation
Score 0-10 for HOW MUCH the text shows signs of being an incomplete or failed page capture rather than a whole self-contained document. Count: (a) stray serialisation characters with no textual role, such as a lone double-quote on its own line, a closing brace '}', or a triple-backtick fence; (b) meta-commentary that describes the page instead of being the page, e.g. sentences beginning 'The provided raw page content does not appear to contain a press release' or 'This page does not appear to contain a press release'; (c) a body that stops mid-sentence or mid-word with no closing; (d) an absent headline, dateline or opening so that the text begins abruptly in the middle of an argument; (e) collapsed whitespace runs or glued-together words where layout was lost. 0 = a clean, complete, self-contained document; 5 = the document is intact but visibly clipped at one end; 10 = the text is mostly capture residue with little or no release content in it. Score only the extent of capture damage. You must NOT judge whether the surviving content is good, and must not reward or penalise the item for what the damage hides.

[X04] Presence of a standard boilerplate 'About' footer block
Score 0-10 for how fully the text carries the conventional trailing furniture of a press release: an 'About <organisation>' paragraph, a forward-looking-statements disclaimer, trademark or copyright lines, a media-contact block. 10 = all of these are present and complete; 0 = none of them appear. Score the presence of the furniture only; do not judge whether it is well written or whether the release is newsworthy.

[X05] Presupposes an earlier installment in series
0-10: rate how much the text uses language that presupposes the reader already knows about a prior, un-recapped announcement in an ongoing series on this topic -- e.g., 'update on,' 'is not a new X, but was uncovered as part of the ongoing...,' 'despite extensions on previous deadlines' -- versus language that introduces the organization and topic from scratch as if for a first-time reader. 0 = fully self-contained, introduces everything from scratch; 10 = the text heavily assumes reader knowledge of an earlier, un-recapped announcement. Score only the EXTENT of this presupposition; do not judge whether the update itself is significant.

[X06] First disclosure here, not restatement of already-public facts
Score whether the central fact appears to be entering the public record with this document. 10 = the core claim is disclosed here, with no indication the audience already knows it and no dependence on a prior announcement to make sense. 5 = a genuinely new increment attached to a substantial recap of prior announcements. 0 = the text openly concedes the news is old -- 'as you have likely heard by now', 'in August 2020 we announced a temporary ban ... today we are officially codifying it', 'last month our CEO announced X ... today we're announcing three steps forward' -- or the body is essentially a chronology of initiatives launched in previous years. Judge only novelty of disclosure within the text, not importance.

[X07] Density of numerals and digit strings in the text
Score 0-10 for how many numerals and digit strings the text contains relative to its length, counting any digits at all -- dates, phone numbers, ticker symbols, percentages, footnote markers. 10 = digits appear constantly; 0 = none at all. Count the characters only; do not judge whether the numbers are informative or whether the release is good.

[X08] Supplies information not already public or self-evident
Score 0-10 for how much of the release is information a reader could not already have -- new findings, previously undisclosed terms, first-time figures, a decision not previously announced -- as opposed to restating the organisation's mission, values or previously announced plans. 10 = the substance is genuinely new; 0 = everything in it is either already public or is generic self-description.

[X09] Reactive rebuttal framing that names an external antagonist
Score 0-10 for HOW MUCH the text is organised as a response to a specific outside actor or outside account, as opposed to a self-initiated announcement. Count: (a) a named external party - a regulator, agency, publication, television programme, petition, blog post, short-seller, court or news outlet - identified in the text; (b) that party's claims restated in order to be contradicted; (c) an enumerated refutation structure ('First ... Second ... Third ... Fourth ...') or a run of paragraphs each opening by correcting a stated allegation; (d) explicit characterisations of the outside account as false, incorrect, misleading, preposterous or spun. 0 = no outside actor is answered anywhere; 5 = an outside actor is named and briefly answered inside a longer item; 10 = the entire item exists to rebut a named outside account point by point. Score only the extent of the rebuttal structure. You must NOT judge who is right, whether the rebuttal is convincing, or whether responding was a good idea.

[X10] Calendar and embargo markers
Score 0-10 for how much the text exposes calendar or news-cycle conventions such as quarter-end references, holiday timing, conference schedules, exact event deadlines, embargo-style dates, or “for immediate release” wording; do not judge whether the timing is good or newsworthy.

[X11] Recurring data-bulletin format
Score 0-10 for how much the item follows a routine statistical bulletin format: fixed-period benchmark reporting, month-over-month or year-over-year comparisons, named indices, ranked geographies, source links, and subscription prompts; score extent only, not the importance or quality of the data.

[X12] Consumer Marketing Call-to-Action
Score 0-10 on the extent to which the text reads like a retail advertisement or product launch aimed at consumers, featuring pricing instructions, shopping deadlines, event booking links, or promotional slogans. Do not judge the quality of the marketing, only the presence of consumer calls-to-action.

[X13] Cross-industry or ecosystem-wide implications
Score 10 if the announcement has direct consequences for multiple companies, entire industries, supply chains, or regulatory regimes—not just the issuing organization. Score 0 if the implications are confined entirely to the issuer's internal operations with no spillover effects.

[X14] Independent celebrity, humor, or stunt hook
Score whether the release contains a hook that would draw journalistic interest on its own, independent of any business substance: a celebrity/public-figure tie-in, deliberate humor, a stunt, or a surprising human-interest angle. A 10 = a strong, self-contained novelty/celebrity/humor hook that could carry a story by itself. A 0 = no such hook, purely conventional business content.

[X15] Formal wire-service dateline and ticker format
0-10: rate how much the item opens with formal newswire conventions -- a capitalized CITY, STATE/COUNTRY dateline, an explicit wire-service name (e.g., 'PRNewswire'), and/or a stock ticker in parentheses (e.g., '(NYSE: CRM)') paired with an explicit 'today announced' construction -- versus an informal blog-post or social-caption opening with no dateline, wire branding, or ticker. 0 = none of these markers present; 10 = fully formatted like a paid-financial-newswire release with dateline, wire branding, and ticker. Score only the EXTENT of this formatting; do not judge whether the formal presentation makes the underlying news more important.

[X16] Concrete actionability for consumers or users
Score 10 if the release tells the audience something they can immediately act on—buy a product today, make a reservation, apply for a benefit, use a new feature, access a service—with specific dates, prices, or URLs. Score 0 if the release describes something abstract, internal, or future with no way for the reader to act.

[X17] Cultural crossover or named celebrity figure
Score 10 if the release features a recognizable celebrity, public figure, or cultural institution (entertainer, major media programme, public official) that extends the story well beyond the usual corporate beat. Score 0 if all named individuals are company executives, analysts, or unknown private parties.

[X18] Breadth of public or consumer impact
Score 10 if the announced change directly and tangibly affects a large population of ordinary consumers or workers (millions of shoppers, all employees company-wide, entire user base). Score 0 if the audience affected is narrow (a single executive, a small community of enthusiasts, an internal team).

[X19] Quantitative Data & Index Dump
Score 0-10 on the extent to which the text consists primarily of dense numerical data, percentages, specific financial metrics, and index values with minimal narrative context. Do not judge the accuracy or importance of the data, only the density of the quantitative dump.

[X20] Character-encoding corruption (mojibake) density
Score 0-10 for HOW MUCH visible character-encoding corruption is present in the text. Count occurrences of: (a) apostrophes, em-dashes or curly quotes mis-rendered as a stray letter 'a' glued inside or between words, e.g. 'Itas', 'thatas', 'worldas', 'Salesforceas', 'Iam', 'Weave' (for 'we have'), 'salariesafor', 'basisamaking', 'community a our Ohana'; (b) runs of isolated capital 'A' characters standing where non-breaking spaces or bullets were, e.g. 'A A A A A'; (c) replacement boxes, question marks or stray '?' where punctuation should be, e.g. a trailing '?' in a headline; (d) sentences that break off at a dropped character, e.g. 'reach out to us at  ' with nothing following. Score 0 if the text is clean throughout; 3 if there are a handful of instances; 7 if corruption appears in most paragraphs; 10 if it is pervasive and affects reading on nearly every line. Score only how much corruption is visible. You must NOT judge whether the writing is good, and must NOT treat corruption as evidence about the care, competence or importance of the item.

[X21] Distinctive quotable voice rather than interchangeable corporate template
Score the register of the writing itself. 10 = an unmistakable authorial voice a reporter would quote verbatim for its manner -- sustained satire, an abrupt one-word sign-off, unhedged internal candour, jokes that land -- copy that no other organisation's communications function could have produced. 5 = competent house style with a few lines that stand out. 0 = fully templated: dateline, ticker, benefit-laden subheads, 'About [Company]' block, and not one sentence anyone would reproduce for how it is written. Judge only manner of expression; a release with no facts at all can still score 10 here, and a fact-rich release can score 0.

[X22] Formal filing or legally consequential step by the issuer
Score whether the item is, or reports, an act by the issuer that has legal force. 10 = a registration statement submitted to a securities regulator under a cited rule, an action filed in court, meeting machinery that carries legal effect (record dates, control numbers, legal proxies, a stated password and deadline), a specification lodged with standards bodies. 5 = a policy that binds the issuer's users with stated enforcement consequences and effective dates. 0 = communication with no legal standing at all: a blog post, a marketing announcement, an aspiration with no instrument behind it. Judge legal consequence, not the readability of the legal language.

[X23] Serious dramatic incident with significant stakes
Score 10 if the release recounts a specific, time-and-place-bound dramatic incident involving physical danger, legal jeopardy, large-scale harm, or criminal conduct. Score 0 if no specific incident is described or if the incident is minor and inconsequential.

[X24] Forwarded or republished internal communication markers
Score 0-10 the EXTENT to which the text frames itself as a forwarded, re-posted or re-published message originally directed at an internal audience (employees, customers, community members) rather than at journalists — look for framing phrases like 'the following email was sent to,' 'below is an email,' 'the message below was posted,' or similar introductory wrapper language around what is clearly an internal memo or customer letter. Do NOT judge whether republishing was appropriate; score only how much of this framing is present.

[X25] Distinctive human-interest access
Score 10 when the release offers unusual access to a recognizable person, remarkable place, or scarce experience with vivid concrete particulars; score 0 when it presents a routine product or corporate activity.

[X26] Quantified, concrete announcement of what actually changed
Score 0-10 for how concretely and specifically the release states what has changed in the world -- what is being launched, closed, priced, hired, recalled or reported -- with figures, dates or scope that make the change checkable. 10 = the substantive change is stated with specific magnitude and scope; 0 = the release never says what concretely happened, only that something is exciting or ongoing.

[X27] Strong time-sensitive news peg
Score 10 when the item is anchored to a clearly dated crisis, deadline, seasonal event, live market movement, or imminent launch; score 0 when its significance is essentially timeless.

[X28] Operational specificity
Score 10 when the item states exactly what will change, for whom, where, when, and how it will work; score 0 when it offers only broad intentions or slogans.

[X29] Self-contained announcement quality
Score 10 when the text is a coherent, dated announcement with an identifiable subject, issuing organization, news peg, and complete supporting details; score 0 when it is merely a product page, listicle, fragment, or generic informational page.
