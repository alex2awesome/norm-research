You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how newsworthy the release is -- whether working journalists at independent outlets would judge it worth writing up as news. It is about the substance of
      what is being communicated, the evidence behind it, and the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how newsworthy the release is -- whether working journalists at independent outlets would judge it worth writing up as news: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: corporate and institutional press releases issued by named organisations. Each item is a press release.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
how newsworthy the release is -- whether working journalists at independent outlets would judge it worth writing up as news too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Clear Depiction of Stakeholder Conflict or Tension
Score 10 if the text highlights a clear, specific conflict between the organization and an identifiable antagonist; score 0 if it presents a sanitized, conflict-free narrative.

[X02] Tangible Public Calls to Action Together With Exact Logistics
Score 10 if the text provides concrete dates, exact pricing, or specific URLs for the public to act immediately; score 0 if it contains vague future promises with no actionable details.

[X03] Non-routine change
Score 10 when the text documents a clear break from prior policy, operating practice, product capability, or organizational model; score 0 when it describes continuity, generic values, routine hiring, or an ordinary promotion.

[X04] Industry Structural Change Scope
Score 10 if the announced change alters how an entire industry operates—a new standard, a cross-platform collaboration, or a move that forces competitors to respond. Score 0 if the announcement affects only the issuing company's internal operations or product line.

[X05] Defensive Legal Posturing
Score from 0 to 10 the extent to which the text is structured as a defensive rebuttal to a lawsuit, accident, or regulatory action, using combative language against external critics. Judge the extent of the defensive posture only; do not judge whether the defense is justified.

[X06] Formal wire-service dateline/distribution convention
On a 0-10 scale, rate how much the text opens with formal wire-service dateline conventions: an ALL-CAPS city name followed by a date, an explicit wire-service credit (e.g. '/PRNewswire/'), or a colon-terminated dateline preceding the lead sentence, as opposed to an undated first-person 'company blog' opening. Score only the presence of this dateline/distribution formatting. Do not judge whether the story behind it is important.

[X07] Attributes claims to a checkable source or method
Score 0-10 for whether factual claims in the release are tied to something a journalist could check -- a named study with its method, a filing, a regulator's decision, a survey with its sample, an audited figure -- rather than asserted by the organisation alone. 10 = the central claims carry a checkable source or method; 0 = every claim rests on the organisation's own say-so.

[X08] Consumer purchase-mechanics and promotional offer density
Rate 0-10 the extent to which the text is built around consumer-facing purchase mechanics. Count: named shelf or booking prices a shopper would pay; percentage-off, spend-and-save or dollar-off offer structures; promotional windows and shop-by or on-sale dates; gift-card, coupon, cleanse-price, limit-per-customer or cannot-be-combined terms; availability and where-to-look tips; second-person urging to shop, book, check a store or hurry; exclamation-marked enthusiasm about a deal. 0 = no purchase-mechanics material at all. 5 = a section of the text is offer mechanics, the rest is not. 10 = the text is essentially a shopping offer from start to finish. Score ONLY how much of this material is present. You must NOT judge whether the offer is attractive, well written, generous or worthwhile.

[X09] Primary-source artifact released with the announcement
Score whether the item hands over primary material rather than only summarising it. 10 = the text reproduces or releases something a reporter can quote and inspect directly - a verbatim internal email or letter, reproduced correspondence with a named third party, downloadable specification or design files, the full underlying data series, a filed document. 5 = extensive verbatim quotation from a single named principal, but no artifact. 0 = nothing but the issuer's own summarising prose, optionally with a marketing link.

[X10] Consequences land on identified parties outside the organisation
Score who is actually changed by the action, and how wide that set is. 10 = the described action alters something concrete for people outside the issuing organisation - every customer of a product, an entire industry's equipment choices, competitors, regulators, investors - and the text makes clear who they are and roughly how many. 5 = external parties are affected but marginally, or only a small named set. 0 = nothing changes for anyone outside the organisation; the item concerns the issuer's own employees, offices, internal processes, culture or self-image only. Rhetorical invocation of 'communities' or 'the world' with no external change scores 0.

[X11] Extent of newswire dateline, ticker and boilerplate formatting
Score 0-10 the EXTENT to which the item is dressed in paid-financial-newswire formatting conventions. Count which of these are present, and how canonically: (a) a capitalized CITY, STATE or CITY, COUNTRY dateline at the head; (b) a date line adjoining it; (c) an explicit wire-service name or branding (e.g. 'PRNewswire', 'Business Wire', 'GlobeNewswire'); (d) a stock ticker in parentheses (e.g. '(NYSE: CRM)', '(Nasdaq: XXXX)'); (e) a 'today announced' or 'today unveiled' construction in the opening sentence; (f) an 'About [Organisation]' boilerplate block; (g) a formal media- or investor-contact block with a name, email or phone; (h) a forward-looking-statements or safe-harbour paragraph; (i) a terminal end mark such as ### or -30-. 10 = essentially all of these present in canonical form. 5 = roughly half present, or several present in loose form. 0 = none present -- the item opens like a blog post, newsletter or social caption with no dateline, wire branding, ticker or boilerplate furniture. Score only the extent of the formatting. This is NOT a quality judgement: do not decide whether the formal presentation is appropriate, professional, or makes the underlying matter more or less important, and do not consider what the item says.

[X12] Internal Comm Repackaging
Score from 0 to 10 the extent to which the text explicitly frames itself as a leaked or shared internal message directed at a specific internal audience (e.g., using phrases like 'The following email was sent to employees...', 'Dear Host', or 'Dear Target Guest'). Judge the extent of this framing only; do not judge whether it is good or bad.

[X13] Presence of a standard boilerplate 'About' footer block
Score 0-10 for how fully the text carries the conventional trailing furniture of a press release: an 'About <organisation>' paragraph, a forward-looking-statements disclaimer, trademark or copyright lines, a media-contact block. 10 = all of these are present and complete; 0 = none of them appear. Score the presence of the furniture only; do not judge whether it is well written or whether the release is newsworthy.

[X14] Extent of un-recapped prior-installment presupposition
Score 0-10 the EXTENT to which the text requires the reader to already know about an earlier, un-recapped announcement from this organisation on this topic. Count or estimate: explicit back-references ('update on', 'further to', 'as previously announced', 'as we said in', 'following our earlier'); continuity framing ('the ongoing', 'continues to', 'phase two', 'the next step in', 'despite extensions on previous deadlines', 'was uncovered as part of the ongoing'); definite descriptions of programmes, products, investigations, agreements or deadlines that the text names as already known and never introduces or explains; unexplained internal shorthand, version numbers or programme codes; and, conversely, the absence of first-time-reader scaffolding (who the organisation is, what the topic is, what happened before). 10 = the text is heavily anaphoric throughout and a first-time reader could not reconstruct the prior installment from anything in it. 5 = some back-reference, but the prior context is largely recoverable from the text. 0 = fully self-contained: everything is introduced from scratch, with no reliance on an un-recapped earlier announcement. Score only the extent of the presupposition. This is NOT a quality judgement: do not decide whether presupposing prior knowledge is good or bad writing, and do not consider whether whatever is being updated is significant or whether anything has actually changed.

[X15] Extent of departure from templated corporate register
Score 0-10 the EXTENT to which the writing departs, in manner alone, from standard corporate-communications register. Count or estimate the presence and density of stylistic departure markers: humour or satire; informal or conversational syntax; first- or second-person address; sentence fragments, dashes, ellipses, all-caps or exclamation marks used for effect; abrupt or one-word closings; self-referential or meta asides; distinctive rhythm or repetition used as a device; slang, profanity or invented coinages; and, on the other side, the ABSENCE of template furniture such as benefit-laden subheads, 'About [Organisation]' boilerplate, and stock phrases ('is pleased to announce', 'end-to-end solution', 'reflects our commitment'). 10 = the manner is thoroughly non-standard throughout -- multiple departure markers in most paragraphs and essentially no template furniture. 5 = ordinary house style with a handful of lines written in a non-standard manner. 0 = uniformly standard corporate register, template furniture intact, no departure markers at all. This is NOT a quality judgement: do not decide whether the departures are effective, charming, appropriate, or embarrassing, do not consider whether the writing is good, and do not consider what the item is about or whether it says anything of substance. A text with the markers and no content whatsoever must be able to score 10; a text full of content and written in flat house style must score 0.

[X16] Substantive change of state beyond restated standing position
Score 0-10 how much of a concrete change of state the item reports -- something that is materially different after this text than before it -- together with the specifics of that change. Credit: a decision now taken; a threshold crossed; a count, scope or figure that has moved, with its old and new values or at least its new one; a date now fixed, met, missed or moved; an outcome resolved, a matter closed, withdrawn, expanded or reversed; a condition that has newly come into force. Judge the change IN CONTEXT: given the situation the item describes, how much has actually moved? You are explicitly instructed NOT to score whether the text assumes prior knowledge: do not reward continuity language ('update on', 'as previously announced', 'the ongoing programme', 'phase two') and do not penalise an item for introducing its organisation and topic from scratch, or for recapping fully. 10 = a short, plainly written item that spells out, from scratch if need be, a specific thing that has changed, with its magnitude and its date. 0 = the item reasserts an existing commitment, posture, value or already-fixed fact and nothing in it has moved, however heavily it leans on the language of an ongoing series.

[X17] Independent evidentiary grounding
Score 10 when important claims are supported by named external data sources, independent institutions, multiple attributable parties, or clearly described evidence; score 0 when claims rely only on the issuer's assertions.

[X18] Editorially usable completeness
Score 10 when the text independently supplies a clear subject, actors, timing, location or scope, concrete particulars, and a concise central proposition; score 0 when it is mostly promotional filler, fragmented content, or lacks an identifiable announcement.

[X19] Distinctive Executive Voice with Personality
Score 10 if the release features a strong, recognisable individual voice using idiosyncratic language, informal phrasing, or personal asides that signal authentic authorship. Score 0 if all quoted or narrative language is corporate-generic and could have been written by any PR department.

[X20] Encoding or extraction damage
Score 0–10 for how much the text contains malformed characters, missing spaces, broken markup, HTML artifacts, or visibly corrupted extraction; do not judge whether the feature is good or newsworthy.

[X21] Named-individual personal-voice signature on the document
Rate 0-10 the extent to which the text is voiced as a personal communication from a named individual rather than as an impersonal organisational statement. Count: first-person-singular pronouns ('I', 'me', 'my'); a salutation opening ('Dear ...'); autobiographical framing of the writer's own tenure, role, career length or feelings; an explicit frame stating that the passage is an email, letter or message from a named person; a closing signature giving a personal name, often with a job title; direct expressions of personal thanks, apology or pride in the first person singular. 0 = no personal-voice markers anywhere; the text speaks only as an organisation or in the third person. 10 = the entire text is a signed first-person letter or message. Score ONLY how much personal-voice marking is visible. You must NOT judge whether the personal voice is sincere, appropriate, effective or well executed, and you must not treat a senior-sounding title as evidence of anything.

[X22] Corpus Duplication / Repetition
Score from 0 to 10 the extent to which the text appears to be a verbatim duplicate or heavily syndicated copy of another release in the exact same format, lacking any original phrasing for its specific issuance. Judge the extent of the formulaic duplication only; do not judge the quality of the content.

[X23] Checkable specifics of a dated, concrete event
Score 0-10 how far the item pins its subject to specifics an outsider could independently check, IN CONTEXT of what it is announcing: a specific date or effective date rather than 'soon' or 'this year'; quantities with units (amounts, counts, durations, prices, headcounts, percentages with their base); named external parties, places, sites, agencies, courts, partners or products rather than unnamed 'customers' and 'leading organisations'; reference to something existing outside the text that could be consulted (a filing, docket, dataset, ruling, contract, study, public register); and a clear statement of who is bound to what and from when. Judge only the density and checkability of these specifics relative to the size of the claim being made. You are explicitly instructed NOT to score presentation: ignore entirely whether there is a dateline, a wire-service name, a ticker, a 'today announced' construction, an 'About' block, a contact block, or a safe-harbour paragraph; ignore polish, length and formality. 10 = an unformatted, plainly written item that states exactly what happens, to whom, in what quantity, on what date, naming the outside parties and pointing to something checkable. 0 = an item that names no date, no quantity and no outside party, and rests entirely on unfalsifiable characterisations ('significant', 'industry-leading', 'transformative'), no matter how completely it is dressed in newswire formatting.

[X24] Material scale and stakes
Score 10 when amounts, populations, geographic scope, usage, or operational capacity are large enough to materially raise the subject’s significance; score 0 when scale is absent or expressed only as vague terms such as “global.”

[X25] Accountable disclosure
Score 10 when the issuer acknowledges uncertainty, criticism, failure, or competing claims and supplies specific facts, safeguards, or explanatory detail; score 0 when the text is entirely self-congratulatory and avoids adverse information.

[X26] On-record candour with concrete disclosure in attributed statements
Score 0-10 the amount of substantive candour in what the item actually says in its own voice or in statements attributed to a named person: specifics an organisation would ordinarily soften or omit -- a named cause, an admitted limit or failure, a stated tradeoff, a number that cuts against the organisation, a direct answer to the obvious hard question the topic raises, an explicit 'we do not yet know', or a disagreement stated plainly rather than smoothed over. Judge only the informational content and directness of what is said, and judge it IN CONTEXT (given what this item is about, how much of the awkward substance is actually on the record?). You are explicitly instructed NOT to reward manner of expression: not wit, satire, jokes, informal register, abrupt sign-offs, first-person asides, unusual punctuation, or any other stylistic distinctiveness; and NOT to penalise flat, plain, short, dry, unfashionable or fully templated prose. 10 = even in colourless boilerplate register, the item plainly puts on the record specific things the organisation had an obvious interest in hedging (named cause, admitted shortfall with its size, an unfavourable figure, a limit on what is claimed). 0 = every statement, however distinctively written, is contentless affirmation ('we are thrilled', 'this reflects our ongoing commitment'), with no admitted limit, cause, tradeoff or unfavourable specific anywhere.

[X27] Supplies information not already public or self-evident
Score 0-10 for how much of the release is information a reader could not already have -- new findings, previously undisclosed terms, first-time figures, a decision not previously announced -- as opposed to restating the organisation's mission, values or previously announced plans. 10 = the substance is genuinely new; 0 = everything in it is either already public or is generic self-description.

[X28] Quantitative Head-to-Head Competitive Benchmark
Score 10 if the release provides specific numerical comparisons against named competitors, industry averages, or alternative technologies. Score 0 if the release makes only self-referential claims with no external comparison.

[X29] Extent of dateline and wire-service formatting conventions
Score 0-10 for how completely the text follows newswire layout conventions: an all-caps city-and-date dateline, a '/PRNewswire/' or equivalent wire marker, 'FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE', a '###' or '-30-' end mark, hyperlinked ticker notation. 10 = the full set is present; 0 = none are. Score the layout conventions only; do not judge the substance.
