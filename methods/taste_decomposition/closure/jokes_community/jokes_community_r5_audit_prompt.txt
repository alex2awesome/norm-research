You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself whether the forum's readers voted this joke into the top quarter of its comparison pool rather than the bottom quarter -- a crowd verdict on how funny the joke is: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation

      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream

      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: short jokes posted to a large public joke-sharing forum, where readers vote on what they find funny. Each item is a joke.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a

whether the forum's readers voted this joke into the top quarter of its comparison pool rather than the bottom quarter -- a crowd verdict on how funny the joke is too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Long scaffolding erected for one terminal pun
Score the ratio of setup material to payoff. 10 = several sentences or paragraphs of narrative, list, or verse exist for no reason other than to make one final pun grammatical, and each setup clause is transparently reverse-engineered from the punchline. 5 = moderate setup, some of which does independent work. 0 = setup is one clause, or the setup is genuinely interesting on its own terms. Reward the diagnosis of reverse-engineered scaffolding, not length as such.

[X02] Uses the question-and-answer riddle template
Score 0-10 for how far the text is laid out as a question followed by an answer -- 'What do you call...', 'Why did...', a question mark followed by a reply. 10 = a pure two-part question-and-answer; 0 = no question form anywhere. Score the template only; do not judge whether the riddle is any good.

[X03] Orthographic-noise signature
Using only the item text, score 0-10 for how much it contains unusual capitalization, spelling errors, punctuation runs, underscores, markdown artifacts, hashtags, handles, spacing anomalies, or other typographic noise; do not judge whether the feature is good or funny.

[X04] Shouting typography and punctuation runs
Score 0-10 for how much the text uses capitalised shouting, repeated exclamation or question marks, stretched-out letters, or emoji. 10 = constant throughout; 0 = none at all. Count the typography only; do not judge whether it is effective or whether the joke is funny.

[X05] Subversion of Common Idioms
Score 10 if the punchline takes a common phrase or idiom and provides a literal, alternate, or punny reinterpretation. Score 0 if standard language is used with no double-meaning or subversion.

[X06] Vivid absurd imagery
Score 10 when an unusual mental image is concrete, surprising, and connected to the punchline; score 0 when imagery is generic or unrelated.

[X07] Presence of a Clear Comedic Payoff
Score 10 if the text definitively resolves the setup's tension with an actual punchline or twist. Score 0 if the joke ends abruptly with no payoff, or masquerades as a joke without delivering one.

[X08] Premise is specific rather than generic
Score 0-10 for how particular the situation is -- named details, a concrete scene, a specific relationship or object doing real work -- as against a stock frame filled with interchangeable placeholders. 10 = the specifics are what make the joke work; 0 = a template with generic slots. Judge specificity, not subject matter.

[X09] Pseudo-logical/mechanistic reasoning absurdity
Score 10 if the humor arises from a chain of literal or pseudo-logical reasoning that is absurd yet internally consistent (e.g., misapplying a fact or taking an instruction too literally). Score 0 if no such reasoning chain is present.

[X10] Platform-specific meta-framing
Score 0-10 on the extent to which the text explicitly references the online platform, its mechanics (upvotes, front page), or specific subcommunities (e.g., Reddit, r/jokes, this sub). Judge the extent of presence only, not whether the reference is good.

[X11] Register-of-taboo content intensity
0-10: how much explicit sexual, scatological, slur-adjacent, or dark/shock subject matter does the item contain? Score extent of taboo content present in the text only; do not judge whether the taboo content is used tastefully, effectively, or is itself funny.

[X12] Absurdist Escalation
Score 10 if the premise starts normally but escalates into a highly bizarre, surreal, or absurd scenario. Score 0 if the scenario remains entirely grounded in reality or a standard joke framework.

[X13] Markup residue, smart typography and composing-device fingerprints
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of markup and typographic residue: literal markdown tokens left in the text (##, *emphasis*, blockquotes, bold markers), curly/smart apostrophes and quotation marks rather than straight ones, ellipsis runs of three or more dots, ALL-CAPS emphasis words, emoji, hashtags, at-handles, unusual spacing or stray trailing whitespace, and block quotations with source citations. 0 = plain straight-ASCII prose with ordinary punctuation; 10 = several such residues present at once. Count occurrences of the residues only. You must NOT judge whether the typography is tasteful, correct, or effective, and must NOT judge the joke.

[X14] Misdirection Quality
Score 10 if the setup leads the reader confidently toward one interpretation and the punchline redirects to a second interpretation that was hidden in plain sight. Score 0 if the punchline is predictable from the setup or if there is no misdirection at all.

[X15] Forum-era idiom
Using only the item text, score 0-10 for how much vocabulary, platform language, topical framing, or cultural reference signals a particular era of the forum; do not judge whether the feature is good or funny.

[X16] Specialist-domain knowledge as the hinge
Score whether the joke turns on a fact from a technical or specialist domain -- chemistry, mathematics, networking, physics, programming, psychoanalysis -- such that the reader must already hold that fact for the punchline to resolve. 10 = the hinge is a genuine domain fact, deployed accurately, and a reader without it gets nothing at all. 5 = a technical veneer over a joke that works anyway. 0 = no specialist content. Score the load the domain fact carries, not how hard the domain is.

[X17] Spelling/grammar cleanliness
Score 10 if the text is free of typos and grammatical slips. Score 0 if the text contains multiple misspellings or grammar errors that could obscure or degrade the intended reading (e.g., 'afreud', 'recpetion', 'cheak').

[X18] Explanatory scaffolding
How much text is devoted to setup exposition, definitions, backstory, repeated premises, glosses, or explanation before the final turn? Score the amount from 0-10, not whether the added detail improves the joke.

[X19] Platform self-awareness
Score 10 when commentary on posting, reading, reposting, or forum behavior is itself part of the joke’s mechanism; score 0 when platform language is generic decoration.

[X20] Evergreen Subject Matter
Score 10 if the joke relies on universal, timeless human conditions. Score 0 if it is inextricably tied to a specific, temporary news cycle, election, or fleeting pop culture moment.

[X21] Phonetic double-use
Score 10 when sound similarity creates two clear meanings that are quickly recoverable; score 0 when there is no phonetic mechanism or the resemblance is strained.

[X22] Relatability of Frustration
Score 10 if the joke taps into a widely shared everyday annoyance or social dynamic (addiction to scrolling, disappointment in content, petty logic) that readers feel personally. Score 0 if the premise is artificial or inaccessible.

[X23] Audience-management furniture: apologies, disclaimers, repost notes
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of explicit audience-management material addressed to readers rather than being part of the joke: apologies or self-deprecation about the post ('I know, I'm a terrible person'), repost or originality disclaimers ('technically a repost', 'sorry if repost'), first-post announcements, requests for votes or feedback, edit notes, tags, content warnings, or instructions to the reader about how to receive the item. 0 = none; 10 = a large share of the text is such furniture. Score presence and volume of the furniture only. Do NOT judge whether the furniture is charming, humble, irritating, or whether it helps the joke.

[X24] Inventive taboo handling
Score 10 when a sensitive or offensive premise is subordinated to an original joke mechanism; score 0 when shock value or the target supplies essentially all the humor.

[X25] Chestnut familiarity: reads as long-circulated stock material
Score how fresh the item feels as a piece of writing, judged from internal signs of prior circulation: chain-email cadence, camp-rhyme meter, riddle-book phrasing, a punchline that is a decades-old set phrase, or an elaborate setup engineered backwards toward a famous pre-existing wordplay. 10 = the phrasing and construction feel newly written for this occasion; nothing about it reads as copied from a stock repertoire. 5 = a familiar joke type but freshly worded. 0 = unmistakably a warhorse -- a widely reproduced pun, rhyme, or chain-letter anecdote reproduced in its canonical form. Do not reward or punish quality; score only apparent circulation age.

[X26] Multi-line narrative build-up length
Score 0-10 the extent to which the item uses a multi-paragraph or multi-line structure with an extended narrative build-up before the punchline, rather than a one-liner or two-line Q&A. Do not judge the pacing quality.

[X27] Unedited or non-US-native English surface traces
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of surface traces of little proofreading or of English written outside a US-native register: misspellings and letter transpositions, wrong or missing punctuation and capitalisation, duplicated or garbled words, non-US spellings ('aluminium'), formal or bookish connectives ('On further enquiry', 'All-in-all'), and phrasing that reads as translated or as second-language idiom. 0 = clean, idiomatic, evidently proofread US-native prose; 10 = pervasive traces. Score only the visible surface traces. Do NOT judge whether the errors are endearing, sloppy, or whether they hurt or help the joke, and do not judge the joke.

[X28] Canonical-chestnut retelling fingerprint (position in retelling chain)
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the text presents as a re-rendering of a joke, pun, rhyme or lyric that already circulates widely, rather than as newly minted: a stock template filled in, wording that reads as the standard version of a known gag, an old schoolyard rhyme or a well-worn pun form, an explicit signal that the item has been seen before ('technically a repost', a line deliberately repeated verbatim), or a payoff that assumes the reader already knows the original. 0 = reads as freshly written for this posting; 10 = reads as a faithful retelling of an already-circulating item. Score the retelling signals in the text only. Do NOT judge whether retelling is lazy or whether the item is funny, and do not check whether it is actually a repost.

[X29] Setup commits to one misleading reading
Score 0-10 for how completely the setup commits the reader to a single interpretation before the ending arrives -- no hedging, no visible second meaning left showing, nothing that tips the reader off early. 10 = the reader is fully committed to one reading; 0 = the second meaning is visible from the first sentence or no particular reading is set up at all.
