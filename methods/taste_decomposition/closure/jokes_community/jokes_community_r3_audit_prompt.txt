CRITERIA:

[X01] Uses the question-and-answer riddle template
Score 0-10 for how far the text is laid out as a question followed by an answer -- 'What do you call...', 'Why did...', a question mark followed by a reply. 10 = a pure two-part question-and-answer; 0 = no question form anywhere. Score the template only; do not judge whether the riddle is any good.

[X02] Shouting typography and punctuation runs
Score 0-10 for how much the text uses capitalised shouting, repeated exclamation or question marks, stretched-out letters, or emoji. 10 = constant throughout; 0 = none at all. Count the typography only; do not judge whether it is effective or whether the joke is funny.

[X03] References are load-bearing, not decorative name-drops
For every shared reference the item invokes -- a named person, a product, a platform, a news event, a place, or an ordinary shared experience such as commuting or a doctor's waiting room -- ask whether the joke's mechanism actually exploits a specific property of it. Score 10 when each reference is load-bearing: the punch turns on something particular and true about that referent, and swapping it for a different referent of the same category would break the joke. An item containing no proper nouns, no slang and no topical material at all can score 10 if the ordinary shared situation it uses is exploited this precisely. Score 0 when references are decorative: the named person, brand or platform is interchangeable with any other, present only as recognition bait or to set a scene, and the punch would survive unchanged if you substituted a different name. Explicitly do NOT reward how many references there are, how current or recognisable they are, or how fashionable the vocabulary is; a single dense cluster of very recognisable names, none of which is exploited, scores 0. Do NOT judge whether the item is funny -- judge only whether its references do work.

[X04] Setup supplies exactly what the punch needs to land
Judge only whether the information needed for the joke's mechanism is present and correctly placed, in context. Ask: when the final line arrives, has the text already given the reader every premise, referent and detail that line depends on -- and has it withheld the thing the line reveals? Score 10 when the setup is complete and load-bearing: every pronoun has an antecedent, every referent the punch turns on has been introduced, nothing the punch needs arrives too late, and nothing that would spoil the punch arrives too early. A misspelt, lowercase, unpunctuated, run-on item can score 10 here if its logical scaffolding is intact and the punch has everything it needs. Score 0 when the mechanism cannot resolve: the punch relies on a fact, character or setting never supplied, an 'it' or 'he' points at nothing, the text stops before the mechanism completes, or the payload was already given away in the setup so nothing remains to land. Explicitly do NOT score spelling, capitalisation, punctuation, paragraph breaks, markup artifacts, or the polish of the prose; do NOT score whether the joke is funny -- an unfunny joke whose logic is fully supplied scores 10.

[X05] Economy and timing of information release before the punch
Judge the handling of information in this specific text, whatever shape it takes. Ask: is every element before the final beat doing work, and does the decisive element arrive last? Score 10 when the text is economical and correctly timed for its own content -- no sentence could be deleted without breaking the mechanism, no detail is repeated, no explanation follows the punch, no signposting tells the reader in advance what kind of turn is coming, and the word or phrase that flips the meaning sits at or near the end where it flips the most. A rambling-looking narrative can score 10 if each digression is a plant the ending uses; a one-line item can score 0 if half of it is redundant. Score 0 when the text wastes the reader: filler preamble, the same beat stated twice, the mechanism explained after it fires, or the revealing element buried in the middle so the ending merely restates it. Explicitly do NOT reward conformity to any template, do NOT reward shortness for its own sake, do NOT reward speaker labels, tidy layout or list formatting, and do NOT judge whether the item is funny; a flat joke with perfect information timing scores 10, and a hilarious one that explains itself afterwards scores low.

[X06] Extent of strict conformity to one recognisable joke template
Identify which single template, if any, the text is attempting -- pure one-liner, question-and-answer riddle, knock-knock, scripted dialogue with speaker labels, narrative build, or numbered/bulleted list -- then score 0-10 for how strictly it holds that template from first word to last. Score 10 when exactly one template is used with no deviation: the structural slots the template requires are all present, in order, with nothing from another template mixed in and nothing appended after the closing slot (no author commentary, no 'edit:', no tag line, no emoji sign-off). Score 5 when a template is recognisable but broken somewhere -- narration spliced into a riddle, a missing speaker label, a stray remark after the final slot. Score 0 when no template is identifiable and the text has no formal shape. This is a structural conformity count only: do NOT judge whether the chosen template suits the material, whether the closing slot is well filled, or whether the item is funny. A completely flat, unfunny question-and-answer riddle in immaculate two-line form scores 10 here.

[X07] Extent of overlap with stock circulated joke material
This is a counting question about textual familiarity, not a quality judgement. Estimate 0-10 how much of the item's material -- its frame, its wording, and its payload -- matches joke material that has been in wide circulation. Consider: (a) is the opening frame a stock template you can name (bar-entry, light-bulb, knock-knock, 'what do you call a', 'my wife and I', 'a man walks into'); (b) can you complete or approximately predict the final line before reaching it; (c) does the wording match a canonical version phrase-for-phrase. Score 10 when frame, wording and payload are all standard circulated material you could recite. Score 5 when one of the three is standard and the others are not. Score 0 when none of the three matches anything in circulation you can identify. Do NOT decide whether being familiar or being unfamiliar is good, funny, lazy or skilled; do not consider whether the item works. A completely inert, unfunny, freshly typed line with no recognisable frame scores 0 here, and a beautifully executed line that is word-for-word a classic scores 10 here.

[X08] Setup commits to one misleading reading
Score 0-10 for how completely the setup commits the reader to a single interpretation before the ending arrives -- no hedging, no visible second meaning left showing, nothing that tips the reader off early. 10 = the reader is fully committed to one reading; 0 = the second meaning is visible from the first sentence or no particular reading is set up at all.

[X09] Premise is specific rather than generic
Score 0-10 for how particular the situation is -- named details, a concrete scene, a specific relationship or object doing real work -- as against a stock frame filled with interchangeable placeholders. 10 = the specifics are what make the joke work; 0 = a template with generic slots. Judge specificity, not subject matter.

[X10] Extent of dated period markers present in the text
Count period-specific tokens; this is an extent estimate, not a quality judgement. Tally occurrences of: named public figures tied to a particular era, specific news events or controversies, named products, brands, apps, sites or platforms, platform-native conventions (hashtags, handles, upvote/karma talk, subreddit or thread references, emoji-as-punctuation), prices or technologies that date the text, and slang or idiom characteristic of a particular period. Score 10 when such markers are dense -- several distinct ones, or the whole premise rests on one specific dated event or platform. Score 5 for one or two incidental markers. Do NOT decide whether being dated or being timeless is good, whether the reference is well used, whether it is obscure, or whether the item is funny. A pointless sentence that merely lists three current celebrity names scores 10 here; a superbly crafted joke about a chicken crossing a road scores 0 here.

[X11] Genuine comic invention performed by this text
Read the item and ask what act of comic construction it performs: does some specific connection, reversal, mis-reading or reframing get built here, such that a reader who follows the setup arrives somewhere they had not already been carried to? A three-word, plainly worded, entirely unfashionable line with no unusual vocabulary and no proper nouns can score 10 if its pivot is genuinely constructed. Score 0 when nothing is constructed: the item merely occupies the shape of a joke, states a topic and then states an attitude toward it, or reproduces a payload that the setup already fully implied, so no step is taken. Explicitly do NOT reward unfamiliarity, obscurity, odd wording, edginess, length, or the mere fact that you have not seen the item before; a phrasing you have never encountered that performs no construction scores 0, and a construction you happen to have met before still scores on the construction itself. Judge the work done in the text, not its novelty as an artifact.

An empty, pointless, perfectly typed sentence scores 10 here; a brilliant, fully comprehensible joke typed with no capitals and four misspellings scores low here.
