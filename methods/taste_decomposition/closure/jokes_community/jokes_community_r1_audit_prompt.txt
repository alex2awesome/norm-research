You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about whether the forum's readers voted this joke into the top quarter of its comparison pool rather than the bottom quarter -- a crowd verdict on how funny the joke is. It is about the substance of
      what is being communicated, the evidence behind it, and the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself whether the forum's readers voted this joke into the top quarter of its comparison pool rather than the bottom quarter -- a crowd verdict on how funny the joke is: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: short jokes posted to a large public joke-sharing forum, where readers vote on what they find funny. Each item is a joke.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
whether the forum's readers voted this joke into the top quarter of its comparison pool rather than the bottom quarter -- a crowd verdict on how funny the joke is too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] External-source trace
Score 0-10 for explicit quotation, attribution, named source material, movie references, or transcription-like framing; judge sourcing signals only, not whether the joke is good or funny.

[X02] Reads as freshly minted rather than a circulated chestnut
Score how much the item reads as a joke the writer constructed versus a standard that has been in circulation for decades and is reproduced here verbatim. 10 = the premise, the pivot and the phrasing all feel newly assembled; a well-read judge does not recognise it. 5 = a familiar frame with a new payload, or a new frame with a familiar payload. 0 = a canonical chestnut whose punchline the judge could recite before reaching it, transcribed with no alteration. Judge only recognisability and staleness of the material as text; do not consider how the item might be received.

[X03] Uses the question-and-answer riddle template
Score 0-10 for how far the text is laid out as a question followed by an answer -- 'What do you call...', 'Why did...', a question mark followed by a reply. 10 = a pure two-part question-and-answer; 0 = no question form anywhere. Score the template only; do not judge whether the riddle is any good.

[X04] Nerdy or Academic Wordplay
Score 10 if the punchline explicitly requires knowledge of STEM, math, grammar, or specific academic trivia to understand; score 0 if the joke requires no specialized academic knowledge.

[X05] Premise is specific rather than generic
Score 0-10 for how particular the situation is -- named details, a concrete scene, a specific relationship or object doing real work -- as against a stock frame filled with interchangeable placeholders. 10 = the specifics are what make the joke work; 0 = a template with generic slots. Judge specificity, not subject matter.

[X06] Presupposed familiarity: variant, remix or knowing-repost framing
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the text presents itself as occupying a position in a chain of already-circulating jokes rather than as newly minted: explicit repost or 'you have seen this' language, self-description as a variant or addition, sentences that assume the reader already knows the joke's form or its original, a punch that is a well-worn stock formula reused with a twist, or a structure whose payoff requires the reader to recognise a prior joke or catchphrase. 0 = presents as freshly written and self-contained, needing no prior joke; 5 = leans on a stock formula or a widely known catchphrase without acknowledging it; 10 = openly names itself as a repost, remix or reply to a joke the audience is assumed to have already seen. Score the framing and dependence on prior circulation only. Do NOT judge whether reuse is lazy or skilful, or whether the twist works.

[X07] Datable topical reference fixing the item to one forum era
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the item is pinned to a specific period by its references: named politicians or public figures in a time-bound role, live news events, election or scandal references, calendar markers ('end of the decade'), platform or technology idiom belonging to one era, and slang whose currency was time-limited. 0 = fully timeless, could have been written in any decade; 4 = one dated reference that is incidental; 7 = the setup depends on a situation that was current only for a period; 10 = the item is unintelligible without knowing a specific news cycle. Score how narrowly the text can be dated, not how old it seems. The judge MUST NOT judge whether the reference is stale, fresh, or well-chosen, and MUST NOT judge the politics involved -- only how tightly the text localises itself in time.

[X08] Compression and economy
Score 10 when every word efficiently supports the comic effect and the joke reaches its point quickly; score 0 when it is repetitive, bloated, or padded.

[X09] Retroactive Reinterpretation
Score 10 when the punchline forces the reader to retroactively reread the setup with an entirely different meaning—the earlier text contained a hidden reading that only becomes visible at the end. Score 0 when the setup's meaning is unchanged by the punchline, or when there is no setup to reinterpret.

[X10] Orthographic error as accident versus orthography as deliberate device
Score the handling of spelling and grammar. 10 = the text is clean, OR any non-standard spelling is unmistakably intentional and load-bearing, functioning as the joke's mechanism. 5 = clean but flat prose with minor slips that do not touch the joke. 0 = multiple unintentional misspellings, dropped words, or broken syntax that the joke does not use, signalling that the item was posted without a reread. Distinguish carefully: an intentional respelling that IS the punchline is a 10, not a 0.

[X11] Shouting typography and punctuation runs
Score 0-10 for how much the text uses capitalised shouting, repeated exclamation or question marks, stretched-out letters, or emoji. 10 = constant throughout; 0 = none at all. Count the typography only; do not judge whether it is effective or whether the joke is funny.

[X12] Platform in-group meta-humor about the joke's own reception
Score whether the joke explicitly references the posting/voting mechanics of the forum itself (reposts, karma, upvotes, front page, comments) as part of its content, and whether that reference is turned into an actual joke rather than a literal complaint. A 10 uses forum-mechanics content as the vehicle for a genuine ironic or self-aware punchline. A 0 either has no forum-mechanics content, or mentions it only as a flat, un-twisted complaint/disclaimer.

[X13] Audience-Management Meta-Framing
Score the extent to which the text includes meta-commentary addressing the forum's mechanics, anticipated reception, or apologizing for the post (e.g., references to 'this sub', repost status, or expected votes). Evaluate only the presence of this framing; do not judge whether it is warranted.

[X14] Verbosity and layout load
Score 0-10 for the amount of prose, number of setup details, line breaks, explanatory padding, and formatting bulk; do not judge whether the length improves timing or humor.

[X15] Textual completeness and legibility
Score 10 when the text is grammatically clear, self-contained, and presents the setup and punch in an easy-to-follow form; score 0 when missing context, broken formatting, typos, or truncation obstruct comprehension.

[X16] Transgression carried by a mechanism, not by shock alone
Composite, for items with taboo, offensive, dark, or sexual content. Score whether a formal comic mechanism (pun, reversal, misdirection, unexpected literalism) does the work. 10 = the transgressive material is the vehicle for a real mechanism that would still be a joke shape if the topic were tame. 5 = a mechanism is present but thin, and the taboo is doing most of the lifting. 0 = the item states something transgressive with no mechanism at all -- the offence IS the content. Items with no transgressive content are not applicable and should score 5.

[X17] Cleanliness of commitment to one delivery format
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the item commits, without deviation, to a single recognisable joke format: pure one-liner, question-and-answer riddle, scripted dialogue with speaker labels, narrative build, or list. 0 = no identifiable format, the text drifts or stops without a formal shape; 5 = a recognisable format that is broken somewhere, mixing narration into a riddle or adding stray commentary after the punch; 10 = one format executed strictly from first word to last, with the punch positioned exactly where that format places it and nothing appended. Score only structural conformity to a template. Do NOT judge whether the format was the right choice, whether the punch is good, or whether the item is funny; a flat joke in a perfectly clean question-and-answer shape scores 10.

[X18] Orthographic noise
On a 0-10 scale, rate the extent of visible spelling errors, malformed grammar, inconsistent capitalization, or title-body mismatches. Judge only the textual presence of these features; do not judge whether they are good, funny, or effective.

[X19] Forum/Platform Meta-Reference
Score the extent to which the text explicitly mentions the platform, its voting mechanisms (e.g., upvotes, front page), or its own status as a post/repost. Do not judge if this makes the text good or bad.

[X20] Vivid concrete imagery
Score 10 when the wording creates an immediate, specific mental image; score 0 when it remains abstract, generic, or difficult to visualize.

[X21] Explicit external attribution or citation line
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the text carries an explicit pointer to an outside source for the material: a credit line naming a film, book, comedian, scripture reference, chapter-and-verse, URL, or a phrase such as 'from the movie X', 'heard this from', 'translated from', 'credit to'. 0 = no attribution anywhere; 4 = a bare source name appended without formatting; 7 = a clearly set-off credit line or formal citation; 10 = the item is structured around the citation, e.g. it reproduces a quoted source block plus a reference. Score presence, explicitness and prominence of the pointer only. The judge MUST NOT judge whether crediting is honest, appropriate, or good practice, and MUST NOT judge the quality of the cited material.

[X22] Transformed cultural allusion
Score 10 when a recognizable song, film, text, or character is actively repurposed into the joke; score 0 when it is merely quoted or name-dropped.

[X23] Clean semantic double-use
Score 10 when a key word or phrase naturally supports both the setup meaning and a distinct punchline meaning; score 0 when there is no double meaning or the connection is forced.

[X24] Punchline closure / resolution
Score whether the text arrives at a clear, self-contained final twist that resolves the setup. A 10 is a joke that ends on an unmistakable payoff word or image with nothing left dangling. A 0 trails off, asks a question it never answers, or reads as an unfinished fragment with no discernible landing point.

[X25] Topical/political specificity
Score how much the joke's humor depends on knowledge of a specific, named contemporary political figure or news event (as opposed to being evergreen). A 10 is entirely dependent on current political personalities/events for its meaning. A 0 has no political content and would read identically in any decade.

[X26] Reliance on Shock or Taboo Subjects
Score the extent to which the text leans on dark subject matter, sexual content, or slur-adjacent identity references. Do not judge the appropriateness or funniness.

[X27] Setup commits to one misleading reading
Score 0-10 for how completely the setup commits the reader to a single interpretation before the ending arrives -- no hedging, no visible second meaning left showing, nothing that tips the reader off early. 10 = the reader is fully committed to one reading; 0 = the second meaning is visible from the first sentence or no particular reading is set up at all.

[X28] Read-aloud cadence
Score 10 when phrasing naturally creates a spoken rhythm, pause, or emphatic landing; score 0 when delivery would be awkward or the rhythm contributes nothing.

[X29] Natural phonetic transformation
Score 10 when a sound-alike smoothly transforms a familiar phrase into the punchline; score 0 when pronunciation or spelling makes the connection strained.
