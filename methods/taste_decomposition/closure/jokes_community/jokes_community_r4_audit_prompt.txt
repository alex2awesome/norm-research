You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about whether the forum's readers voted this joke into the top quarter of its comparison pool rather than the bottom quarter -- a crowd verdict on how funny the joke is. It is about the substance of
      what is being communicated, the evidence behind it, and the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself whether the forum's readers voted this joke into the top quarter of its comparison pool rather than the bottom quarter -- a crowd verdict on how funny the joke is: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: short jokes posted to a large public joke-sharing forum, where readers vote on what they find funny. Each item is a joke.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
whether the forum's readers voted this joke into the top quarter of its comparison pool rather than the bottom quarter -- a crowd verdict on how funny the joke is too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Premise Novelty
Score the freshness of the central comic premise. 10 = an uncommon combination of subject, action, and angle is immediately legible; 0 = a familiar stock joke, duplicate, or generic one-liner has no distinctive angle.

[X02] Formatting as Comic Timing
Use 10 when line breaks, pauses, repetition, quotation layout, or abrupt spacing create deliberate timing or a second beat; use 0 when formatting is accidental noise or adds no comic rhythm.

[X03] Overall length, paragraph count and setup verbosity
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of textual bulk and elaboration: total word count, number of paragraphs or line breaks, number of independent sentences before the final clause, and the amount of descriptive scene-setting or enumerated detail preceding the ending. 0 = a single short line under roughly a dozen words with no setup beyond the minimum; 5 = two or three sentences, one short setup paragraph plus an ending; 10 = several paragraphs, extended narrative or multi-clause enumeration, or a long block quotation. Judge bulk only. You must NOT judge whether the length is appropriate, whether the item is padded or tight, or whether it is funny.

[X04] Overall length of the post
Score 0-10 for how long the text is. 10 = many sentences or paragraphs; 5 = a few sentences; 0 = a single short line. Judge extent only -- do not consider whether the length is warranted, whether the build is worth it, or whether anything in it lands.

[X05] Fresh recombination
Score 10 when the text combines familiar ingredients into an unusual premise-detail-mechanism pairing; score 0 when it is explicitly duplicated, generic, or framed as recycled material.

[X06] Meta-Forum Self-Awareness
Score 10 if the joke explicitly references its own existence as a forum post, a repost, community upvotes, or posting culture. Score 0 if it contains no such meta-references and stands purely as a standalone joke.

[X07] Reveal forces a rereading of the setup
Score 0-10 for how far the ending makes the reader go back and reinterpret what the setup meant -- the setup supported one reading, and the last move shows a second reading was there all along. 10 = the whole setup flips meaning on the final move; 0 = the ending adds information but nothing earlier is re-read. Judge the mechanism, not whether the joke amused you.

[X08] Defensive posting furniture
Score 0-10 for the amount of apology, hedging, preemptive defense, disclaimer, trend-setting language, or self-deprecation surrounding the joke. Do not judge sincerity or effectiveness.

[X09] Nothing in the text is removable
Score 0-10 for how much of the text is load-bearing: every clause either plants something the ending uses or is required to make the situation legible. 10 = removing any clause breaks the joke; 0 = whole sentences could be deleted without the joke changing. Judge necessity, not brevity -- a long text in which everything is used scores high.

[X10] Setup-to-payoff economy ratio
Score 10 if the ratio of build-up length to punchline strength is maximally tight -- very little setup text is spent per unit of payoff delivered. Score 0 if the setup is long and rambling relative to a weak, unclear, or absent payoff.

[X11] Coherent Absurd Detail
Score how purposefully concrete odd details build toward the ending. 10 = every notable detail accumulates into one intelligible, sharply absurd payoff; 0 = details are random, decorative, or disconnected.

[X12] Register of taboo/shock content
Score 0-10 how far the item leans on shock value via sexual content, scatological content, slur-adjacent language, or dark/taboo subject matter (0 = no taboo content present, 10 = saturated with taboo/shock material). Do not judge whether the taboo content is used skillfully or is funny -- score only its presence and intensity.

[X13] Orthographic noise: typos, missing apostrophes, non-native phrasing
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of orthographic and mechanical irregularity in the text: misspelled words, dropped or wrong apostrophes ('arent', 'dont', 'wont'), lowercase 'i', subject-verb disagreement or dropped articles, missing terminal punctuation at a setup/punch boundary, spacing anomalies, inconsistent quotation marks, and phrasings that read as translated or non-native. 0 = clean, conventionally spelled and punctuated throughout; 5 = two or three small slips; 10 = pervasive misspelling, agreement errors and missing punctuation. Count mechanical irregularities only. You must NOT judge whether the errors hurt the joke, and you must NOT treat clean text as better text.

[X14] Throat-clearing and setup-to-punch word ratio
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of preamble relative to punch: scene-setting sentences, background exposition, first-person framing narrative ('I was going to...', 'So there was a...', 'I recently attended...'), and elaboration before the payoff arrives. Estimate the share of the item's words that precede the final turn: 0 = essentially no preamble (the item is one line and the turn is immediate); 10 = a long multi-sentence or multi-paragraph build with a short terminal tag. Score length and proportion only. The judge must NOT judge whether the build is entertaining, well paced, or whether the payoff was worth it.

[X15] Controlled anti-joke framing
Score 10 when the text deliberately sets up a conventional joke expectation and makes banality, refusal, or withholding the actual payoff; score 0 when incompleteness appears accidental.

[X16] Pre-owned chestnut: the pun arrives already familiar
Rate how strongly the item reads as a long-circulating, widely reproduced joke whose punchline a reader is likely to complete before reaching it. 10 = a canonical, anthologised-feeling construction, the kind reprinted for decades, where the wording itself feels standardised. 5 = a familiar template with a mildly fresh filling. 0 = a construction that reads as newly assembled for this occasion, with no sense of prior circulation. Score familiarity of form and phrasing only; do not reward or punish the joke's cleverness.

[X17] Knowledge threshold is common schooling, resolving instantly
Composite. Identify the knowledge the joke presupposes, then score two things together: how widely held it is and how fast it resolves. Score 10 when the required knowledge is common-schooling level - basic chemistry, basic algebra, well-known scripture, a famous name - AND recognition is instantaneous on reading the punchline. Score 5 when the knowledge is common but recognition takes a beat. Score 0 when the joke requires specialist or regional vocabulary, or a chain of two separate recognitions, such that most readers must reconstruct the reference before they can evaluate it.

[X18] Retelling-chain positioning: repost or remix framing
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the text positions itself somewhere in the life-cycle of a joke the audience has already met: explicit repost/repetition talk, claims of novelty or of starting something new, 'you know the one' presupposition, jokes whose subject is the recycling of jokes, near-verbatim self-repetition inside the item, or a punchline that only lands if the reader already knows a prior version or a stock joke form. 0 = the item presents itself as a self-contained joke with no relation to prior circulation; 10 = its relation to prior circulation is the entire content. Count the presence and prominence of such positioning only. The judge must NOT judge originality, must NOT decide whether the item actually is a repost, and must NOT judge quality.

[X19] Ends on a short final line
Score 0-10 for how short the last line is relative to the rest of the text -- a clipped closing line set off on its own scores high, a final line as long as the body scores low. 10 = the final line is a few words on its own; 0 = there is no distinct short closing line. Count the layout only; do not judge whether the ending works.

[X20] Retrospective Reframing
Use 10 when the ending forces an earlier phrase or image to acquire a surprising second meaning; use 0 when the ending leaves the setup unchanged or predictable.

[X21] Traces of external sourcing rather than local origination
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT of evidence in the text that the material came from somewhere outside this post: pasted URLs or domain names, block quotations with a citation, scripture or book references, SEO/marketing/keyword-stuffed phrasing, attribution of the joke to another speaker or to a named comedian, translation framing ('the original German word for', 'in X language they say'), or wording that reads as transcribed speech or lifted copy rather than written for this post. 0 = the text reads as composed for this posting with no external source markers; 10 = the item is substantially a quotation, link, or lifted copy. Count source markers only. The judge must NOT judge whether the sourcing is legitimate, lazy, or whether the borrowed material is funny.

[X22] Final inferential step left to the reader
Score how much cognitive work the reader must perform after the last word to complete the joke. Score 10 when the text stops one step short and the reader must supply the connection themselves - resolve an unstated homophone, perform a small computation, or notice an absence. Score 5 when the punchline states the joke but does not explain it. Score 0 when the text delivers the payoff and then explains, restates, or spells out the connection, leaving nothing to be discovered. Difficulty is not the criterion; the criterion is whether the last step is transferred to the reader.

[X23] Delivery-format commitment
How much does the item visibly commit to a recognizable delivery format—one-liner, question-answer, dialogue, riddle, narrative build, list, or technical wordplay? Score only the format footprint from 0-10; do not judge how well the format works.

[X24] Era-dating topical and current-events references
Score 0-10 for the EXTENT to which the item is anchored to a datable moment or period by its references: named politicians or public figures in a then-current situation, elections, named news events, calendar moments, internet fads and platform idioms of a particular period (e.g. clickbait formulas, 'one weird trick'), product or brand moments. 0 = the references are timeless or absent; 10 = the item cannot be read without knowing a specific period's news or fads. Count how strongly the text is pinned to a period, NOT whether the reference is apt, fresh, stale, or amusing. The judge must not judge quality and must not guess the actual date.

[X25] Orthographic or phonetic strain in the wordplay
Score how much the wordplay depends on the reader deliberately distorting spelling or pronunciation to make the pun resolve. 10 = the pun exists only under mangled orthography, invented compounding, forced accent, or a spoonerism the reader must mentally re-run (fake foreign compounds, letter-shuffles, email-header puns). 5 = a mild homophone that resolves on ordinary reading. 0 = the humour needs no phonetic manipulation whatsoever. Score the amount of work demanded, not the ingenuity of the result.

[X26] Site-native self-reference that still executes a turn
Composite. First, does the item draw on the culture of the posting forum itself - posting, reposting, karma, commenting, front page, the sub? If not, score 5 and stop. If it does, score whether the in-group material is used as the joke's mechanism rather than as its subject: 10 when the community reference sets up a genuine reversal, self-implication, or unexpected turn that would collapse if the community frame were removed; 0 when the item merely voices a familiar complaint about the community, or asserts community membership, with no turn at all. Insider vocabulary alone earns nothing.

[X27] Meta-Platform and Community References
Score the extent to which the text explicitly mentions online platforms, subreddits, karma, upvotes, reposting, or the act of posting itself. Do not judge if this makes the joke good or bad; score only the presence of community-specific meta-references.

[X28] Transformed taboo material
Score 10 when controversial, crude, or tragic material is transformed into an inventive comic mechanism; score 0 when it functions mainly as shock, insult, or exploitation.

[X29] Textual coherence as a genuine joke vs. fragment/spam
Score 10 if the text reads as a coherent, purposeful joke in fluent English. Score 0 if the text is incoherent, looks like ad copy/spam/keyword-stuffing, or is a fragment that doesn't cohere into any recognizable joke structure.
