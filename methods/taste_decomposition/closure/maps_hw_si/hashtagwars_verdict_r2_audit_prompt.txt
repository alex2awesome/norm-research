You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the tweet is as an entry in that hashtag contest. It is about the comic idea, the
      craft, the wording choices, the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the tweet is as an entry in that hashtag contest: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: reader-submitted one-line tweets entered into a nightly comedy hashtag contest on Twitter. Each item is a tweet.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
how good the tweet is as an entry in that hashtag contest too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Trailing furniture: hashtags, handles, parenthetical byline
Score 0-10 for how much non-sentence furniture is appended at the end of the text -- trailing hashtags, @-handles, a parenthetical name and town, or similar tags. 10 = several such tags appended; 0 = the text ends with the sentence itself. Count the furniture only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X02] Narrative Escalation
Score 10 when an ordinary premise sharply escalates into a disproportionate or unsettling endpoint; score 0 when there is no progression or surprise.

[X03] A specific concrete detail carries the joke
Score 0-10 for whether the comic work is done by a specific, concrete particular (a named thing, an exact number, a precise action) rather than by a generic category word. 10 = one vivid particular is doing the work; 0 = only generic labels, nothing specific enough to picture. Judge the specificity of the comic content, not the length.

[X04] Joke mechanism matches the hashtag's named genre
Score 10 if the specific comedic technique used (e.g. genuine fright/unease for a 'scary story' tag, a plausible-sounding title for a 'movies' tag) is exactly what the contest's named theme calls for, such that the joke would lose its point if pasted into a different hashtag's contest; score 0 if the tweet's content is generic and could be pasted under an unrelated contest hashtag with no loss of sense or effect.

[X05] Pun stacks two or more meanings at once
Score 10 if the tweet's central wordplay simultaneously activates two distinct meanings or reference registers at once (e.g. a title pun that ALSO carries a separate topical/political meaning); score 5 if there is a pun but only a single register of meaning; score 0 if there is no pun or double meaning present.

[X06] Transgressive Integration
Score 10 when taboo or shocking material is integrated into a specific reversal, image, or narrative mechanism; score 0 when shock value is the entire joke.

[X07] Obvious-first-take fingerprint
Score 0-10 for how strongly the text reads as a default, immediately available association to the prompt, using familiar topical ingredients or a conventional premise; judge only those first-association cues, not originality or quality.

[X08] Reliance on franchise scaffolding: sequel numbers, colons, subtitles
Score inversely to how much of the construction is carried by borrowed title furniture. 0-3 = the entry leans on sequel numerals, colons, subtitles, or roman numerals to hold the joke together, so the structure comes pre-supplied by the source and the contributed content is a single grafted word or phrase. 5 = one such device used but the contributed material carries the line. 10 = no borrowed scaffolding; the entry stands as a complete phrase whose shape the writer chose. Judge the presence and load-bearing role of the furniture, not the topic.

[X09] Contest boilerplate load
Score 0-10 for how much of the text consists of contest/platform boilerplate such as @midnight, the contest hashtag, or their repeated placement; judge visible boilerplate only, not whether the entry is good.

[X10] Title Reengineering Depth
Score 10 when a recognizable title is transformed into a coherent, substantive new premise; score 0 when the change is merely a superficial name swap.

[X11] Alliteration, rhyme, or rhythmic sound-play
Score 10 if the tweet uses clear alliteration, internal rhyme, or a marked rhythmic/metrical pattern in its wording, independent of any title pun; score 0 if the wording has no notable sonic patterning.

[X12] Concrete Cultural Texture
Score 10 when specific objects, places, or practices create a vivid, culturally grounded world; score 0 when the entry relies only on vague labels.

[X13] Temporal anchoring
Score 0-10 for the extent of explicit years, numbered installments, current-event references, or time-sensitive cultural anchors; judge only temporal specificity, not whether the reference is timely or good.

[X14] Stereotype Avoidance
Score 10 when the entry approaches the theme through an unusual, non-interchangeable detail; score 0 when it is an expected stereotype bundle that could fit almost any entry.

[X15] Morphological Wordplay
Score 10 when a coined, blended, or altered word creates an immediate layered joke; score 0 when the wording contains no meaningful linguistic transformation.

[X16] Spoken in character versus described from outside
Score how far the entry is an utterance a person from the prompt's world would actually say, rather than a third-party description of such a person. 10 = the line is pure in-character speech, with the speaker's own diction, deixis and attitude carried by the words themselves (a quoted brag, threat, complaint, or aside that presupposes a whole speaker). 5 = a noun phrase with some voiced coloring but no clear speaker. 0 = a flat outside-observer label or field-note about the type, with no one speaking, e.g. a bare descriptive noun phrase an anthropologist could have written. Judge only from the wording: presence of second person, contractions, dialect spelling, interjections and vocatives raise the score; bare nominal inventories lower it. Do not reward mere rudeness.

[X17] Presence of an attached URL or media pointer
Score how much of the item consists of, or points to, material outside the text. 10 = the item carries a t.co or other URL, or otherwise explicitly refers the reader to an attached picture, video or linked page. 5 = a quoted or reported fragment implying an external source but with no link present. 0 = plain text with no link and no pointer. Score only the presence and prominence of the pointer. Do not attempt to judge whether the link helps or hurts the entry, and do not speculate about what is on the other end.

[X18] Proper-noun reference density
Score 0-10 for how much the tweet uses named people, franchises, films, institutions, or other recognizable entities; judge only reference density, not whether the references are effective.

[X19] Taboo-trigger density
Score 0-10 for how much the text invokes sex, drugs, bodily functions, violence, death, or other taboo material; judge taboo presence only, not whether the entry is good.

[X20] Partisan-trigger density
Score 0-10 for how much the text contains explicit partisan, ideological, or politically polarizing cues; judge cue intensity only, not whether the entry is good.

[X21] Reference predictability / top-of-mind obviousness within its category
Score 0-10 for how likely it is that many different people, independently and without seeing each other's answers, would land on the exact same reference the tweet uses (i.e. how canonical/first-guess the referenced title, phrase, or figure is for this prompt category): 0 = an obscure or highly idiosyncratic reference unlikely to be duplicated; 10 = the single most obvious, textbook reference for the category, of the kind many entrants would independently reach for. Score only this predictability, never whether the joke built on it is good.

[X22] Consistent in-character dialect/persona voice
Score 10 if the entire tweet is phrased as a first-person or address-form line in a sustained, distinct social voice/dialect appropriate to the contest's target persona; score 5 if a persona voice is present but inconsistent or thin; score 0 if the tweet is written in neutral, voiceless description with no persona.

[X23] Stock phrase reuse
Score 0-10 for how much the wording resembles a familiar slogan, catchphrase, idiom, or reusable template; judge only recognizability and reuse, not whether the phrase is effective.

[X24] Self-contained text; no link dependency or off-topic tags
Score whether the joke is fully delivered by the visible words, with no reliance on an attached URL, image, or extra hashtags, and no tag clutter beyond the contest hashtag and the host handle. 10 = every element of the humor is recoverable from the text alone and nothing beyond the contest hashtag and host handle is present. 5 = self-contained but carrying one stray extra hashtag or redundant tag. 0 = the line is a caption or setup whose payload evidently sits in a linked image or video, or is buried under additional unrelated hashtags that narrow the audience to a subgroup. Score the dependency, not the topic.

[X25] Extent of capitalised or shouted typography
Score 0-10 for how much of the text is set in capital letters or otherwise typographically shouted (ALL-CAPS words, repeated letters, strings of exclamation marks). 10 = the text is dominated by shouted typography; 0 = ordinary sentence case throughout. Count the typography only; do not judge whether it is good or whether the item is funny.

[X26] Sets up an expectation and then turns it
Score 0-10 for whether the wording first leads the reader toward one reading and then supplies a different one. 10 = a clear setup and a clear turn away from it; 0 = a single flat statement with no setup and nothing turned. Judge the comic structure, not the typography.

[X27] List whose final item swerves against the earlier ones
For entries built as a list or triple, score whether the last item breaks the pattern the earlier items set, retroactively reframing them. 10 = the first items establish a comfortable category and the final item lands in a different register, turning the list into an argument. 5 = a list with a rhythmically satisfying but categorically flat close. 0 = an undifferentiated inventory in which the items are interchangeable and the order does not matter. Entries that are not lists should be scored on whether their final word similarly redirects what came before.

[X28] Extra hashtags beyond the contest tag
Score the extent of hashtag material that is not the contest hashtag. 10 = two or more additional hashtags are present, including tags that address a fan community, a sports allegiance, a personal occasion, or the contest's own scoring culture. 5 = exactly one additional hashtag. 0 = only the contest hashtag appears. Count tags; do not assess whether they are relevant, witty, or annoying, and do not let the joke's content affect the score.

[X29] Surface-Meaning Irony
Score 10 when the literal wording clearly conflicts with an implied comic meaning; score 0 when the statement is straightforward and has no interpretive reversal.
