You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the tweet is as an entry in that hashtag contest. It is about the comic idea, the
      craft, the wording choices, the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the tweet is as an entry in that hashtag contest: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: reader-submitted one-line tweets entered into a nightly comedy hashtag contest on Twitter. Each item is a tweet.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
how good the tweet is as an entry in that hashtag contest too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Implies an off-screen scene with a before and after
Score how much unstated story the line forces the reader to construct. 10 = a short line that unmistakably implies events preceding and following it, so the reader involuntarily pictures the whole scene ('Y'all watch this'; 'Mother visits me, stays FOREVER'; 'Forgot Sunscreen Silly'). 5 = a situation is evoked but static, with no implied consequence. 0 = a static attribute, ingredient list, or label with no temporal extent at all ('Corn syrup and salt'). Judge implied narrative, not explicit narration; explicitly narrating the whole event should score LOW on this criterion, not high.

[X02] Orthographic irregularity
Score 0-10 for the extent of misspellings, grammatical anomalies, malformed wording, or unusual encoding artifacts; judge only visible irregularity, not whether the text is good.

[X03] Raw length of the content span excluding tags
Measure the EXTENT of text once handles, hashtags and URLs are stripped. Score inversely by length: 10 = very short, roughly three words or under twenty characters. 5 = moderate, roughly five to seven words. 0 = long, roughly eight or more words or over forty-five characters. Count only; do not read for meaning. Do NOT judge whether the length is appropriate to the prompt, whether the entry is tight or padded, or whether brevity suits the joke. This channel is a ruler, not a critic.

[X04] Hashtag-specific reframing
Score 10 when the prompt is reinterpreted through an unexpected, tightly fitting premise; score 0 when the response is a literal or generic association.

[X05] Stops on the beat: no explanation, parenthetical, or hedging second joke
Score restraint after the punchline. 10 = the entry ends exactly at the funny word, with nothing appended ('Tell you what.'; 'Up the Holler'). 5 = a slight tail that does no damage. 0 = the joke is followed by material that explains it, insures it, or adds a second attempt — a clarifying parenthetical ('16 Angry Men (and one woman)'), an appended tag supplying the missing context ('...#STLCards'), a link, or a redundant final clause. Judge only what follows the punchline.

[X06] Prompt-language echo
Score 0-10 for how much the substantive text repeats or closely evokes the contest prompt's wording or semantic frame, excluding the required hashtag and handle; judge only presence, never whether the reuse is good.

[X07] Contest-wrapper arrangement
Score 0-10 for how much the required contest hashtag and @midnight handle appear in a standardized, boilerplate-like order, spacing, or adjacency pattern; judge only formatting extent, never whether the arrangement is good.

[X08] Emphasis typography
Score 0-10 for the extent of all-caps words, conspicuous capitalization, repeated punctuation, or emphatic typographic styling; judge only amount, never whether the emphasis is good.

[X09] Sets up an expectation and then turns it
Score 0-10 for whether the wording first leads the reader toward one reading and then supplies a different one. 10 = a clear setup and a clear turn away from it; 0 = a single flat statement with no setup and nothing turned. Judge the comic structure, not the typography.

[X10] Information density
Give 10 when nearly every word supplies necessary comic context or escalation; give 0 when wording is padded, redundant, or too underdeveloped to support a premise.

[X11] Well-formed utterance versus bare noun-pile filling the word count
Score whether the entry is a syntactically complete unit of language or a stack of juxtaposed nouns that merely reaches the required word count. 10 = a grammatical clause or idiomatic phrase with a verb, an imperative, or a genuine predication ('Married My Truck'; 'But we're we really?'; 'Dog: My Copilot'). 5 = an elliptical but idiomatic noun phrase that reads as natural English ('Road kill buffet'). 0 = two or three unconnected nouns concatenated with no syntax and no idiom, readable only as a keyword list ('raccoon penis toothpick'; 'Rodeo Baby Conception'). Do not reward or penalise topic.

[X12] Taboo-content density
Score 0-10 for the extent of sexual, incestuous, bodily, or otherwise taboo vocabulary; judge only how much taboo material is present, not whether it is funny, effective, or appropriate.

[X13] Reference-stacking clutter
Score 10 when multiple names or borrowed references crowd out a single coherent premise; score 0 when every reference is necessary and mutually reinforces one joke.

[X14] Graphic/visceral taboo imagery
Score 10 if the entry describes a taboo, transgressive, or gross-out subject with vivid, specific, sensory physical detail (texture, body part, act). Score 0 if any taboo content present is only referenced abstractly or euphemistically with no sensory specificity.

[X15] A specific concrete detail carries the joke
Score 0-10 for whether the comic work is done by a specific, concrete particular (a named thing, an exact number, a precise action) rather than by a generic category word. 10 = one vivid particular is doing the work; 0 = only generic labels, nothing specific enough to picture. Judge the specificity of the comic content, not the length.

[X16] Idiomatic repurposing
Give 10 when a familiar conversational phrase is naturally redirected toward the hashtag premise; give 0 when wording is literal, stiff, or merely clichéd.

[X17] Concrete visuality
Give 10 when the wording instantly creates a vivid, specific physical scene; give 0 when it remains abstract, generic, or visually ungrounded.

[X18] Political-figure/current-event audacity
Score 10 if the tweet directly names a real, currently contested political figure, party, or event and stakes out a blunt, unhedged opinion about it. Score 0 if the tweet avoids all real-world political specificity and controversy.

[X19] Depth beyond title substitution
Score 10 when a borrowed title structure is transformed into a specific new situation through several interacting changes; score 0 when it is mostly a single obvious word replacement.

[X20] Franchise-fatigue / sequel-exhaustion meta-joke
Score 10 if the joke's content specifically comments on a franchise, series, or format being over-extended, running out of ideas, or reaching an absurd Nth installment. Score 0 if the entry contains no commentary on repetition, sequels, or franchise longevity.

[X21] Boilerplate tag placed before vs. after the punchline
Score how much of the required boilerplate (the @midnight handle and/or the contest hashtag) appears BEFORE the joke's content words rather than after. 10 = all boilerplate (handle and hashtag) precedes the punchline text; 0 = all boilerplate follows the punchline text, which appears first. This is an extent measure of ordering only -- do not judge whether the entry is funny or well-written.

[X22] Stereotype-list dependence
Score 10 when familiar identity markers are merely accumulated to signal a type; score 0 when each element creates a distinct premise, contrast, or turn.

[X23] Trailing furniture: hashtags, handles, parenthetical byline
Score 0-10 for how much non-sentence furniture is appended at the end of the text -- trailing hashtags, @-handles, a parenthetical name and town, or similar tags. 10 = several such tags appended; 0 = the text ends with the sentence itself. Count the furniture only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X24] Non-authored mechanical residue: whitespace runs, entities, shortened URLs
Score the EXTENT of tokens and spacing that no author would deliberately type as part of a joke. Count: runs of two or more consecutive spaces; HTML entities appearing as literal text ('&amp;'); t.co or other shortened URLs; stray leading/trailing spacing; any encoding debris. 10 = several such artifacts present. 5 = exactly one mild artifact, such as a single double-space. 0 = clean single-spaced text with no entities or links. Score presence and quantity only. Do NOT treat artifacts as sloppiness or as evidence of care; this channel is a measure of transport residue, not of the writer.

[X25] Trailing or leading single-word ALL-CAPS emphasis
Score how much the entry uses a single word rendered in ALL CAPS purely for emphasis (not an acronym). 10 = a clear emphasis word in full caps is present; 0 = no capitalized emphasis word anywhere. Do not judge whether the emphasis is effective or funny.

[X26] Expectation reversal
Give 10 when the ending sharply overturns the interpretation established at the beginning; give 0 when the line has no meaningful turn.

[X27] Named-reference density
Score 0-10 for the amount of recognizable proper names, films, franchises, celebrities, politicians, teams, or other external cultural references; judge only density, never whether the references are good.

[X28] Shock independence
Give 10 when the joke remains substantially funny after its taboo or grossest term is mentally removed; give 0 when provocation is essentially the entire mechanism.

[X29] Extent of capitalised or shouted typography
Score 0-10 for how much of the text is set in capital letters or otherwise typographically shouted (ALL-CAPS words, repeated letters, strings of exclamation marks). 10 = the text is dominated by shouted typography; 0 = ordinary sentence case throughout. Count the typography only; do not judge whether it is good or whether the item is funny.
