You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the tweet is as an entry in that hashtag contest. It is about the comic idea, the
      craft, the wording choices, the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the tweet is as an entry in that hashtag contest: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: reader-submitted one-line tweets entered into a nightly comedy hashtag contest on Twitter. Each item is a tweet.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
how good the tweet is as an entry in that hashtag contest too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Specific situation brought into view, typography disregarded
Read the item together with the contest prompt it was written for. Score 0-10 for how sharply one particular situation comes into view: identifiable actors, a place, an action, an object, or a moment that could not be swapped for a generic stand-in. Judge only what the words denote here, in this context. A 10 is an item where a reader instantly sees one specific scene or gesture, even if the item is five plain lowercase words with no handles, no hashtags and no punctuation. A 0 is an item that stays abstract, categorical or label-like, naming a topic without putting anything in view, even if it is long, heavily capitalised and crowded with handles and hashtags. Do not reward length, uppercase letters, exclamation marks, emoji, handles or hashtags, and do not treat a longer item as more specific for being longer. Piled-up adjectives or bare brand names with no situation behind them score low.

[X02] Count of handles, hashtags, and repeated topic tokens
This is a counting question, not a quality question. Score 0-10 for how much tag and keyword furniture the item carries: count at-handles, count hash-tagged tokens including any repetition of the contest tag, and count verbatim restatements of the contest topic wording inside the body text. 10 means three or more such tokens, or the topic wording repeated more than once. 5 means one or two such tokens. 0 means none beyond the single contest tag the item was entered under. Do not consider whether the tagging is apt, whether the repetition is funny, or whether the item is any good. Report the count as an extent only.

[X03] Extent of uppercase and emphatic typography
This is an estimation question, not a quality question. Score 0-10 for how much of the item is set in emphatic typography: estimate the share of alphabetic characters that are uppercase, and count all-caps words, stretched or repeated letters, and runs of exclamation or question marks. 10 means most of the item is in capitals or otherwise shouted. 5 means one or two words in capitals, or a scattering of emphatic punctuation. 0 means ordinary sentence casing or all lowercase with no emphatic marks. Do not consider whether the item is funny, whether the emphasis is earned, or whether shouting suits the joke. Report extent only.

[X04] List/enumeration structure with a pattern-breaking final item
Score the presence and effectiveness of a short list (2+ items) where the final item breaks the pattern set by the earlier items, producing a twist. A 10: a clean list whose last item clearly subverts or undercuts the expectation set by the prior items. A 0: no list structure, or a list whose items are flat, interchangeable restatements with no capstone twist.

[X05] Self-contained delivery
Give 10 when the complete comic premise and payoff are present in the text alone; give 0 when understanding depends on an unavailable link, image, or unstated context.

[X06] Doubled/extra whitespace between words, tag, or handle
Score the extent of extra/doubled whitespace (two or more consecutive space characters) appearing between the tweet's words, hashtag, or handle. 0 = no doubled whitespace anywhere in the text; 10 = multiple instances of doubled whitespace throughout the text. Score only the presence of this typographic/formatting artifact -- do NOT judge whether it affects readability.

[X07] Rhythm and sound pattern when said aloud, plain text
Say the item aloud in your head, in the plain neutral voice its words would have if they were typed in ordinary lowercase with no tags. Score 0-10 for whether its stress pattern, syllable rhythm, repetition, rhyme, alliteration or parallel construction make the line satisfying to speak, with the last stressed word falling where the phrasing wants it. A 10 is a line whose cadence is clean and deliberate and whose ending lands, even when it is short, entirely lowercase and unpunctuated. A 0 is a line that is sonically flat or that stumbles: clogged consonant runs, a limp trailing clause, or wording that has to be re-read before it can be spoken. Judge the sound of the words only. Do not reward capital letters, repeated exclamation marks, stretched spellings, emoji, handles or hashtags, and do not treat a longer line as more rhythmic; strip that furniture away before you listen to the line.

[X08] Topic tied to a named behavior, contradiction, or institution
Read the item with the contest prompt shown beside it. Score 0-10 for whether the item does something with the topic rather than only naming it: does it identify a specific behavior people actually perform, a concrete contradiction or rule collision, or a named institution, role or procedure, such that the joke depends on that mechanism and would collapse if the mechanism were removed? A 10 is an item where one concrete behavior, contradiction or institutional detail carries the line, even if the item is short, lowercase and carries no tags beyond the contest hashtag. A 0 is an item that only sets topical keywords, brand names or category labels beside one another, or that would read identically under a different topic, even if it is long, capitalised and tag-dense. Do not reward the number of topical words, hashtags or handles, and do not treat repetition of the topic wording as engagement with the topic.

[X09] Ironic Premise Reversal
Score 10 when the literal wording turns back on itself to expose a clear comic contradiction; score 0 when the statement is entirely literal and unreversed.

[X10] A specific concrete detail carries the joke
Score 0-10 for whether the comic work is done by a specific, concrete particular (a named thing, an exact number, a precise action) rather than by a generic category word. 10 = one vivid particular is doing the work; 0 = only generic labels, nothing specific enough to picture. Judge the specificity of the comic content, not the length.

[X11] Lands on first read without puzzle-decoding
Score the reading latency to the laugh. 10 = the joke resolves in one pass, at the speed of scrolling. 5 = a brief double-take that pays off. 0 = the reader must hold two source titles in mind, reverse a word order, or reconstruct a hidden phrase before anything is funny; the line reads as a crossword clue.

[X12] Constraint-Native Phrasing
Score 10 when the requested word-count format feels deliberately exploited as part of a natural, complete phrase; score 0 when the wording feels truncated, padded, or accidentally shaped by the constraint.

[X13] Punchline Closure
Score 10 when the final word or phrase delivers a complete, decisive comic payoff; score 0 when the line stops at setup, ambiguity, or an unfinished thought.

[X14] Bare shortened link indicating an unseen attached image
Score the extent to which the tweet points at content you cannot see. 10 = a t.co or other shortened URL is present and the remaining text does not itself complete a joke, so the payload evidently lives in an attached photo or linked page. 5 = a URL is present but the text stands on its own. 0 = no URL at all. Report only the presence of the pointer and how much of the payload appears to be missing from the text. Do NOT infer that the missing content is either funny or unfunny, and do NOT judge whether linking is good practice.

[X15] External-artifact residue
Score 0-10 for how much the text contains a URL, attachment cue, quoted copied wording, or other platform artifact; judge only the artifact's presence, never whether it improves the entry.

[X16] Contest hashtag casing fidelity to the canonical form
Compare the hashtag as written in the tweet against the canonical CamelCase form given in the CONTEST HASHTAG header. 10 = the casing deviates (all-lowercase, ALL-CAPS, or a partial-case hybrid). 5 = casing matches but spacing or placement of the tag is unusual. 0 = the tag is reproduced in exactly the canonical casing. Report the deviation only. Do NOT judge whether deviant casing looks careless or whether canonical casing looks polished.

[X17] Trailing furniture: hashtags, handles, parenthetical byline
Score 0-10 for how much non-sentence furniture is appended at the end of the text -- trailing hashtags, @-handles, a parenthetical name and town, or similar tags. 10 = several such tags appended; 0 = the text ends with the sentence itself. Count the furniture only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X18] Sets up an expectation and then turns it
Score 0-10 for whether the wording first leads the reader toward one reading and then supplies a different one. 10 = a clear setup and a clear turn away from it; 0 = a single flat statement with no setup and nothing turned. Judge the comic structure, not the typography.

[X19] Unexpected Semantic Collision
Score 10 when two unlike concepts collide unexpectedly yet form an immediately intelligible joke; score 0 when the elements remain unrelated or predictable.

[X20] Tweet opens with the @midnight mention as first token
Score how much the tweet's opening literally begins with the '@midnight' mention/handle before any other content. 0 = @midnight does not appear at the very start of the text; 10 = the text opens with '@midnight' as its first token. This is an extent measure of a formatting/positional convention only -- do NOT judge whether opening with the handle is a good or bad choice.

[X21] Concrete Visualizability
Score 10 when the tweet evokes a vivid, specific scene that can be pictured instantly; score 0 when it remains vague, abstract, or generic.

[X22] Off-contest metadata load: promo tags, self-reference, show flattery
Score how much of the tweet is doing work other than answering the prompt. 10 = additional hashtags unrelated to the contest, self-branding, or text whose subject is the author's own relationship to the show rather than the prompt. 5 = one small off-prompt element. 0 = nothing but the answer plus the required tag and handle. Count the off-prompt material. Do NOT judge whether self-promotion is tacky or whether a clean entry is admirable.

[X23] Title transformation
Give 10 when a recognizable title is altered into a fresh, coherent comic premise; give 0 when the title is merely copied or the reference is unintelligible.

[X24] Transformation depth beyond a one-token swap
For title/name mashups and puns, score how much idea the substitution adds beyond naming its source. 10 = the swapped element carries a genuine second thought, so the line would still be funny to someone who half-recognizes the source. 5 = a clean substitution that produces one modest joke. 0 = a single letter or word is changed and the entire payload is source recognition, with no observation attached.

[X25] Raw word count of the joke content
Score the raw word count of the tweet's joke content, excluding the '@midnight' mention and hashtag(s), mapped to a 0-10 extent scale (0 = 2 words or fewer, 10 = 8 words or more). Score only the count of words present -- do NOT judge whether the length is appropriate or effective.

[X26] Extent of textual bulk and tag furniture
This is a counting question, not a quality question. Score 0-10 for how much mechanical text and tag furniture the item carries, estimating from the raw string: its overall character length, and the number of at-handles and hash-tagged tokens it contains. 10 means the item runs near the maximum length the platform allows and carries several handles or hashtags. 5 means a mid-length item with about one handle or extra hashtag. 0 means a very short item with no handles and no hashtags beyond the single contest tag it was entered under. Do not consider whether the item is funny, vivid or well written, and do not decide whether the length and tagging help or hurt it. Report extent only: a bloated item and a well-used long item of the same size receive the same score.

[X27] Memorable Cadence
Score 10 when rhythm, repetition, or sound makes the line especially punchy when read aloud; score 0 when its cadence is inert, clumsy, or forgettable.

[X28] Borrowed stock phrase bent, not merely quoted
Applies when the line imports a recognizable pre-existing phrase (lyric, hymn, slogan, bumper sticker, catchphrase). Score how much work the import does beyond recognition. 10 = the borrowed phrase is bent, recontextualized, or turned so the old frame says something new in this contest's world. 5 = lightly repurposed but the meaning is unchanged. 0 = reproduced verbatim as a quotation with no alteration and no new angle, or no borrowed phrase is present and none is needed (score 5 in that case and note it).

[X29] Extent of capitalised or shouted typography
Score 0-10 for how much of the text is set in capital letters or otherwise typographically shouted (ALL-CAPS words, repeated letters, strings of exclamation marks). 10 = the text is dominated by shouted typography; 0 = ordinary sentence case throughout. Count the typography only; do not judge whether it is good or whether the item is funny.
