You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the entry is as an entry in that week's contest. It is about the comic idea, the
      craft, the wording choices, the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the entry is as an entry in that week's contest: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: reader-submitted entries to a weekly newspaper humour contest (the Washington Post Style Invitational). Each item is a contest entry.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
how good the entry is as an entry in that week's contest too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Engages the prompt's specific conceit, not just its category
Read the qualifying language in the prompt - the adjective, the twist, the stated purpose - and score whether the entry answers that qualifier or only the broad category. Score 10 when the entry could only have been written for this exact prompt because it turns on the qualifier itself. Score 5 when the entry fits the category well and glances at the qualifier. Score 0 when the entry satisfies the category generically and would serve equally well as a submission to a differently-qualified version of the same prompt.

[X02] Truncation residue: fragment, dangling colon, mid-sentence stop
Score how much the text reads as a cut-off or partial capture rather than a complete submission. Score 10 when it stops mid-sentence or mid-phrase, ends on a bare colon or dash, or consists solely of a heading, a title, a category label, a lead-in, or a quoted stimulus with no response attached. Score 5 when it is grammatically complete but conspicuously missing a component the prompt names. Score 0 when the text is a whole, self-contained submission. Score the extent of the truncation fingerprint only. You must NOT judge whether the fragment is nonetheless witty.

[X03] Full multi-line structural completion, not a fragment
Score the degree to which the entry executes a COMPLETE multi-part form (e.g. all five limerick lines with consistent meter and AABBA rhyme, or a full narrative arc) rather than delivering only a title, a fragment, or a single clever line. 10 = every structural unit the invoked form requires is present and internally consistent. 0 = entry is a title only, a sentence fragment, or otherwise stops well short of the form it invokes.

[X04] Reference as comic machinery
Score 10 when a required famous person or institution actively generates the joke through a distinctive association; score 0 when the reference is only an attached name-drop.

[X05] High semantic compression
Score 10 when every word contributes a distinct layer of premise, reference, or punchline and the entry remains immediately interpretable; score 0 when brevity produces genericness or incompleteness.

[X06] Self-contained logical or lexical riddle operation
Score how cleanly the entry works as a self-contained riddle -- a precise logical inversion, category trick, or lexical operation performed on the prompt's own constraint -- that resolves without requiring any outside cultural knowledge. 10 = an elegant, symmetric logical or lexical twist a reader can verify using only the prompt and the entry text. 0 = no logical or lexical operation is present; the entry is a flat statement or requires outside facts to make sense.

[X07] Entry text is complete, not cut off mid-thought
Score whether the entry reads as a self-contained, finished unit of text versus appearing to break off before a clause, sentence, or thought is finished. 10 = entry is unambiguously complete as given. 0 = entry visibly stops mid-sentence or mid-clause with no closing punctuation resolving the thought.

[X08] A specific concrete detail carries the joke
Score 0-10 for whether the comic work is done by a specific, concrete particular (a named thing, an exact number, a precise action) rather than by a generic category word. 10 = one vivid particular is doing the work; 0 = only generic labels, nothing specific enough to picture. Judge the specificity of the comic content, not the length.

[X09] Non-Arbitrary Concept Bridge
Score 10 when disparate ideas are joined by one discoverable, exact relationship; score 0 when they are merely placed together for surreal or random effect.

[X10] In-text irregularity reads as clearly intentional, not noise
Score how unambiguously a spelling or grammar irregularity in the entry is signaled as INTENTIONAL and load-bearing for the joke, as opposed to being indistinguishable from an accidental typo or a transcription/formatting artifact. 10 = impossible to read the irregularity as anything but a deliberate joke given the surrounding context. 0 = the irregularity could easily be mistaken for noise, with nothing signaling it is meant as humor.

[X11] Trailing furniture: hashtags, handles, parenthetical byline
Score 0-10 for how much non-sentence furniture is appended at the end of the text -- trailing hashtags, @-handles, a parenthetical name and town, or similar tags. 10 = several such tags appended; 0 = the text ends with the sentence itself. Count the furniture only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X12] Title-only compression
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the entry is a very short title, slogan, label, or noun phrase without explanation or elaboration. Do not judge whether the brevity is clever or appropriate.

[X13] Entry breaks frame to comment on itself or the contest
Score how much the entry steps outside its ostensible task to comment on its own quality, on the contest, or on the act of judging or submitting, with that self-aware commentary itself constituting the joke. 10 = the entry is substantially or entirely meta-commentary that succeeds as humor about the contest or joke-making process itself. 0 = the entry stays entirely within the fiction of the prompt with no acknowledgment of the contest or of itself as an entry.

[X14] Prompt-Form Fusion
Score 10 when the required form actively creates the joke’s timing, meaning, or payoff; score 0 when the same joke would work unchanged in any format.

[X15] Extent of capitalised or shouted typography
Score 0-10 for how much of the text is set in capital letters or otherwise typographically shouted (ALL-CAPS words, repeated letters, strings of exclamation marks). 10 = the text is dominated by shouted typography; 0 = ordinary sentence case throughout. Count the typography only; do not judge whether it is good or whether the item is funny.

[X16] Convergent attribution: multiple independent entrants credited
Score the extent to which the attribution indicates that more than one person submitted this same item independently. Score 0 when a single entrant or none is named, 5 when two names appear or the word 'also' introduces a second submitter, and 10 when three or more separate name-and-city entries are listed. Score the visible multiplicity of credited submitters only. You must NOT infer or judge anything about the entry's merit from how many people are credited.

[X17] Orthographic and encoding anomalies
Score 0-10 for the extent of misspellings, malformed characters, unusual transliterations, missing accents, or visibly corrupted typography. Do not judge whether the anomalies help or hurt the entry.

[X18] Late-riff residue
Score 0-10 for the extent to which the entry shows a second-order, self-referential, defensive, or residual riff that reads as reacting to already-obvious approaches. Judge only the textual signs; do not infer actual submission order or judge originality.

[X19] Trailing parenthetical attribution block present and long
Score how much trailing attribution apparatus appears at the end of the entry: a parenthetical containing personal names and place names. Score 0 when there is none, 3 when a single name-and-city appears, 6 when a name-and-city plus a second locality or an out-of-state marker appears, and 10 when the parenthetical runs to multiple full names with cities. Score only the presence and size of this block. You must NOT judge whether attribution is desirable, nor let the block's contents influence your reading of the joke itself.

[X20] Adverbial Semantic Snap
Score 10 when a quoted statement and its manner adverb are both natural yet jointly expose a second meaning; score 0 when the adverb is merely decorative.

[X21] Entry's own form embodies or enacts its content
Score whether the entry's linguistic form demonstrates or contradicts the very property it names or describes (self-reference), rather than merely describing that property from outside. 10 = the form is inseparable from the content -- the entry could not make its point without exhibiting the property itself. 0 = form and content are unrelated; the entry simply states something without enacting it.

[X22] First-obvious-reading of the prompt versus late-arriving riff
MODE 3 - position fingerprint. Score how much the entry reads like the first thing a competent person would produce on reading the prompt, as opposed to something written by someone who has already exhausted the obvious takes. Score 0 for the most available response: a single stock phrase with one letter or syllable altered, a dictionary-level pairing, the canonical example of the category. Score 5 for a response requiring one non-obvious pivot. Score 10 for a response that argues with the prompt, answers a different question than the one asked, addresses the contest itself, or presupposes that the obvious takes are already used up. Score availability-of-the-idea only. You must NOT judge whether obvious or late responses are better; both ends can be excellent or terrible.

[X23] Construction Beyond Shock
Score 10 when crude, dark, or offensive material is transformed by exact wordplay, irony, or targeting; score 0 when shock or offensiveness is the only substantial mechanism.

[X24] Submission-template residue
Score 0-10 for the extent of headings, labels, prompt restatements, answer markers, quote numbers, colons, or unfinished-looking scaffolding. Do not judge whether the formatting is appropriate.

[X25] Raw word/token length of the entry text
Count or estimate the total number of words in the ENTRY text only, excluding the attribution parenthetical. Score 0 = extremely short (a title, a single phrase, under about 5 words) up to 10 = long, multi-sentence text (a full poem, paragraph, or multi-clause joke). This is a pure length measurement -- do not judge whether the length is appropriate or whether the entry is good.

[X26] Density of early-1990s news-cycle proper nouns
Score how many references to people, institutions or events that were prominent in the news of a narrow period appear in the entry. Count named public figures, current officeholders, athletes, litigants, scandals and product names. Score 0 for none, 4 for one, 7 for two, 10 for three or more; add nothing for how apt the reference is. Score topical-name density only. You must NOT judge whether the reference is funny, well chosen, or recognisable to you.

[X27] Sets up an expectation and then turns it
Score 0-10 for whether the wording first leads the reader toward one reading and then supplies a different one. 10 = a clear setup and a clear turn away from it; 0 = a single flat statement with no setup and nothing turned. Judge the comic structure, not the typography.

[X28] Stable false logic
Score 10 when an apparently sensible statement survives an initial reading but collapses under a precise, internally coherent contradiction; score 0 when it is simply nonsensical or conventionally paradoxical.

[X29] Exact two-reading wordplay
Score 10 when the altered word or phrase supports two clear, related readings and the change is minimal but consequential; score 0 when it is merely a misspelling, loose homophone, or one-step pun.

{"verdicts": [
