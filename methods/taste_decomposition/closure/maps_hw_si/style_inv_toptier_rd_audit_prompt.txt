You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about how good the entry is as an entry in that week's contest. It is about the comic idea, the
      craft, the wording choices, the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself how good the entry is as an entry in that week's contest: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: reader-submitted entries to a weekly newspaper humour contest (the Washington Post Style Invitational). Each item is a contest entry.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
how good the entry is as an entry in that week's contest too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Sheer quantity of text and punctuation marks supplied
Estimate EXTENT only. Score 0-10 for how much written material the entry puts on the page: total characters and words, plus the number of punctuation marks (commas, dashes, colons, semicolons, parentheses, quotation marks, ellipses, exclamation and question marks) relative to that length. 10 = a long entry, many sentences or a dense multi-clause construction, heavily punctuated throughout. 0 = a very short entry, a handful of words, at most one terminal mark. 5 = one ordinary sentence with ordinary punctuation. Do NOT decide whether this amount of text or punctuation is good, appropriate, clear, or excessive; do not let the entry being funny, clear, or confusing move this number. You are counting, not evaluating.

[X02] Extent of capitalised or shouted typography
Score 0-10 for how much of the text is set in capital letters or otherwise typographically shouted (ALL-CAPS words, repeated letters, strings of exclamation marks). 10 = the text is dominated by shouted typography; 0 = ordinary sentence case throughout. Count the typography only; do not judge whether it is good or whether the item is funny.

[X03] Sets up an expectation and then turns it
Score 0-10 for whether the wording first leads the reader toward one reading and then supplies a different one. 10 = a clear setup and a clear turn away from it; 0 = a single flat statement with no setup and nothing turned. Judge the comic structure, not the typography.

[X04] Volume of explanatory scaffolding words and connective marks
Estimate EXTENT only. Score 0-10 for how much scaffolding apparatus is physically present: word count and character count devoted to stating, quoting, or glossing a source, plus the count of connective and pointing marks (colons, arrows, dashes, parentheses, quotation marks, equals signs, slashes, ellipses) relative to the entry's length. 10 = an entry with a quoted or labelled source, glosses, and many such marks in a long text. 0 = a short entry with none of this apparatus and at most a terminal mark. Do NOT judge whether the scaffolding is needed, helpful, clumsy, or excessive; do not let the transformation's actual legibility move this number. Count apparatus and length only.

[X05] Follows accreted house conventions of this contest form
Score 0-10 for how closely the entry conforms to settled, form-specific conventions of this contest series rather than answering the prompt in a generic way: an established answer template or fixed grammatical shape, standard tag or byline conventions, house-typical formatting habits for this kind of round, a stock closing move, or the idiom the form has settled into. 10 = the entry is built entirely on such conventions and would look out of place in any other venue. 0 = the entry follows no venue-specific convention and could have been written for any humour prompt anywhere. This is NOT a quality judgement: convention-following is neither skilled nor unskilled here; do not let execution, freshness, or humour move this number.

[X06] Topical reference names a specific contradiction or consequence
Score 0-10 for how specifically the entry's topical or public-affairs reference identifies a contradiction, a consequence, or a hypocrisy, rather than merely invoking a current event, institution, or public figure. 10 = the entry pins down a particular tension - this actor said or did X while Y - so the reference does argumentative work; plain everyday vocabulary and short simple sentences are entirely compatible with 10. 5 = the reference is apt but the criticism it carries is familiar or general. 0 = the topical element functions as name-dropping only, or is absent, even if the entry uses long Latinate vocabulary, difficult prose, and heavy punctuation. You must NOT reward long or unusual words, hard-to-read or complex sentences, dense punctuation, or an air of sophistication; simple wording is not less biting, and difficult wording is not more biting.

[X07] Reads as a riff arriving after the obvious takes are gone
Score 0-10 for how strongly the entry reads as written by someone who already knows the near-field responses to this prompt have been used: it deliberately swerves from the available reading, inverts or comments on the expected answer, stacks a second constraint on top of the prompt's one, or works a corner of the source material a first-pass reader would skip. 10 = the entry is legible mainly as a move AWAY from an assumed default response. 0 = the entry shows no awareness that any other response exists and simply answers the prompt. This is NOT a quality judgement; swerving is not better or worse than answering directly, and you must not let cleverness, wit, or appeal move this number.

[X08] Reads as a first, most obvious response to the prompt
Read the contest prompt, then the entry. Score 0-10 for how strongly the entry reads like one of the first responses that would occur to almost anyone holding that prompt: the nearest source word, the most available public figure, the most immediate rhyme or letter-swap, the reading the prompt itself all but names. 10 = you would expect a large fraction of entrants to land on essentially this move independently. 0 = the move is remote enough that arriving at it requires having searched past the near field. This is NOT a quality judgement: an obvious move may be executed superbly and a remote move may fall flat; do not let execution, wit, polish, or your enjoyment move this number. Estimate only how near the front of the search space the entry's move sits.

[X09] Intended comic relation recoverable on one careful reading
Read the contest prompt and then the entry once, carefully. Score 0-10 for how completely the intended comic relation between prompt and entry can be reconstructed from what is on the page, without you supplying a missing referent, a missing logical step, or a repaired reading. 10 = every referent is anchored and the relation between source material and comic result lands on the first pass, even if the entry is three words long, has no punctuation beyond a period, and is typed plainly. 0 = you must guess at a referent or invent a connecting step before any comic relation exists, even if the entry is long, elaborately punctuated and immaculately typed. You must NOT award points for length, for amount of explanation, for punctuation, for capitalisation, or for tidy typography; extra words that restate rather than anchor add nothing. If the entry is longer, hold it to the same standard as a short one - do not credit thoroughness. Score only what a reader of that week's prompt could grasp unaided.

[X10] Quantity of citation marks, capitals, and surrounding text
Estimate EXTENT only. Score 0-10 for how much reference-flagging apparatus and text the entry contains: number of quotation marks, apostrophes, parentheses, colons, dashes and other marks per unit of text; the proportion of capitalised words and any all-caps emphasis; and the overall character count of the entry. 10 = a long entry with frequent marks and many capitalised or all-caps words. 0 = a short, all-lowercase or minimally capitalised entry with no marks. Do NOT judge whether any target is actually recognisable, nor whether the marking is appropriate or overdone; count the apparatus and the length only.

[X11] Control of grammar and phrasing in service of the joke
Score 0-10 for whether the entry's grammar, word choice, and phrasing are under the writer's control, judged IN CONTEXT: a deviation from standard usage scores high when it clearly serves voice, form, dialect, or wordplay, and low only when it looks unintended and obscures or weakens the joke. 10 = every choice reads as chosen - including ungrammatical, misspelled, run-on, unpunctuated, or all-lowercase choices when the comic effect requires them; a plainly typed fragment with no punctuation at all can score 10. 5 = minor awkwardness that does not block the joke. 0 = clumsy or erroneous construction that materially obscures the joke, even in an entry with immaculate punctuation, correct capitalisation, and tidy typography. You must NOT score punctuation volume, correctness of typography, capitalisation, or length as such; a heavily punctuated, typographically clean entry with muddled syntax scores low, and a bare unpunctuated entry with exact phrasing scores high.

[X12] Intended target identifiable from the wording without repair
Score 0-10 for whether the saying, name, work, institution, object, or social type the entry is playing on can be identified from the wording as given, without the reader repairing a malformed version or guessing among several candidates. 10 = the target is unmistakable from the words present, including when the entry is a short bare phrase with no quotation marks, no capitalisation cues, and no punctuation - a deliberately mangled version still counts as 10 if the original is unmistakable through the mangling. 5 = the target is recoverable but vague, obscure, or so distorted that identification takes effort. 0 = identifying the target requires a guess the text does not support, even when the entry is long and studded with quotation marks, capitals, and other apparatus. If the entry attempts no reference, parody, or target-based joke, score 0. You must NOT reward the presence of quotation marks, capitalisation, italic or emphasis markers, length, or typographic tidiness; marking something as a reference is not the same as making it recognisable.

[X13] Trailing furniture: hashtags, handles, parenthetical byline
Score 0-10 for how much non-sentence furniture is appended at the end of the text -- trailing hashtags, @-handles, a parenthetical name and town, or similar tags. 10 = several such tags appended; 0 = the text ends with the sentence itself. Count the furniture only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.

[X14] Path from source to comic result is legible unaided
Score 0-10 for how clearly a reader can trace the route from the source word, phrase, name, convention, or premise to the comic result, WITHOUT being told the route and without inventing an intermediate step. 10 = the source is identifiable and each alteration step follows from the last, so the reader reconstructs the derivation immediately; a bare short entry with no connectives can score 10 if the source shines through the result. 5 = the transformation is visible but one step is strained or unexplained. 0 = the claimed result bears no legible relation to any identifiable source, however long the entry or however many arrows, colons, parentheses, or quotation marks it uses to gesture at one. You must NOT reward the PRESENCE of explanatory scaffolding - stated sources, glosses, connectives, punctuation, capitalisation, or length. Spelling out the derivation is not the same as the derivation being legible; if the entry has to explain itself, that is not extra credit. If no transformation is attempted, score 0.

[X15] Presupposes a shared running joke among regulars
Score 0-10 for how much the entry presupposes in-group knowledge shared by habitual participants in this contest: a recurring bit, a stock character or persona, a catchphrase, a house obsession, a callback to earlier rounds, or an insider address to the contest community, all used without being explained. 10 = the entry is largely unintelligible or flat to a first-time reader because it leans on a running joke it never sets up. 0 = the entry assumes nothing beyond the printed prompt and general public knowledge. This is NOT a quality judgement: insider entries are not better or worse than self-contained ones; do not let humour, appeal, or clarity move this number. Estimate only the amount of unexplained in-group presupposition.

[X16] Stakes out a narrow, unclaimed sliver of the prompt material
Score 0-10 for how narrow the slice of the prompt's material the entry works with is. 10 = the entry builds entirely on a small, peripheral, or easily overlooked detail of the prompt or source object - a minor item on a list, a single letter, an incidental word, an edge case of the stated rule. 0 = the entry works with the central, headline element of the prompt that any reader would treat as the main material. This is NOT a quality judgement: narrow slices are not better or worse than central ones; do not let ingenuity, aptness, or humour affect this number. Estimate only how peripheral the chosen material is.

[X17] Frames itself as one of many entries in a batch
Score 0-10 for how explicitly the entry's own wording frames it as one item among many rather than as a standalone text: serial or numbered framing, 'and another', variant-of-a-previous-idea framing, an aside to the contest or its judge, an author persona or signature convention, or wording that only makes sense as part of a submitted run. 10 = the entry cannot be read without noticing it is a member of a set. 0 = the entry is written as a single self-standing text with no batch framing whatsoever. This is NOT a quality judgement; batch framing is neither a merit nor a defect, and you must not let wit, tone, or appeal move this number.

[X18] One governing comic idea that every detail serves
Score 0-10 for whether the entry has a single governing comic idea and whether each supporting detail reinforces THAT idea rather than opening a competing one. 10 = one identifiable comic centre; every element present is subordinate to it; a very short entry that consists solely of the comic centre scores 10, since it has nothing that competes. 5 = a main idea is present but shares the floor with an unrelated aside or a second, independent mechanism. 0 = material accumulates with no discernible comic centre, however long, however varied its vocabulary, however neatly typed. You must NOT reward or penalise length, word count, average word length, punctuation, or typography as such; brevity is not automatically focus, and length is not automatically sprawl. Judge whether the parts point at one target, counting only the parts actually present.

[X19] Lexical heaviness, reading difficulty, and punctuation density
Estimate EXTENT only. Score 0-10 for how lexically heavy and hard to read the entry's prose is: average word length (many long, Latinate, multisyllabic words versus short common ones), sentence complexity and reading difficulty (low readability = high score), and density of punctuation marks per unit of text. 10 = long words, tangled or subordinate-clause-rich construction, frequent commas, dashes, colons, and parentheses. 0 = short, common words in a plain, easily read line with minimal punctuation. Do NOT decide whether this style is good, apt, pretentious, or clear; do not let the topic or the sharpness of the joke move this number. Estimate the mechanical texture only.

[X20] Amount of material and marks the alteration is packaged in
Estimate EXTENT only. Score 0-10 for the bulk of the packaging around whatever alteration is present: number of words and characters devoted to setting up, extending, or commenting on the alteration, plus the density of punctuation marks used in that packaging. 10 = many words, several clauses or sentences, and frequent marks (dashes, parentheses, quotation marks, exclamation points). 0 = the altered item is presented bare, with almost no surrounding words and no marks. Do NOT judge whether the packaging is clever, warranted, funny, or bloated; do not let the quality of the alteration itself affect this number. Count the wrapping, not the gift.

[X21] Overall bulk and word heaviness of the entry
Estimate EXTENT only. Score 0-10 for how much text the entry occupies and how heavy its words are: total characters, total words, and average word length combined. 10 = a long entry, many words, with a high proportion of long multisyllabic words. 0 = a very short entry of a few short words. 5 = a single ordinary-length sentence of ordinary words. Do NOT decide whether this bulk is warranted, focused, rambling, economical, or funny; do not let the presence or absence of a comic centre change this number. Estimate size and word weight only.

[X22] A specific concrete detail carries the joke
Score 0-10 for whether the comic work is done by a specific, concrete particular (a named thing, an exact number, a precise action) rather than by a generic category word. 10 = one vivid particular is doing the work; 0 = only generic labels, nothing specific enough to picture. Judge the specificity of the comic content, not the length.

[X23] Alteration opens a new apt implication beyond substitution
Score 0-10 for how far the entry's alteration of its source travels past the most immediate route, and whether what it produces is APT - a new implication that actually fits the altered thing. 10 = the alteration is one a competent entrant would not reach first, and the result carries a fresh implication about its subject that the unaltered source did not carry; a four-word entry can score 10. 0 = the alteration is arbitrary, purely decorative, or a plain one-for-one swap that produces no implication at all, however long or elaborately typed the entry is. Judge the DISTANCE and FIT of the move, not the amount of material moved. You must NOT reward word count, character count, punctuation volume, capitalisation, or neat typography; a long entry does not demonstrate more ingenuity than a short one, and extra clauses are not extra invention. If no transformation is attempted, score 0.

[X24] Vocabulary and references pin the entry to a datable moment
Score 0-10 for how tightly the entry's wording fixes it to a specific short window in time: named current events, officeholders, products, technologies, prices, slang, media properties, or usages that were current only briefly. 10 = a reader could name the period, roughly to the month or season, purely from the entry's vocabulary and references. 0 = nothing in the wording dates it; it could have been written in any year. This is NOT a quality judgement: timeless entries are not better or worse than time-stamped ones, and topicality here is neither credited nor penalised. Estimate only the tightness of the temporal fix.

[X25] Density of punctuation and typographic marks per unit text
Estimate EXTENT only. Score 0-10 for how densely the entry is punctuated and marked up relative to its length: commas, periods, semicolons, colons, dashes, hyphens, parentheses, quotation marks, apostrophes, ellipses, exclamation and question marks, plus any capitalisation-based emphasis and length of the text overall. 10 = frequent and varied marks throughout a substantial text. 0 = essentially unpunctuated, a bare short string of words. Do NOT judge whether the punctuation is correct, necessary, elegant, or overdone; do not let grammatical quality or comic effect influence this number. Count marks and length only.
