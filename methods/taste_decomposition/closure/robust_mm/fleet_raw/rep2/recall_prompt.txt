You are a BLIND adjudicator in a controlled measurement.

Below are 60 CANDIDATE criteria (ids K01..K60) that a panel of writers proposed for
judging machine-learning paper abstracts, and 16 TARGET criteria (ids T01..T16) drawn
from a separate, older rubric bank written in general scientific-reporting language.

For EACH target, decide whether any candidate names the SAME underlying evaluative
concept -- i.e. a judge scoring an abstract on the target would be scoring essentially
the same property as a judge scoring it on that candidate.

Calibration, and it matters:
  * Register differs by design. The targets are written in general scientific-reporting
    language (the vocabulary of reporting guidelines); the candidates are written in
    machine-learning language. Judge the underlying property, NOT the vocabulary.
  * Do NOT accept mere topical adjacency. If the candidate is clearly broader and merely
    contains the target as one sub-case, or narrower and covers only a fragment of it, or
    shares a topic while scoring a different property -- that is NOT a match.
  * A match means substitutable in practice: swapping one for the other in a scorecard
    would produce nearly the same per-abstract scores.
  * Some targets will have NO match. Reporting no match is a perfectly good answer and is
    expected for a substantial share of them. Do not force matches.

OUTPUT. Emit exactly one JSON object and nothing else -- no markdown fences, no
commentary:

{"results": [
  {"target": "T01",
   "match": true or false,
   "candidate_ids": ["K07", ...],   // every candidate you judge to be the same concept; [] if none
   "confidence": "high" or "low",
   "why": "<one sentence>"},
  ... one entry for every target T01..T16 ...
]}

=== CANDIDATES ===

K01. Concrete, named real-world deployment stakes
     Score 10 if the abstract names a specific real-world deployment context or consequential comparison (e.g., a named operational system, a direct comparison to human professional performance, a named application domain with concrete stakes). Score 5 if it gestures at 'real-world applicability' only generically. Score 0 if the work is framed purely as an internal benchmark/metric improvement with no application context.

K02. Non-obvious conceptual synthesis
     Score 10 when the work combines ideas from distinct areas into a clearly motivated, non-trivial principle; score 0 when it mainly concatenates familiar modules without a new conceptual connection.

K03. Transferable capability evidence
     Score 10 when the abstract demonstrates that the learned capability transfers to new objects, domains, task types, or deployment conditions beyond the training formulation; score 0 when performance is shown only on the directly optimized task.

K04. Failure-mode diagnosis
     Score 10 when experiments or analysis isolate specific failure modes, biases, or component effects and use them to explain improvements; score 0 when the abstract reports only aggregate performance.

K05. Conditional, scoped claims of superiority
     Score 10 if the abstract specifies particular conditions, subsets, or regimes under which the proposed method is better (or worse) than alternatives, rather than claiming uniform dominance. Score 5 if superiority is claimed with a partial hedge ('in most cases'/'on most datasets'). Score 0 if the abstract claims blanket state-of-the-art / superiority everywhere with no scoping.

K06. Heterogeneity of evaluation conditions
     Score 10 if the method is evaluated across genuinely different task types, modalities, languages, or domains (e.g., multiple distinct benchmark families, multiple input languages, multiple downstream application types). Score 5 if evaluated on several datasets that are all variants of the same single task/domain. Score 0 if evaluated on only one dataset/benchmark.

K07. Expectation-violating finding stated with its scope condition
     Score how much the abstract reports a specific claim that cuts against a reasonable default expectation in its subfield, together with the condition under which that claim holds. 10 = a clearly identified surprise plus its boundary, stated concretely enough that a reader could try to falsify it (e.g. a component widely assumed necessary turns out unnecessary; or which of two choices wins flips depending on a named factor). 5 = a mild or partial surprise, or a surprise asserted with no condition attached. 0 = nothing in the abstract could have come out otherwise; it reports only that the proposed method beats prior methods on the usual benchmarks.

K08. Explanatory insight
     Score 10 when the work explains why a phenomenon occurs or why the method succeeds in a way that generalizes beyond the immediate experiment; score 0 when it offers only performance improvement without explanation.

K09. Implementation specificity
     Score 10 when the abstract makes the method sufficiently concrete to reconstruct its essential data flow, training procedure, and evaluation protocol; score 0 when the proposal is dominated by slogans, unexplained acronyms, or vague claims.

K10. Quantitative results interpreted, not just listed
     Score 10 if quantitative results are woven into explanatory sentences that state what the number means for the paper's claim (e.g., 'reconstructs the score with X% error, showing Y'). Score 5 if numbers are reported with only light surrounding context. Score 0 if the abstract is dominated by a bare list of percentages/multipliers with no interpretive sentence connecting them to the claim.

K11. Mechanistic novelty
     Score 10 when the abstract identifies a genuinely new operation, representation, or learning mechanism whose role is clear; score 0 when novelty is only a renamed architecture or a minor parameter change.

K12. Operational realism
     Score 10 when the abstract specifies realistic constraints, inputs, failure modes, or deployment conditions and shows the method addresses them; score 0 when the application framing is aspirational with no operational constraints.

K13. Conceptual reframe
     Score 10 when the work introduces a genuinely new way to formulate, represent, or reason about the problem; score 0 when it is primarily a routine module, tuning change, or renamed combination of familiar techniques.

K14. Human-expert judgment of outputs, not only automatic metrics
     Score 10 if the abstract explicitly reports domain-expert or human-annotator evaluation of the method's own outputs (e.g., a stated number of human judges, or a human-labeled benchmark used to validate results/quality). Score 5 if humans are mentioned only for data curation/labeling but not for judging output quality. Score 0 if evaluation is entirely automatic metrics (accuracy, F1, AUC, BLEU, WER, etc.) with no human judgment mentioned.

K15. Theory instantiated as a usable method, not a bound alone
     Score 10 if a formal theoretical result (proof, guarantee, bound) is explicitly connected to and instantiated as a concrete, usable algorithm or design choice that is then empirically tested. Score 5 if theory and practice are both present but only loosely linked. Score 0 if the contribution is a theoretical result alone with no resulting tested algorithm, or an algorithm with no theoretical grounding at all.

K16. Plain-language explanation of why the mechanism works
     Score 10 if the abstract includes at least one sentence explaining, in intuitive non-jargon terms, why the proposed mechanism should work (e.g., 'we hypothesize that X prevents Y'). Score 5 if the mechanism is named and technically described but its rationale is only implied. Score 0 if the abstract never explains why the method should work, only asserts that it does via reported numbers.

K17. Explicitly flagged counterintuitive finding
     Score 10 if the abstract explicitly signals that a result defies expectation or prior belief (e.g., 'surprisingly,' 'contrary to,' 'intriguing') and indicates what was expected instead. Score 5 if a notable/unexpected result is reported without being flagged as surprising. Score 0 if all reported findings are presented as expected confirmations of the proposed design.

K18. Cross-field grounding of the approach
     Score 10 if the abstract explicitly draws on and names concepts/techniques from more than one distinct field (e.g., complexity/game theory plus deep learning, planetary robotics plus reinforcement learning) to justify or construct the method. Score 5 if a single borrowed concept from another field is mentioned only in passing. Score 0 if the approach and its motivation are framed entirely within one subfield's own vocabulary.

K19. Comparison integrity: baselines treated as tunable, not fixed
     Score whether the abstract shows awareness that its comparison could be unfair and says what was done about it. 10 = explicit protocol control: baselines re-tuned or upgraded, matched training schedules, matched evaluation method, a named strong baseline reported with its own number, or identified and mitigated evaluation biases. 5 = comparison to recognized strong baselines without discussion of tuning. 0 = the count of baselines or datasets is offered as the entire evidence of rigor.

K20. Contribution removes a requirement rather than adding a part
     Score the extent to which the central contribution is subtractive: something previously treated as necessary is shown to be unnecessary, and the abstract names both what is dropped and what is retained. 10 = an explicit elimination (no negative examples needed, no architectural change needed, no hand-engineered features needed, no explicit uncertainty quantification needed) that is the paper's main claim. 5 = a simplification mentioned as a side benefit. 0 = purely additive: new modules, losses, or stages stacked onto an existing pipeline.

K21. End-to-end integration coherence
     Score 10 when multiple components form a complementary pipeline whose interactions are necessary for the claimed capability; score 0 when the abstract presents an arbitrary bundle of techniques with no explanation of how they work together.

K22. Quantified efficiency–quality tradeoff
     Score 10 when the abstract quantifies both resource savings and the resulting accuracy, quality, or capability cost; score 0 when efficiency or performance is only described qualitatively.

K23. Scope under meaningful variation
     Score 10 when evaluation deliberately varies domains, tasks, shifts, scales, or deployment settings that test the claimed scope; score 0 when evidence comes from one narrow, convenient setting.

K24. Specific mechanism of baseline failure identified
     Score 10 if the abstract names a precise, mechanistic failure mode of prior/existing approaches (what specifically goes wrong and why) rather than a generic limitation. Score 5 if a limitation is named but not explained mechanistically. Score 0 if prior work is only described as 'limited,' 'underexplored,' or 'insufficient' with no specifics.

K25. Motivation names a specific defect of the current best approach
     Score how concrete and attributable the stated gap is. 10 = the motivation is a named failure mode of a named existing method or family, with an account of HOW it fails (this method degrades in this regime, for this reason). 5 = a real but generic limitation of a family of approaches. 0 = field-level throat clearing: the area is important, challenges remain, existing work is limited.

K26. Fair re-audit of existing methods or benchmarks
     Score 10 if the abstract's central contribution is re-evaluating existing algorithms/models/benchmarks under corrected, fairer, or more systematic conditions and reports how prior claims change as a result. Score 5 if it performs some comparative analysis of existing components without reframing field-level claims. Score 0 if the abstract only proposes a new method with no critical re-examination of prior evaluation validity.

K27. Causal mechanism clarity
     Score 10 when the abstract clearly connects a specific limitation to a concrete design choice and then to the claimed effect; score 0 when it mainly lists components and results without explaining why they should work.

K28. Problem–mechanism fit
     Score 10 when the proposed mechanism directly addresses a specifically diagnosed bottleneck and the abstract explains why; score 0 when the method is weakly connected to a generic problem statement.

K29. Comparison fairness
     Score 10 when baselines, tuning, schedules, metrics, and compute are explicitly made comparable; score 0 when superiority is asserted without credible control of comparison conditions.

K30. Claim-scope discipline
     Score 10 when conclusions are carefully limited to the demonstrated settings and conditions, with dependencies or limitations made clear; score 0 when narrow evidence is used to support sweeping claims about broad capability or safety.

K31. Absence of promotional self-description
     Score the neutrality of the language the abstract uses about ITS OWN contribution. 10 = contribution described in flat, checkable terms; the reader is left to judge significance. 5 = occasional mild boosterism (novel, effective) alongside substance. 0 = self-applied superlatives doing the work evidence should do (breakthrough, innovative, first-ever, whistleblower, remarkable). Enthusiasm about the field or about prior work is NOT penalized; only self-praise.

K32. Explicit mechanism given for why the method should work
     Score whether the abstract states a causal account of why the proposed change helps, specific enough to be wrong. 10 = a named mechanism or diagnosis (this fails because of X, so we do Y, which removes X), including hypotheses flagged as such. 5 = a vague gesture at intuition (leverages richer information, better captures structure). 0 = the method is described only procedurally and its effectiveness is established purely by results.

K33. Transfer beyond the training regime
     Score 10 when the method demonstrably transfers across unseen objects, domains, tasks, or distributions central to the claim; score 0 when evaluation remains within the same narrow regime used to motivate training.

K34. Released artifact described with scientific substance, not just linked
     Score how well any dataset, benchmark, or code release is characterized in the abstract itself. 10 = scale, composition, coverage, and what the artifact makes possible are stated, so a reader can judge its value without downloading it. 5 = named and linked, with partial description. 0 = a bare anonymized URL, or a promise contingent on acceptance or publication, with no description of what the artifact contains.

K35. Reproduction-ready detail
     Score 10 when the abstract specifies the inputs, principal operations, training or inference procedure, evaluation setting, and relevant scale well enough to reconstruct the contribution conceptually; score 0 when the method remains opaque despite technical terminology.

K36. Constraint-aware efficiency
     Score 10 when the abstract identifies a practical resource, data, latency, or privacy constraint and quantifies a favorable accuracy-quality tradeoff; score 0 when efficiency is asserted only vaguely or is unrelated to the central contribution.

K37. Abstract reads as one coherent argument, not a fragment list
     Score 10 if the abstract flows as one integrated narrative (problem, insight, method, validation) with clear logical connectives. Score 5 if it is readable but reads as a loosely connected sequence of claims. Score 0 if it reads as a fragmented list of disconnected technical assertions/results, or relies on gimmicks such as spelling out an acronym letter-by-letter in place of plain description.

K38. Benchmark or dataset utility
     Score 10 when a new benchmark or dataset measures an important, previously missing capability with coverage, task design, and baselines that enable meaningful future comparison; score 0 when it is merely a collection of routine examples or lacks evidence of usefulness.

K39. Relative gains reported with absolute base and measurement conditions
     Score whether relative claims can be reconstructed by the reader. 10 = every fold-change, percentage, or speedup is accompanied by the absolute quantity it is relative to and the setting it was measured in (model, hardware, dataset, baseline identity). 5 = some anchored, some floating. 0 = large relative numbers with no base value, no setting, and no identified comparator.

K40. Headline result anchored to an externally interpretable reference point
     Score whether the main quantitative result is placed against a reference a reader outside this paper can independently interpret. 10 = the result is stated against human performance, a published record on a named dataset, a competition outcome, or a specific named prior system with ITS number also given. 5 = numbers on named benchmarks but compared only to generic baselines or unnamed prior work. 0 = improvement expressed only as outperforms baselines / achieves state of the art, with no interpretable anchor.

K41. Benchmark ecosystem value
     Score 10 when a dataset or benchmark has clearly defined coverage, challenging evaluation dimensions, useful baselines, and a stated research purpose; score 0 when it merely announces data or environments without showing why they enable better science.

K42. Theory–algorithm correspondence
     Score 10 when a stated theorem or analysis directly motivates the algorithm and predicts an observable behavior tested experimentally; score 0 when theory and experiments are disconnected or theory is merely decorative.

K43. Bottleneck-to-solution fit
     Score 10 when every major method component directly addresses a well-defined bottleneck under the stated setting; score 0 when the proposed technique is only loosely related to the motivating problem.

K44. Claimed virtues tied to evidence rather than stacked as adjectives
     Score how tightly each desirable property claimed is bound to a specific measurement, dataset, or argument. 10 = every claimed property (accuracy, speed, robustness, generalization, scalability) is attached to something checkable. 5 = a mix, with some properties asserted bare. 0 = a run of virtues asserted in a single breath, none of them attached to a number, a condition, or a mechanism.

K45. Diagnostic evidence of causality
     Score 10 when ablations, controlled variants, or targeted analyses isolate which design choice causes the improvement; score 0 when the abstract reports only aggregate end-to-end gains.

K46. Fair comparative design
     Score 10 when comparisons use strong, clearly matched baselines across multiple controlled settings and distinguish algorithmic effects from tuning or protocol effects; score 0 when comparisons are narrow, ambiguous, or vulnerable to unfair baselines.

K47. Definite claim paired with an acknowledged limit
     Score whether the abstract states a boundary on its own result while still committing to a claim. 10 = a clear positive claim plus an explicit limit: where it does not hold, what remains unsolved, that results are preliminary, or a reported failure of the thing being studied. 5 = a hedge with no specific content. 0 = uniformly positive; consistent superiority everywhere, no stated boundary, no negative finding.

K48. Practical consequence
     Score 10 when the target problem has a concrete scientific, societal, or deployment consequence and the proposed method addresses that consequence directly; score 0 when the task is presented mainly as an abstract benchmark exercise with no demonstrated relevance.

K49. Scale of a newly constructed resource as a stated contribution
     Score 10 if the abstract describes building a new large-scale dataset or benchmark, with a concrete scale figure (image/hour/sample count), that is itself framed as a primary contribution. Score 5 if a new resource is mentioned but its scale/originality is not quantified or emphasized. Score 0 if no new dataset/resource is constructed.

K50. Information density: content per sentence
     Score the fraction of sentences that carry a specific fact, quantity, mechanism, or falsifiable claim. 10 = nearly every sentence adds content; the abstract reaches its actual contribution within the first two sentences. 5 = a conventional two-sentence setup followed by dense content. 0 = a long motivational preamble about the importance of the area, plus a closing aspiration (we hope to inspire, paving the way), with the actual contribution occupying a small minority of the text.

K51. Theory-to-algorithm coupling
     Score 10 when the theoretical result directly motivates the algorithm or its guarantee and the experiments test that connection; score 0 when theory and empirical method are presented as largely independent claims.

K52. Formal result paired with an experiment that could disconfirm it
     Score the coupling between theory and evidence. 10 = a stated formal result AND experiments whose design follows from it, so the experiment is a test of the theory and not merely a separate benchmark table. 5 = theory and experiments both present but logically unconnected. 0 = a guarantee invoked with no stated content (we provide theoretical guarantees) and/or experiments that bear on a different claim than the theory does.

K53. Open research question posed before the solution
     Score 10 if the abstract explicitly frames an unresolved research question or conceptual gap (in question form or a clear gap statement) before presenting the technical solution, with the solution presented as an answer to that question. Score 5 if a gap/limitation is stated but not posed as an actionable question. Score 0 if the abstract moves straight from topic to method with no explicit question or gap framing.

K54. Evaluation–claim alignment
     Score 10 when the metrics and test tasks directly measure the paper’s central capability and avoid known leakage or proxy failures; score 0 when headline metrics are poorly matched to the stated objective.

K55. Consequence extends beyond the proposed method
     Score whether, if the paper's claim were true, researchers who never adopt this particular method would have to change what they do. 10 = the finding revises how a task should be measured, how baselines should be built, or what a whole family of methods can be assumed to need. 5 = useful to a specific subfield's practitioners. 0 = the claim's consequences are confined to users of this exact method or artifact.

K56. One load-bearing idea versus bolted-together named components
     Score whether the contribution is a single idea carried through, or an assembly. 10 = one mechanism, developed and tested; any sub-parts are shown to be facets of that one mechanism. 5 = a main idea with one auxiliary trick. 0 = the contribution IS an enumeration of two or more separately named or acronymed modules whose only stated relationship is that they are used together, or a laundry list of independent additions.

K57. Concrete code/data/artifact release commitment
     Score 10 if the abstract gives a specific, verifiable commitment to release code/data/models (e.g., a working repository link, an anonymous.4open.science link, a named dataset host, or 'code and data are available at X'). Score 5 if it makes only a vague future promise ('code will be released') with no destination or specificity. Score 0 if there is no mention of code, data, or artifact availability at all.

K58. Failure-mode awareness
     Score 10 when the abstract identifies important limitations or failure modes and tests how the method behaves under them; score 0 when it presents only unconditional success language.

K59. Cross-setting stress coverage
     Score 10 when the method is tested across substantially varied datasets, domains, modalities, or task conditions that probe the stated claim; score 0 when evidence comes from one narrow or homogeneous setting.

K60. Quantitative claim specificity
     Score 10 when gains are reported with concrete metrics, baselines, datasets, scales, and relevant conditions; score 0 when the abstract relies mostly on unqualified phrases such as substantial or state of the art.

=== TARGETS ===

T01. Novelty and positioning vs prior work
     Evaluates whether the contribution is genuinely original and clearly contrasted with prior work, with the novelty and importance communicated explicitly (e.g., abstract/cover letter).

T02. Discussion and conclusions — interpretation and implications
     Findings are interpreted in light of objectives, prior evidence, and theory; alternative explanations and limitations are considered; conclusions are clear and appropriately restrained; practical/scientific implications and synthesis are articulated.

T03. Discussion and conclusions: evidence‑aligned, contextualized, and balanced
     Interpretations and conclusions are supported by the data, appropriately contextualized with prior work, distinguish speculation, emphasize limitations, and articulate practical and research implications without overreach; the narrative is coherent.

T04. Claim–evidence alignment and causal caution
     Claims and conclusions are directly supported by presented evidence, calibrated in tone to evidence strength, avoid causal/ecological fallacies, consider confounders and alternatives, and show epistemic humility.

T05. Novelty and significance of the contribution
     The work is original and non‑incremental, advances the field in a meaningful way, and demonstrates clear significance/utility (e.g., methodological, theoretical, or practical impact).

T06. Accessibility and inclusive communication
     Ensure accessible materials and clear, inclusive language for diverse audiences (including readers with disabilities and non‑native speakers); provide multilingual resources where appropriate.

T07. Theoretical framing, coherence, and use of theory
     A coherent theoretical framework motivates the study and interprets findings; concepts/terms are correctly used and integrated; propositions/hypotheses are logically derived where relevant.

T08. Dataset provenance, composition, and representativeness
     Document dataset origin/provenance, collection timeframe, composition/coverage, inclusion/exclusion/filtering, representativeness of the final sample, and required accessions/acknowledgments.

T09. Clarity, readability, and organization for the intended audience
     Writing is clear and well‑structured for target readers; terminology is consistent; organization and length support comprehension and assessment.

T10. Abstract accuracy, completeness, and balance
     The abstract concisely and accurately reflects background/objectives, design/methods, key quantitative/qualitative results, and conclusions without hype.

T11. Citation practice quality, coverage, and ethics
     References are current, relevant, accurate, and fairly represent sources; practices acknowledge precedence and avoid manipulation or excessive self‑citation.

T12. Computational resources, efficiency, and convergence comparisons
     Report compute resources/budget and compare runtime, scalability, and convergence behavior versus baselines; discuss computational trade‑offs and efficiency.

T13. STROBE adherence for observational studies
     Observational studies (cohort, case–control, cross-sectional) comply with STROBE and relevant extensions (e.g., RECORD), including setting/dates and data sources/measurement.

T14. Data/code/materials availability statement with access details
     Manuscript includes a clear availability statement specifying whether, where, and how underlying data/code/materials can be accessed, with repository links/identifiers and any access conditions.

T15. Title, abstract, and keyword quality
     Title is specific/accurate; abstract is concise and aligned with methods/results/conclusions; keywords/subjects are appropriate and, where applicable, use standardized/controlled vocabularies for discoverability.

T16. Review/synthesis design quality and reporting
     For scoping/systematic reviews, aims/feasibility are appropriate; search/selection/synthesis methods are fit for purpose, transparently reported, and judiciously applied (including narrative vs quantitative synthesis).
