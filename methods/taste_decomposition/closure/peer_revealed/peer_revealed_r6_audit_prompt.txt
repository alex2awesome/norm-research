You are auditing a pool of scoring criteria that were written by other
models. Your job is a ROUTING decision, made blind: you are NOT told who wrote each
criterion or what it was written for.

For each criterion, decide:

  "quality_relevant" -- the criterion measures a property that a careful expert would
      count as genuine evidence about research quality. It is about the comic idea, the
      craft, the wording choices, the content of the item.

  "incidental" -- the criterion measures something that may well PREDICT how the item
      fares but is not itself research quality: length or verbosity, typography, punctuation
      or capitalisation habits, formatting and trailing furniture, boilerplate or
      template phrasing, community/venue style markers, subject-matter or topic markers,
      temporal tells, orthographic habits, or a textual fingerprint of some upstream
      circumstance of the item's production (who wrote it, with what practice or
      following, when, where in the run of entries).

The corpus is: abstracts of machine-learning conference paper submissions. Each item is a abstract.

SECOND JUDGEMENT, only for criteria you label "incidental". Some incidental channels have
an upstream cause that plausibly ALSO produces genuinely better work (for example, a
practised entrant may both be favoured for irrelevant reasons AND actually write better
entries). Return `mixed`: true when you think the upstream cause plausibly produces real
research quality too, false when the channel looks like pure nuisance. For criteria you label
"quality_relevant", return mixed=false.

Judge each criterion ON ITS OWN TEXT. Do not try to guess which pool it came from, and
do not let the ORDER of the list influence you.

CRITERIA:

[X01] Theory-to-Algorithm Closure
Score 10 when formal assumptions, theoretical claims, and the implemented algorithm are explicitly connected; score 0 when theory is ornamental, disconnected from the method, or unsupported by stated conditions.

[X02] Proportion of sentences written in the passive voice
Score 0-10 for what fraction of the sentences are in the passive voice. 10 = essentially every sentence is passive; 0 = every sentence is active. Count the grammatical construction only; do not judge whether passive voice is good or bad here.

[X03] Boundary-condition characterization
Score 10 when the abstract clearly states when the method works, fails, or changes behavior, including relevant data or parameter conditions; score 0 when it makes unqualified claims.

[X04] Novel Problem Formulation Together with Infrastructure
Score 0 if the abstract tackles a heavily studied, standard benchmark task. Score 10 if it formalizes a completely new problem space that connects previously disjoint domains, and provides the necessary infrastructure (datasets, simulators) to explore it.

[X05] Claim auditability
Score 10 when the abstract gives precise contributions, settings, procedures, scope, and reproducibility details that let a reader audit its claims; score 0 when it is mostly a title, slogan, or unsupported general assertion.

[X06] Mathematical Notation Density
Rate the extent to which the text contains raw LaTeX formatting, inline math symbols, or mathematical notation (e.g., $x$, \emph{}, l_\infty). Do not judge the correctness of the math.

[X07] Broad Applicability Together with Minimal Architectural Overhead
Score 0 if the proposed method requires a highly specialized, custom-built architecture to function. Score 10 if it presents a plug-and-play technique that can be applied to a wide variety of standard base models without adding significant inference overhead.

[X08] Gives a mechanism for why the proposed approach should work
Score 0-10 for whether the abstract supplies a reason or mechanism for why what it proposes should work, rather than only asserting that it does. 10 = an explicit causal or conceptual account; 0 = bare assertion with no reasoning.

[X09] Branded System or Acronym Prominence
Rate the extent to which the abstract explicitly introduces, capitalizes, or heavily uses a specific acronym, system name, or branded method (e.g., ALFWorld, DARTS-, MMT). Do not judge whether the name is good or creative.

[X10] Code/repo release boilerplate mention
Rate 0-10 how much this abstract contains an explicit mention of released code, a GitHub link, or a project webpage (e.g. 'Code is available at...', 'alfworld.github.io'). 0 = no such mention, 10 = prominent explicit link/boilerplate. Do not judge whether releasing code is good practice.

[X11] Raw formatting and LaTeX residue
Score from 0 (clean, rendered text) to 10 (pervasive unrendered LaTeX commands, stray hyphens, or copy-paste artifacts such as a placeholder). Measure only the extent of formatting artifacts; do not judge whether they harm readability.

[X12] Lexical Economy Together with High Conceptual Density
Score 0 if the text is cluttered with dense acronyms, hyper-specific jargon, and buzzwords that obscure the core idea. Score 10 if it uses plain, precise language with zero unnecessary jargon while conveying deep, complex conceptual content.

[X13] Distinguishes what is new here from what was already known
Score 0-10 for how clearly the abstract separates its own new contribution from prior knowledge it is building on. 10 = the new part is stated and delimited against what already existed; 0 = no distinction is drawn at all.

[X14] Principled cross-regime generalization
Score 10 when variation across domains, tasks, scales, or environments is motivated by the proposed mechanism; score 0 when breadth consists only of unrelated applications.

[X15] Insider citation-and-lineage naming inside the abstract
Score 0-10 the extent to which the text names prior work inside the abstract itself — author-year citations (e.g. 'Downey et al.', 'Ganin & Lempitsky, 2015'), named prior systems positioned as the parent of this work, or quoted titles of prior papers. 0 = no prior work named; 10 = several explicit author-year citations and named predecessor systems woven through the text. Score only how much prior-work naming is present; do not judge whether the citations are appropriate or whether being connected to prior work is good.

[X16] Encoding of Structural Inductive Biases
Score 10 if the method explicitly and mathematically encodes known physical, geometric, or topological constraints into the model's architecture or objective function; score 0 for generic, black-box function approximators applied blindly to structured data.

[X17] Abstract length and clause elaboration
Score 0-10 the sheer length and syntactic elaboration of the text: total word count, number of sentences, average sentence length, and use of multi-clause enumerations. 0 = a title only or one or two short sentences; 10 = a long, densely subordinated multi-paragraph-length abstract. Score length and elaboration only; do not judge whether the length is warranted or whether more content indicates a better paper.

[X18] Cross-level interface coherence
Score 10 when the abstract defines coherent interfaces between abstract representations and concrete observations, actions, or outputs; score 0 when its stages are disconnected or underspecified.

[X19] Framed-as-response-to-recent-work positioning
Rate 0-10 how much the abstract frames its contribution explicitly as addressing a gap, weakness, or limitation in a 'recent' or 'existing' method/approach (e.g. 'a recent attempt...has a weakness', 'existing approaches...however', 'despite promising, state-of-the-art results...are far from satisfactory'). 0 = frames itself as opening a new problem with no reference to a recent predecessor's shortcomings, 10 = explicitly and prominently positioned as a corrective response to a specific recent prior method. Do not judge whether the criticism of prior work is fair or the fix is good.

[X20] Causal Isolation of Contributions
Score 10 when the abstract describes controlled comparisons or ablations that isolate each component's contribution; score 0 when several changes are introduced together without any stated way to distinguish them.

[X21] Superlative/marketing adjective density
Rate 0-10 the density of superlative or promotional phrasing such as 'state-of-the-art', 'novel', 'significantly', 'substantially', 'unprecedented', 'excellent performance'. 0 = plain descriptive prose, 10 = saturated with such phrasing. Score only how much such language is present, not whether the claims are true or the paper is actually strong.

[X22] Tradeoff Articulation
Score 10 when the abstract names competing objectives, explains their tension, and describes how the method controls both; score 0 when it presents only one metric or vaguely claims simultaneous improvement.

[X23] Late-arriver framing: repairs a crowded, already-named literature
Score 0-10 the extent to which the text positions itself as arriving AFTER an established and crowded body of work on the same topic rather than opening a topic: markers include phrases such as 'recent advances', 'a large stream of works', 'existing methods', 'state-of-the-art methods ignored X', 'despite the fast development of', 'previous works report mixed results', 'the common practice is', and framings that fix, unify, re-evaluate, stabilise, or fairly compare things others already built. Contrast with 0 = the text introduces a problem or setting as if little or nothing exists yet, defining terms from scratch and naming no crowd of predecessors. 10 = the text is explicitly a corrective, unifying, or robustifying response to a named and already-saturated line of work. Score only how strongly the late-arriver framing is present in the wording; do not judge whether arriving late or early is better, and do not judge the merit of the correction.

[X24] Conceptual unification
Score 10 when the proposal derives meaningful relationships among previously separate methods or objectives within one framework; score 0 when it merely places familiar techniques side by side.

[X25] Baseline difficulty is calibrated and honestly low
10 = the abstract reports how hard the problem is, including a baseline that performs poorly, and reports its own still-imperfect number. 0 = only final favorable numbers appear, with no evidence the task is difficult or the headroom is real.

[X26] Paradigm-Shifting Challenge to Dogma
Score 10 if the paper successfully challenges a widely held assumption or standard practice in the field (e.g., 'normalization is strictly necessary') and proves it is not required; score 0 if it simply iterates on the current dominant paradigm.

[X27] Density of exact quantitative claims
Score from 0 (no specific numbers) to 10 (dense with exact percentages, benchmark scores, or multipliers like 14.4% or 5x). Count only explicit numerical figures; do not judge whether the results are impressive.

[X28] Root-cause diagnosis vs. symptom patching
Score 10 if the abstract identifies and addresses an underlying causal mechanism behind a known failure (naming *why* it happens), rather than proposing a heuristic that reacts to symptoms or thresholds. Score 0 if the fix is a threshold/indicator/heuristic with no stated causal account.

[X29] Extent of British rather than American spelling
Score 0-10 for how much the text uses British rather than American spelling (-ise/-isation, -our, -re endings, doubled consonants). 10 = British spelling throughout; 0 = American spelling throughout or no distinguishing words. Count the orthography only; this is not a judgement of writing quality.
