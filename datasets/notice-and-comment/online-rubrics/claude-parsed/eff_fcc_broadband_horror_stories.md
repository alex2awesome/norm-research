# The FCC Wants Your Broadband Horror Stories (EFF Deeplinks, 2021)

## Core Message
"Now that the FCC is finally seriously asking for citizen comments, this is _your_ chance to let them know just how badly you've been treated."

## Implicit Value of Submissions
Comments should address whether you have "been adequately served by your internet service provider (ISP)." Such feedback "informs future broadband availability as well as funding, standards, and federal policy."

## Examples of Problems Worth Documenting
Recurring issues like "upload throttling customers" and "harassing customers seeking to cancel service" — these concrete documented problems are exactly what the FCC needs to hear. Specific, dated incidents from named providers are more usable than aggregate complaints.

## Practitioner Takeaway
EFF treats individual subscriber experiences as the proof-of-harm record the FCC needs to justify rulemaking — the agency cannot cite anecdotes it does not have. The advocacy theory: build the evidentiary base for the rule you want.
