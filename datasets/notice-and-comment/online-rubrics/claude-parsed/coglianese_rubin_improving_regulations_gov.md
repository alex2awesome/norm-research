# Improving Regulations.gov (Coglianese & Rubin, The Regulatory Review)

## Current Functionality Critiques
- **Search Difficulties** — document categorization is "unusual and confusing"; document types like "advance notices of proposed rulemaking" are inconsistently labeled across agencies.
- **Multiple Dockets Problem** — agencies create separate dockets for the same rulemaking without linking them; sub-agency proposes in one docket; parent finalizes in another.
- **Missing Context** — many dockets "lack important context"; some have no supporting materials despite inviting comment. "Long-term action" status often appears only on Reginfo.gov.

## Requirements for Effective Citizen Participation
Meaningful public engagement demands access to "well-informed views and relevant information" the agency relied upon during development. Citizens cannot submit quality comments without reviewing studies and background documents underlying proposed regulations.

## Three ACUS Recommendations
1. **User-Centered Design** — involve actual users in labeling decisions; "agile approaches" consistent with the U.S. Digital Service playbook.
2. **Automated Alert System** — back-end alerts prompting agency managers when creating new dockets, suggesting related dockets and enabling consolidation.
3. **Cross-Database Integration** — interoperability with Reginfo.gov and other rulemaking databases.

## Implication for Commenters
Commenters must work harder than the platform suggests: search across multiple dockets, cross-check Reginfo.gov, and demand the agency's underlying analytic documents (RIA, studies) before drafting.
