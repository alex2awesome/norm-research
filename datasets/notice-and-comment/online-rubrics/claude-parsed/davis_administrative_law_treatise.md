# Davis, "Administrative Law Treatise" (1958)

Davis's foundational treatise was the first systematic exposition of administrative law and
established the analytical framework for thinking about procedure choice — when agencies should
use rulemaking vs. adjudication, formal vs. informal procedures, and the role of public
participation in each.

## Davis's Lasting Frame for Comment Quality

Davis's central contribution was the argument that **rulemaking with notice and comment**
should be the preferred mode of policymaking — superior to case-by-case adjudication for
broad policy questions. The implications for what counts as a substantive comment:

### 1. Rulemaking Comments Are Quasi-Legislative
Comments to a rulemaking are inputs to a quasi-legislative function. They should provide:
- Information about real-world conditions
- Predictions about consequences
- Value judgments about competing interests
- Alternative approaches the agency might consider

### 2. Discretion Should Be Confined, Structured, and Checked
Davis's famous trilogy. Comments should help agencies:
- **Confine** discretion through clearer rules
- **Structure** discretion through articulated standards
- **Check** discretion through transparency and review opportunities

### 3. Informal Rulemaking Trades Formality for Participation Breadth
Notice-and-comment is informal because it sacrifices cross-examination for broader access.
Comments should leverage this breadth — diverse perspectives, geographic spread, varied
expertise — in ways formal hearings cannot.

## Implications for the Rubric

A high-quality comment, in Davis's frame:

- Treats the agency as a quasi-legislative body needing input on facts, values, and predictions
- Helps the agency confine, structure, or check discretion
- Brings perspectives or information that formal proceedings would have excluded

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment provide factual information about real-world conditions?
- [ ] Does the comment make a prediction about consequences supported by evidence or reasoning?
- [ ] Does the comment articulate a value judgment with reasoning?
- [ ] Does the comment propose an alternative the agency should consider?
- [ ] Does the comment help the agency confine, structure, or check its discretion?
- [ ] Does the comment bring a perspective or information that adversarial proceedings would
      not surface?
