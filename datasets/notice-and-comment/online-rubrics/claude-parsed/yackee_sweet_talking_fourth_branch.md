# Yackee - Sweet-Talking the Fourth Branch: The Influence of Interest Group Comments on Federal Agency Rulemaking

Susan Webb Yackee, Journal of Public Administration Research and Theory, Vol. 16, Issue 1, January 2006.

## Study Design

Yackee analyzed an original dataset composed of 1,444 interest group comments in reaction to forty federal agency rules. The study examines whether interest groups can directly influence federal agency policymaking through formal participation in the rulemaking process.

## Key Findings

The research found that the formal participation of interest groups during rulemaking can, and often does, alter the content of policy within the "fourth branch" of government, and those who voice their preferences during the notice and comment period rulemaking are often able to change government policy outputs to better match their preferences.

The findings indicate that it matters what interests pose what kind of threat in regulatory rulemaking, with certain group types becoming more influential while others lose out when making specific kinds of threats.

## Implications for Comment Effectiveness

- Comment direction (support/oppose/modify) can shift final rule outcomes
- Repeated similar comments from interest groups carry weight
- Subsequent research has found that public organizations seem to weigh comments from expert organizations more highly than public comments in general

This influential study challenged existing assumptions about interest group influence on federal agencies by demonstrating direct effects on rulemaking outcomes through the notice and comment process.
