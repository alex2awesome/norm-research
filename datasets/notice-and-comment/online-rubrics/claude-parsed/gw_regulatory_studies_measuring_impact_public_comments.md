# GW Regulatory Studies Center: Measuring the Impact of Public Comments

## Overview
This April 2014 commentary examines how public comments influence regulatory rulemaking, focusing on a new analytical approach called RegRank developed by Kirilenko, Mankad, and Michailidis.

## Assessment Rubric/Criteria for Comment Impact

The commentary identifies several methodologies researchers use to evaluate comment effectiveness:

1. **Quantifiable Changes**: Tracking measurable modifications between proposed and final rules, such as "dollar amounts of government allocations," then correlating these shifts with submitted comments.

2. **Manual Content Coding**: Hand-analyzing the substance of comments alongside regulatory revisions to identify connections.

3. **Regulator Testimony**: Drawing on statements from agency officials describing instances where comments shaped their decisions.

4. **Topic Modeling (RegRank)**: Using automated natural language processing to assess the "structure and sentiment" of documents and determine alignment between comment tones and final rule adjustments.

## Key Findings

The RegRank study of 60,000 comments on CFTC rulemakings (2010-2013) revealed:

- "Comments mattered" and the agency adjusted rules directionally toward comment substance
- Finance industry comments held substantially greater influence than other occupational groups
- Public interest comments increased finalization likelihood but showed less direct influence
