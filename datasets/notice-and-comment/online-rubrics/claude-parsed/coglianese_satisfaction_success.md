# Coglianese, "Is Satisfaction Success?" (2002)

Coglianese argues participant satisfaction is a poor proxy for whether comments produce good
policy. He uses the EPA reformulated gasoline negotiated rulemaking — where stakeholders agreed,
but the resulting rule produced groundwater contamination from MTBE — to show that **consensus
among commenters is not evidence of good rule quality**.

## Three Better Evaluation Criteria for Comment Effectiveness (per Coglianese)

Instead of "did the commenters feel heard?", evaluate comments by:

### 1. Effectiveness
Did the comment lead to a rule that achieved its statutory objective at lower cost or higher
benefit than alternatives? **A good comment improves outcomes**, not just process.

### 2. Efficiency
Did the comment surface information that reduced decision costs (e.g., by identifying a
problem that would have required later correction)?

### 3. Equity
Did the comment introduce or address distributional concerns the agency would have missed?

## Implications for the Comment Rubric

A high-quality comment is one that, if adopted, would advance one of these three substantive
ends — not merely one that the commenter is satisfied with submitting.

### Specifically, effective comments:

- **Introduce information unavailable to the agency** (not just signaling preferences)
- **Identify implementation pitfalls** the agency might not see (the MTBE-style downstream
  problems)
- **Surface excluded perspectives** that should be considered for equity reasons
- **Propose changes that reduce overall regulatory cost or increase regulatory benefit**

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment provide information likely to improve substantive rule outcomes?
- [ ] Does the comment identify potential downstream/implementation problems?
- [ ] Does the comment address distributional or equity dimensions?
- [ ] Does the comment propose a change that would improve cost-effectiveness?
- [ ] Does the comment avoid the "consensus trap" — recognizing that broad agreement may
      reflect captured discussion rather than good policy?

## Caution

Coglianese's MTBE example warns that **agreement among elite commenters is not validation**.
Effective rubrics must value comments that disrupt premature consensus by surfacing risks
others overlooked.
