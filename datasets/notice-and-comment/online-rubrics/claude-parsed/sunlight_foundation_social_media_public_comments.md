# Social Media and Public Comments in Rulemaking (Sunlight Foundation)

## Overview
Government increasingly uses Twitter, Facebook, and other channels to engage citizens. ACUS's Committee on Rulemaking, with consultant Michael Herz, explored legal and policy barriers to incorporating social media into rulemaking — encouraging "appropriate and innovative ways to use social media to facilitate broader, more meaningful public participation."

## Main Recommendations
- **Embrace New Participants** — A Cornell Law School study emphasized that agencies must "adapt to what rulemaking newcomers can provide" without dismissing traditional expert evidence.
- **Apply Crowdsourcing Principles** — Reddit's voting mechanism as a model: surface meaningful comments while filtering noise. Could expose officials to diverse public perspectives.
- **Balance Accessibility with Substance** — providing meaningful input channels without overwhelming officials is hard but the benefits outweigh implementation difficulties.

## Civic Tech Framework
Use social media to refine existing platforms like Regulations.gov, creating structured dialogue that highlights experiential knowledge and controversial issues alongside traditional technical expertise.

## Implication for Commenters
Commenters who couple a regulations.gov filing with a public social media thread can amplify and structure the dialogue around their submission, creating a "highlight reel" version of their substance.
