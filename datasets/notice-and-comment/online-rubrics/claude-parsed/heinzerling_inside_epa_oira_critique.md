# Heinzerling, "Inside EPA" (2014)

Former senior EPA official's account of how OMB/OIRA review transformed agency rulemaking.
Heinzerling identifies systemic problems that **commenters can document and challenge** when
addressing significant rules.

## Heinzerling's Diagnosis

OIRA, in her account:
- Demands cost-benefit analysis even where statutes prohibit it
- Operates opaquely — failing to comply with EO transparency requirements
- Diffuses accountability — no one is responsible for what OIRA blocks
- Disproportionately reviews and changes environmental rules

## Implications for Comments on Significant Rules

### 1. Document Statutory Limits on CBA
Some statutes (NAAQS under CAA § 109; FFDCA tolerances) prohibit cost-balancing in
rulemaking. **Comments should explicitly identify when CBA reasoning is statutorily
inappropriate** and argue against its use.

### 2. Demand Transparency on OIRA Engagement
Comments can request:
- Disclosure of all OIRA-EPA communications during interagency review
- The agency's draft rule before OIRA changes
- An explanation of which changes are agency vs. OIRA-driven

### 3. Build a Record on the Pre-OIRA Position
Comments filed before the rule reaches OIRA help establish the agency's pre-OIRA position,
making subsequent changes attributable.

### 4. Engage OIRA Directly During Review
Heinzerling's account suggests that **OIRA-stage engagement is necessary** for those who
want to influence significant rules — agency-stage comments alone are insufficient when OIRA
will rewrite the rule.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment identify whether the rule's statute permits or prohibits cost-balancing?
- [ ] Does the comment demand transparency on OIRA engagement (memos, drafts, meeting logs)?
- [ ] Does the comment build a record sufficient to track changes between draft, NPRM, and
      final rule?
- [ ] Does the commenter consider OIRA-stage engagement separate from agency-stage comments?
- [ ] Does the comment identify substantive changes likely attributable to OIRA review?
- [ ] Does the comment cite EO 12866 transparency requirements where OIRA practices fall
      short?

## Cautions

Heinzerling's critique is contested by defenders of OIRA review (e.g., Sunstein, Revesz).
Comments should engage with the procedural critique without presuming bad faith on the
agency or OIRA staff.
