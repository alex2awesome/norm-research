# Wagner, Barnes & Peters, "Rulemaking in the Shade" (2011)

Empirical study of 90+ EPA air toxic rules across the full lifecycle. Striking findings:

| Stage | Industry | Public Interest |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| Pre-proposal informal communications | 84 / rule | 0.7 / rule |
| Comments filed | 81% | 4% |
| Changes favoring side after notice-and-comment | 4× | 1× |

## Implications for Effective Public-Interest Comments

### 1. Pre-Proposal Engagement Is Where the Rule Is Built
Industry's 120× advantage in pre-proposal engagement explains why final rules so often
favor industry. **A public-interest comment must therefore engage the agency before the NPRM**
through:
- Petitions for rulemaking
- Sign-on letters from technical experts
- ANPRM submissions
- Direct meetings with rule writers
- FOIA requests during pre-proposal development

### 2. Quality Engagement Means Repeated Substantive Touches
Industry's 84 contacts per rule are not formal comments but technical exchanges with rule
writers. **Public-interest comments should aspire to similar substantive depth** — providing
research, data, modeling, and personnel availability for follow-up.

### 3. Document the Asymmetry on the Record
Comments should explicitly note **what data the agency relied on came from industry**,
demand disclosure of pre-proposal communications, and call for parallel public-interest
input. This builds a record for hard-look review.

### 4. Quantify Agency-Industry Convergence
Comments should compare proposed rule language to industry pre-proposal positions and
**flag where the agency adopted industry framing** without independent justification.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment engage with pre-proposal exchanges between agency and industry (FOIA,
      records of meetings)?
- [ ] Does the comment provide public-interest counter-data of comparable technical depth to
      industry submissions?
- [ ] Does the comment identify which agency assumptions originated with industry?
- [ ] Does the comment make available follow-up technical engagement (expert availability,
      modeling support)?
- [ ] Does the comment build a record on the **asymmetry** of stakeholder engagement?
