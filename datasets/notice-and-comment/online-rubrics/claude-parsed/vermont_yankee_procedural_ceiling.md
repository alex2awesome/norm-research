# Vermont Yankee - The APA Procedural Ceiling for Notice-and-Comment

## Holding

Vermont Yankee Nuclear Power Corp. v. NRDC (1978) held that lower courts could not impose procedural requirements on administrative agencies beyond those specified in the Administrative Procedure Act (APA).

The Court held that Congress had established the **maximum procedures** that may be required of an agency undertaking informal rulemaking, which are established by section 553 of the APA, and courts generally are prohibited from imposing additional procedures upon agencies.

## Floor vs. Ceiling

The procedural framework can be understood as creating both a floor and a ceiling for rulemaking requirements. In rejecting the view that APA § 553 merely sets the procedural floor that courts can require, Vermont Yankee cited legislative history specific to the APA. This suggests that Vermont Yankee established that the **APA requirements represent the ceiling of mandatory procedures**—agencies cannot be required to do more than what the APA specifies, though they may choose to do so voluntarily.

## Implications for Comment Effectiveness

- Agencies have broad discretion in HOW they consider comments
- Cross-examination, formal hearings, etc. cannot be required by courts
- The duty to respond comes from State Farm/hard-look doctrine, not from procedural innovation
- Comments must work within the basic § 553 framework: notice, opportunity to comment, concise general statement
- Agencies' procedural innovations (e.g., online docket discussions) are voluntary and can vary

## What This Means for Commenters

- Don't expect a court to force agencies to consider comments more carefully than § 553 requires
- Substantive content matters more than demanding additional process
- The ceiling principle means agency response can be terse if defensible
- Comments should be self-sufficient: don't rely on procedural follow-up
