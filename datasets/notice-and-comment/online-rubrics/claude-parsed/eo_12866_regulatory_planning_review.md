# Executive Order 12866: Regulatory Planning and Review (Clinton, 1993)

## Overview

Executive Order 12866 was issued by President William J. Clinton in 1993. It provides that significant regulatory actions be submitted for review to the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA) in the Office of Management and Budget (OMB).

## Objectives

The objectives of this Executive order are to:
- Enhance planning and coordination with respect to both new and existing regulations
- Reaffirm the primacy of Federal agencies in the regulatory decision-making process
- Restore the integrity and legitimacy of regulatory review and oversight
- Make the process more accessible and open to the public

## Key Regulatory Principles (Used as Comment Rubric)

The Order established a "regulatory philosophy" and several "principles for regulation":

1. **Identify the problem to be addressed** - Comments should explain what real problem the rule addresses
2. **Determine whether existing regulations created/contributed to the problem** - Address market vs. regulatory failures
3. **Assess alternatives to direct regulation** - Information disclosure, economic incentives, performance standards
4. **Design regulations in the most cost-effective manner possible**
5. **Use best available science and technical information**
6. **Tailor regulations to impose least burden on society**
7. **Avoid inconsistencies with other regulations**

## Cost-Benefit Standard

EO 12866 requires a cost-benefit analysis for any new regulation that is "economically significant," defined as having an annual effect on the economy of $100 million or more, or adversely affecting in a material way the economy, a sector of the economy, productivity, competition, or jobs.

## Implications for Public Comments

Comments are most influential when they engage with these principles - especially when they:
- Provide information about benefits/costs the agency hasn't quantified
- Identify regulatory alternatives the agency hasn't considered
- Challenge the problem definition
- Show inconsistencies with other rules
- Provide better data than the agency's RIA
