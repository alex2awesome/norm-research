# Union of Concerned Scientists: Federal Rulemaking Participation Guide

## Available Resources

The organization provides five primary guides:

1. **Inside the Federal Rulemaking Process** - Explains how federal agencies develop implementing rules
2. **How to Participate in Federal Rulemaking** - Guidance on making your voice heard and using science in the process
3. **A Citizen's Guide to the Federal Regulatory Process** - Detailed guide covering rulemaking, permitting, and enforcement
4. **How to Testify at a Government Hearing** - Structured approach to oral public comments
5. **Fair Access: Guidance for Meaningful Public Participation** - Recommendations for ensuring equitable access to government decision-making

## Core Principles

UCS emphasizes that "submitting a public comment to the record is an important way to make your voice heard in the policymaking process." The organization believes "laws and regulations would be improved if they were better backed by science."

## Key Focus Areas

The guides address:
- Writing effective written comments
- Testifying at public hearings
- Understanding permitting and enforcement decisions
- Ensuring frontline communities can participate meaningfully

UCS positions public participation as essential to helping agencies "develop the best regulations possible" and ensuring scientific expertise informs policy decisions.

## Key Comment Quality Criteria (UCS Standard)

For science-informed comments, UCS recommends:
- Cite peer-reviewed studies
- Distinguish between consensus science and emerging research
- Acknowledge uncertainty ranges
- Critique the agency's scientific evidence base where appropriate
- Suggest specific scientific updates the rule should incorporate
- Identify environmental justice/equity dimensions of the science
