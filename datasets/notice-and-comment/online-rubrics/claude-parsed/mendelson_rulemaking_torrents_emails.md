# Mendelson, "Rulemaking, Democracy, and Torrents of E-Mail" (2011)

Mendelson argues that agencies have systematically discounted mass and value-laden comments,
treating only "technical" comments as worthy of consideration. She contends this approach
undermines democratic legitimacy and proposes that agencies must give weight to
**value preferences** expressed even in mass comment campaigns.

## Mendelson's Distinction Between Comment Types

| Comment Type | Conventional Agency Treatment | Mendelson's Argument |
|--------------|-------------------------------|----------------------|
| Technical/scientific | Heavily weighted | Should remain weighted but not exclusive |
| Value-laden / policy preferences | Systematically discounted | Should receive consideration; values matter |
| Mass campaign (form letters) | Often dismissed as one comment | Should at least trigger investigation |
| Individual lay comments with values | Discounted as uninformed | Should be assessed on substance |

## Implications for What a Good Comment Should Do

A comment that is value-laden but well-formed should:

- [ ] **Make explicit the underlying value or policy preference** at stake
- [ ] **Connect that value to the statutory authority** (e.g., "the CAA's purpose is public health,
      and this proposed rule subordinates health to industry cost")
- [ ] **Articulate why the agency is required to weigh that value** (statutory text, legislative
      history, Executive Orders)
- [ ] **Indicate the scope/scale** of those who share the value preference (e.g., "this comment
      reflects the views of X residents living within Y miles of facilities")

## Why Mass Comments Should Trigger Investigation (per Mendelson)

Even if duplicative, a torrent of comments may constitute a **fire alarm** that the agency has
mis-estimated public preferences. Effective mass-comment design therefore should:

- Include at least some unique factual or value content per comment to be coded as substantive
- Direct commenters to a specific decision the agency must make
- Provide the agency with a quantifiable basis for further inquiry (sample sizes, geographic
  distribution, demographics)

## Caution

Mendelson is candid that her piece is "an initial foray" — not a full rubric. She advances
the **principle** that values comments matter; the operationalization is left to others
(e.g., Farina, Cuéllar, Coglianese).
