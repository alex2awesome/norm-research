# Coglianese, "The Internet and Citizen Participation in Rulemaking" (2004)

Coglianese argues that **technology alone does not democratize rulemaking** — the deeper
obstacles to citizen participation are interest, expertise, and time. Effective participation
requires more than digital access; it requires substantive engagement skills.

## The Three Underlying Obstacles to Citizen Participation

1. **Interest** — Most citizens are unaware of pending rules or their relevance. Effective
   comments require the commenter to be paying attention to issues that affect them.
2. **Expertise** — Technical rules require technical engagement. Most citizens lack the
   subject-matter knowledge to engage meaningfully.
3. **Time** — Drafting a substantive comment takes hours. Most citizens have other obligations.

## What Effective Citizen Comments Do (Per Coglianese's Analysis)

To overcome the three barriers, effective citizen comments:

### Compensate for Limited Expertise Through Specificity
- Anchor to a specific paragraph or provision
- Describe a concrete experience or impact
- Avoid attempting to engage agency-wide policy debates beyond the commenter's knowledge

### Compensate for Limited Time Through Focus
- Make a single, clear request
- Provide one well-developed factual basis
- Resist boilerplate or copying mass-comment text

### Demonstrate Genuine Interest
- Identify the commenter's stake (resident, worker, family member, profession)
- Indicate why this rule matters to the commenter specifically
- Describe what the commenter has personally observed

## Coglianese's Skepticism of Technology Solutions

Coglianese cautions against assuming digital tools (online dockets, comment portals) will
increase participation quality. **Substantive participation requires the agency to provide:**

- Plain-language summaries
- Structured comment forms eliciting specific information
- Feedback to commenters about how comments were used
- Engagement with diverse formats (narrative, video, in-person testimony)

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment identify the commenter's specific stake/interest?
- [ ] Does the comment focus on a single, well-developed point rather than attempting broad
      coverage?
- [ ] Does the comment anchor to a specific provision of the proposed rule?
- [ ] Does the comment demonstrate the commenter has read the proposal (not just an
      advocacy summary)?
- [ ] Is the comment unique in voice and content (not boilerplate or mass-campaign text)?
