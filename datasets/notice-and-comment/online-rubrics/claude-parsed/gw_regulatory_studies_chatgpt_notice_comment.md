# Will ChatGPT Break Notice and Comment for Regulations?

GW Regulatory Studies Center analysis of how generative AI affects public commenting.

## Key Question

Combining generative AI with mass comment campaigns could lead to comments that look less duplicative as the same idea could be expressed in many different ways, which will strain current agency tools and likely increase resources dedicated to comment analysis.

## Effects on Agency Practice

- Existing dedup tools may become less effective
- Resources dedicated to comment analysis must increase
- Substantive vs. low-information comment distinction becomes harder
- Agency response burden increases proportionally
- Comments may look "personalized" without being so

## Effects on Comment Effectiveness

### For Sophisticated Commenters
- AI can help draw out specific details
- Can substantiate comments with research
- Can tell stories more compellingly
- Helps non-native English speakers and those uncomfortable with professional English

### Risks for Comment Process Integrity
- Mass campaigns become harder to detect
- The line between "form letter with personalization" and "substantive original comment" blurs
- Agencies may adopt new authentication or content-distinctness standards

## Implications for Effective Comments (Post-LLM Era)

A truly effective comment that won't be confused with AI-mass-generated content should:
- Include specific personal details that AI can't fabricate (specific dates, places, names of relevant officials)
- Reference the unique factual circumstances of the commenter
- Cite documents the commenter has personally reviewed
- Make arguments tied to specific data the commenter has access to
- Engage with the agency's specific questions in ways that show genuine understanding

## Future Detection Approaches

Agencies are likely to:
- Develop better duplicate-detection algorithms
- Implement identity verification (CAPTCHA, email confirmation)
- Cluster comments by semantic similarity (not just exact match)
- Cross-reference commenter identity with claimed credentials

## Open Questions

- Will ChatGPT help create sufficiently distinctive comments that elude agency tools designed to identify mass comment campaigns?
- Should agencies treat AI-assisted comments the same as fully human comments?
- What disclosure should be required when AI generates substantial portions of a comment?
