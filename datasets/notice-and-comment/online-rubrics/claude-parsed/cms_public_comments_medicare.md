# CMS Guide for Writing Public Comments

## Key Tips for Writing Effective Comments

### State Your Position Clearly
Clearly indicate if you are for, or against, the proposed regulation or some part of it and why. It is helpful to structure comments based on the order of the rule and cite the section under which your comment belongs, with headings.

### Focus on Sound Reasoning and Evidence
CMS regulatory decisions are based largely on law, clinical and scientific evidence, and program experience. CMS reviewers look for **reasoning, logic, and good science** in the comments they evaluate. Include a copy of articles or other references that support your comments. Only relevant material should be submitted.

Public comments citing **published, methodologically strong clinical evidence** that assesses the relative magnitude of an intervention or technology's benefits and risk of harm to Medicare beneficiaries or is generalizable to the Medicare population often give CMS instructive and valuable information.

### Quality Over Quantity
Agencies make determinations for a proposed action based on sound reasoning and scientific evidence rather than a majority of votes. **A single, well-supported comment may carry more weight than a thousand form letters.**

### Protect Privacy
To protect privacy when submitting medical information, remove names or other information that would identify patients.

## How to Submit Comments

CMS migrated regulations to the Federal Dockets Management System (FDMS) at Regulations.gov in 2008. CMS continues to accept regulatory comments via mail or courier.

## Comment Periods

Usually, the docket stays open for comments at least 60 days for regulations, though some comment periods may differ.

## Specific Recommendations for CMS-Style Comments

- Cite Medicare statutes (42 U.S.C. § 1395, etc.) and CMS sub-regulatory guidance
- Engage with prior CMS reasoning in the preamble
- Quantify provider/beneficiary impact
- Address Medicare populations (rural, dual-eligibles, specific conditions)
- Recommend specific regulatory language changes
- Distinguish between coding/billing implementation and substantive coverage policy
