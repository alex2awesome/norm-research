# Loper Bright v. Raimondo (2024) - Implications for Public Comment Strategy

## Overview

On June 28, 2024, the U.S. Supreme Court overturned four decades of judicial precedent that required deference to federal agency interpretations of ambiguous or absent statutory text. In Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo, the 6-to-3 decision declared the 1984 precedent set in Chevron USA, Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council "is overruled."

## Holding

The Court held that the Administrative Procedure Act requires courts to exercise **"independent judgment"** in determining whether an agency's actions align with its statutory authority. Loper Bright removes Chevron's deference but retains the ability of agencies to offer persuasive arguments that their interpretation is the right reading of the statute. In the end, the court decides the best reading of an ambiguous or silent statute.

## Effects on Agency Rulemaking

The decision will require agencies to provide more compelling justifications for their rulemaking. As such, agencies may further emphasize transparency and public participation in the rulemaking process to build a stronger administrative record.

Agencies may become more conservative with their rulemaking—they can no longer count on absolute judicial deference. They may take greater pains to express the reasoning underlying their interpretation, in hope of convincing a reviewing court to adopt their interpretation.

## Implications for Comment Strategy (Post-Loper Bright)

### For Industry/Regulated Entities
- More opportunities to challenge final rules in court
- Comments should preserve **statutory interpretation arguments** for appeal
- Emphasis on the "best reading" of the statute, not just reasonable
- Cite Skidmore-style persuasive authority arguments
- Build the record showing agency interpretation is wrong as a matter of statutory construction

### For Public Interest Commenters
- Defending strong rules requires building stronger statutory authority arguments
- Comments should support agency's "best reading" not just "reasonable reading"
- Engage with statutory text, structure, history more deeply
- Cite legislative history, related statutes, traditional interpretive canons

### For All Commenters
- Statutory interpretation arguments are now more central
- The administrative record becomes the basis for de novo statutory review
- Fact-based comments still essential for the policy record
- Comments should distinguish statutory authority issues from policy choice issues

## Comment Rubric Updates Post-Loper Bright

A high-quality post-Loper comment:
1. Engages with statutory text using interpretive canons (textualism, purposivism)
2. Cites legislative history when relevant
3. Identifies statutory ambiguities the agency should resolve through best reading
4. Anticipates judicial review framework (Skidmore deference factors)
5. Builds factual record supporting policy choices independently of deference
6. Addresses Major Questions Doctrine considerations for significant rules
7. Notes congressional acquiescence/repudiation patterns where relevant
