# Krawiec, "Don't 'Screw Joe the Plummer'" (2013)

Empirical analysis of ~8,000 public comments to the Financial Stability Oversight Council on
Volcker Rule implementation. Krawiec's dramatic finding: **citizens dominated numerically
(7,831 of 8,000 comments) but their input was almost entirely useless to decision-makers**
because 7,316 were identical brief form letters with no data or analysis.

## The Asymmetry Krawiec Documents

| Source | Volume | Content |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Citizens (form letters) | 7,316 | Brief, no data, no analysis |
| Citizens (substantive) | ~515 | Variable quality |
| Financial institutions | ~150 | Lengthy, cogent legal arguments, statutory citations, legislative history, empirical data |

## Why Financial Institution Comments Won

Krawiec quotes the financial institution submissions as featuring:
- **Detailed legal arguments** relying on numerous statutes and cases
- **Reference to Dodd-Frank legislative history**
- **Thorough empirical data**
- Generally narrow interpretation of the rule's scope

## Implications: What Public-Interest Comments Need to Match

For non-industry comments to compete:

### 1. Match Industry Length and Substance
Industry comments are extensive because they need to be. **Public-interest comments cannot
win on volume alone — they must match substantive content** with similar depth.

### 2. Use Statutory and Legal Analysis
Industry comments cite statutes, cases, and legislative history. **Effective non-industry
comments must do the same** — citing the statute the rule implements and authorities
supporting the public-interest reading.

### 3. Provide Empirical Data
Industry comments come with data. **Public-interest commenters need empirical evidence too**
— not necessarily as much, but enough to be credible.

### 4. Build a Coalition of Substantive Submissions
Krawiec's finding doesn't mean public-interest sides should give up but should recognize that
**515 substantive citizen comments outweighed 7,316 form letters**. Coordinated, substantive
public-interest comments can compete with industry — form letters cannot.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment include legal analysis citing relevant statutes and authorities?
- [ ] Does the comment include empirical data or evidence?
- [ ] Is the comment substantively distinguished from form-letter content?
- [ ] Does the comment engage the legislative history or statutory purpose?
- [ ] Is the comment part of a coordinated set of substantive public-interest submissions
      rather than a form-letter campaign?
- [ ] Does the comment match (in some dimension) the analytical depth of likely industry
      submissions?

## The Lesson From "Joe the Plumber"

The eponymous comment ("don't let big banks screw joe the plummer") captured the citizen
campaign — and was useless. **Sincerity and volume are not enough**. Effective public-interest
comments need substantive content; agencies and OIRA reviewers cannot use raw frustration as
input.
