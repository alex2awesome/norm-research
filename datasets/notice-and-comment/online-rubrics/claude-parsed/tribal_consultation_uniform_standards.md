# Uniform Standards for Tribal Consultation

## Overview

Federal agencies are required to engage in government-to-government consultation with federally recognized Indian Tribes on matters with Tribal implications. This is distinct from notice-and-comment but often runs in parallel.

## Key Standards

### Notice and Time
- 30 days' notice of consultation is the standard
- When time constraints prevent 30 days' notice, the notice should explain why
- Upon a tribe's request, agencies should consider adjusting deadlines for notice and for accepting written comments
- When practicable, a comment period of **60 to 120 days** will be provided, to allow sufficient time for Tribal Officials to consult with their members and legal counsel

### Definition
Tribal Consultation involves the **direct, timely, and interactive process** of receiving input from Indian Tribes regarding proposed Treasury actions on Policies that have Tribal Implications.

### Outcomes Disclosure
The head of each agency shall **timely disclose** to the affected Tribe or Tribes the outcome of the consultation and decisions made as a result of the consultation.

### Accountability Record
The Division shall maintain a consultation record that includes:
- A summary of the Tribal input received
- A general explanation of how the Tribal input received influenced or was incorporated

## Implications for Tribal Comments

For Tribal commenters, effective comments:
- Identify the Tribal government and authorized representative
- Cite Tribal sovereignty principles
- Reference specific federal trust obligations
- Address the Tribal Implications dimension explicitly
- Cite federal trust statutes (Indian Self-Determination Act, etc.)
- Provide traditional ecological knowledge where relevant
- Address consultation procedural adequacy as well as substance
- Distinguish from individual citizen comments through government-to-government framing

## Distinct from APA Notice-and-Comment

Tribal consultation is a separate statutory and policy obligation:
- EO 13175 (Consultation and Coordination With Indian Tribal Governments)
- Each agency's tribal consultation policy
- Government-to-government, not stakeholder-to-agency
