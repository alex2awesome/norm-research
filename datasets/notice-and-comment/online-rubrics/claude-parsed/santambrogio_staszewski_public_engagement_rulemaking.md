# Public Engagement in Rulemaking (Sant'Ambrogio & Staszewski, The Regulatory Review)

## Core Arguments
Federal agencies rely too heavily on notice-and-comment, which may be "dominated by certain sophisticated stakeholders." Meaningful participation requires expanding beyond the minimum requirement. Agency rule development has been treated as a "black box," but agencies do engage publics during agenda-setting and early development — these efforts are unstructured and ad hoc.

## Key Recommendations
- **Strategic Planning & Early Engagement** — formal engagement policies; plan participation as early as feasible, including problem identification and priority-setting.
- **Targeted Outreach** — "not everyone reads the *Federal Register*"; agencies must proactively reach traditionally absent stakeholders.
- **Three supplemental tools**: (1) information requests / advance notices before formal proposals; (2) targeted outreach to experts and non-traditional participants; (3) convening diverse groups for dialogue on regulatory alternatives.

## What Makes Participation Effective
- Personnel trained in public engagement best practices
- Carefully structured meetings with accessible background materials
- Strategic assessment of what information stakeholders lack at each stage
- Cost-effective tool selection based on potential information value

The ultimate goal is "democratizing the regulatory process" through comprehensive information gathering.

## Implication for Commenters
If you submit during the formal comment phase only, you've missed the high-leverage windows. Watch for ANPRMs, RFIs, and informal listening sessions and engage upstream when the agency hasn't committed to a frame.
