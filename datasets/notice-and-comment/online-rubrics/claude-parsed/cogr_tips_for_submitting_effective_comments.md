# COGR Tips for Submitting Effective Comments

## Overview

The Council on Governmental Relations (COGR), an association of research universities, distributes a tip sheet on submitting effective comments — adapted from the regulations.gov canonical guide. This version is widely used by university research offices when responding to federal proposed rules affecting research.

## Key Tips

### Understand the Regulation Before Commenting
Most regulations have an introduction and background section where the agency lays out the purpose of the regulation and any applicable laws.

### Take a Clear Position
Clearly state whether you agree with or disagree with the proposed rule (or an aspect of it).

### Be Specific and Detailed
Constructive, detailed comments (whether positive or negative) are most helpful.

### Provide an Alternative
If you disagree with a proposed action, suggest an alternative (including not regulating at all) and include an explanation of how the alternative might meet the same objective or be more effective.

### Support Your Arguments
Agency reviewers look for sound science and reasoning in the comments they receive.

### Address Specific Issues
Provide the page number, column, and/or paragraph from the Federal Register if you are commenting on a particular word, phrase, or sentence.

### Plan Ahead
The comment period closes at 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on the date comments are due.

## University Research Comment Strategy

For research universities, effective comments on proposed rules:
- Identify the research community impacted (PIs, postdocs, students)
- Quantify research dollars affected
- Address compliance burden distinct from research mission
- Cite specific applicable circulars (Uniform Guidance 2 CFR 200, Common Rule 45 CFR 46)
- Engage with research integrity considerations
- Address impacts on specific research domains (clinical trials, animal research, classified research, dual-use research)
- Coordinate with peer institutions for sign-on letters
- Provide compliance officer perspective on implementation feasibility
