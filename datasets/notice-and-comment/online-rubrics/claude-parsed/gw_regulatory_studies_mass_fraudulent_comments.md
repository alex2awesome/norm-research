# GW Regulatory Studies Center: Mass, Computer-Generated, and Fraudulent Comments

## Report Overview
The Regulatory Studies Center at George Washington University published a comprehensive report on June 2, 2021, examining three problematic forms of commenting in federal rulemaking processes.

## Three Comment Types Analyzed

**Mass Comments:** These occur when agencies receive substantially higher volumes than typical, particularly through "mass comment campaigns" where organizations coordinate submission of "identical or nearly identical comments."

**Fraudulent Comments:** The report uses the term "malattributed comments" to describe submissions "falsely attributed to persons by whom they were not, in fact, submitted."

**Computer-Generated Comments:** These are "generated not by humans, but rather by software algorithms," removing the need for human content creation.

## Key Challenges Identified

The report identifies three cross-cutting problems:
- Difficulty extracting useful information from such comments
- Risks to public trust in specific rules and the overall rulemaking process
- Resource demands on regulatory agencies

Type-specific issues include legal concerns under the Administrative Procedure Act, potential deception of agencies, and challenges distinguishing algorithmic from human submissions.

## Recommendations
The report concludes with agency recommendations addressing technology implementation, inter-agency coordination, and docket management strategies.
