# Guide to Writing Effective Public Comments (Environmental Data and Governance Initiative)

## Key Recommendations

**1. Assess Your Strategy**
Before submitting, evaluate whether a public comment best serves your goals. Agencies hold public meetings, request information on certain topics, react to pressure from U.S. legislators, and seek comment on guidelines and plans. Consider alternative engagement methods for your specific situation.

**2. Develop Specific Arguments**
Avoid vague statements about general concepts. Instead, directly address the proposed rule with detailed reasoning and supporting evidence that agencies haven't already considered.

**3. Contribute Original Insights**
Submit novel arguments, data, or analyses with technical and legal foundations. Keep content relevant to the specific rule—extraneous information may be overlooked. Review existing regulatory analyses (like Regulatory Impacts Analysis documents) to verify your arguments' validity and originality.

**4. Enhance Readability**
Write clearly, recognizing that contractors using specialized software often review comments. Subtlety gets lost; explicit clarity increases comprehension and impact.

**5. Reference Specific Rule Sections**
Clearly identify the section(s) of the rule your comment is addressing. Use Federal Register labels and features, or include section numbers and agency questions from the proposed rule.

**6. Include Proper Citations**
Format references so staff can easily locate sources. Make non-accessible data publicly available through cited websites or attached files in accessible formats.

**7. Differentiate Your Comment**
Mass letter campaigns typically count as single submissions for consideration. Original comments have greater influence on specific rules.

**8. Seek Clarification**
Contact agency staff members listed in documents if you need rule explanations.
