# Net Neutrality Fake Comments — Practitioner Postmortem (NextGov / NY AG report)

## What Happened
The FCC's docket on the 2017 net neutrality repeal generated more than 22 million comments — more than 8.5 million of which came from a campaign led by broadband providers. 7 million additional comments were traced to a computer science student using computer-generated names and addresses.

## Mechanics of the Fakes
"Unsophisticated 'search and replace' techniques" — the same base comment repeated with key words swapped each time. This made the fakes easy to detect and separate from organic feedback.

## What the Authentic Record Showed
Of 22 million comments, 17 million (~77%) appeared to support repeal. After researchers stripped likely fakes, only ~800,000 authentic responses remained — and **the vast majority (up to 99% in some samples) supported keeping net neutrality**. Authentic public sentiment was the opposite of the headline numbers.

## Implications for Effective Commenting
- Mass form-letter campaigns are routinely treated as a single comment by agencies — their political weight is dwarfed by their numerical appearance.
- "Real" voices stand out far more in datasets dominated by template fraud — original, identifiable, locally specific comments rise above the noise.
- Agencies and courts increasingly inspect comment authenticity; submissions that *demonstrate* personal engagement (specifics, dated facts, named experiences) gain credibility.
- The post-2017 enforcement actions against three companies for generating fake comments raised the legal cost of astroturfing.
