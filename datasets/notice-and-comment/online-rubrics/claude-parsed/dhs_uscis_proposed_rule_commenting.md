# DHS / USCIS Guide on Commenting on Proposed Rules

## Where to Submit Comments

Comments can only be submitted online through the Federal Register document page by clicking "Submit a Public Comment." You may submit comments through the Federal eRulemaking Portal at regulations.gov, identified by the specific DHS Docket number.

## Comment Format and Requirements

You can either:
- Enter your comment in the text box (must be fewer than **5,000 characters**)
- Upload your comment as a **text-searchable PDF** (PDF submission is recommended)

Comments must be submitted in English, or an English translation must be provided.

## What to Include

Comments that will provide the most assistance to USCIS will:
- Reference a specific portion of the proposed rule
- Explain the reason for any recommended change
- Include data, information, or authority that support such recommended change

DHS invites all interested parties to submit written data, views, comments and arguments on all aspects of the proposed rule, and also invites comments relating to economic, environmental, or federalism effects that might result from the proposed rule.

## Important Notes

- Comments submitted by email or mail, or hand-delivered/couriered, **will not be considered** comments on the proposed rule and may not receive a response from DHS.
- Any comments should be **unique**, as DHS may bundle any comments that are too similar to each other.

## Effective DHS/USCIS Comments

For immigration rules, effective comments:
- Identify the affected immigrant population (visa categories, status types)
- Include personal/family stories where appropriate
- Cite specific INA sections being implemented
- Address economic impact (employer/employee, US economy, immigrant communities)
- Engage with Service Center processing capacity issues
- Cite peer-reviewed research on immigration impacts
- Note disparate impact on particular nationalities/regions
- Address legal challenges to similar prior rules
- Reference federal court decisions (DACA, TPS, public charge cases)
