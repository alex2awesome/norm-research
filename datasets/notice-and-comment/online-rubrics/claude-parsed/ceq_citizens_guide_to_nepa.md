# CEQ Citizen's Guide to NEPA: Having Your Voice Heard

## Overview

The Council on Environmental Quality publishes "A Citizen's Guide to NEPA" to help the public understand and participate in the federal environmental review process under the National Environmental Policy Act.

## Three Phases of NEPA Review

1. **Categorical Exclusion (CE)** - Routine actions not requiring full review
2. **Environmental Assessment (EA)** - Brief assessment determining whether full EIS needed
3. **Environmental Impact Statement (EIS)** - Comprehensive review for major federal actions with significant environmental impacts

## Key Engagement Points

### Scoping (Earliest)
- Notice of Intent published in Federal Register
- Public meetings and written comment opportunities
- Most influence: this stage shapes what alternatives and impacts are studied

### Draft EIS (Substantive)
- Minimum 45-day comment period
- Comments inform Final EIS
- Agency must respond to substantive comments

### Final EIS (Limited)
- 30-day waiting period before Record of Decision
- Comments can be received but typically less impact

### Record of Decision (Implementation)
- Documents the chosen alternative
- Comments at this stage are typically procedural

## Tips for Effective NEPA Comments (CEQ Guidance)

- Be specific about the impact you're concerned about
- Identify physical, biological, social, economic, or cultural resources
- Suggest specific alternatives
- Offer mitigation measures
- Provide relevant data, studies, or local knowledge
- Identify procedural concerns (notice, comment opportunity, etc.)
- Cite EJSCREEN or other environmental data tools
- Address cumulative impacts (this project + other projects)

## What Counts as a "Substantive" NEPA Comment

A substantive comment:
- Questions, with reasonable basis, the accuracy of information in the EIS
- Questions, with reasonable basis, the adequacy of methodology
- Presents new information relevant to analysis
- Presents reasonable alternatives that vary from those analyzed
- Causes changes or revisions in the proposal

A non-substantive comment:
- Merely expresses preference (support/oppose)
- Repeats issues already addressed
- Lacks specific factual support
