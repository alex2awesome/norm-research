# Sunstein, "Simpler: The Future of Government" (2013)

Sunstein, former OIRA Administrator, argues that effective regulation depends on
**simplification, plain language, readable summaries, and clear guidance**. For commenters,
this implies that comments designed to influence OIRA-reviewed rules should mirror these
norms.

## Sunstein's Eight Rules for Simpler Government (Adapted for Commenters)

1. **Speak in plain English** — Avoid jargon; if technical terms are necessary, define them.
2. **Make the request unambiguous** — Tell the agency exactly what to change.
3. **Provide readable summaries** — Lead with a summary, follow with detail.
4. **Reduce cognitive burden** — Structure the comment so a busy reader can navigate it.
5. **Use defaults wisely** — Where the rule sets defaults, propose better defaults.
6. **Use disclosure** — Demand or provide better disclosures where the rule is opaque.
7. **Use behavioral insights** — Reference behavioral economics where relevant to predicted
   responses.
8. **Connect to costs and benefits** — Frame the comment to support the agency's CBA.

## Implications for Comments to Significant Rules (OIRA-Reviewed)

Significant rules are reviewed by OIRA, which assesses cost-benefit analysis. **Effective
comments on significant rules:**

- Engage with the agency's RIA (regulatory impact analysis) directly
- Identify deficiencies in cost or benefit estimates with specific numbers
- Propose alternatives with their own cost-benefit characterization
- Address behavioral assumptions in the agency's analysis
- Use plain-language summaries OIRA staff can quickly digest

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Is the comment written in plain English (or with terms defined)?
- [ ] Does the comment lead with a clear summary of the requested change?
- [ ] Does the comment engage with the agency's cost-benefit analysis directly?
- [ ] Does the comment propose alternatives with their cost-benefit consequences?
- [ ] Does the comment make a specific, unambiguous request?
- [ ] Does the comment address behavioral assumptions where relevant?

## Caution

Sunstein's framework is friendly to cost-benefit reasoning. Comments that resist that frame
(e.g., on equity or dignitary grounds) should still be **structured for readability** even
if they challenge the dominant analytical approach.
