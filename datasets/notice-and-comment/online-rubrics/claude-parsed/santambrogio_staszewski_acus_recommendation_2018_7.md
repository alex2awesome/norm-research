# ACUS Recommendation 2018-7: Public Engagement in Rulemaking

## Overview

Michael Sant'Ambrogio and Glen Staszewski (Michigan State College of Law) prepared the underlying study. ACUS adopted Recommendation 2018-7 based on their research.

## Key Findings

Their research found that public engagement in rulemaking improves the quality, legitimacy, and accountability of agency decision making.

In conducting their research, the authors spoke with numerous agency officials who described significant efforts to engage the public during agenda setting and rule development, with **most agency engagement with the public beginning long before the publication of a notice of proposed rulemaking**.

## Core Recommendations

The ACUS recommendation strongly emphasizes:
- **Strategic planning** for public engagement
- **Targeted outreach** to traditionally absent stakeholders
- **Reaching beyond the usual suspects** who comment on proposed rules
- Adopting "general policies for public engagement in their rulemakings"
- Planning for public participation in specific rulemaking proceedings as early as possible

## Identified Barriers

- Agencies' engagement efforts tend to be **unstructured and ad hoc**
- Affirmative, carefully structured efforts could encourage greater participation by traditionally absent stakeholders
- Rulemaking novices face significant barriers to participation

## Implications for What Counts as "Effective" Public Comment

From the agency-side perspective:
- Comments from underrepresented stakeholders are more highly valued
- Engagement at scoping/pre-NPRM is more influential than NPRM-only
- Structured engagement (workshops, listening sessions) produces better comments than open dockets
- Plain-language framing of comments matters
- Demographic diversity of commenters supports rule legitimacy
