# Steptoe & Johnson: How to Write a Better Comment Letter on Agency Proposals

By Matt Kulkin and Josh Oppenheimer, Law360 (September 2, 2020).

## Six Key Tips

**1. Address the actual proposed rule text**
Make sure your comments address the proposed rule text, which is the most important part of the proposal as it has the potential to become regulation.

**2. Engage with the preamble**
Review the preamble that precedes the proposed rule text. The preamble explains the agency's rationale and reveals which arguments will resonate.

**3. Reference specific sections**
An effective comment letter should refer to particular sections of the proposal, including specific proposed regulatory text and relevant statutes to make it easy for staff to categorize comments with others on the same topic.

**4. Keep it focused**
Keep the letter focused and not overly verbose, remembering that staff will review many letters, and the easier it is to lift something from your letter, the more effective it will be.

**5. Offer alternatives**
Follow critiques with alternative solutions—any criticism of a proposed rule should be followed with an appropriate alternative solution, providing specific amendments to the language if you oppose particular rule text.

**6. Meet the deadline**
Meet the Federal Register deadline for comments submissions, which is usually 30 to 90 days.

## Practitioner Insights

The article emphasizes that comments are most effective when they:
- Make staff's job easier (via clear organization, specific section citations)
- Provide ready-to-adopt regulatory language alternatives
- Frame arguments in terms the agency can use in its preamble response
- Avoid overly broad or political framings

## Summary

Effective comment letters function as drafting aids for the agency, providing specific text that can be incorporated or used as a basis for the agency's reasoning in the final rule.
