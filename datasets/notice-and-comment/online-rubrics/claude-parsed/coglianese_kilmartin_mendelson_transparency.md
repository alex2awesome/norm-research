# Coglianese, Kilmartin & Mendelson, "Transparency and Public Participation in the Federal Rulemaking Process" (2009)

Report of a nonpartisan Task Force convened to identify deficiencies in agency rulemaking
procedures. Recommendations are organized into three categories: **transparency**,
**public participation**, and **strategic management**. The report's underlying frame is
that comments serve **substantive quality** and **procedural legitimacy**.

## What Effective Comments Should Demonstrate (Per the Task Force Frame)

### 1. Substantive Quality Contribution
Comments should add information, analysis, or perspective that improves the rule's likely
effectiveness. The Task Force identifies:
- Empirical evidence the agency lacks
- Identification of unintended consequences
- Alternative regulatory text
- Implementation expertise from regulated entities or beneficiaries

### 2. Procedural Legitimacy Contribution
Comments should reflect the **breadth of affected interests**, not just well-organized ones.
The Task Force urges agencies to reach out earlier and more evenhandedly.
*Implication for commenters*: A comment from an under-represented constituency carries unique
legitimacy weight and should make explicit who it represents.

## Task Force Recommendations Relevant to Comment Quality

### Earlier Engagement
- Comments at the ANPRM or pre-proposal stage are more impactful
- Petitions for rulemaking are an underused vehicle
- Direct stakeholder meetings during rule development add weight

### Even-Handed Outreach
- Comments from outside the usual stakeholder coalitions are valuable
- Coalition-building across diverse constituencies signals legitimacy
- Geographic and demographic diversity in commenters strengthens collective influence

### Plain Language
- Comments should be readable by agency staff who are not subject-matter experts in the
  commenter's specific area
- Key requested changes should appear in the first paragraph or executive summary
- Technical detail belongs in appendices

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment contribute new information, analysis, or perspective beyond what is
      already on the docket?
- [ ] Does the comment represent or articulate an under-represented constituency?
- [ ] Does the comment engage with the rule at the earliest practical stage?
- [ ] Does the comment use plain language with technical detail appropriately deployed?
- [ ] Does the comment serve both substantive quality and procedural legitimacy?
