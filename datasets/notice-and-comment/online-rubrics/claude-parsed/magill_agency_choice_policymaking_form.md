# Magill, "Agency Choice of Policymaking Form" (2004)

Magill identifies that agencies have multiple policymaking tools — formal rulemaking, informal
notice-and-comment rulemaking, formal adjudication, informal adjudication, guidance,
interpretive rules, policy statements — and choose largely without judicial constraint.
**For commenters, the policy form chosen affects both the participation channel and the legal
weight of the comment.**

## What Magill's Framework Reveals

### 1. Notice-and-Comment Is Not the Only Channel
Agencies often make policy through:
- Guidance documents (no formal comment required, though some agencies do solicit comments)
- Interpretive rules
- Policy statements
- Adjudications that establish precedent
- Informal staff statements

A complete commenter strategy includes **monitoring and engaging across all these channels**.

### 2. Form Choice Has Procedural Consequences
- Notice-and-comment requires response to substantive comments
- Guidance documents have weaker procedural protections
- Adjudications privilege parties to the proceeding
- Interpretive rules and policy statements receive deferential review

### 3. Procedural Form Can Itself Be Challenged
Where an agency uses guidance to do what should require notice-and-comment (a "substantive
rule" disguised as guidance), commenters can challenge the form itself. **This is the
"rulemaking by guidance" objection** that has produced significant case law.

## Implications for Comments

### 1. Engage All Policymaking Forms
Don't wait only for the formal NPRM. Engage:
- Petitions for rulemaking
- Comments on guidance documents (when invited)
- Interpretive rule announcements
- Policy statements

### 2. Challenge Misuse of Form
A comment can argue the agency should use notice-and-comment rulemaking rather than guidance
when:
- The "guidance" effectively binds regulated parties
- The "guidance" departs significantly from prior agency practice
- The "guidance" creates new substantive obligations

### 3. Preserve Form-Choice Objections
Comments should explicitly object when the agency's chosen form is procedurally inadequate,
preserving the issue for judicial review.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the commenter monitor and engage all relevant agency policymaking channels (not
      only formal NPRMs)?
- [ ] Where guidance functions as a substantive rule, does the comment object to the form
      choice?
- [ ] Does the comment preserve form-choice objections for potential judicial review?
- [ ] Does the comment engage the legal weight of the form chosen (binding rule vs.
      interpretive guidance)?
- [ ] Does the commenter consider filing a petition for rulemaking when guidance is being
      used inappropriately?
