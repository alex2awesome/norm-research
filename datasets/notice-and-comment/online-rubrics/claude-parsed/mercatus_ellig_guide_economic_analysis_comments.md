# A Guide to Writing Public Interest Comments Using Economic Analysis (Ellig, Mercatus Center)

## Overview
"A constructive, information-rich comment that clearly communicates and supports its claims is more likely to have an impact." Designed as a neutral resource for anyone seeking to improve regulators' understanding of proposed rules' economic consequences.

## Six Fundamental Actions for Effective Comments
1. **Understand the regulatory process** — how Congress delegates authority to agencies and the procedures agencies must follow.
2. **Know the agency's public-interest definition** — align arguments with how regulators interpret their mandate.
3. **Master economic analysis topics** — identify key subjects regulatory economists should examine (market failure analysis, baseline scenarios, alternative comparisons, distributional impacts).
4. **Recognize legal constraints** — understand statutory authority and limitations.
5. **Use appropriate format and tone** — substantive, professional, in proper style.
6. **Share your work** — distribute comments to other interested stakeholders so they can build on or echo your analysis.

## Regulatory Process Context
Regulations originate from congressional laws. Agencies announce developing regulations in the *Unified Agenda of Federal Regulatory and Deregulatory Actions*, published biannually. Not all regulations ultimately proposed appear in the Agenda; track multiple sources.

## Implication for Commenters
Mercatus's tradition: economic-analysis comments that probe whether the agency identified the right market failure, used the right baseline, and considered less-restrictive alternatives are more persuasive than rhetorical objections.
