# Mendelson, "Should Mass Comments Count?" (2012)

Mendelson's reply to Farina, Newhart & Heidt's "Rulemaking vs. Democracy." She defends and
extends her argument that **mass comment campaigns deserve agency consideration when they
express value preferences**, even if they lack the technical sophistication insiders prize.

## Mendelson's Core Position

Where Farina et al. emphasize comments that contribute "situated knowledge," Mendelson argues
that **value-laden mass comments also have legitimate weight** because:

1. Rulemaking involves value judgments, not only technical decisions
2. Agencies' refusal to consider mass value-comments treats those preferences as irrelevant
3. This produces a democratic legitimacy deficit
4. The proper agency response is engagement, not dismissal

## What Counts as a "Substantive Mass Comment" Under Mendelson's Framework

A mass comment is substantive — and deserves agency response — when it:

### 1. Articulates a Value Preference
The comment says "we (the signers) value X over Y" in relation to a value the agency is
weighing.

### 2. Identifies the Decision Implicated
The comment connects the value preference to a specific decision the agency must make
(stringency level, exemption, methodology).

### 3. Demonstrates the Scale of the Preference
The comment is part of a campaign with documented scale (number of signers, geographic
distribution, demographic breadth).

### 4. Connects to a Legitimate Statutory Purpose
The value preference is connected to a purpose the statute permits the agency to weigh.

## Implications for Mass-Comment Campaign Design

For a mass comment campaign to count under Mendelson's standard:

- **Provide a clear value claim** — what do signers value, in what tradeoff?
- **Make the campaign's scale legible** — sample sizes, distribution, demographics
- **Connect to statutory authority** — show the value is one the agency may legitimately weigh
- **Allow personalization** — signers should be able to add their own content
- **Identify the specific decision** — which provision of the rule is at stake?

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the campaign comment articulate a clear value preference?
- [ ] Does the comment identify the specific agency decision affected?
- [ ] Does the campaign document its scale (signers, distribution, demographics)?
- [ ] Does the comment connect the value preference to a statutory purpose the agency may
      weigh?
- [ ] Does the comment template allow personalization?
- [ ] Does the campaign coordinate with separate substantive submissions providing factual
      and analytical content?

## Caution

Mendelson does not argue mass comments should be the only factor. They should be one input
among many, with **technical and analytical comments retaining their substantive role**.
