# Rulemaking in the Shade (Wagner, Barnes, Peters)

## Study Design

The study examined air toxic emission standards set by EPA for over 100 major industries with respect to participation and influence of interest groups at key points of the rulemaking life cycle - before the proposed rule is published, between notice and comment and the final rule, and after the final rule is published.

## Key Findings on Comment Asymmetry

During the comment process, **industry provided approximately 81% of the total comments while public interest groups provided 4%**. Additionally, changes made to the final rule after notice and comment favored industry by a factor of **4 to 1** compared to the changes benefitting the public interest.

## Implications

- High levels of participation but stark imbalances among participating groups at each stage
- Industry dominates the public comment period
- Pre-proposal contact with agencies (not visible in dockets) drives much of the influence
- Public-interest groups have considerably less influence due to resource asymmetries
- Comments are most likely to affect final rule when they come from heavily participating regulated parties

## Practical Lessons for Comment Effectiveness

- Pre-proposal engagement is critical
- Volume + sophistication + repeated engagement = influence
- The "shadow" rulemaking process favors well-resourced commenters who can engage at all stages
