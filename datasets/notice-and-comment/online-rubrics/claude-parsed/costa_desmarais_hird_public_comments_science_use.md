# Costa, Desmarais, & Hird: Public Comments' Influence on Science Use in U.S. Rulemaking

## Key Findings

**Overall Impact**: The study examined how public comments influence the scientific evidence cited in EPA regulatory impact analyses (RIAs). Researchers analyzed the Portland Cement NESHAP rule, comparing two RIA versions (2009-2010) and tracking 249 substantive comments.

## What Makes Comments Effective

**Commenter Type Matters Most**

The research reveals a striking disparity in influence:

- **Members of Congress and industry groups** demonstrated the strongest effect on which scientific citations appeared in revised RIAs
- Individual citizens and interest groups showed minimal measurable influence on cited research
- Abstracts added to later RIA versions showed "approximately 55% lower similarity with comments" from Congress members and industry compared to citations that were dropped

**The "Fire Alarm" Pattern**

The findings support what researchers call subgovernment theory. When industry commenters raised concerns about specific studies, Members of Congress would echo those concerns, prompting EPA officials to remove challenged citations from revised documents—even when the final rule itself remained unchanged.

**Evidence Quality Paradox**

Interest groups were most likely to cite research (42% included citations), yet had negligible influence. Conversely, industry groups cited less frequently but wielded disproportionate power when they did comment.

## Study Limitations

The analysis examined a single salient rule during a science-friendly administration, limiting generalizability to broader rulemaking patterns.
