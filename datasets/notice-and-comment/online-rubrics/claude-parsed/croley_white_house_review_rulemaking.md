# Croley, "White House Review of Agency Rulemaking: An Empirical Investigation" (2003)

Empirical examination of OIRA review across the Reagan, Bush I, and Clinton administrations.
Quantifies how often OIRA approves rules without changes vs. demands changes — and reveals
**OIRA review is itself a forum where comment-quality matters**.

## What the Empirical Findings Tell Commenters

### 1. OIRA Review Is Real Leverage
OIRA does not rubber-stamp agency rules. It returns rules, demands changes, and engages
deeply with cost-benefit analysis. **Comments that arm OIRA reviewers with critique points**
can shape the rule even after the agency has signed off.

### 2. Comments to the Agency Often Reach OIRA
Substantive comments are typically forwarded to OIRA when the rule moves to interagency
review. **A well-crafted comment to the agency is also a comment to OIRA**.

### 3. Direct OIRA Engagement Is a Distinct Channel
Sophisticated commenters meet directly with OIRA staff during the review period. While
formal notice-and-comment is the official channel, OIRA logs reflect substantial parallel
engagement. **A complete comment strategy includes engaging OIRA directly when the rule is
significant**.

### 4. CBA Quality Is What OIRA Cares About
OIRA's stated mandate is regulatory analysis quality. **Comments that address CBA
deficiencies** — wrong discount rates, omitted benefit categories, unrealistic cost
estimates — speak OIRA's language.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment include cost-benefit analysis content of a quality OIRA can use?
- [ ] Does the comment identify deficiencies in the agency's RIA?
- [ ] Does the comment propose alternative analytical assumptions (discount rates, baselines)?
- [ ] Does the comment quantify impacts the agency may have under-estimated?
- [ ] Does the commenter consider directly engaging OIRA during interagency review?
- [ ] Is the comment formatted so OIRA staff can extract the analytical points quickly?

## Caution

OIRA engagement is most useful for "significant" rules (those with $100M+ annual effects or
otherwise meeting EO 12866 criteria). For non-significant rules, the agency's response is
the primary leverage point.
