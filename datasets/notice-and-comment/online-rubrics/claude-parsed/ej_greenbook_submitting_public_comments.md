# Environmental Justice Green Book: Guide to Submitting Public Comments

## Overview

Public comments enable ordinary citizens to participate in government decision-making affecting their communities. They must be "clearly written, detailed, and directly applied" to the proposed regulation or permit under consideration.

## Types of Public Comments

**Rulemaking Comments**: Address proposed federal regulations published in the Federal Register. Access proposals at regulations.gov or Georgia's EPD website.

**Permitting Comments**: Enable community input on environmental permits (air, water discharge, etc.). Use Georgia's GEOS portal to access and comment on draft permits.

**Enforcement Decision Comments**: Allow communities to weigh in on Consent Orders addressing violations, examining whether penalties adequately deter future violations.

**Lawmaking Comments**: Many cities and counties allow public participation during local government meetings on proposed legislation.

## Key Tips for Effective Comments

**Research & Preparation:**
- Conduct thorough research before the comment period begins
- Collect relevant data, community observations, and documentation
- Review public files and access government records
- Identify local impacts (schools, parks, sensitive populations)

**Writing Strategy:**
- Keep comments "clear, organized, fact-driven, and to the point"
- Establish your credentials and personal connection to the issue
- Include ALL concerns (future legal challenges depend on this)
- Provide specific citations with sources
- Tell your community's story with concrete examples
- State explicitly what action you want the agency to take

**Essential Elements:**
- Your name, address, email, and phone number
- Agency name, regulation name, and docket ID
- Relevant scientific data or credible facts
- Specific violations of law you've identified
- Questions requiring agency response

## What to Avoid

Don't include theories without evidence, gossip, vague concerns, personal financial information, hearsay, or attacks on specific officials. Avoid making unsubstantiated claims.

## After Submission

Track your comment via regulations.gov using your tracking number, attend public meetings, engage local officials, and consider press outreach to maintain momentum on your issue.
