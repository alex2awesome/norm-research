# Farina, Epstein, Heidt & Newhart, "Knowledge in the People" (2012)

Drawing on the Cornell Regulation Room project, the authors argue that the traditional
"insider" criteria for evaluating comments **systematically devalue the kind of knowledge
non-expert participants are best positioned to provide**: situated, experiential knowledge of
regulated activities.

## Traditional "Insider" Comment Criteria

Insiders value comments that:
1. Distinguish regulations from statutory requirements
2. Demonstrate statutory understanding through interpretive text
3. Propose explicit regulatory changes
4. Provide examples or logical arguments supporting positions
5. Include legal, policy, or empirical background

These criteria reward "thinking like a lawyer" — premise-argument-conclusion analytical reasoning
backed by quantitative evidence.

## "Situated Knowledge" — What Lay Commenters Uniquely Offer

Situated knowledge is "highly contextualized, experiential information" rooted in lived
experience of the regulated environment. Effective lay comments contribute:

- **Impact information** from ground-level experience
- **Knowledge of ambiguities and enforcement gaps** that insiders cannot see
- **Unintended-consequence prediction** from the user perspective
- **Local contextual factors** affecting how a rule will operate in practice

## Four Types of Valuable Experiential Comments (Farina et al.'s Typology)

1. **Complexity accounts** — reveal contradictions inside seemingly unified interest groups
   (e.g., a small landlord whose interests differ from large landlords)
2. **Contributory context** — identify root causes outside the agency's regulatory authority
3. **Unintended-consequences accounts** — predict downstream effects the agency missed
4. **Reframing accounts** — surface overlooked competing values

## Alternative Vetting Criteria for Experiential Comments

Where statistical representativeness is impossible, Farina et al. propose:

- **Internal coherence** — does the narrative hang together?
- **External plausibility** — is it consistent with known facts?
- **Repeated corroboration** — do multiple commenters describe the same phenomenon?

## Rubric Items for Lay/Experiential Comments

- [ ] Does the commenter describe a specific lived experience with the regulated activity?
- [ ] Does the comment surface a contradiction, gap, or unintended consequence the agency may
      have missed?
- [ ] Does the narrative cohere internally and align with known facts?
- [ ] Does the comment introduce a value or perspective absent from the proposal?
- [ ] Does the comment's experiential claim recur in other comments (suggesting a real pattern)?
