# EPA Pesticide Registration Public Participation

## Statutory Basis

The Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act (FIFRA) and Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FFDCA) require EPA to engage the public at specific times during the registration process, including:
- When EPA receives an application for registration of a new pesticide active ingredient or one that would entail a changed use pattern
- When EPA is issuing notices related to tolerance changes or cancellation orders

## Comment Period Timeframes

- For actions related to **biopesticide active ingredients**, EPA offers a 15-day comment period (or 30 days when appropriate)
- For most **conventional pesticides**, EPA announces a 30-day comment period
- 15-day periods apply when: low risk active ingredient, reduced risk status granted, or proposed for IR-4-supported minor uses

## Additional Engagement Opportunities

EPA offers additional opportunities for public engagement beyond those initial comment periods required by FIFRA and FFDCA, since public input helps inform risk assessment and risk management.

## Effective Pesticide Comments

For FIFRA registration/registration review:
- Identify yourself (grower, applicator, farmworker, public health professional, environmental advocate)
- Cite specific application/use patterns
- Reference EPA's risk assessment documents
- Provide use data (rates, frequency, geography)
- Cite peer-reviewed toxicology studies
- Address Endocrine Disruptor Screening Program (EDSP) data
- Engage with Endangered Species Act consultation considerations
- Address farmworker exposure (Worker Protection Standard)
- Reference dietary exposure assessments and tolerance levels
- Address resistance management
- Engage with alternatives (IPM, organic, mechanical)
- Note pollinator impacts where relevant
- Cite USDA NASS data on pesticide use

## Comment Stages
- Public comment on registration applications
- Registration review docket comments (every 15 years per active ingredient)
- Special review and re-registration eligibility decisions
- Tolerance and exemption rulemakings
