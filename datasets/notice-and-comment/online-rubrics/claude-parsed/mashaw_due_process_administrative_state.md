# Mashaw, "Due Process in the Administrative State" (1985)

Mashaw advances a **dignitary theory** of administrative due process: the value of participation
is not just instrumental (producing better decisions) but constitutive (recognizing affected
persons as bearers of dignity). This frames a deeper rationale for what counts as a substantive
comment.

## Mashaw's Three Theories of Administrative Legitimacy

| Theory | Source of Legitimacy | What Comments Should Provide |
|--------|---------------------|------------------------------|
| Transmission belt | Faithful execution of legislative will | Statutory interpretation; intent evidence |
| Expertise | Specialist knowledge | Technical analysis, data, methodology |
| Dignitary | Recognition of affected persons | Lived experience, identity, value claims |

## Why the Dignitary Theory Matters for Comments

A comment that asserts an affected person's perspective — even without technical expertise or
statutory citation — has **independent value** under Mashaw's framework. The agency is
constitutionally and normatively obligated to recognize the personhood of those affected by
its decisions.

### Implications

1. **Identity-grounded comments are substantive** when they articulate how the rule will
   affect the commenter's self-respect, autonomy, or community standing.
2. **Narrative form is appropriate** for dignitary comments. The comment need not be
   structured as a legal argument.
3. **Acknowledgment, not merely persuasion, is the relevant agency obligation**. Even comments
   the agency rejects deserve responsive engagement.

## What Effective Dignitary Comments Look Like

- Identify the commenter as a member of an affected community
- Describe the human impact of the rule in concrete terms
- Articulate the value at stake (dignity, autonomy, equality, recognition)
- Claim the agency's obligation of acknowledgment, not merely policy change

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment articulate the human impact in terms of dignity or recognition?
- [ ] Does the commenter identify themselves as part of an affected community?
- [ ] Does the comment claim acknowledgment as the relevant remedy (not just policy change)?
- [ ] Does the comment use narrative form effectively to communicate lived experience?
- [ ] Does the comment connect to a value (autonomy, equality, dignity, fairness) that the
      statutory framework should respect?

## Caution

Mashaw's framework is theoretically rich but operationally indeterminate. Comments grounded in
dignity claims are **important but harder to evaluate** by standard quality criteria. A robust
rubric should preserve dignitary value alongside instrumental criteria.
