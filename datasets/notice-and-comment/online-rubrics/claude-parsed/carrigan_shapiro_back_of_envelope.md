# Carrigan & Shapiro, "What's Wrong with the Back of the Envelope?" (2017)

Carrigan and Shapiro argue regulatory impact analyses (RIAs) have grown from 31,000 to
128,000 words (2000-2012) and morphed into **post-hoc justifications** rather than decision
inputs. They propose Back-Of-The-Envelope (BOTE) analyses on multiple alternatives, exposed
to public comment **before** the NPRM.

## Key Implications for Comments

### 1. Demand BOTE-Style Alternatives Analysis
Effective comments should ask: **what alternatives did the agency consider but reject, and
why?** Where the agency presented only one option, the comment should:
- Propose specific alternatives (more stringent, less stringent, different design)
- Provide approximate cost-benefit comparisons of alternatives
- Argue why the agency should have analyzed those alternatives

### 2. Push for Earlier Analytical Engagement
Carrigan and Shapiro want analysis to inform decisions, not justify them. **Comments should
be filed (or supplemented) at the ANPRM, RFI, or pre-rule stage** when alternatives are still
genuinely open.

### 3. Substance Over Length
Their critique of bloated RIAs implies that a **focused, concise comment** is more useful
than a 200-page treatise. Quality > volume.

### 4. Approximate Estimates Are Acceptable
Effective comments need not produce precise estimates. **Approximate, defensible numbers**
that change the analytical landscape are sufficient — and often more useful than the agency's
over-precise figures.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment propose at least one specific alternative the agency did not analyze?
- [ ] Does the comment provide approximate cost or benefit estimates for the proposed
      alternative?
- [ ] Does the comment make clear the trade-offs between the proposed alternative and the
      agency's preferred option?
- [ ] Was the comment filed at the earliest practical analytical stage (ANPRM, RFI,
      pre-rule)?
- [ ] Is the comment focused and proportionate, not a volume dump?
- [ ] Does the comment recognize that approximate but useful estimates are valid analytical
      inputs?

## Caution

The BOTE framework is procedurally novel and not currently required. Comments should still
engage the agency's RIA on its own terms while also pressing for more BOTE-style alternatives
analysis.
