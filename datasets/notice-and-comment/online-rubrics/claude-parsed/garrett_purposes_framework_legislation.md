# Garrett, "The Purposes of Framework Legislation" (2005)

Garrett identifies five purposes served by framework legislation (laws structuring future
legislative procedures or executive decision-making). For commenters, this clarifies what the
APA's notice-and-comment procedure is for — and therefore what counts as serving its purposes.

## Garrett's Five Purposes (Adapted to APA Notice-and-Comment)

The APA's procedural framework serves:

### 1. Symbolic Response to a Problem
Notice-and-comment signals democratic accountability for agency policy.
*Implication for comments*: Comments contribute to symbolic legitimacy by demonstrating
participation. Even comments unlikely to change the rule serve this function.

### 2. Neutral Rules for Future Decision Making
The APA structures the playing field for all rules.
*Implication for comments*: Comments should leverage the procedure's neutrality — invoking
the same procedural protections regardless of the substantive direction.

### 3. Solving Collective Action Problems
Where individual stakeholders cannot effectively participate alone, the procedure aggregates
voices.
*Implication for comments*: Coalition-building and aggregated comments serve the procedure's
collective-action-solving function.

### 4. Entrenching Macro-Objectives
The APA entrenches deliberation, transparency, and reasoned decision-making as macro-values.
*Implication for comments*: Comments should appeal to these macro-values when challenging
specific agency actions ("This action undermines the APA's commitment to reasoned decision-
making").

### 5. Changing the Internal Balance of Power
Procedural rules shift power among agencies, Congress, courts, and stakeholders.
*Implication for comments*: Comments should be aware of the institutional context — which
audiences (Congress, courts, OMB) the comment may also reach.

## Rubric Items Distilled From Garrett's Framework

- [ ] Does the comment leverage the APA's procedural neutrality regardless of substantive
      position?
- [ ] Does the comment serve the collective-action function (aggregating voices for diffuse
      interests)?
- [ ] Does the comment appeal to APA macro-values (deliberation, transparency, reasoned
      decision-making)?
- [ ] Does the comment recognize and address multiple audiences (agency, Congress, courts,
      OMB)?
- [ ] Does the comment contribute to the procedure's symbolic legitimacy by demonstrating
      genuine participation?

## Why Framework Legislation Matters

Garrett's framework explains why commenters who invoke procedural protections are not just
making technical objections — they are defending the broader regulatory system's commitment
to reasoned, deliberative governance.
