# Magat, Krupnick & Harrington, "Rules in the Making" (1986)

Pioneering statistical analysis of regulatory rulemaking — among the earliest empirical
studies of what factors predict whether final rules are more or less stringent than
proposals. Foundational for the empirical literature on comment influence.

## Key Findings (and Implications for Effective Comments)

### 1. Final Rules Often Differ From Proposals
Magat et al. document that **final EPA rules sometimes are more stringent than initially
proposed**. This contradicts the assumption that rulemaking always weakens proposals. The
direction depends on the balance of comments and analytical inputs.

### 2. Statistical Predictors of Rule Stringency
Factors that predicted final rule stringency in the data included:
- Initial proposal's stringency (anchoring effect)
- Volume and direction of comments
- Cost-benefit characteristics
- Political environment
- Agency staff judgment
- Industry technological capacity

### 3. Comments Matter Within a Causal Web
A single comment is unlikely to shift a rule, but comments operating within a coherent set of
political, analytical, and technical factors can. **Effective comments contribute to one or
more of these factors**.

## Implications for the Rubric

### Comments Should Engage Multiple Causal Channels
Rather than focusing only on persuading agency staff, effective comments:

- Provide cost or benefit data that affects the CBA channel
- Document operational/technical feasibility (or infeasibility)
- Marshal political support visible to agency leadership
- Provide analysis usable by agency staff as drafting input
- Build the coalition or coalition-counterweight that affects perceived consensus

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment provide quantitative data affecting the cost-benefit calculus?
- [ ] Does the comment document operational feasibility or infeasibility?
- [ ] Does the comment marshal evidence of political support (other commenters, congressional
      letters, public opinion)?
- [ ] Does the comment provide drafting input agency staff could use?
- [ ] Does the comment contribute to building a coherent record across multiple causal
      channels?
- [ ] Does the comment recognize that influence often comes from contributing to a web of
      factors, not single-factor persuasion?
