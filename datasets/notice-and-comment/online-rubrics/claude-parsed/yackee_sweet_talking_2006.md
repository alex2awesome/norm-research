# Yackee, "Sweet-Talking the Fourth Branch" (2006)

Empirical analysis of 1,444 interest group comments on 40 federal agency rules. Key finding:
**comments that voice consistent preferences during notice-and-comment can and do alter final
rule content** in directions matching commenter preferences.

## Empirical Findings That Shape Rubric Criteria

### 1. Consistency of Commenter Preferences Predicts Influence
When comments cluster around a consistent direction of change, agencies move the rule that way.
**Effective comments align with similar arguments from other commenters**, or explicitly
acknowledge and address opposing positions.

### 2. Direct Influence Is Real (Not Merely Indirect Through Congress)
Yackee disproves the myth that interest groups influence agencies only via Congress. Comments
themselves are mechanisms of policy change. **A well-targeted comment to the docket is not
"shouting into the void."**

### 3. Specificity of Requested Change Matters
Comments that requested **particular textual changes to the proposed rule** were more likely
to be reflected in the final rule than diffuse policy objections.

## Derived Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment request a **specific change** to a specific paragraph/section of the
      proposed rule (rather than a generic position)?
- [ ] Does the comment connect to or anticipate other commenters' likely positions to build a
      consistent record?
- [ ] Does the comment provide the agency with the **textual language** it could adopt for the
      change being requested?
- [ ] Does the comment estimate the magnitude of harm/benefit from the proposed change vs. the
      alternative?
- [ ] Does the comment supply information the agency does not already possess (cost data,
      operational impact, downstream effects)?

## Methodological Caveats Relevant to the Rubric

Yackee's sample over-represents formal organized interests. The rubric implication is that
**individual comments can succeed when they emulate the structural features that organized
interests use**: specificity, consistency with other comments, and providing usable agency input.
