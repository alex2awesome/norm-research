# Regulations.gov: Tips for Submitting Effective Comments

## Overview

The official guide provides systematic recommendations on how to write effective public comments on federal regulations. This is the canonical federal government rubric for what makes effective public comments.

## Key Tips for Effective Comments

### 1. Read and Understand the Regulation
Effective, relevant comments provide regulators with information to help them improve draft rules, and are typically most effective when they provide information directly relevant to analyzing the rule and its potential impact.

### 2. Quality Over Quantity
A single, well-supported comment may carry more weight than a thousand form letters.

### 3. Identify the Issues with Specificity
Identify the docket ID number and other identifying information in your subject line, as comments cannot be considered if they are misdirected or get lost because the identifying information was not included.

Clearly identify the section(s) of the rule your comment is addressing and make the section you are addressing clear at every point in your comment.

### 4. Provide Specific Arguments
Simply stating that you support or oppose a policy is not as persuasive as explaining how the policy would positively or negatively impact the specific situation with which you are familiar.

### 5. Don't Assume You Need to Be an Expert
Some of the most effective comments are from individuals who describe the impacts of a proposal in terms of their own lived experience.

### 6. Include Specific References
Provide the page number, column, and/or paragraph from the Federal Register if you are commenting on a particular word, phrase, or sentence.

### 7. Include Relevant Data
Provide relevant data, statistics, or studies to support your comments.

### 8. Particularly Influential Comments
Particularly influential public comments are often those that simply provide good reasoning and evidence or a perspective that the agency had not previously considered.

### 9. Agency Discretion
While members of the public are allowed and welcome to express their support or opposition to a proposed regulation, an agency is not required to change its proposed policy simply because a commenter suggests it should.

### 10. Be Constructive
A comment that is constructive and provides information to support an argument is more likely to inform the agency's decision-making process.

## Comment Process Reminders

- Comments must be submitted by the deadline (11:59 PM Eastern on the deadline date)
- Submissions become part of the public record
- Agencies must consider all "relevant matter presented" but are not required to respond to every comment individually
- Form letters typically count as one comment regardless of how many people send them
