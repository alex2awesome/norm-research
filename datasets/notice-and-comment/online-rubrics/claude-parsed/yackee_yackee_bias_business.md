# Yackee & Yackee, "A Bias Towards Business?" (2006)

Empirical analysis of 1,700+ comments on 30+ rules finds business commenters significantly
influence final rule content while non-business commenters do not. Critically, **the proportion
of business comments matters**: as the share of business commenters rises, so does business
influence.

## Implications for Effective Non-Business Comments

If business comments drive policy partly via volume and partly via professional content,
non-business comments must:

### 1. Match Business Commenters in Specificity
Generic objections from non-business interests do not move the rule. Effective non-business
comments **propose specific textual changes** at the same level of detail business commenters do.

### 2. Counter Business Cost Estimates Directly
A high-impact non-business comment includes **its own quantitative estimate** of costs/benefits
to challenge industry submissions, rather than asserting harm without numbers.

### 3. Coordinate Submissions
Because proportions matter, non-business interests should coordinate to ensure substantive
non-business presence in the docket — not necessarily through mass form letters but through
multiple, distinct, substantive comments.

### 4. Document Distributional Impact
Non-business comments win when they make visible distributional consequences (who pays, who
benefits) that business comments have an incentive to omit.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment provide a quantitative cost or benefit estimate countering industry
      submissions?
- [ ] Does the comment propose specific regulatory text the agency could adopt?
- [ ] Does the comment make distributional consequences explicit?
- [ ] Does the comment coordinate (cite or align with) other non-business comments to
      establish a credible counterweight to business volume?
- [ ] Does the comment identify gaps or assumptions in industry submissions?

## Caution

Yackee & Yackee's findings suggest that procedural openness alone has not democratized
rulemaking. Rubrics for non-business comments must equip ordinary commenters with
**counter-expertise**, not just civic enthusiasm.
