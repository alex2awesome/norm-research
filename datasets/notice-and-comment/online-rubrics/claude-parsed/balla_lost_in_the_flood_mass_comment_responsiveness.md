# Balla et al. - Lost in the Flood? Agency Responsiveness to Mass Comment Campaigns in Administrative Rulemaking

Steven J. Balla, *Regulation & Governance*, 2022.

## Research Question

What happens when agencies receive massive numbers of similar comments through organized mass campaigns? Do agencies respond to them substantively, dismiss them, or selectively engage?

## Key Findings

Mass comment campaigns affect rulemaking in nuanced ways:
- Agencies typically count mass comments together and respond to the substantive points raised
- Numerical volume doesn't translate proportionally to influence
- Form letters with novel substantive arguments are more likely to be addressed than truly identical text
- Agencies face procedural challenges in deduplication and substantive analysis
- Mass campaigns can crowd out individual substantive comments through agency resource constraints

## Practical Implications

For a mass campaign comment to be more effective:
- Even within a campaign, individual personalization (a single sentence with personal experience) increases likelihood of being treated as substantive
- Campaigns that include unique factual or analytical claims fare better than pure expressions of preference
- Volume matters less than reaching agencies that find the underlying argument new

## Ongoing Work

Balla also published "Between Commenting and Negotiation: The Contours of Public Participation in Agency Rulemaking" — examining how some rulemakings unfold according to the conventional notice and comment procedure, while others incorporate more interactive processes (public meetings, advisory committee consultations, negotiations oriented toward stakeholder consensus).

## Implications for Rubric Design

Balla's work helps identify what counts as a "substantive" mass comment:
- Has unique sentences (not 100% template)
- Includes a specific personal example
- Cites a unique data point or study
- Suggests a specific implementation alternative
- Addresses one of the agency's stated questions

vs. a pure-template mass comment that:
- Is 100% identical to thousands of others
- States only support/opposition
- Has no engagement with specific provisions
- Has no personal context
