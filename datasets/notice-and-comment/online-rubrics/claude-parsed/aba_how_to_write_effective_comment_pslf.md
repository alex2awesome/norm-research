# American Bar Association: How to Write an Effective Comment

## Key Principles for Effective Comments

Comments should stay respectful and focused on practical, legal, or professional consequences; not partisan opinions.

A well-written, specific comment will carry far more weight than a form letter. Your story and professional insight help show real-world consequences the proposed rule might have.

## Structure and Format

Aim for **2–4 paragraphs**. You do not need legal citations to be effective.

## ABA Suggested Template Format

```
My name is [Your Name], and I am a [Your Role] working in [Location/Community].
I rely on the Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) program to [explain how
you benefit or how your organization uses PSLF]. If the proposed rule is
implemented, it would jeopardize [describe the risk or impact]. PSLF has been
critical for our ability to [recruit attorneys in rural areas/provide legal
services/serve vulnerable clients]. I urge the Department to protect this vital
program by revising or withdrawing the proposed rule.
```

## Implications for Effective Comment Writing

The ABA template demonstrates several rubric elements:
- Personal/professional identification with credentials
- Specific connection to the regulatory program
- Concrete impact statement
- Vulnerable population/public benefit framing
- Clear ask (revise/withdraw)
- Brevity (one paragraph minimum)

## Additional Resources

The ABA's PSLF campaign provides members with guidance on crafting effective comments for the Department of Education. Comments are submitted through Regulations.gov.
