# UK Cabinet Office Consultation Principles 2018

## Overview

The UK government's revised set of consultation principles give clear guidance to government departments on conducting consultations. Last updated in 2018.

## Key Principles

### A. Consultations should be clear and concise
- Use plain English; avoid acronyms
- Be clear what questions are being asked
- Limit questions to those necessary
- Avoid lengthy documents when possible

### B. Consultations should have a purpose
- Don't consult for the sake of it
- Take responses into account when taking policy forward
- Consult on policies at a formative stage rather than where there's already a final view

### C. Consultations should be informative
- Give enough information to ensure those consulted understand the issues
- Enable informed responses

### D. Consultations are part of an engagement process
- Not just about formal documents
- Use new digital tools for informal iterative consultation
- Consultation is an ongoing process

### E. Consultations should last for a proportionate time
- Judge based on legal advice
- Take into account nature and impact of the proposal

### F. Consultations should be targeted
- Consider the full range of people, business and voluntary bodies affected
- Make sure these stakeholders are aware of the consultation

### G. Consultations should take account of accessibility
- Consider groups affected by the proposals
- Use suitable means and channels

### H. Government responses should be timely
- Publish within 12 weeks of close (or explain why not possible)
- For statutory instruments, publish before/at the same time as instrument is laid

## Implications for Effective Comment Writing (UK Context)

- Address the specific consultation questions
- Cite evidence proportionate to the proposal's impact
- Identify the affected community/group you represent
- Note accessibility/equality impacts
- Submit during the formative stage when influence is highest
