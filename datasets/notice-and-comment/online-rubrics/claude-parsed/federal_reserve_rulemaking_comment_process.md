# Federal Reserve Board: Proposals for Comment

## How to Submit Comments

Comments can be submitted through the Federal Reserve's web site using the "Submit Comment" links (the preferred method of submission). The submission form allows for attachments. Comments may also be submitted in writing or by electronic mail (see text of each proposal for contact information).

## Finding Proposed Rules

Proposed regulations are published in the Federal Register, the daily journal of the U.S. government, and the Board also maintains a list of its proposed regulations on its website. You can find, review, and submit comments at Regulations.gov.

## Comment Period Details

Proposed regulations typically ask for public comment by a specific date. Comment periods often are 60 days, but the period may be longer or shorter depending on the circumstances.

## Public Disclosure

Comments received are subject to public disclosure and in general, comments received will be made available without change and will not be modified to remove personal or business information including confidential, contact, or other identifying information.

## Effective Federal Reserve Comments

For Fed rulemakings, effective comments typically:
- Cite specific Fed Regulations (Reg B, Reg E, Reg Z, Reg CC, etc.) by part
- Reference Federal Reserve Act, FDIA, BHCA provisions
- Engage with quantitative impact analysis
- Address safety and soundness implications
- Distinguish impacts on different bank size categories ($100M, $10B, $250B thresholds)
- Address community bank relief considerations
- Cite empirical research on bank behavior, market impacts
- Reference Basel and international harmonization where relevant
- Consider macroprudential implications

## Key Areas Where Fed Comments Influence Rules

- Capital and liquidity rules (Basel III implementation)
- Stress testing and CCAR
- Volcker Rule
- Consumer compliance regulations (CRA, fair lending)
- Payment systems
