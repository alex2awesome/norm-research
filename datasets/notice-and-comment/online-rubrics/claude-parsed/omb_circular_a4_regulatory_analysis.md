# OMB Circular A-4: Regulatory Analysis

## Overview

OMB Circular A-4 provides guidance to Federal agencies on the development of regulatory analysis as required under Section 6(a)(3)(c) of Executive Order 12866, "Regulatory Planning and Review," the Regulatory Right-to-Know Act, and a variety of related authorities.

## Purpose and Design

The Circular is designed to assist analysts in regulatory agencies by defining good regulatory analysis and standardizing the way benefits and costs of Federal regulatory actions are measured and reported.

## Required Analysis Elements

A regulatory impact analysis should include three basic elements:
1. **A statement of the need for the regulatory action** (problem definition, market failure analysis, legal authority)
2. **A clear identification of regulatory alternatives** that the agency considered
3. **An evaluation of the benefits and costs** — quantitative and qualitative — of the proposed action and the main alternatives

## Categorization of Effects

The agency should categorize the benefits and costs into three mutually exclusive and exhaustive categories:
1. Quantified and monetized
2. Quantified but not monetized
3. Neither quantified nor monetized

For each of the regulatory alternatives, the agency should calculate benefits and costs relative to a common baseline.

## 2023 Updates

The first Circular A-4 was 48 pages. The revised and newly operative Circular is 93 pages and makes numerous significant changes to virtually all aspects of agency cost-benefit analysis. Recent updates respond to Administration directive to promote public health and safety, economic growth, social welfare, racial justice, environmental stewardship, human dignity, and equity through the regulatory review process.

Key updates include:
- New discount rate guidance (1.7% real for primary analyses)
- Distributional analysis requirements
- Treatment of effects on people abroad and future generations
- Greater attention to non-quantifiable benefits

## Implications for Comment Writers

Effective comments engage with A-4's analytical framework:
- Challenge the problem definition
- Provide better data on benefits or costs
- Identify alternatives the agency missed
- Critique the discount rate or distributional analysis
- Provide evidence on non-quantifiable effects (equity, dignity)
- Challenge the baseline assumptions
