# Golden, "Interest Groups in the Rule-Making Process" (1998)

Empirical study of 11 rules at EPA, NHTSA, and HUD. Three core findings shape the rubric:
(1) citizen commenters are extremely rare, (2) business interests dominate participation,
and (3) **whose voices get heard varies by rule and agency** — there is no uniform bias.

## Findings That Shape Effective Comments

### 1. Show Up at All
Golden's most basic finding: **citizen voices are absent**. A substantive comment from a
non-business commenter has **outsized marginal value** simply because the docket is so
unbalanced. Even one well-formed citizen or community comment can stand out.

### 2. Match the Agency's Decisional Context
Different agencies respond differently. EPA processes are dominated by environmental and
industry coalitions; NHTSA is industry-heavy; HUD is more diverse. **A rubric must be
sensitive to which agency the rule comes from** — what counts as adequate engagement varies.

### 3. Issue Networks Reward Iterative Engagement
Comments are most influential within issue networks where commenters have established
credibility through repeated participation. **Effective comments build on prior submissions,
position papers, or research from the commenter or allied organizations**.

### 4. Balance of Voices Matters for Agency Cognition
Golden finds no discernible bias in *whose voices get heard given who comments* — agencies
listen to all commenters. The bias is in **who shows up**. This means a substantive comment
from an unrepresented group is genuinely heard, not filtered out.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment represent a perspective absent or under-represented in the docket?
- [ ] Does the comment build on the commenter's (or allied organizations') established expertise
      and prior engagement?
- [ ] Is the comment tailored to the specific agency's analytical conventions?
- [ ] Does the comment provide the substantive content (data, analysis, narrative) that a
      regulated industry would not supply?
- [ ] Does the comment identify itself as part of an issue-network position (citing aligned
      research, prior comments, or coalitions)?
