# Comment Integrity and Management Act of 2024

## Overview

The Comment Integrity and Management Act of 2024 passed in the House without opposition on a voice vote, putting a legislative framework around existing efforts to identify and manage the flow of computer-generated comments.

## Key Provisions

The Act addresses:
- Detection and disclosure of AI-generated comments
- Agency authentication procedures
- Tracking and reporting on mass comment campaigns
- Standards for distinguishing substantive from non-substantive comments
- Resource support for agencies dealing with mass campaigns

## Background Context

The legislation responds to:
- Net neutrality fraud (8.5+ million fake comments in 2017)
- AI tools enabling personalized mass comment generation
- Senate PSI 2019 report on rulemaking abuses
- Growing capability of LLMs to produce variation in templated content

## Implications for Comment Effectiveness

For commenters submitting genuine substantive comments:
- Maintain clearly identified email and contact information
- Provide content that is verifiably yours
- Include personal context that authenticates identity
- Engage with specific regulatory provisions
- Avoid using AI to mass-produce substantively similar content

For agencies:
- Develop better authentication tools
- Create scalable approaches to evaluate comment substance
- Distinguish AI-assisted (legitimate) from AI-generated mass (problematic) content
- Use semantic clustering rather than exact-match deduplication
- Disclose how comments were processed in final rule preambles

## Related Initiatives

- Federal CIO Council guidance on AI in regulatory processes
- OMB M-23-22 on Generative AI procurement
- Various state rulemaking authentication pilots
- ACUS recommendations on virtual public engagement

## What This Means for Commenters

The legislative trend supports rather than undermines the principles of effective commenting:
- Substantive content matters more
- Identity verification is becoming more important
- Section-specific, evidence-based comments are favored
- Mass identical comments are dis-favored
- AI-assisted (but human-authored) comments remain legitimate
