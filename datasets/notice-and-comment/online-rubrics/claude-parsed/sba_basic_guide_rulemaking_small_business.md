# SBA Office of Advocacy: Basic Guide to Federal Rulemaking and Small Business

## Overview

Federal agencies endeavor to provide notice of regulatory flexibility agendas to small entities and invite comments upon each subject area on the agenda.

## The Regulatory Flexibility Act (RFA)

The RFA and related laws and executive orders require federal agencies to consider the effects of regulations on small entities. The law allows small firms to engage in regulatory decision-making and directs the Office of Advocacy to monitor how well federal agencies comply.

## Effective Comment Strategies for Small Businesses

When commenting under RFA framework:

### Identify Yourself as a Small Entity
- State your status as a small business, small organization, or small government jurisdiction
- Provide NAICS code and revenue/employment thresholds
- Explain how RFA Section 601 categories apply to you

### Quantify the Disproportionate Impact
- Calculate per-employee compliance cost
- Show fixed-cost regulatory burden falls disproportionately on small entities
- Estimate market exit/concentration effects
- Document time-to-compliance challenges

### Demand RFA-Required Analyses
- Initial Regulatory Flexibility Analysis (IRFA) for proposed rule
- Final Regulatory Flexibility Analysis (FRFA) for final rule
- If agency claims "no significant economic impact" certification, challenge with data

### Suggest Regulatory Alternatives
The RFA specifically requires agencies to consider:
- Different compliance/reporting requirements for small entities
- Differing timetables
- Performance rather than design standards
- Exemptions for small entities

### Engage SBREFA Panels
For EPA, OSHA, and CFPB rules, demand a Small Business Advocacy Review (SBAR) panel under SBREFA before NPRM.

## Office of Advocacy Role

By conducting small business outreach, commenting in the federal regulatory process, and helping agencies develop less burdensome regulations, the Office of Advocacy amplifies the voice of small business owners. Advocacy files comment letters with federal agencies to alert them to their rules' small business impacts and to propose alternative approaches to achieving regulatory goals.
