# McKay & Yackee, "Interest Group Competition on Federal Agency Rules" (2007)

Analysis of ~1,700 comments on 40 federal rules. Two key findings:
(1) Diverse coalitions and **consensus across coalitions** strongly predict policy influence.
(2) **Mass comment campaigns from individuals tend to have low informational content** and
elicit little direct policy response.

## Findings That Shape the Rubric

### 1. Consensus Across Diverse Coalitions Wins
When multiple comments from different sectors (industry, NGOs, states, academics) converge on
the same recommendation, agencies move. **Effective comments cite or reference allied
positions across diverse stakeholders** to demonstrate consensus.

### 2. One-Sided Pressure Without Diversity Is Less Effective
Comments from a single sector (e.g., only environmental groups, only industry) face more
agency skepticism. **Comments that show their position is shared by typically opposed
stakeholders** are particularly powerful.

### 3. Mass Comment Campaigns: Low Informational Value
Form letters generally fail to alter policy. McKay & Yackee confirm Shulman's finding that
mass campaigns have low informational value. **A high-quality individual comment is therefore
more powerful than a thousand form letters from one's own side.**

### 4. Substantive Content > Volume
Information content is what moves agencies. **Effective comments include unique factual,
analytical, or experiential content** that the agency could not have obtained otherwise.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment cite or align with positions from typically opposed stakeholders to
      demonstrate cross-coalition consensus?
- [ ] Does the comment differ substantively from form-letter language and contribute unique
      content?
- [ ] Does the comment provide information the agency does not already possess?
- [ ] Is the comment part of a coordinated diverse coalition rather than a single-sector
      campaign?
- [ ] Does the comment articulate the breadth of support for its position (geographic, sectoral,
      ideological)?
