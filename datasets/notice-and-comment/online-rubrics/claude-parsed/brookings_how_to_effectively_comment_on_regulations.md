# Brookings: How to Effectively Comment on Regulations

By Philip Wallach (2018, updated November 2022).

## Core Argument

Public comments are often most influential when they introduce new information or perspectives the agency hasn't considered, rather than simply expressing support or opposition.

## Key Recommendations

### 1. Understand the Process
Federal agencies write rules to implement laws passed by Congress. The notice-and-comment rulemaking process under the Administrative Procedure Act is the primary mechanism for public input.

### 2. Choose Your Battles
Focus on rules where:
- You have specific expertise or experience
- The economic or social stakes are high
- The agency has discretion that comments can shape
- Pre-proposal influence opportunities have closed

### 3. Frame Comments to be Useful to Agencies
Effective comments help agency staff, who are working with significant time and resource constraints, understand:
- Real-world implementation problems
- Affected stakeholders the agency may not have engaged
- Cost or burden estimates with verifiable assumptions
- Alternative regulatory approaches with their tradeoffs

### 4. Engage with the Regulatory Analysis
Agencies must conduct cost-benefit analysis under EO 12866. Comments can:
- Challenge specific assumptions (discount rates, valuation of statistical life, etc.)
- Provide better data than the agency has
- Identify costs or benefits the agency missed
- Engage with the alternatives the agency considered

### 5. Cite Specific Provisions
Reference specific section/paragraph numbers from the Federal Register publication.

### 6. Recognize the Limits
- Agencies don't tally support vs. opposition
- A "vote" is not what is sought
- Comments must be on the rulemaking record to support later challenges
- Mass campaigns can be useful for political signaling but rarely for substantive influence

## Practical Realities

Agencies receive thousands of comments on major rules. For a comment to be noticed:
- It should be findable (clear subject line, docket ID)
- It should be useful (information the agency can use)
- It should be referencable (specific claims with citations)

## Implications for Civil Society

For nonprofits and individual citizens:
- Personal stories with specific facts beat generic concern
- Coordinate technical and grassroots efforts
- Submit early (before opposition coalesces)
- Track agency responses to your comments in the final rule
