# Coglianese, Kilmartin, Mendelson - Transparency and Public Participation in the Rulemaking Process

Cary Coglianese, Heather Kilmartin, and Evan Mendelson, *George Washington Law Review*, 2009.

## Overview

A nonpartisan Task Force on Transparency and Public Participation met in 2008 to identify current deficiencies in agency rulemaking procedures and develop recommendations for the new presidential administration.

## Key Themes

The Task Force focused on:
- Agencies doing better at seeking citizen comment **earlier** in the rulemaking process
- **Reaching out to all affected groups** in an evenhanded manner
- Improving the quality and accessibility of regulatory documents
- Strengthening the administrative record

## Key Recommendations

### Targeted Recommendations
- Improve the e-rulemaking system (Regulations.gov)
- Focus on (i) ease of access and usability of Regulations.gov, (ii) quality of data being uploaded, (iii) timeliness of agency data entry into the Federal Docket Management System
- Provide plain-language summaries of proposed rules
- Use multiple notification channels beyond Federal Register

### Strategic Management Recommendations
Over-time improvements requiring sustained attention to:
- Public participation metrics
- Administrative record completeness
- Comment response practices
- OIRA review transparency

## Implications for Effective Public Comments

The Task Force's analysis reinforces these comment effectiveness factors:
- Engaging EARLY (pre-NPRM) is more influential than late
- Comments that build out the administrative record support later challenges
- Comments from diverse stakeholders matter for legitimacy
- Plain language matters - both from agency to public AND public to agency
- Substantive engagement with the agency's reasoning is critical
