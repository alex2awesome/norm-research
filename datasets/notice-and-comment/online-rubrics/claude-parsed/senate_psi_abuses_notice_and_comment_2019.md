# Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations: Abuses of the Federal Notice-and-Comment Rulemaking Process

## Overview

Released October 24, 2019, the bipartisan staff report from the U.S. Senate Committee on Homeland Security & Governmental Affairs (Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations) examines abuses of the notice-and-comment rulemaking process.

## Key Findings

### Mass / Fraudulent Comment Problems
- Many high-profile rulemakings have received fraudulent comments
- Stolen identities used to submit comments without consent
- Bots and automated systems generated millions of comments
- Net neutrality and other proceedings particularly affected

### Examples Investigated
- 2017 FCC net neutrality proceeding (~22 million comments, large fraction fraudulent)
- CFPB payday lending rule
- FERC pipeline projects
- HHS/CMS payment rules

### Agency Identification Challenges
- Agencies lack robust authentication mechanisms
- Difficult to distinguish AI-generated, bot, and human comments
- Mass campaigns blur substantive vs. preference signaling

## Implications for Effective Comment Standards

The report indirectly establishes a "anti-rubric" - what comment quality looks like when it FAILS:
- Identical text across submissions (mass campaign indicator)
- Identity attribution problems (impersonation)
- Submission patterns inconsistent with claimed identity
- No engagement with specific provisions
- No verifiable factual content

## Recommendations from Report

- Agencies should improve identity verification mechanisms
- Better deduplication tools
- Enhanced transparency about how comments were processed
- Coordination across agencies on common standards
- Enforcement against parties submitting fraudulent comments

## Implications for Comment Writers

- Submit your comment from clearly identified email/account
- Make it substantively distinct from any campaign template
- Include personal details that demonstrate authentic identity
- Provide verifiable factual claims
- Engage with specific regulatory provisions

These steps maximize the probability your comment is treated as substantive rather than dismissed as part of a mass campaign.
