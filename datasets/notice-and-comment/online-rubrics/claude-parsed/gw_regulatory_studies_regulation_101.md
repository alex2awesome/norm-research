# GW Regulatory Studies Center's "Regulation 101"

## Overview
The Regulatory Studies Center presents regulation as a critical federal policy tool that remains poorly understood. The program explains how each government branch contributes to developing regulations and how citizens can participate.

## Key Components

**The Rulemaking Process:**
Agencies typically issue a "notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM), or even an advanced notice of proposed rulemaking (ANPRM)" for public feedback. The standard comment period lasts at least 60 days, though timelines vary by rule.

**Three Branches' Roles:**
- **Legislative:** Congress creates the regulatory framework and delegates authority to agencies
- **Executive:** Agencies draft, propose, and finalize rules through established procedures
- **Judicial:** Courts review cases challenging agency implementations

## Public Participation Opportunities

The center emphasizes that interested parties can:
- Submit written comments during designated periods
- Request individual meetings with agency representatives and the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA)
- Attend public hearings when held
- Challenge final rules through congressional resolutions or litigation

The center notes this process "isn't a clear-cut, linear one" and encourages active civic engagement in rulemaking.

## Available Resources
The center offers *Regulation: A Primer* by Susan E. Dudley and Jerry Brito (available in English and Korean), plus extensive educational materials on regulatory theory and practice.
