# DOE Community Guide to Environmental Justice and NEPA Methods

## Overview

A Community Guide outlines a framework for how Federal agencies generally consider EJ in the NEPA process and summarizes opportunities that communities may use to work with these agencies to advance environmental justice.

## NEPA as Public Comment Tool

NEPA is one of the most powerful environmental laws because it requires the government consider the environmental impacts of major federal actions and give the public a chance to comment on those actions. NEPA is also an important environmental justice tool because it empowers individuals to communicate directly with federal agencies and offer alternatives to proposed actions.

## Public Comment Effectiveness Framework

In general, all public comments must be read and acknowledged by the agency, but government agencies can produce one response that addresses several related comments together.

## Key EJ Comment Elements

Effective EJ comments should:

1. **Identify the affected community** - demographic data on race, income, language access
2. **Document existing burdens** - cumulative pollution, health disparities, prior contamination
3. **Identify disproportionate impacts** - how the proposed action affects this community vs. others
4. **Demand alternative analyses** - alternative locations, technologies, mitigation measures
5. **Reference EJSCREEN data** - EPA's environmental justice screening tool
6. **Cite executive orders** - EO 12898, EO 14008
7. **Highlight cumulative impacts** - aggregation across pollution sources
8. **Demand meaningful engagement** - language access, time/place accessibility, in-person hearings
9. **Document community assets** - cultural sites, sacred lands, traditional ecological knowledge

## Community Participation Requirements

A core principle of Environmental Justice (EJ) is the full and fair participation by all affected communities in the transportation decision-making process. The goal of the EJ portion of the NEPA process is to foster meaningful participation.

## Stages for Comment

- Scoping (early - frame the analysis)
- Draft EIS (substantive analysis comments)
- Final EIS / Record of Decision (preserve for litigation)
- Permit/license stage (specific to facility approval)
