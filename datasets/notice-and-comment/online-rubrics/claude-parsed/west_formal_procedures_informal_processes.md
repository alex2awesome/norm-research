# West, "Formal Procedures, Informal Processes, Accountability, and Responsiveness" (2004)

West examined 42 rules and concluded that **formal notice-and-comment matters less than
informal pre-proposal processes**: by the time a rule is published, agency thinking is largely
locked in. Effective participation often happens during the pre-NPRM phase that the public
never sees.

## Implications for When and How to Comment

### 1. Engage Pre-Proposal When Possible
West shows that comments on the proposed rule rarely shift agency thinking significantly because
the agency has already made commitments. **Effective comment strategy includes:**
- Submitting comments to the **Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM)** when issued
- Petitioning for rulemaking before the agency drafts a proposal
- Participating in stakeholder meetings, technical workshops, and Reg.gov pre-proposal dockets
- Filing FOIA requests on agency working drafts

### 2. Use Notice-and-Comment to Preserve Issues for Litigation
Even if comments don't shift the rule, they **preserve issues for judicial review** and force
the agency to respond on the record. This is itself a substantive function of comments.

### 3. Identify and Address the Real Decision-Makers
West emphasizes that informal processes involve OMB/OIRA, the White House, congressional
committees, and other agencies. **Effective comments may need to be supplemented by direct
engagement** with these actors.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment engage with the agency at the earliest possible stage (ANPRM, petition,
      pre-proposal meeting)?
- [ ] Does the comment preserve specific issues for judicial review by clearly stating the
      objection and the requested alternative?
- [ ] Does the comment identify other decision-makers (OMB, White House, Congress) whose
      involvement is implicated?
- [ ] Does the comment address informal commitments the agency has already made (e.g., as
      revealed in speeches, draft documents, or guidance)?
- [ ] Does the comment recognize the stage of the rulemaking and tailor the ask to what is
      still movable at that stage?
