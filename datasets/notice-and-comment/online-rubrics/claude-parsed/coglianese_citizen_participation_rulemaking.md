# Coglianese - Citizen Participation in Rulemaking: Past, Present, and Future

Cary Coglianese, Duke Law Journal, 2006.

## Key Argument

The paper compares empirical observations on citizen participation in the past, before e-rulemaking, with more recent data on citizen participation after the introduction of various types of technological innovations.

## Key Findings

The findings indicate that **the more significant barriers to citizen participation are cognitive and motivational**, as even with e-rulemaking, it takes a high level of technical sophistication to understand and comment on regulatory proceedings.

Contrary to prevailing predictions, empirical research shows that e-rulemaking makes little difference: citizen input remains typically sparse.

## Implications for Comment Effectiveness

- The bar to writing an "effective" comment is high - requires technical/regulatory literacy
- Lowering technological barriers alone does not democratize participation
- Most rulemakings receive few comments - a thoughtful comment can stand out
- Real participation requires understanding of:
  - The specific statutory authority for the rule
  - The agency's prior reasoning in the preamble
  - The economic and scientific analyses (RIA, etc.)
  - The legal/procedural standards for judicial review

## Related Work

Coglianese also contributed to a nonpartisan Task Force on Transparency and Public Participation that met in 2008 to identify current deficiencies in agency rule making procedures and develop recommendations, indicating ways that federal agencies could do a better job of seeking citizen comment earlier in the rule making process.
