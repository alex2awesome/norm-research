# Balla et al., "Lost in the Flood?" (2022)

Empirical analysis of 1,049 mass comment campaigns across 22 EPA rulemakings (2012-2017).
Findings:

- **EPA references mass campaigns in responses but at lower rates than other comments**
- **Mass campaigns are particularly unlikely to be associated with substantive rule changes**
- **Even procedural acknowledgment is reduced** for campaign-style submissions

## Implications for Rubric Design

### 1. Identical Comments Get Discounted
A comment that is verbatim identical to thousands of others is functionally counted as one
voice (or less). **Mass-campaign duplicate text dramatically reduces a comment's effective
weight**, even where the agency formally counts each submission.

### 2. Modifications Boost Substantive Weight
Adding even a paragraph of personalized content can lift a comment from the "mass campaign"
category into the "substantive" category. **Encourage commenters from mass campaigns to add:**
- A personal experience or example
- A locally-relevant fact (geography, occupation, family situation)
- A specific concern about a specific provision

### 3. Mass Campaigns Need to Be Coupled With Substantive Submissions
A campaign that produces 100,000 form letters but no separately-filed substantive comment
from the sponsoring organization is **less effective** than a campaign with a strong umbrella
comment from the organization plus the form letters as supporting volume.

### 4. The Procedural Discount Is Documented
Balla's finding that EPA cites mass campaigns at lower rates means commenters who care
about formal acknowledgment in the response-to-comments document should not rely on form
letters alone.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment go beyond verbatim mass-campaign text?
- [ ] Does the comment include a personalized experiential or factual element?
- [ ] Does the comment localize the impact (geography, sector, demographic)?
- [ ] If part of a campaign, is it accompanied by a separately-filed substantive submission
      from the sponsoring organization?
- [ ] Does the comment identify at least one specific provision of the proposed rule?

## Caution

Balla's findings should not be read as endorsing the procedural marginalization of mass
campaigns — Mendelson's argument that values comments deserve weight remains live. But as a
matter of agency practice, **substantive personalization is what produces both procedural
acknowledgment and substantive influence**.
