# Stewart, "The Reformation of American Administrative Law" (1975)

Stewart's seminal article identifies the "interest representation model" as the central framework
animating modern administrative law. Comments to agencies must be evaluated through the lens of
ensuring that all affected interests—not just regulated industries—are represented in agency
decision-making.

## Core Framework: Interest Representation Model

Three pillars structure modern participation rights:
1. **Standing expansion** — broadening who may seek judicial review of agency action
2. **Expansion of participation rights** before agencies during rule development
3. **Adequate consideration requirement** — agencies must address claims raised by participants

## Implications for Effective Comments

A high-quality comment under the interest representation model:

- **Identifies the affected interest** the commenter represents and explains why that interest
  has been historically under-represented or might be ignored
- **Demonstrates the comment is not duplicative of regulated industry submissions** — diffuse,
  consumer, environmental, or beneficiary interests deserve special weight because they have
  structural barriers to participation
- **Frames arguments to trigger the "adequately consider" judicial requirement** — comments
  should raise issues the agency must address in the final rule's preamble
- **Counters the risk of agency capture** by introducing factual claims, alternative cost
  estimates, or distributional analyses the regulated industry will not supply
- **Asserts a procedural right** (e.g., to additional data disclosure, supplemental comment
  period, hearing) where the record is incomplete

## Diagnostic Questions Stewart's Framework Raises

- Does the comment surface an interest the agency would otherwise discount?
- Does the comment force the agency to confront a tradeoff it would prefer to elide?
- Does the comment enlarge the universe of facts/values the agency must address on the record?
- Does the comment lay a foundation for judicial review by preserving an objection?

## Cautions

Stewart warns that participation rights without substantive constraints can produce **interest-group
pluralism** rather than public-regarding decisions. Effective comments therefore should not merely
assert preferences but should provide reasoned justifications grounded in the statute's purposes.
