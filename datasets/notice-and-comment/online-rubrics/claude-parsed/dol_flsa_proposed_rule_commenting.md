# DOL Wage and Hour Division: Commenting on FLSA Rules

## How to Submit Comments

Comments may be submitted, identified by Regulatory Information Number (RIN), through the Federal eRulemaking Portal at https://www.regulations.gov.

## Comment Period

Comments must be received on or before the deadline specified in the Federal Register notice. Recent FLSA rules have had ~60-day comment periods.

## Effective FLSA Comments

For wage and hour rules (overtime, employee/contractor classification, joint employer, tip pooling, etc.):
- Cite specific FLSA sections (29 USC §§ 201-219)
- Reference 29 CFR provisions being amended
- Engage with relevant DOL Field Operations Handbook sections
- Cite Wage and Hour Opinion Letters
- Reference federal court FLSA decisions (Encino Motorcars, etc.)
- Provide industry-specific data on wages, hours, classification
- Address impact on small employers (RFA)
- Cite economic impact estimates with verifiable assumptions
- Engage with state wage-hour law parallel provisions
- Address worker misclassification concerns
- Cite IRS Form 1099 and W-2 data trends
- Address gig economy / platform worker considerations

## Common FLSA Comment Topics

- Overtime exempt/non-exempt salary thresholds
- Independent contractor vs. employee classification
- Joint employer standards
- Tip pooling and tip credit
- Companionship services exemption
- Computer professional exemption
- White collar exemptions (executive, administrative, professional)
- Outside sales exemption
- Bonus and incentive payment treatment
- Hours worked / portal-to-portal issues

## DOL Comment Strategy

Effective DOL comments are typically:
- Detailed (10-30 pages for substantive rules)
- Heavily cited to economic literature
- Include compliance cost estimates
- Address impact on specific industries (restaurants, retail, healthcare, construction)
- Engage with both employer and employee perspectives
- Address transition timelines and good-faith compliance
