# Rulemaking (Wikipedia)

## Overview
Rulemaking is the process by which U.S. executive and independent agencies create regulations. Congress establishes broad policy mandates; agencies develop detailed rules with scientific and technical expertise.

## Public Participation Framework
- The public receives advance notice of proposed rules
- Citizens can submit written comments and provide data
- The rulemaking record becomes publicly accessible
- Agencies must analyze and respond to public input
- A permanent documentation trail is maintained
- Judicial review remains available

## Notice-and-Comment Process
1. Proposed rule in Federal Register with justification and analysis
2. Public Comment Period (30-180 days depending on complexity)
3. Agency Response — must address every issue raised
4. Final Rule — published with comment responses, updated analysis, final language
5. Codification in the CFR

## What Comments Accomplish
Comments provide the agency with data it may lack, identify gaps in analysis, and create an official record the agency must address. Mechanism balances expert agency decision-making with democratic accountability.

## Rulemaking Types
- **Informal rulemaking** (notice-and-comment) — standard
- **Hybrid rulemaking** — formal hearing procedures with cross-examination, only when Congress specifically authorizes
