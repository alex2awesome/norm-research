# ACUS Recommendation 2011-2 — Rulemaking Comments

The Administrative Conference's formal best-practices recommendation for the comment process,
addressing both agency obligations and what makes a comment effective.

## Best Practices for Agencies (and Implied Norms for Commenters)

ACUS recommends:

### Adequate Comment Period Length
- **At least 60 days** for significant regulatory actions
- **At least 30 days** for other rulemakings
- *Rubric implication*: A timely-filed comment is one filed within these periods; commenters
  benefit from filing early enough to be processed.

### Public Guidance on How to Submit Effective Comments
Agencies should provide guidance describing what constitutes a substantive comment.
*Rubric implication*: A good comment follows the agency-specific guidance provided in the NPRM
or on the docket.

### Posting of Received Comments
Agencies should make submitted comments publicly available.
*Rubric implication*: Comments should be drafted assuming public scrutiny — commenters can
build on others' submissions and avoid duplication.

### Definition of "Significant Adverse Comment" (in direct final rule context)
A significant adverse comment is one where the commenter:
- Explains why the rule would be inappropriate (challenges underlying premise or approach), OR
- Explains why the rule would be ineffective or unacceptable without a change

## Rubric Items Distilled from ACUS 2011-2

- [ ] Was the comment filed within the official comment period (and ideally in the first half
      to allow time for agency processing)?
- [ ] Does the comment follow any agency-specific submission guidance (formatting, length,
      docket reference)?
- [ ] Does the comment explain why the proposed rule would be inappropriate, ineffective, or
      unacceptable as written?
- [ ] Does the comment propose a specific change rather than just expressing opposition?
- [ ] Does the comment challenge the rule's underlying premise where relevant (not merely
      requesting marginal adjustment)?

## Why ACUS Recommendation 2011-2 Matters

ACUS recommendations are the consensus position of administrative law experts (academics,
practitioners, agency staff) and inform agency practice. **Agencies are much more likely to
treat as substantive a comment that meets ACUS's articulated criteria.**
