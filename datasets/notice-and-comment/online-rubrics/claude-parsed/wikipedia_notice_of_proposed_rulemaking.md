# Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (Wikipedia)

## Definition
"A public notice that is issued by law when a U.S. federal agency wishes to add, remove, or change a rule or regulation" as part of administrative rulemaking.

## Legal Basis
Federal agencies must publish NPRMs under Section 553 of the Administrative Procedure Act (APA). Congress required this to ensure agencies consider input from affected parties.

## Process
- NPRMs published in the Federal Register
- Standard comment period: 60 days for initial; 30 additional days for reply

## Required Contents
1. Time, place, nature of the proceeding
2. Authority citation for the action
3. Description of subjects and proposed regulation terms
4. Deadline for written comments
5. Methods for public participation

## Preliminary Procedures
- **Notice of Inquiry (NOI)**: comments before proposals are drafted
- **Advanced Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM)**: input on potential changes
- After comments, agencies may issue a **Further NPRM (FNPRM)** if substantial changes need additional public input

## Public Access
The federal government launched Regulations.gov in 2002 to improve public access.

## Implication for Commenters
The required-contents list shows what's mandatory in the NPRM — anchor your comment to the agency's stated authority, the specified subjects, and the deadline. Comments should make explicit how they map to the published proceeding.
