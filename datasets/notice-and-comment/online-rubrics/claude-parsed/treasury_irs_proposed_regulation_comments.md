# IRS/Treasury - Commenting on Proposed Tax Regulations

## How to Submit Comments

Proposed Treasury regulations are published in the Federal Register as a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, which invites the public to review and comment.

Treasury and the IRS publish a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking ("NPRM" or "proposed rule") that contains:
- A description of the proposed rules
- The legal authority for those rules
- An outline of relevant analysis and issues
- The text of the proposed regulations
- A deadline for comments

## Comment Period

Treasury and the IRS typically set the comment period at **30, 60, or 90 days**, with the comment period running from the date of the NPRM's publication on the Federal Register website.

Although Treasury and the IRS are required to consider comments submitted before the deadline, they will often consider comments that are submitted after the comment period is closed.

## Public Hearings

A public hearing may be held if a sufficient number of requests to speak at a hearing are received.

## Effective Tax Comments

For Treasury/IRS rules, effective comments typically:
- Cite specific Internal Revenue Code sections (§ 162, § 263A, § 481, etc.)
- Reference Treasury Regulations sections (Reg. § 1.XXX-X) being amended
- Engage with prior IRS guidance (revenue rulings, revenue procedures, notices)
- Address Tax Court and other case law where relevant
- Include taxpayer-impact estimates with quantification
- Distinguish impacts on different taxpayer categories
- Engage with relevant legislative history (Joint Committee Blue Books, conference reports)
- Address compliance burden estimates
- Engage with alternative methodologies for accounting issues
- Consider international tax implications (treaty interactions)

## Public Hearing Practice

- Submit outline of topics in advance
- Prepare 10-minute presentation
- Anticipate technical questions
- Coordinate with other commenters to avoid duplication
