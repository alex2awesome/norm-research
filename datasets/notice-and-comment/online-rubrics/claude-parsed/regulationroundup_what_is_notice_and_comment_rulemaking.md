# What Is Notice-and-Comment Rulemaking? (Regulation Roundup)

## What It Is
"The process federal agencies use to create, amend, or repeal regulations" mandated by the Administrative Procedure Act of 1946.

## Six-Step Process
1. **Agency identifies a need** — Congress directs action or an agency spots a problem in its authority.
2. **Draft proposed rule** — subject matter experts develop the regulation; OIRA review for significant rules.
3. **Publish in Federal Register** — NPRM goes public with comment deadline.
4. **Public comment period** — typically 30-90 days; submit via Regulations.gov.
5. **Revise and finalize** — agency reviews comments and publishes final rule with response to significant feedback.
6. **Rule takes effect** — legally enforceable on the specified date.

## Making Your Comments Count
"Quality and specificity of your comment supported by data, cost estimates, or legal analysis is what counts." Mass form-letter campaigns carry minimal weight compared to substantive, evidence-based submissions.

Agencies must provide "reasoned consideration" to significant comments but aren't bound by majority opinion. Well-documented technical objections from affected industries or state agencies often prove more influential than volume alone.

## Timeline Reality
Most major rules take 2-5 years from conception to enforcement.
