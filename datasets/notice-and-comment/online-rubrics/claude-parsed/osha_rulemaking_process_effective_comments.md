# OSHA Rulemaking Process & Public Comment Guidance

## Overview
OSHA initiates rulemaking through new technologies, worker injuries/illness, advisory committee recommendations, petitions, presidential direction, or research. The process has seven stages from pre-rule through publication.

## Public Participation
OSHA emphasizes worker input is especially valuable since employees "often know the most about hazards on the job and how to fix the hazards." Past example: revised vertical tandem lift regulations based on trade association concerns about feasibility.

## Seven-Stage Rulemaking
1. **Pre-Rule** — RFI or ANPRM to determine if regulation is needed
2-3. **Proposed Rule** — NPRM in Federal Register; small business meetings; advisory committees
4-5. **Final Rule Development** — agency analyzes comments and develops final rule
6-7. **Publication/Outreach** — final rule with compliance dates and educational materials

## Effective Comments
- "Clearly communicate whether you are for or against the proposed action, why, and how it impacts you"
- Include personal stories about workplace impacts
- Provide specific details and suggestions
- Address only relevant sections (you needn't comment on everything)

## Submission and Tracking
- Online at Regulations.gov, by mail, or in person
- 11:59 PM ET deadline on stated due date
- Track via Regulations.gov, RegInfo.gov, Federal Register, OSHA QuickTakes
