# Sunstein, "The Cost-Benefit Revolution" (2018)

Sunstein chronicles and defends the use of quantitative cost-benefit analysis as the
dominant analytical framework for U.S. federal rulemaking. **For commenters, the implication
is that engagement with the agency's CBA is the most direct route to influence.**

## What Sunstein's Framework Reveals About Effective Comments

### 1. Quantification Matters
Comments that monetize impacts (using accepted methods like willingness-to-pay or
value-of-statistical-life) are more persuasive than comments that assert harm without
numbers. **A good comment translates qualitative concerns into quantified estimates** with
clear methodology.

### 2. Information About Costs and Benefits
Sunstein notes officials often lack information. **Comments that supply unique cost or
benefit data** — operational figures, observed health effects, market behavior data —
fill a real informational gap.

### 3. Behavioral Insights
Behavioral economics suggests rules can be improved by recognizing how people actually
behave. **Effective comments anticipate behavioral responses** to the rule and identify
where the rule's design assumes unrealistic rationality.

### 4. Distributional Concerns Need Engagement, Not Just Assertion
CBA traditionally aggregates costs and benefits without weighting. **Comments seeking to
elevate distributional concerns** must engage CBA's terms — proposing weighting schemes,
identifying who bears costs and benefits, and arguing why aggregation is inappropriate.

## Rubric Items for CBA-Engaged Comments

- [ ] Does the comment provide quantified estimates of costs or benefits with stated methods?
- [ ] Does the comment identify cost or benefit categories the agency omitted?
- [ ] Does the comment propose alternative discount rates, time horizons, or counterfactuals?
- [ ] Does the comment address behavioral assumptions in the agency's analysis?
- [ ] Does the comment engage distributional concerns with specific affected populations?
- [ ] Does the comment use accepted CBA methodologies (VSL, WTP, social cost of carbon)
      where relevant?

## Cautions

Sunstein's defense of CBA is contested. Comments engaging CBA should also flag where:
- The statute prohibits CBA-balancing (e.g., NAAQS under the Clean Air Act)
- The CBA monetizes things commenters consider non-monetizable (cultural sites, dignity)
- The CBA's distributional aggregation conceals an unjust outcome
