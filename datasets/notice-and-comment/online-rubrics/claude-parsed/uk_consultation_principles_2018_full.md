# UK Cabinet Office, Consultation Principles 2018 — Eleven Principles

The UK guidance is structured around **eleven principles** governing how government should
consult. While addressed to government, the principles imply norms for what consultees should
provide.

## The Eleven Principles

1. **Clear and concise** — Use plain English; avoid acronyms
2. **Purposeful** — Consult only when necessary; not on settled matters
3. **Informative** — Provide context and impact assessments
4. **Part of engagement** — Use informal, iterative approaches and digital tools
5. **Proportionate duration** — Balance thoroughness against unnecessary delay
6. **Targeted** — Reach affected populations and representative groups
7. **Considerate of groups** — Adapt methods (e.g., charities need more time)
8. **Pre-agreed** — Seek collective approval before publishing
9. **Facilitates scrutiny** — Publish responses alongside original consultation
10. **Timely responses** — Publish responses within 12 weeks of consultation closing
11. **Avoid election periods** — Don't launch during local/national elections

## Implications for Effective UK Consultation Responses

Drawing on the principles, an effective consultation response:

### Mirrors the Government's Plain-Language Standard
- Uses plain English with terms defined
- Limits length to what is necessary
- Structures the response so a non-expert reader can navigate it

### Engages with the Specific Consultation Questions
The guidance encourages government to ask focused questions. Effective responses **answer
the questions asked**, not adjacent issues.

### Provides Evidence Proportionate to the Issue
Detailed evidence is appropriate for substantial issues; brief responses are appropriate
for narrower questions.

### Is Targeted to the Government's Stated Decision
Effective responses identify which decision points the response addresses and how the
proposed change would affect outcomes.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Is the response in plain English with technical terms defined?
- [ ] Does the response answer the specific consultation questions?
- [ ] Is the response proportionate in length to the issue?
- [ ] Does the response identify the specific decision points it addresses?
- [ ] Does the response provide evidence to support its claims?
- [ ] Does the response acknowledge the consultation's stated purpose and scope?

## Comparative Note

The UK principles emphasize **government's obligations** more than commenters' duties — a
contrast with US guidance, which often focuses on what commenters must do to be heard. The
UK's "facilitates scrutiny" principle (publish responses) creates an implicit norm that
responses are public-facing documents.
