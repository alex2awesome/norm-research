# McGarity, "Some Thoughts on 'Deossifying' the Rulemaking Process" (1992)

McGarity's diagnosis of "ossified" rulemaking identifies the procedural and analytical demands
that have made notice-and-comment slow and rigid. For commenters, McGarity's framework reveals
the **categories of objection that agencies are most legally vulnerable to** — and therefore
the categories that effective comments should raise.

## Sources of Ossification (= Vectors of Effective Comment Attack)

McGarity catalogues procedural and analytical burdens commenters can press:

### 1. Hard-Look Judicial Review
Courts demand agencies "consider the relevant data and articulate a satisfactory explanation."
**Effective comments force the agency to address specific data and rationales** — comments that
present such data preserve them for the record and trigger response obligations.

### 2. Multiple Required Analyses
Agencies must comply with regulatory impact analysis (EO 12866), Regulatory Flexibility Act
(small business), Unfunded Mandates Reform Act, NEPA, Paperwork Reduction Act, and others.
**Comments that identify deficiencies in any required analysis create remand risk.**

### 3. Alternatives Analysis
Agencies must consider reasonable alternatives. **Comments that propose specific, viable
alternatives the agency did not consider** create a legal vulnerability.

### 4. Logical Outgrowth Doctrine
Final rules must be a logical outgrowth of the proposal. **Comments that highlight novel
elements** in the proposal force the agency to provide more notice or face remand.

### 5. Information-Quality Challenges
The Data Quality Act allows challenges to the quality of agency-relied-upon data. **Comments
documenting data deficiencies** can prompt corrections.

## Derived Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment identify a relevant data point the agency failed to address?
- [ ] Does the comment propose a specific alternative the agency failed to consider?
- [ ] Does the comment challenge the quality, currency, or applicability of agency data?
- [ ] Does the comment identify deficiencies in any required analysis (RIA, RFA, NEPA, etc.)?
- [ ] Does the comment surface a novel element of the proposal that may exceed the notice given?

## McGarity's Caution

These same procedural levers can be used to **block public-protective rules**. A comment rubric
should be neutral as to direction; the test is whether the comment raises a legally cognizable
defect, not which side it favors.
