# Furlong & Kerwin, "Interest Group Participation in Rule Making: A Decade of Change" (2005)

Survey-based comparison of interest group rulemaking tactics in 1992 and 2002. Documents the
**tactical mix that organizations use** to influence rules — not just submitting comments, but
combining comments with other actions.

## Most Effective Tactics (Furlong & Kerwin's Survey Results)

Organizations rated tactics by perceived effectiveness:

1. **Submitting formal comments during notice-and-comment** — most universally used and
   highly rated
2. **Direct contact with agency staff** — informal meetings with rule writers
3. **Participating in agency-sponsored meetings or workshops**
4. **Submitting comments at the proposed rule and final rule stages with continuity of position**
5. **Filing comments at the ANPRM stage**
6. **Coordinating with other organizations to file aligned comments**
7. **Contacting the OMB during regulatory review**
8. **Engaging Congress to pressure the agency**
9. **Litigation as last resort**

## Implications for a Comment Rubric

A high-quality comment is part of an **integrated participation strategy**, not a one-shot
filing. Comments should:

### 1. Reference Prior Engagement
The comment should reflect (and reference where appropriate) prior meetings, ANPRM
submissions, or related research the commenter or allied organizations have produced.

### 2. Anticipate Subsequent Steps
Comments should be drafted with awareness that the agency, OMB, and Congress will all see them.
Tone and content should serve all audiences.

### 3. Build a Record for Multiple Decision Points
Effective comments preserve issues at the proposed rule stage, the final rule stage, and
potentially in litigation. Phrasing should be precise enough to be quoted later.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment reference (or build on) prior engagement at the ANPRM, workshop, or
      petition stage?
- [ ] Does the comment coordinate with allied submissions to build a consistent record?
- [ ] Does the comment serve multiple audiences (agency staff, OMB, Congress, courts)?
- [ ] Does the comment preserve specific issues for the final rule stage and beyond?
- [ ] Is the comment part of a documented advocacy strategy (e.g., the organization is a known
      stakeholder, has a position paper, has met with staff)?
