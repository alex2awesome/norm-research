# Mercatus Center, "A Guide to Writing Public Interest Comments Using Economic Analysis"

Mercatus's six-step framework for writing comments grounded in economic analysis. Treats
comments as economic-analysis submissions: their persuasive value lies in helping the agency
do better cost-benefit reasoning.

## Six Fundamental Actions for an Effective Comment

1. **Understand the regulatory process** — Know how Congress delegates authority and the
   procedures the agency follows
2. **Appreciate the regulatory agency's definition of "public interest"** — Align analysis
   with the agency's stated framework
3. **Master the key topics of good economic regulatory analysis** — Address the substantive
   economic considerations the agency must weigh
4. **Recognize the agency's legal authority and constraints** — Understand statutory limits
   and procedural requirements
5. **Use appropriate format, language, and tone** — Present professionally and clearly
6. **Disseminate the work** — Share the comment with relevant stakeholders, allies, and
   policymakers

## Topics a "Good Economic Regulatory Analysis" Should Address

A high-quality comment evaluates the proposed rule against:

- **Systemic problem identification** — Is there a real market failure or other systemic
  problem the regulation addresses?
- **Causal mechanism** — How will the rule actually produce the intended outcome?
- **Alternatives considered** — Has the agency examined non-regulatory or different
  regulatory alternatives?
- **Cost analysis** — Are direct, compliance, and opportunity costs appropriately
  considered?
- **Benefit analysis** — Are benefits properly quantified and traced to the rule?
- **Distributional analysis** — Who bears costs vs. who receives benefits?
- **Behavioral assumptions** — Does the rule rest on realistic assumptions about how
  affected parties will respond?
- **Uncertainty and sensitivity** — Has the agency addressed the range of outcomes under
  alternative assumptions?

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment evaluate whether the rule addresses a real systemic problem?
- [ ] Does the comment trace the causal mechanism from rule to outcome?
- [ ] Does the comment examine alternatives the agency may have overlooked?
- [ ] Does the comment offer specific cost or benefit analysis with stated methods?
- [ ] Does the comment address distributional consequences?
- [ ] Does the comment test behavioral assumptions in the agency's analysis?
- [ ] Does the comment address uncertainty and sensitivity?

## Caution

Mercatus's frame is economic-efficiency-oriented. Comments grounded in non-monetary values
(equity, dignity, environmental integrity) should still benefit from the structural rigor of
the framework but may need to push back on its monetization presumptions.
