# OECD Regulatory Policy Outlook 2025 — Stakeholder Engagement

The OECD's cross-country assessment of stakeholder engagement in regulation, with explicit
indicators and best practices.

## OECD Key Findings on Stakeholder Engagement Quality

- **Over 97% of OECD members** require public participation in consultations on at least some
  primary laws and subordinate regulations.
- **82% systematically require** public participation.
- Yet **only one-third of OECD members provide post-consultation feedback** to participants.
- **Most countries consult only after a draft proposal exists** — few consult at problem-
  definition stage.

## OECD Best Practices for Stakeholder Engagement

The OECD recommends:

### 1. Early Consultation on Problem Definition
Consultations conducted at the policy-problem-definition stage produce better evidence than
consultations on draft proposals alone. **A high-quality stakeholder input contributes at
this stage**, not just on the formal draft.

### 2. Diversified Channels of Input
Consultations should use multiple formats — surveys, focus groups, written submissions,
roundtables — to capture different stakeholder voices.

### 3. Transparent Mechanisms Showing How Feedback Shaped the Final Result
Stakeholders should be able to see how their input affected the outcome. **Effective
submissions therefore should be specific enough that the agency can cite them.**

### 4. Feedback to Participants
The OECD identifies this as the area of greatest deficit globally.

## Stakeholder Categories (Per OECD)

The OECD recognizes diverse stakeholders:
- Citizens
- Businesses
- Consumers
- Employees and their representative organizations
- Public sector
- NGOs
- International trading partners
- Other stakeholders

## Rubric Items Distilled from OECD Indicators

- [ ] Does the input engage at the earliest practical stage of regulatory development?
- [ ] Is the input specific enough to be cited in the consultation outcome report?
- [ ] Does the input clearly identify the stakeholder category and interests represented?
- [ ] Does the input acknowledge the consultation's stated objectives?
- [ ] Does the input use one of the consultation's offered channels appropriately?
- [ ] Does the input provide evidence (data, examples, expert opinion) proportionate to the
      claim?

## Comparative Note

The OECD framework is more **demand-side** focused than US guidance: it emphasizes what
governments must do to elicit good input. But the implicit complement is that good input
matches the structure governments provide — answering the questions asked, contributing at
the stages governments offer, and using the channels designed for the input.
