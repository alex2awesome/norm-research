# Center for Progressive Reform: The Regulatory Process FAQs

## Overview

The Center for Progressive Reform (CPR) provides educational resources on the regulatory process, focusing on how members of the public, advocates, and affected communities can engage with rulemaking.

## Key FAQs

### What is rulemaking?
Federal agencies create rules to implement statutes Congress has enacted. The notice-and-comment rulemaking process under APA § 553 is the default procedure.

### Who can comment?
Any member of the public—individuals, organizations, businesses, state/local governments, tribes—can submit comments.

### How does my comment get considered?
Comments enter the docket. Agency staff review them. Significant comments must be addressed in the agency's preamble to the final rule. Important rules receive detailed comments from industry, public interest groups, and often state governments, with each comment requiring a careful response.

## CPR's Distinctive Recommendations

- **Push back against industry dominance** - public-interest commenters should counter industry's volume and technical sophistication advantage
- **Engage at the OIRA stage** - the meeting log shows who's getting White House access
- **Address regulatory underkill** - many comments should advocate for stronger, not weaker, rules
- **Watch for "regulatory capture"** signals - excessive deference to industry data, weak alternatives
- **Use the rulemaking record for litigation** - comments preserve issues for judicial review
- **Coordinate with allied groups** - amplifying expert technical comments adds weight

## Strategies CPR Highlights

- Demand the agency apply Congress's full statutory mandate
- Cite when an agency is using industry-supplied data without independent analysis
- Highlight environmental justice impacts
- Note when an agency's RIA undercounts benefits or overcounts costs
- Track and respond to industry comments substantively
