# Levin - The Duty to Respond to Rulemaking Comments

Ronald M. Levin, Yale Law Journal Forum.

## Overview

The essay identifies and evaluates several interesting features of agencies' duty to respond to comments during the public comment period.

## Key Doctrinal Points

The article examines:

- The legal foundations for agencies' duty to respond to **significant** comments received during the public comment period
- The policies that support it
- The circumstances in which it comes into play

## Evolution of Doctrine

The duty serves as a case study illustrating institutional reform—courts and agencies have added considerable interpretive meaning to the Administrative Procedure Act (APA) over eight decades.

## Standing Issues

Legal obstacles to enforcing the duty to respond, including standing issues related to whether breach of the duty without tangible injury provides sufficient grounds for litigation.

## Costs and Benefits

The essay analyzes the costs and benefits of the duty, including:
- Its potential to lead to **"ossification"**—the slowing of the rulemaking process
- The benefits of forcing genuine engagement with comments
- The risks of creating perverse incentives to file frivolous comments to delay rules

## Implications for Comment Writers

A comment is more likely to compel response if it:
- Is "significant" in the legal sense (not duplicative, not frivolous)
- Raises specific issues the agency must address
- Provides data, evidence, or alternatives the agency cannot easily dismiss
- Is well-organized so individual claims are visible
- Cites the agency's own analysis to identify gaps
