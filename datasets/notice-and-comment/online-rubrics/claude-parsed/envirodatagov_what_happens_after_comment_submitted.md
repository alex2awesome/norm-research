# What Happens to a Public Comment After It Is Submitted (EDGI)

## The Journey After Submission
Once the comment period closes, agencies begin systematic review. Staff and contractors sort comments, grouping form-letter campaigns and dividing lengthy submissions into parts matched to specific rule sections. This allows specialists to address relevant comments efficiently.

## What Agencies Actually Consider
The EPA must respond to "significant comments" — those that are on-topic and provide "new data and/or specific and reasoned arguments for or against elements of the proposed rule." The agency evaluates whether comments contain scientific, technical, economic, or legal rationale worth incorporating.

Agencies "disregard comments they deem off-topic, vague, or otherwise inappropriate."

## Tactical Implications for Commenters

**What strengthens your comment:**
- Present new data or analyses not in the agency's original work
- Provide specific, reasoned arguments with evidence
- Clearly label which rule sections your points address
- Avoid vague language or off-topic material

## Important Limitation
While "final versions of many rules incorporate feedback" from public comments, "it is quite rare" that rules change fundamentally between proposed and final versions. Public comments are one of several engagement approaches available to advocates — combine with media, litigation, and legislative pressure for maximum effect.
