# McCubbins, Noll & Weingast, "Administrative Procedures as Instruments of Political Control" (1987)

The "McNollgast" thesis: notice-and-comment procedures exist not (or not only) for democratic
participation but to **stack the deck** — they ensure that the political coalitions that
enacted a statute remain represented in subsequent agency implementation. Effective comments
exploit this design.

## Core Insights for Commenters

### 1. Procedures Stack the Deck for Originally Intended Beneficiaries
The APA was designed so that the people the statute meant to protect would be alerted and
empowered to participate. **Effective comments invoke the original statutory bargain**: who
was Congress trying to help? How does the proposed rule depart from that intent?

### 2. "Fire-Alarm" Oversight
Congressional control of agencies relies on interest groups to sound alarms. A comment that
**explicitly flags a deviation from statutory intent**, identifies who will be harmed, and
provides documentation Congress could use is more powerful than one that simply objects.

### 3. Information Asymmetry Reduction
Procedures force agencies to disclose information. **Effective comments demand specific data,
challenge agency assumptions, and identify gaps in the record** — converting private agency
information into public information.

### 4. Auto-Pilot Enforcement
Procedures impose costs on agencies that try to favor unintended beneficiaries. **Comments
should document procedural deficiencies** (inadequate notice, undisclosed studies, foregone
alternatives) that create grounds for political and judicial pushback.

## Rubric Items Derived from McNollgast

- [ ] Does the comment cite the statutory purpose and identify the intended beneficiary class?
- [ ] Does the comment expose information the agency has not disclosed?
- [ ] Does the comment provide ammunition for congressional oversight (specific harms, costs,
      affected districts)?
- [ ] Does the comment document a procedural failing (inadequate alternatives, missing data,
      truncated comment period)?
- [ ] Does the comment connect the rule to the political coalition that enacted the underlying
      statute (e.g., labor for OSHA rules, environmentalists for CAA rules)?

## Caution

McNollgast's framework explains why **procedural arguments are systematically underweighted by
naive commenters but overweighted by repeat players**. A novice rubric should encourage even
substantive commenters to flag procedural defects.
