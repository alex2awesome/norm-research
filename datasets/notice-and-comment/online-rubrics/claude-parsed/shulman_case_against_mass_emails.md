# Shulman - The Case Against Mass E-mails: Perverse Incentives and Low Quality Public Participation in U.S. Federal Rulemaking

Stuart W. Shulman, *Policy & Internet*, 2009.

## Study Design

The paper examined 1,000 longest modified MoveOn.org-generated e-mails sent to the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) about its 2004 mercury rulemaking.

## Key Findings

- Only a tiny portion of these public comments constituted potentially relevant new information for the EPA to consider.
- The vast majority of MoveOn comments were either exact duplicates of a two-sentence form letter, or variants of a small number of broad claims about the inadequacy of the proposed rule.

## Implications - Rubric for Low-Quality Comments

The article presents a "theory of perverse incentives" identifying signs of low-quality mass campaign comments:
- Form-letter duplication
- Generic, broad claims rather than specific regulatory text
- Lack of new information or unique perspective
- No engagement with the agency's analytical framework
- No proposed alternatives or specific changes

## Practical Lesson

A small number of substantive, well-crafted comments can have more value than tens of thousands of identical form letters. Mass campaigns paradoxically can dilute public influence by overwhelming agencies with low-information submissions.
