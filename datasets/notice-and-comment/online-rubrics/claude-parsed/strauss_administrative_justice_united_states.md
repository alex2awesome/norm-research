# Strauss, "Administrative Justice in the United States" (1989, 2002, 2016 eds.)

Strauss's overview text — long the standard for orienting non-US lawyers to US administrative
law — provides clear analytical categories for what makes a rulemaking process legitimate and
how comments contribute. He emphasizes the **explanation obligation** as the key locus of
participation's value.

## Strauss's Frame: The Explanation Obligation

The agency's duty to explain — in the preamble to the final rule — its reasoning, its response
to significant comments, and its choice among alternatives is **the central mechanism through
which participation works**. Comments matter because the agency must explain itself in
relation to them.

## Implications for High-Quality Comments

### 1. Force a Specific Explanation
A good comment requires a specific response. Generic objections require only generic responses.
**Specificity in the comment compels specificity in the agency's explanation**.

### 2. Surface Issues That Require Reasoned Choice
Where the agency is making a choice (between alternatives, methodologies, levels of
stringency), a good comment **frames the choice clearly** so the agency must explain why it
chose as it did.

### 3. Connect to the Statutory Framework
Strauss emphasizes that explanation must be in relation to statutory authority. Comments that
**identify the statutory standard the rule must satisfy** make agency explanation more
demanding.

### 4. Operate at the Level of Reasoning, Not Conclusions
Comments that merely state preferences ("we oppose the rule") allow agency dismissal. Comments
that engage the **reasoning process** ("the agency's reliance on Study X is misplaced because
of Y") force engagement.

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment require a specific response that cannot be answered generically?
- [ ] Does the comment frame a choice the agency must explain its resolution of?
- [ ] Does the comment connect its objection to the relevant statutory standard?
- [ ] Does the comment engage the agency's reasoning process, not just its conclusions?
- [ ] Does the comment give the agency something new to explain (alternatives, factors,
      considerations)?
- [ ] Is the comment specific enough that the agency cannot dismiss it through summary
      acknowledgment?
