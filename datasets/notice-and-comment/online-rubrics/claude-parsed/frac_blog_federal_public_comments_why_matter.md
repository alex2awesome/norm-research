# Federal Public Comments: What They Are and Why They Matter (FRAC blog)

## Overview
Federal public comments are a legally mandated mechanism allowing citizens to weigh in on government decisions. The Administrative Procedure Act and Paperwork Reduction Act require agencies to "seek input from the public on new rules, rule changes, and information collection activities."

## Three Types of Items Open to Comment
- **Rules** — when federal agencies implement, modify, or remove regulations.
- **Requests for Information** — when agencies seek expertise from outside stakeholders.
- **Information Collection Activities** — when the government gathers data, balancing collection goals against respondent burden.

## Process
1. Agencies publish notices in the Federal Register
2. Comments typically remain open for 60 days (longer for certain requests)
3. Public submissions through regulations.gov (text or document uploads)
4. Agencies review and group comments by topic and campaign
5. Agencies must legally consider all substantive comments before finalizing rules

## Who Should Comment
Everyone — individuals, organizations, unions, local governments. Comments carry more weight when from experts or affected stakeholders.

## Do Comments Make a Difference?
Yes. Example: USDA withdrew its 2019 SNAP categorical-eligibility proposal after receiving 158,000 opposing comments.

## Crafting Effective Comments
- Clearly state your position (support or opposition)
- Identify yourself and explain your connection to the issue
- Stay focused on the agency's specific proposals
- Include data, evidence, statistics, and personal narratives
- Use specific examples: "As a parent of a special needs child..."
