# Wagner, "Administrative Law, Filter Failure, and Information Capture" (2010)

Wagner identifies a structural pathology of modern rulemaking: **agencies cannot filter the
volume and complexity of submitted information**, allowing well-resourced parties to capture
the process by overwhelming it with technical material. This frames a "good comment" not just
as informationally rich but as **navigable** by the agency.

## What Counts as "Information Capture"

Wagner identifies tactics regulated industry uses to capture rulemaking through information:

- **Volume dumps** — submitting thousands of pages of technical analysis the agency cannot
  fully process
- **Complexity escalation** — using highly specialized models, jargon, and methods that only
  the regulated industry can interpret
- **Strategic late filings** — submitting key analyses near the close of the comment period
- **Information silos** — releasing technical data only as part of comments that frame it
  favorably

## Implications for "Good" Comments (per Wagner's Diagnosis)

A good comment is not maximally voluminous. It is:

### 1. Navigable
- Includes a **short executive summary** with the key requested change
- Indexes appendices and technical material clearly
- Provides **plain-language translations** of any technical analysis

### 2. Transparent About Methods
- Discloses underlying data, models, and assumptions
- Uses methods reproducible by the agency or third parties
- Provides citations to publicly available sources

### 3. Counters Industry Information Capture
- Re-runs industry models with different assumptions and reports differences
- Identifies undisclosed assumptions in industry submissions
- Makes proprietary data demands of the agency or industry

### 4. Earlier Rather Than Later
- Files at ANPRM stage when possible to allow the agency to digest
- Uses the comment period efficiently rather than dumping at the deadline

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Is the comment navigable (executive summary, table of contents, plain-language overview)?
- [ ] Does the comment disclose its data sources, methods, and assumptions transparently?
- [ ] Does the comment identify or challenge undisclosed assumptions in opposing comments?
- [ ] Does the comment use reproducible methods rather than black-box analyses?
- [ ] Was the comment filed early enough for the agency to process meaningfully?
- [ ] Does the comment avoid contributing to "filter failure" by being targeted rather than
      a volume dump?
