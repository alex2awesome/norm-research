# Kerwin & Furlong - Rulemaking: How Government Agencies Write Law and Make Policy

Cornelius M. Kerwin and Scott R. Furlong. SAGE/CQ Press. Multiple editions (4th-6th).

## Core Argument

Rulemaking is the single most important function performed by government agencies. While Congress and the president provide the general framework for the government's mission, rulemaking fills in the details that define the law and delineate how each agency carries out its responsibilities.

## Key Themes Relevant to Effective Comments

The book covers:

1. **The structure of rulemaking** - Pre-rule, proposed rule, final rule stages and how each provides distinct opportunities for input
2. **Interest group participation** - How well-organized interests dominate dockets through technical expertise, repeat play, and resource advantages
3. **Influence techniques** - Beyond formal comments: ex parte meetings, OIRA contacts, advisory committee participation
4. **Empirical evidence on what changes rules** - Detailed economic data, legal arguments about statutory authority, technical/scientific analyses
5. **The Trump-era and recent administrative changes** - How political environment shapes responsiveness to comments
6. **Judicial review and the comment record** - Why preserving arguments in comments matters for any later litigation (exhaustion doctrine)

## Practical Implications for Comment Writers

- Comments must be on the rulemaking record to support later challenges
- Good comments anticipate and respond to the cost-benefit analysis
- Substantive comments that go uncited in the agency response create vulnerability for the rule on judicial review
- Multi-stage engagement (pre-proposal + comment + post-finalization) is the model used by sophisticated commenters
