# Public Comment Template - Structure & Organization

## Basic Three-Part Structure

Public comments are generally composed of:
1. **An introduction** - identification, credentials, statement of interest
2. **A reaction to the proposed law** - detailing what sections should be kept, removed, or altered and why
3. **An overall summary or conclusion**

## Template Example

```
Thank you for the opportunity to comment on [Document Title]. I am... [1-2 sentences introducing yourself and your credentials, as they relate to the comment you are writing].

I would like to... [raise concerns / inform you of new information / provide supporting evidence] regarding [Document Title].

In summary, [1-2 sentences summarizing your comment]. Please see below for additional details and pertinent literature.

[First major point]
Several short paragraphs supporting your first point. Include references to literature that is cited in the bibliography.

[Second major point]
Several short paragraphs supporting your second point. Include references to literature that is cited in the bibliography.

BIBLIOGRAPHY
List of your sources; include links or DOI when appropriate.
```

## Key Elements

- **Heading with regulation name and docket ID number** - demonstrates careful review
- **Opening sentence to establish credibility** - state who you are and summarize relevant experiences
- **Use specific section references** when commenting on a particular provision
- **Cite sources** for any factual claims
- **Conclude with a clear ask** - what action should the agency take

## What Differentiates Strong from Weak Templates

Strong templates leave space for substantive customization:
- Personal experience field
- Specific section citations
- Local impact details
- Quantitative data fields

Weak templates (high duplicate risk):
- Pre-filled positions only
- No engagement with specific provisions
- No personalization mechanism
- One-click submission with identical text
