# State Farm Hard-Look Doctrine — Drafting Comments to Preserve Issues

The hard-look doctrine, established in *State Farm*, requires agencies to:
1. Articulate the policies they purport to further
2. Show that the chosen course is **reasonable in light of relevant policies, alternatives,
   and facts**
3. Demonstrate that findings have **support in the rulemaking record**
4. Show consideration of **important aspects** of the problem

## What This Means for Comment Drafters

A comment that is to preserve an objection for hard-look review must do four things.

### 1. Identify a Specific Factor the Agency Must Consider
"Relevant data" must be considered. **An effective comment puts a specific factor on the
record** — such as a downstream effect, a cost the agency overlooked, a population at risk —
that the agency must address or risk reversal.

### 2. Identify a Specific Alternative the Agency Failed to Consider
Hard-look requires consideration of reasonable alternatives. **A good comment names a
specific viable alternative**, ideally with implementing detail.

### 3. Challenge a Factual Basis in the Rulemaking Record
The agency's findings must have record support. **A good comment identifies a finding
without record support** or one contradicted by record evidence.

### 4. Connect Each Objection to a Specific Statutory or EO Authority
Hard-look review tests reasonableness in light of the agency's statutory authority. **A good
comment explains why the agency's chosen course departs from statutory or EO requirements.**

## Rubric Items

- [ ] Does the comment add a specific factor (data, fact, perspective) to the rulemaking record?
- [ ] Does the comment name a specific viable alternative and explain why it is reasonable?
- [ ] Does the comment challenge specific factual findings or data the agency relies on?
- [ ] Does the comment connect each objection to a specific statutory or EO authority?
- [ ] Does the comment use precise language that could be quoted in litigation papers?
- [ ] Does the comment identify "important aspects of the problem" the agency overlooked?

## Why This Doctrine Matters

A comment that meets these criteria not only has greater persuasive force during rulemaking
but also **preserves the issue for judicial review** under the arbitrary-and-capricious
standard. Comments that fail to raise an issue with sufficient specificity may be deemed
**waived for judicial purposes**.
