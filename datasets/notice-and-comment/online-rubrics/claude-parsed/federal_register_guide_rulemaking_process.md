# Federal Register: A Guide to the Rulemaking Process

## Overview

The Office of the Federal Register's official guide to how the federal rulemaking process works.

## Phases of Rulemaking

### Pre-Proposal
- Agencies identify regulatory needs through statutes, court orders, petitions, or self-initiated review
- Pre-rule notices may include Requests for Information (RFI) or Advance Notices of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM)

### Proposed Rule (NPRM)
- Published in the Federal Register
- Must include the legal authority, the substance and purpose, and an opportunity for public comment
- Comment period typically 30-60 days
- Significant rules undergo OIRA review before publication

### Final Rule
- Published in the Federal Register after agency analyzes comments
- Includes preamble explaining response to significant comments
- Effective date typically 30 days after publication
- Substantively significant rules subject to Congressional Review Act

## Public Participation

Members of the public can:
- Submit written comments via Regulations.gov
- Participate in public hearings
- Request meetings with agency staff or OIRA
- Petition for new rules (5 USC § 553(e))
- Challenge final rules in court

## Effective Public Comment Standards

The Federal Register guide emphasizes:
- Comments must be timely (before deadline)
- Comments should be substantive (not just preference statements)
- Comments should reference specific provisions
- Comments should provide evidence, alternatives, or analysis
- Comments become part of the docket and the basis for any judicial review
