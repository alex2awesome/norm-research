# AILA (American Immigration Lawyers Association) Comment Patterns

## Overview

AILA and the American Immigration Council jointly file comment letters on most major immigration regulations. Their letters demonstrate the rubric for sophisticated legal-technical commenting.

## Effective AILA Comment Patterns

### Opening
- Identification of AILA (37,000 lawyers) and the Council
- Joint letter identification
- Subject line with specific docket and rule

### Substantive Body
- **Statutory authority analysis** - whether agency has power under INA
- **Constitutional analysis** - due process, equal protection issues
- **Procedural analysis** - APA notice, comment, logical outgrowth
- **Practical impact** - case management, asylum processing, detention conditions
- **Empirical data** - case statistics, processing times, denial rates
- **Comparative analysis** - prior administration practices, other countries
- **Specific section-by-section comments**
- **Suggested alternative regulatory text**

### Recent Examples

**BIA Appellate Procedures Interim Final Rule (April 2026)**
AILA argued the IFR unlawfully cuts appeal time to 10 days, makes summary dismissal the default without a complete record, burdens counsel, and shifts errors and caseloads to federal courts.

**Asylum Restrictions at the U.S./Mexico Border**
Submitted joint comment expressing concerns that changes to asylum rules would constitute a significant departure from the laws enacted by Congress, noting that both rules conflict with immigration law since Congress established that the manner of a noncitizen's entry should not impact their ability to seek asylum.

## Effective Practice Patterns

For immigration rule comments by attorneys/legal organizations:
- Cite specific INA provisions (8 USC § 1101, 1158, etc.)
- Reference 8 CFR provisions being amended
- Cite federal court decisions (BIA, AAO, federal district/circuit, Supreme Court)
- Cite the Convention Against Torture and other international obligations
- Provide specific case examples (anonymized as needed)
- Address attorney-client representation impacts
- Cite EOIR and USCIS performance data
- Engage with immigration court backlog and processing times
- Reference Office of Refugee Resettlement and ORR shelter capacity
- Address Service Center processing implications
