# Rulemaking 3.0: Incorporating AI and ChatGPT Into Notice and Comment Rulemaking

Stephen M. Johnson, *Missouri Law Review* (2023).

## Overview

Artificial intelligence, including ChatGPT, is the latest tech trend to create opportunities to transform notice and comment rulemaking. This paper examines both risks and opportunities.

## Key Themes

### Risks
- Mass campaigns become harder to detect when AI personalizes outputs
- Comments may obfuscate public voice
- Overwhelming comment volumes
- Authentication and identity verification challenges
- "Astroturfing" becomes more sophisticated

### Opportunities
- AI can help non-experts write substantive comments
- Translation and accessibility improvements
- Help understanding complex regulatory documents
- Enable broader, more meaningful participation
- Agencies could use ChatGPT to create tools that help members of the public understand proposed rules, the rulemaking process and the types of comments that are effective in that process

## Rubric for Evaluating AI-Era Comments

Effective comments in the AI era should still demonstrate:
- **Authenticity of identity** (verifiable identifying information)
- **Substantive originality** (content the agency hasn't seen)
- **Specific factual content** that AI cannot fabricate
- **Engagement with specific provisions**
- **Personal expertise/experience** that grounds the comment

Low-quality (AI-generated mass) comments typically:
- Lack specific factual details only known to commenter
- Use generic templates with surface variation
- Do not engage with specific section/page citations
- Do not include verifiable personal identifiers
- Repeat agency's own language back without analysis

## Recent Legislative Response

The **Comment Integrity and Management Act of 2024** passed without opposition on a voice vote, putting a legislative framework around existing efforts to identify and manage the flow of computer-generated comments.

## Open Questions

- Should agencies treat AI-assisted comments the same as fully human comments?
- What disclosure should be required when AI generates substantial portions of a comment?
- How should agencies authenticate identity at scale?
- Can semantic clustering replace exact-match deduplication?

## Implications for Comment Writers

In the AI era, distinctive substantive content matters more than ever:
- Personal stories with verifiable specifics
- Citations to documents the commenter has personally reviewed
- Original data or analysis
- Specific section-by-section engagement
- Identifiable expertise or institutional affiliation
