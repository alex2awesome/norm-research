# OSHA's Rulemaking Process and Public Comment Guide

## Overview of Rulemaking

OSHA initiates rule development through various triggers, including "new technologies or practices that better protect workers" and "injuries/illness affecting workers." The agency creates rules to safeguard worker safety and health.

## Seven-Stage Process

**Stage 1: Pre-Rule** — OSHA gathers information through Requests for Information (RFI) or Advanced Notices of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) to determine if regulation is necessary.

**Stages 2-3: Proposed Rule** — The agency publishes a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM) explaining the proposed changes and conducts Small Business Regulatory Enforcement Fairness Act (SBREFA) meetings.

**Stages 4-5: Final Rule Development** — OSHA analyzes public comments and creates the final rule with implementation dates.

**Stages 6-7: Publication/Outreach** — The final rule appears in the Federal Register alongside educational activities.

## Public Participation Opportunities

Citizens can engage through:
- Written comments submitted online via Regulations.gov
- Testimony at public hearings
- Participation in federal advisory committee meetings
- SBREFA consultations for small business concerns

## How to Submit Effective Comments

The guidance emphasizes: "Tell us how the issue impacts you, other workers, or your industry. Provide as much detail as possible."

Key tips include planning ahead (comments close at "11:59 p.m. Eastern Time"), contacting OSHA with questions, and sharing personal workplace experiences.
