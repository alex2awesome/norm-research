# Small Business Guide to Comment Letter Writing (SBA Office of Advocacy, full guide)

## Why Comment Letters Matter
Direct business input is invaluable: agencies need to hear from small entities themselves, not just trade associations. Reasons to comment include expressing concerns about proposed-rule impacts, suggesting modifications or alternatives, and shaping the regulatory landscape for your industry. "Not all small businesses are the same" — individual perspectives add value beyond association representation.

## Five Steps to Effective Comment Letters
1. **Understand the Rule**: review the Federal Register notice; identify highest-impact areas.
2. **Be Clear and Concise**: state purpose early; plain language, no jargon.
3. **Explain Business Impact**: concrete examples; cost estimates; data.
4. **Offer Solutions**: suggest practical alternatives or modifications and their benefits.
5. **Maintain Professionalism**: respectful constructive tone; follow agency submission instructions.

## Comment Letter Structure
**Introduction** — who you are, the rule name, business background, summary of concerns.
**Body** — provisions you support/oppose with explanations; provide data without disclosing confidential information.
**Conclusion** — brief summary; thank the agency.

## Three-Component Argument Framework
1. **Claim**: state your main argument at each section's beginning.
2. **Supporting Data**: quantify impacts — agency cost assumptions, compliance expenses (equipment/staffing/paperwork), revenue impacts, cost-benefit responses.
3. **Impact Statement**: connect evidence to claim; suggest specific remedies (regulatory section modifications, deadline extensions, industry-specific exclusions).

## General Recommendations
- "The more specific information you can provide, the better."
- Cite sources; document data origins.
- Establish credibility with relevant field experience.
- Avoid anger or sarcasm; plain English.
- Proofread; have someone else review or read aloud.

## Timing
Comment periods typically span 30-60 days; submit early to allow agency consideration time.
