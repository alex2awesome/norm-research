D.C. Circuit Panel Split Over EPA ‘Project Accounting’ NSR Permit Rule

A three-judge panel of the D.C. Circuit appears to be split on the lawfulness of EPA’s “project emissions accounting” rule that allows permit applicants to count emissions decreases, as well as increases, at the first step of New Source Review (NSR), as judges grappled during oral arguments on petitioners’ standing and EPA guidance.

New Coalition Seeks Trump EO, Rule Curbing EPA’s Use Of 404(c) Vetoes

A new coalition of mining and other groups is urging President Trump to issue an order and launch a rulemaking to curb EPA’s use of its Clean Water Act (CWA) section 404(c) authority to “veto” dredge-and-fill permits, raising concerns that future Democratic officials could use the authority to retroactively kill projects approved by his administration.

From Inside TSCA
IRIS Memo Expected To Have Less Effect On TSCA Than Other Programs

The Trump EPA’s recommendation that program offices reconsider their past use of Integrated Risk Information System (IRIS) hazard values in regulatory decisions is expected to have relatively less impact on the TSCA program and its evaluation and management of existing chemicals than other programs, former officials say.

Environmentalists Challenge Key NRC Order Extending Nuclear Plant License

Environmentalists are urging a federal appeals court to overturn a key decision by the Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) to allow continued operation of a nuclear plant in southern Florida for 20 years beyond its current license, which nuclear advocates say will spur the industry to restore to service retiring reactors across the country.

D.C. Circuit Primed To Hear Arguments Over EPA Steel Air Toxics Stays

A D.C. Circuit panel is poised to hear oral arguments in environmentalists’ consolidated suit challenging EPA’s extension of compliance deadlines for Biden-era air toxics regulations for steel plants, as the agency works on a reconsideration that will likely weaken or reverse the 2024 limits.

Recent Trump administration actions to scrap offshore wind leases and slow the pace of federal approvals for solar projects are fueling renewed doubts that lawmakers can reach a bipartisan deal on expedited environmental permitting for energy and major infrastructure projects this Congress.
