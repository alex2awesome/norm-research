# Berger & Milkman (2012) — What Makes Online Content Viral?

Empirical analysis of all NYT articles published over a 3-month period to identify drivers of email-sharing virality.

## Key Findings

### Arousal-Based Virality
- Virality is partially driven by **physiological arousal**.
- High-arousal **positive** emotions (awe) → MORE viral
- High-arousal **negative** emotions (anger, anxiety) → MORE viral
- Low-arousal emotions (sadness) → LESS viral

### Valence
- Positive content is more viral than negative content (controlling for arousal).

### Content Features Predicting Virality
- **Awe-inspiring** content (scientific discoveries, overcoming adversity)
- **Surprising** content
- **Interesting** content
- **Practically useful** content
- High **emotional arousal** of any valence
- **Prominently featured** (placement) — confirms placement amplifies sharing

### Less Viral
- Sad content (low arousal)
- Boring/utilitarian without emotional hook

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for Digital Sharing

1. **Awe** — Inspiration, transcendence
2. **Anger** — Outrage, indignation
3. **Anxiety** — Threat, fear
4. **Surprise** — Unexpectedness
5. **Practical utility** — Actionable information
6. **Interest / curiosity gap**
7. **Positivity** (when matched with arousal)
8. **Featured placement** (editorial promotion)
