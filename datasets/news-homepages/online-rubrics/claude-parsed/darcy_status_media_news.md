# Status (Oliver Darcy) — Newsworthiness Inside the Media Industry

Darcy's beat is the media industry itself; his rubric reflects what the people who decide news judge as news.

## Status's Newsworthiness Filters
- **Media organization business news** — layoffs, ownership changes, exec moves
- **Editorial controversies** — how outlets covered something contentious
- **Cable news rating shifts**
- **Right-wing media ecosystem dynamics** — Fox / Newsmax / Daily Wire moves
- **Platform decisions affecting publishers**
- **Star talent moves and contract drama**

## Editorial Tests
- "Will newsroom Slack be talking about this tomorrow?"
- "Does this affect how news is made or distributed?"
- "Is this a structural change or one-off?"
- "Are the people involved decision-makers or talent?"

## Stories Status Demotes (Implicitly)
- Internal social-media drama without business impact
- Critic disputes without consequence
- Repeated cycles of established narratives

## Stories Status Elevates
- Quiet executive transitions revealing strategy
- Negotiations / contract terms leaks
- Subscriber numbers changes
- Editorial standards updates
- Disinformation / propaganda business models

## Note
Status reveals an inside-baseball rubric: newsworthiness within the press itself follows distinct criteria — power moves, business stakes, talent shuffles — that often differ from public-facing news judgment.
