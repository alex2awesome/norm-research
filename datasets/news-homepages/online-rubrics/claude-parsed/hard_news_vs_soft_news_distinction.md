# Hard News vs. Soft News — Definitions and Criteria

A foundational distinction in journalism. Different newsworthiness criteria for each.

## Hard News Definition

"Breaking news and up-to-the-minute news about serious, timely, or hard-hitting topics that are timely and urgent, usually based on facts and rigorous research."

## Soft News Definition

"Journalistic style and genre that blurs the line between information and entertainment."

## 3-Dimension Hardness Scale (Reinemann et al. 2011)

A story is "harder" if:
1. **Topic dimension** — politically relevant
2. **Focus dimension** — focuses on societal consequences
3. **Style dimension** — impersonal, unemotional

A story is "softer" if:
- Personal lives (vs. policy)
- Individual focus (vs. societal)
- Personal and emotional style

## Hard News Topics

- Government and politics
- Economics and business
- International relations
- Welfare and policy
- Scientific developments
- Crime
- Public safety
- Disasters
- Major court decisions

## Soft News Topics

- Celebrity
- Lifestyle
- Entertainment
- Human interest
- Consumer products
- Travel
- Food
- Fashion
- Sports (some treatments)
- Health (lifestyle-side)

## Newsworthiness Criteria — Hard News

1. **Timeliness** — extreme urgency
2. **Impact** on policy or large groups
3. **Public significance**
4. **Magnitude of consequence**
5. **Power involved**
6. **Conflict** with civic stakes
7. **Verifiability**
8. **Source credibility**

## Newsworthiness Criteria — Soft News

1. **Human interest**
2. **Emotional resonance**
3. **Identifiable subject**
4. **Surprise / oddity**
5. **Inspirational value**
6. **Visual appeal**
7. **Lifestyle relevance**
8. **Cultural moment**

## The Continuum

Hard and soft news are NOT distinct categories — they exist on a continuum:
- Topics traditionally hard can be softened (climate via personal story)
- Soft news can carry political/societal information (celebrity activism)
- Same topic can be treated either way

## Editorial Implications

- **Hard news** typically goes A1, lead segment
- **Soft news** typically inside, later segment, weekend
- **Tabloids** lead with soft framing of hard topics
- **Quality press** lead with hard
- **Digital** mix changes by audience segment

## Hybrid Genres

- Political infotainment
- Celebrity politics
- Disaster human interest
- Service journalism
- Explainer + feature
- Personal essay + analysis

## Implicit Newsworthiness Implication

- Hard news prioritizes **importance**
- Soft news prioritizes **interest**
- Best journalism finds where they intersect
- Audience-driven trend toward soft
- Mission-driven push toward hard
