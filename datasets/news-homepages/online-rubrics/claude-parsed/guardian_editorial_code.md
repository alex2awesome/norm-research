# Guardian News & Media — Editorial Code of Practice and Guidance (2023)

The Guardian's most comprehensive editorial code (30+ pages), covering print, online, and social. Updated 2023 for AI-era considerations.

## Purpose

"To protect and foster the bond of trust between GNM and its readers, and therefore to protect the integrity of GNM and its journalism."

## Three Parts
1. GNM editorial code of practice
2. Supplementary guidelines
3. Internal legal & professional conduct guidance

## Core Standards Affecting Newsworthiness

### Accuracy
- Verify facts before publication
- Distinguish news from opinion
- Use named sources where possible
- Cite/credit other journalists' work

### Privacy
- Default to privacy unless overriding public interest
- Special protection for children, vulnerable people
- Respect for grief and shock

### Discrimination
- Avoid prejudicial reference to race, religion, sex, sexual orientation, disability
- Reference only when genuinely relevant

### Intrusion into Grief
- Sensitivity in approach
- Justified by public interest

### Right of Reply
- Subjects of allegations get opportunity to respond
- Reply published with prominence

### AI Guidance (2023 addition)
- Material created with generative AI raises bias, ownership, plagiarism, IP issues
- Disclosure expected
- Verification still required

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria

- **Public interest** — overrides default privacy
- **Truth and accuracy**
- **Genuine relevance** of identifying details
- **Right of subjects to respond**
- **Sensitivity** to victims
- **Source credibility** required
- **Anti-discrimination** in framing
- **Distinction of news from opinion**
- **Transparency** about AI / methods

## What Should Not Be Published
- Unverified claims
- Privacy intrusions without public interest
- Discriminatory framing
- Material from undisclosed payments
- Subterfuge without justification
