# Popular Information (Judd Legum) — Accountability Reporting Rubric

Legum's newsletter operationalizes "newsworthiness = corporate / political accountability."

## Legum's Story Selection Filters
- **Hidden corporate-political linkages** — donations / lobbying / board seats inconsistent with public branding
- **Hypocrisy receipts** — company says X, funds Y
- **Voter suppression actions** by elected officials and corporate enablers
- **Anti-LGBTQ legislation funders** — who quietly bankrolls
- **Disinformation finance** — who pays the influencers
- **Worker-treatment vs. PR**
- **DEI promises vs. behavior**

## Reporting Tests Legum Applies
- Is there a paper trail? (FEC, IRS 990, SEC filing)
- Can the company / official respond on the record?
- Does this reveal a pattern, not just an incident?
- Will this story create accountability pressure?
- Are mainstream outlets ignoring it because it's "old news" / partisan?

## What Mainstream Coverage Misses (per Popular Information)
- Quiet PAC donations
- Trade-association memberships
- Subsidiary brand entanglements
- Local-level officials with national impact
- Long-tail consequences of "old" stories

## Legum's Implicit Norm
Newsworthiness = potential to change behavior of the powerful by exposing inconsistency.

## Note
Popular Information shows that newsworthiness often depends on patient document review, not breaking events — accountability over recency.
