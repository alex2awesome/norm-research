# Lee & Tandoc (2017) - When News Meets the Audience: How Audience Feedback Online Affects News Production and Consumption

Eun-Ju Lee and Edson Tandoc's review essay synthesizing the empirical literature on how online audience feedback (clicks, comments, shares, dwell time) reshapes news selection. Published in *Human Communication Research* 43(4): 436–449.

The contribution: Provides a systematic mapping of mechanisms by which audience metrics infiltrate newsroom decisions — and where they don't. Empirically grounds the "news values" tradition in the platform era.

## Three Forms of Audience Feedback

1. **Direct Behavioral Metrics** - Clicks, page views, dwell time, scroll depth.

2. **Engagement Metrics** - Comments, shares, likes, reactions on social platforms.

3. **Survey/Panel Feedback** - Audience research, reader surveys.

## Empirical Findings on Audience-Journalist Alignment

1. **Persistent Misalignment** - "Quantitative studies comparing most-reads or -views to a story's position of prominence on a variety of media outlets' homepages found gaps between what readers like to read and what journalists choose to showcase."

2. **Limited Direct Influence** - Despite the availability of metrics, traditional news values still dominate front-page placement decisions.

3. **Indirect Influence Through Topic Selection** - Editors use metrics not to choose individual stories but to inform *topic-level* decisions (more health coverage if health stories perform well).

4. **Soft News More Affected than Hard News** - Audience metrics shift coverage of soft / lifestyle / entertainment more than political/serious news (echoes Welbers et al. 2016).

5. **Younger / Online-Only Reporters More Receptive** - Newsroom subcultures vary in their willingness to integrate metrics; younger reporters and digital-first outlets more responsive.

## How Audience Feedback Affects News Production

1. **Story Pursuit Decisions** - Reporters increasingly check whether topics will engage before pursuing.

2. **Headline Optimization** - A/B testing for clickability (especially in digital-first outlets).

3. **Placement and Promotion** - Decisions about social-media promotion, push-notification, and email newsletter inclusion driven by predicted engagement.

4. **Iteration Within Story** - Stories updated mid-cycle based on early click-through.

5. **Topic-Level Resource Allocation** - Beats with high-performing stories receive more reporter time.

## Audience-Side Effects

- **Cascade and Bandwagon Effects** - Knowing other readers liked a story increases own engagement (popularity feeds popularity).
- **Comment Tone Effects** - Negative comments below stories shift readers' interpretations independent of article content.
- **Algorithmic Personalization** - Personalized feeds shift exposure away from front-page agenda toward individual preference clusters.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Two-Track System** - Modern news selection runs *two tracks*: traditional editorial values (Galtung & Ruge tradition) and audience-engagement signals; both contribute, but neither dominates.

- **Misalignment is structural, not bug**: The gap between "most read" and "front page" reflects a deliberate editorial choice to maintain professional newsworthiness norms against pure popularity metrics.

- **Front-page composition continues to express editorial newsworthiness**: This validates using homepage placement as a label for "newsworthiness" in research, separable from "engagement."

- **Predicts diverging outlets**: Outlets that prioritize click metrics will systematically diverge from outlets that maintain editorial filters; both axes can be operationalized.
