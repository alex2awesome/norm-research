# Stroud (2008) - Media Use and Political Predispositions: Revisiting the Concept of Selective Exposure

Natalie Jomini Stroud's foundational empirical confirmation that *partisan selective exposure* — the tendency to consume news that confirms one's political beliefs — is a major driver of audience behavior in the modern fragmented media environment. Published in *Political Behavior* 30(3): 341–366.

Used 2004 National Annenberg Election Survey data; tested whether respondents' partisanship predicted their consumption of newspapers, talk radio, cable TV news, and Internet news.

## Core Findings

1. **Strong Selective Exposure Effects** - Conservatives consume conservative-leaning outlets; liberals consume liberal-leaning outlets, beyond what topic interest alone explains.

2. **Effect Strongest in High-Choice Media** - Cable TV and Internet show stronger selective exposure than newspapers; the more choice available, the stronger the partisan sorting.

3. **Talk Radio Most Polarized** - Highest selective exposure of any medium tested.

4. **Cumulative Across Media** - People who selectively expose in one medium tend to do so across all media — partisan media diet is consistent.

## Implications for Newsworthiness Research

Stroud's findings reframe newsworthiness as *audience-segment specific*. Different stories are newsworthy for different audiences:

1. **Audience-Targeted Selection** - Outlets tailor selection to their partisan audience; what's newsworthy on Fox News differs from what's newsworthy on MSNBC even controlling for editorial values.

2. **Partisan-Salience Premium** - Stories that resonate with the outlet's partisan audience receive better placement.

3. **Outgroup-Negativity Bias** - Stories framing the political outgroup negatively perform better than stories framing them positively, on partisan-leaning outlets.

4. **Confirmation Premium** - Stories that confirm the audience's worldview earn higher engagement and therefore better placement.

5. **Belief-Dissonance Penalty** - Stories that challenge audience priors are demoted unless they can be reframed (e.g., as "elite hypocrisy").

## Five Selection Mechanisms in High-Choice Partisan Media Environments

1. **Pre-screening for compatibility** - Editors anticipate audience reaction and pre-screen stories for partisan compatibility.

2. **Differential framing of same events** - The same wire story gets different headlines and emphasis in different outlets.

3. **Differential weighting of source perspectives** - Some outlets foreground sympathetic sources, others foreground critical ones, on the same event.

4. **Topic agenda differentiation** - Beyond framing, outlets differ in which topics they cover at all (e.g., Hunter Biden coverage vs. Trump coverage volume varies markedly).

5. **Engagement-driven amplification** - Stories that generate selective-exposure-friendly engagement are pushed harder.

## The Filter-Bubble / Echo-Chamber Connection

Stroud's work predates but undergirds the algorithmic-personalization debates of the 2010s. Selective exposure existed before Facebook; algorithms amplify a pre-existing audience tendency.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Outlet segmentation as a measurable variable**: Newsworthiness varies by outlet partisan profile; homogeneous newsworthiness scores across outlets are misspecified.

- **Front-page placement is partisan-encoded**: Editors implicitly select for partisan-friendly stories even at outlets that disclaim partisan orientation.

- **Audience metrics amplify selective exposure**: When metric-driven outlets (Vu 2014) cater to existing audience, they reinforce selective-exposure patterns.

- **Operationalizable**: Outlet-level partisan scores (AllSides, Media Bias/Fact Check) can be combined with story content to test partisan-selection patterns at scale.
