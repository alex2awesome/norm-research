# Westerståhl & Johansson (1994) - Foreign News: News Values and Ideologies

A landmark Swedish study of foreign-news selection that explicitly added *ideology* as a fourth class of news selection determinants alongside Galtung & Ruge's structural factors. Published in the *European Journal of Communication* 9(1): 71–89.

The argument: News-value research has traditionally treated selection as a function of event properties (importance, proximity, etc.) and supply (resources, source access). Westerståhl & Johansson show that *ideological orientation* — the implicit world-political map of editors and audiences — is a major and irreducible additional determinant.

## Four Classes of News Selection Determinants

### Class 1 — Importance (Structural News Values)

1. **Magnitude of Event** - Number of people affected, severity of impact (Galtung & Ruge threshold).
2. **Proximity** - Geographic and cultural closeness.
3. **Power Position** - Eliteness of nations and persons involved.
4. **Predictability** - Schedulability for news cycles.

### Class 2 — Identification

5. **Cultural Affinity** - Similarity of foreign actors to domestic readers.
6. **Linguistic Compatibility** - English-language sources preferred globally; native-language sources preferred locally.
7. **Familiar-Type Events** - Events fitting recognizable templates.

### Class 3 — Sensation (Audience-Pull News Values)

8. **Drama** - High-stakes outcomes with uncertainty.
9. **Conflict** - Violence, war, dispute.
10. **Negativity** - Bad news amplified.

### Class 4 — Ideology (Their Distinctive Contribution)

11. **Cold War Bloc Coverage Asymmetry** - Pre-1989 Western media systematically over-covered Eastern bloc instability, under-covered Western bloc instability of comparable magnitude.

12. **Friend-vs-Enemy Framing** - Same event categorized differently depending on which side the actor was on (a coup becomes "liberation" or "putsch" depending on alignment).

13. **Civilizational Privileging** - Stories from "our" civilization (Western Europe, North America) carry more news value than equally important stories from "other" civilizations.

14. **Implicit National Interest Hooks** - Foreign stories preferred when they bear on the home country's foreign-policy priorities.

15. **Source-Country Media-System Bias** - Coverage flows along ideological lines via international news agencies; the agency's worldview leaks into source selection.

## Methodological Contribution

Comparing foreign-news coverage in multiple countries' media against a fixed event-list, Westerståhl & Johansson showed that:

- Even controlling for Galtung-Ruge factors, ideological alignment explained substantial selection variance.
- Different countries' media show *different* selection patterns for *the same events* — proving that selection depends on the audience's ideological frame, not just universal news values.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Ideology as a fourth dimension**: Joins the structural / supply-side / audience-pull frameworks. Modern homepage models that ignore ideology will systematically misclassify foreign-news selection.

- **Predictive of comparative coverage gaps**: The model predicts e.g. the systematically lower coverage of African coups vs. European political instability.

- **Operationalizable**: Story selection can be benchmarked against a global event list and tested for ideological asymmetries.
