# Tuchman (1972) - Objectivity as Strategic Ritual: An Examination of Newsmen's Notions of Objectivity

Gaye Tuchman's foundational paper on the procedural rituals reporters use to claim "objectivity" — and to defend selection decisions against complaints, libel claims, and editorial pushback. Published in the *American Journal of Sociology* 77(4): 660–679.

The argument: "Objectivity" is not a property of stories but a set of *defensive routines* that license what gets called news. These rituals are themselves newsworthiness criteria — only items that can be processed through them are publishable.

## Three Factors Defining "Objective" News

1. **Form** - Attributes of the story exemplifying news procedures (e.g., quotation marks, conflict-of-views structure, supporting evidence sequencing).
2. **Content** - Notions of social reality the newsman takes for granted; which institutions are presumed legitimate, which sources presumed reliable.
3. **Inter-organizational relationships** - The reporter's experience with regular sources/beats shapes what is treated as factual.

## Four Strategic Procedures Reporters Use to Demonstrate Objectivity

1. **Presentation of Conflicting Possibilities** - When a fact is contested, present "both sides." Reporting one source's claim against another's claim insulates the reporter from accusations of bias.

2. **Presentation of Supporting Evidence** - Buttress claims with corroborative facts (statistics, dates, documents) so the assertion appears self-evidently true rather than the reporter's opinion.

3. **The Judicious Use of Quotation Marks** - Attributing claims to a named source ("the senator said") removes the reporter as author of the assertion. Direct quotes shift epistemic responsibility.

4. **Structuring Information in an Appropriate Sequence (Inverted Pyramid)** - Putting the most "important" facts first signals that selection followed a recognized professional convention rather than a personal whim.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Procedural eligibility**: An event is more newsworthy if it can be reported through these rituals — i.e., if there is an authoritative source willing to be quoted, if there are clear "two sides," if there are documents to cite.
- **Source-rich events win**: Stories with named, on-the-record spokespeople are systematically preferred over stories where evidence requires the reporter to speak in their own voice.
- **Defensive bias**: News selection skews toward items that protect the reporter from libel suits and editor questioning.
- **Ritual ≠ truth**: Tuchman explicitly notes these procedures often function to *suppress* news judgment rather than enable it.

## Why It Matters

Together with Tuchman 1973 ("Routinizing the Unexpected") and the 1978 book *Making News*, this paper grounds the constructionist sociology of news. Objectivity rituals are now standardly cited as *constraints on what becomes news*: events incompatible with the ritual structure (slow-moving conditions, contested facts without an authoritative spokesperson) are systematically under-covered.
