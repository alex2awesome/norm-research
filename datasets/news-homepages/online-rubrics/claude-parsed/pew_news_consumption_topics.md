# Pew Research — Audience Interest in News Topics

Pew survey data on what news topics audiences consume. Implicit "audience-defined newsworthiness."

## Topic Interest by Demographics

### Younger Adults (under 30) — Less Likely to Get
- Government and politics
- Science and technology
- Business and finance
- Slightly less: sports

### Younger Adults — More Likely to Get
- Entertainment news (32% vs. 13% for 65+)
- Celebrity / pop culture
- Sports (about equal)
- Lifestyle / wellness

### Older Adults (65+) — More Likely to Get
- Government and politics
- Local news
- Business
- International news

## News Consumption Patterns (2025 data)

- 36% follow news "all or most of the time" (down from 51% in 2016)
- Young adults: more incidental exposure than intentional
- 70% of young adults get political news incidentally vs. 30% intentionally
- Social media news: TikTok 55%, X 57%, Truth Social 55%

## Implicit Newsworthiness by Audience

If editorial decisions reflect audience interest:
- **Demographic targeting** matters
- **Older audiences** prioritize politics, business, government
- **Younger audiences** prioritize entertainment, sports, lifestyle
- **Cross-cutting interests**: weather, breaking emergencies, local issues

## Topics That Drive Sustained News Consumption

1. Politics and elections
2. International conflicts
3. Health (especially during crises)
4. Local news
5. Weather and natural events
6. Sports
7. Celebrity / entertainment
8. Tech (younger audiences)
9. Business and economy
10. Crime

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- "Newsworthy" is partly audience-defined
- Different newsrooms calibrate to different audience demographics
- Rising audience disengagement → newsrooms shift toward engagement-friendly topics
- Incidental discovery means headline/visual quality more important
