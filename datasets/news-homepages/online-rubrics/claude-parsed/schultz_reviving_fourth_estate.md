# Schultz — Reviving the Fourth Estate

Cambridge University Press, 1998. Foundational text on journalism's democratic/civic newsworthiness criteria.

## Core Argument

Journalism's purpose is rooted in its role as the Fourth Estate — independent watchdog of power on behalf of citizens.

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria (from democratic theory of news)

1. **Public interest / civic significance** — Stories that inform self-governing citizens
2. **Accountability** — Holding power (government, corporate, institutional) to account
3. **Independence from interests** — Free from political/commercial influence
4. **Diversity of perspectives** — Range of voices and viewpoints
5. **Information citizens need** — Not just want
6. **Investigative depth** — Going beyond official statements
7. **Critical analysis** — Not stenography
8. **Responsiveness** to citizen needs
9. **Ethical standards** — Truth-telling
10. **Concentrated ownership concerns** — Plurality of voices

## Tensions Identified

- Independence vs. commercial pressures
- Boldness vs. ethical caution
- Investigative ambition vs. concentrated ownership
- Public service vs. profit motive
