# White (1950) - The "Gate Keeper": A Case Study in the Selection of News

David Manning White's landmark study of "Mr. Gates," a wire editor at a 30,000-circulation morning newspaper in a 100,000-population Midwestern city. White asked Mr. Gates to retain every piece of wire copy he rejected during one week (Feb 6-13, 1949) and write down why. Nine-tenths of wire copy was rejected. The reasons formed the first empirical taxonomy of news rejection criteria.

Published in *Journalism Quarterly* 27(3): 383–390. Foundational paper for gatekeeping theory; introduced Kurt Lewin's "gatekeeper" concept to journalism studies.

## Mr. Gates's Categories of Rejection (Subjective)

1. **"Not interesting"** - Personal taste judgment that the story would not engage readers.
2. **"Dull writing"** - Wire copy stylistically unappealing.
3. **"No good"** - Generic dismissal reflecting unspecified editorial intuition.
4. **"B.S."** - Stories Mr. Gates judged exaggerated, propagandistic, or untrustworthy.
5. **"Propaganda"** - Stories pushing a political or institutional agenda.
6. **"Don't care for suicides"** - Personal moral aversion (extends to other taboo topics).
7. **"Too far away"** - Geographic remoteness from the audience.
8. **"Too Red"** - Political ideology Mr. Gates personally opposed (anti-Communist filter).
9. **"Sour grapes"** - Stories about complaints, recriminations.
10. **"Never use this"** - Categorical exclusion of topic types.

## Mr. Gates's Categories of Rejection (Mechanical)

11. **"No space"** - Page-fit constraint.
12. **"Would use if space"** - Marginal stories rejected for layout reasons.
13. **"Used another story"** - Duplicate / overlapping coverage of same event.
14. **"Too late — no space"** - Filed after deadline.
15. **"Goes into morgue"** - Useful for files but not for today's edition.

## Key Empirical Findings

- **Rejection rate: ~90%** of incoming wire copy was killed.
- **Subjective dominance**: A majority of rejections rested on personal value judgments rather than mechanical constraints.
- **Two master classes of rejection**: (a) "not worthy of being reported" (subjective) and (b) "another story on the same event" (selection among substitutes).
- **Stable preferences**: Mr. Gates's category counts (e.g., volume of human interest, political, crime) closely matched reader preference surveys he had completed earlier — i.e., the gatekeeper reproduced an audience model in his head.
- **Personal beliefs leak through**: Anti-Communist and anti-suicide aversions visibly shaped output.

## Why It Matters for Newsworthiness

White established that "newsworthiness" is not a property of events but the product of an individual decider's experiences, attitudes, and expectations operating against organizational constraints. Every later sociology-of-news study (Breed, Tuchman, Gans, Shoemaker) builds on this insight.
