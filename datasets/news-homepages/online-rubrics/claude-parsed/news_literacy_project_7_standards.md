# News Literacy Project — 7 Standards of Quality Journalism

A widely-used pedagogical rubric for what constitutes news vs. opinion vs. propaganda.

## The Seven Standards

1. **Truth and accuracy** — Verified facts; correction policies; distinction between confirmed and reported

2. **Independence** — Free of conflicts of interest; not beholden to sources, advertisers, or government

3. **Fairness and impartiality** — Multiple perspectives represented; no pre-judgment

4. **Humanity** — Respect for sources and subjects; minimize harm

5. **Accountability** — Acknowledge mistakes; explain choices; engage criticism

6. **Transparency** — Disclose methods, sources where possible, funding

7. **Original reporting** — First-hand work, not aggregation

## Distinguishing News from Opinion

| News | Opinion |
|------|---------|
| Aims to inform | Aims to argue |
| Multiple perspectives | Author's view |
| Minimal bias | Explicit POV |
| Verifiable facts | Includes interpretation |
| Quotes named sources | Quotes selectively for argument |
| "What" / "How" / "Why" | "Should" / "Why we should" |

## Quality Markers (Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria)

- **Source diversity** — Multiple credible sources
- **Specific details** — "Biggest flood since 1914" > "biggest in recent memory"
- **Five W's + H** answered, especially WHY it matters
- **Straightforward language** (not "shocking," "unbelievable")
- **Fact-opinion separation**
- **Hedge words noted** ("think," "likely," "probably" → caution)
- **Lead has core information** without bias
- **Original reporting evident** — first-hand observation
- **Editor's labels** — opinion clearly marked

## Red Flags (NOT Newsworthy / Quality)

- Sensational headline
- Single anonymous source on a major claim
- No links/citations to sources
- Heavy adjectives
- Unanswerable claims
- Click-bait formatting
- "Shocking," "you won't believe," "doctors hate"
- All-caps headlines
- No date or author byline
