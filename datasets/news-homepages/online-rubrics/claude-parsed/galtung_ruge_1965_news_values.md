# Galtung & Ruge (1965) - The Structure of Foreign News

The seminal study identifying twelve factors describing newsworthiness, published in the Journal of Peace Research. Tested on news from four Norwegian newspapers covering the Congo and Cuba crises (July 1960) and Cyprus crisis (March-April 1964).

## The 12 News Factors

1. **Frequency** - The time-span needed for an event to unfold and acquire meaning. Events fitting the news organization's schedule (a murder, fitting the daily cycle) are more reportable than gradual occurrences (long-term economic trends).

2. **Threshold** - The size or intensity of an event. Events must pass an absolute threshold of magnitude/intensity to be reported, and the larger they are, the more likely.

3. **Unambiguity** - The clearer the meaning of an event, the more likely it is to be selected. Clear implications make better copy than ambiguous events requiring complex context.

4. **Meaningfulness** - Cultural proximity (events with cultural relevance to the audience) and relevance (events that may affect the audience) increase newsworthiness.

5. **Consonance** - Events fitting the journalist's pre-existing image or expectations are more likely to be selected. Predictable events that match an outlet's mental map.

6. **Unexpectedness** - Within the realm of meaningful and consonant events, the unexpected, rare, or surprising are more newsworthy.

7. **Continuity** - Once a story is defined as news, it will continue to be defined as such for some time, even if its absolute amplitude has been reduced.

8. **Composition** - Stories are selected to form a balanced whole — editors balance coverage types and prioritize accordingly to fill the news bulletin/page.

9. **Reference to Elite Nations** - Events concerning powerful nations (US, UK, etc.) receive more attention than less influential nations.

10. **Reference to Elite People** - Actions of elite persons (politicians, celebrities) are more newsworthy than actions of ordinary people.

11. **Personalization** - Events portrayed as the actions of individuals rather than as the result of impersonal social forces or structures attract more coverage.

12. **Negativity** - "Bad news is more newsworthy than good news." Often described as the basic news value.

## Three Core Hypotheses

- **Additivity**: The more factors an event satisfies, the higher the probability it becomes news.
- **Complementarity**: If one factor is strongly present, others may be less necessary.
- **Exclusion**: Events satisfying none or very few factors will not become news.

## Chain of News Communication

- **Selection**: The more factors an event satisfies, the more likely it becomes news.
- **Distortion**: Once selected, the newsworthy factors are accentuated.
- **Replication**: Selection and distortion are repeated at all stages from event to reader.

## Citation
