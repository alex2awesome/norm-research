# Heider — White News: Why Local News Programs Don't Cover People of Color

Routledge, 2000. Ethnographic study of newsrooms in New Mexico and Hawaii (intentionally chosen for diverse populations beyond African American).

## Core Concept: Incognizant Racism

A new theoretical concept introduced by Heider: news workers consistently ignore news in significant portions of communities they cover **without being aware of doing so**. Not overt racism but systematic blind spots.

## Key Findings

- **Communities of color are largely ignored** in everyday news
- **Exceptions**: crime stories, festivals (exoticized)
- "**White news**" delivered by non-white anchors creates appearance of inclusion
- Editorial decisions made by mostly white managers
- Source rolodexes skew white

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria Revealed (Critical View)

### What Makes Stories Cover White Communities
- Suburb school board meetings
- White-owned business openings
- White churches' charity events
- White teen sports teams
- Crime AGAINST white victims

### What Excludes Communities of Color
- Lack of source relationships in those communities
- Reporters don't live there
- News agenda set by majority-white editors
- Neighborhoods labeled "high-crime" → covered for crime only
- Cultural events labeled "ethnic" → exoticized
- Routine community life invisible

## Newsroom Practices Reinforcing Disparity

- Beat structures avoid minority neighborhoods
- "Color-blind" editorial talk masks pattern
- Press releases from elite (white) institutions get covered
- Spanish-language media not consulted
- Indigenous nations under-covered

## Reform Recommendations

- Diversify newsroom staff at all levels (not just on-air)
- Diversify sources / contact lists
- Cover positive community life of all communities
- Editorial accountability for representation
- Audit who appears in stories

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria (Critical Analysis)

Heider's research reveals that news values are NOT neutral:
- "Significance" is socially located
- "Routine" stories reflect dominant group's life
- "Exotic" framing applied to non-dominant cultures
- "Crime" coverage racialized
- "Heroic" coverage racialized
- "Newsworthy people" filtered by social network
