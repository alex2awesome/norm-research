# NPR — Newscast Story Selection

How NPR's hourly newscasts decide what makes the cut. Reflects radio newsworthiness criteria.

## Two Primary Selection Factors

1. **Relevancy to the lives of listeners**
2. **Immediacy** — what's happening now

## Additional Filters

- **National appeal** — must matter beyond a single locale
- **Spectacular or unique** required for local stories to break through
- **Interesting / fun** — engaging enough for the team
- **Audience curiosity** — what would NPR audience want

## Newscast Production Cycle

- A.M. team / Midday team / P.M. team
- Editorial meeting at start of each shift
- Producer leads, decides story lineup
- Override for breaking news
- Stories swapped throughout shift

## Newscast Style Standards

- Brevity (short sentences)
- Clarity (no jargon)
- Listener empowered "to make a decision or take an action"
- Audio quality
- Voice variety
- Sound elements where possible

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for Radio

- **Voice / sound potential** (interview clip exists?)
- **Brevity-amenable** (can be told in 30-60 seconds)
- **Aural drama** (sounds of event)
- **Clear narrative arc** for short form
- **Pronounceable names**
- **Listener identification** (commute audience)
- **National significance**
- **Fresh enough** (within hour for breaking)
- **Entertaining** for kicker stories

## NPR One Algorithm (Personalization)

NPR also developed NPR One, a personalized audio app that combines:
- Editorial curation
- User listening behavior
- Topic preferences
- Skip patterns
- Time-of-day patterns

Combines algorithm with human editorial responsibility.
