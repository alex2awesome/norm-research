# Schudson — The Sociology of News

W.W. Norton, 2011 (2nd ed.). Comprehensive overview of how journalism is socially constructed and what determines selection.

## Five Explanations for News Content (per Schudson)

Schudson organizes theoretical explanations of why news looks the way it does:

1. **Economic explanation** — News as commodity shaped by market
2. **Political explanation** — News shaped by political/state institutions
3. **Sociological explanation (organizational)** — News shaped by newsroom routines
4. **Sociological explanation (cultural)** — News shaped by broader cultural assumptions
5. **Cultural explanation** — News drawing on durable cultural traditions

## News Selection Criteria (Per Schudson)

- **Elite nation / elite people** — Power-centric coverage
- **Long-running stories** — Continuity-driven
- **Institutional source dominance** — Government/corporate spokespeople
- **Conflict / drama** — Inherent dramatic structure
- **Sequenced events** — Scheduled press conferences, court hearings
- **Visual potential** for TV
- **Pre-packaged events** — PR and pseudo-events

## Cultural Constraints

- News reflects "common sense" assumptions of a society
- Frames events in already-familiar narratives
- Reinforces ideological consensus while masking it as objectivity

## Schudson on Democracy and News

- Reluctant to define journalism as inherently democratic
- Notes journalism's actual role often falls short of democratic ideal
- Newsworthiness in democracies privileges "informed citizen" framing but rarely lives up to it
