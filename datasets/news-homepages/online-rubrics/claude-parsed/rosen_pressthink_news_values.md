# PressThink (Jay Rosen) — A Theory of News Judgment

NYU's Jay Rosen has been the most influential academic-practitioner critic of mainstream news judgment.

## Rosen's Core Concepts and Their Newsworthiness Implications

### "Citizens Agenda" Approach
- Lead with what voters say they need to know to cast a vote
- Demote what insiders find interesting
- Use audience surveys / listening to set priorities

### Asymmetry Test
- "Both sides" framing fails when one side breaks norms more
- Newsworthiness criteria must update when reality is asymmetric

### "Production of Innocence"
- Beware coverage that lets the press appear neutral while taking sides by omission
- Newsworthy = what a press accountable to citizens, not insiders, would cover

### "View from Nowhere" critique
- News judgment laundered through "professional neutrality" hides ideological choices
- A defended news judgment beats a hidden one

## Rosen's Tests
- "Is this story serving accountability or access?"
- "Did we let the powerful set the agenda this week?"
- "Are we covering what they want covered, or what citizens need?"
- "Is our framing pro-democracy or pseudo-balanced?"

## Demoted by Rosen's Framework
- Process / horse-race / palace-intrigue
- Stenography of partisan claims
- "Both-sides" treatment of factual disputes

## Elevated
- Stakes journalism
- Citizen-agenda topics (jobs, healthcare, schools)
- Fact-first coverage even when partisan-coded
- Pre-publication accountability of the press itself

## Note
Rosen is arguably the most-cited Anglophone critic of inherited news values; his rubric is essentially "newsworthiness must be earned by demonstrated democratic utility."
