# Washington Journalism Education Association — Breaking News Guidelines

Used in scholastic journalism contests. Codified rubric for what counts as breaking news.

## What Qualifies as Breaking News

A story qualifies as breaking news if:
- **Just happened** (within hours, not days)
- **Demands immediate audience attention**
- **Unfolding** — facts still emerging
- **Unscheduled** — could not be planned for
- **Has potential to change** as new information arrives
- **Of community / national / global significance**

## Breaking News Categories
- Disasters (natural, accidents, attacks)
- Major crime (shootings, attacks)
- Deaths of major figures
- Major political/government events (resignations, votes)
- Sports outcomes (championship game ending)
- Court verdicts in major cases
- Stock market crashes/surges

## Reporting Standards for Breaking News

1. **Verify before publishing** — even if behind competitors
2. **Sourcing** — official sources for facts
3. **Caveats** — "according to," "early reports"
4. **Updates** — return to story as facts emerge
5. **Corrections** — promptly fix errors
6. **Avoid speculation**
7. **Distinguish what's confirmed vs. reported elsewhere**
8. **Identify deaths only after family notified** when possible

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for Breaking News

- **Recency** (most extreme version)
- **Magnitude** of immediate impact
- **Public safety** implications
- **Geographic relevance**
- **Velocity of information**
- **Audience curiosity / urgency**
- **Disruption to normal life**
- **Political / institutional significance**
- **Photographic / video potential**

## Best Practices Reminders

- Quality > Speed
- Verification > Scoop
- Caution > Sensationalism
- Update notices > Quiet edits
