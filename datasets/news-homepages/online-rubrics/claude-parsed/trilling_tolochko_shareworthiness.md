# Trilling, Tolochko & Burscher (2017) — From Newsworthiness to Shareworthiness

Coined the concept of **shareworthiness**: digital-era news values that predict whether a story will be widely shared on social media.

## Theoretical Move

Adapts traditional newsworthiness frameworks (Galtung-Ruge, Harcup-O'Neill) to predict social-media sharing behavior. Adds dimensions about online identity and self-representation: people share to express who they are.

## Predictors of Sharing (from study)

### Traditional News Values that Carry Over
- **Geographical proximity** — Local stories share well within a community
- **Cultural proximity** — Stories matching audience identity
- **Conflict** — Disagreement triggers reaction
- **Negativity** — Negative news shares more
- **Eliteness** — Elite actors drive engagement
- **Magnitude** — Large-impact stories

### Distinctively Digital Sharing Drivers
- **Emotion-evoking content** — High-arousal emotions drive sharing
- **Identity-signaling** — People share to align with values
- **Practical utility** — Actionable info shares well
- **Surprise / Novelty**
- **Connection to existing online conversations**

### Negative Predictors
- **Length** — Very long articles share less
- **Complexity** — Hard to summarize, hard to share

## Key Implication

Newsworthiness ≠ Shareworthiness. A story can be deemed newsworthy by editors but flop on social media, and vice versa.

## Citation

Trilling, D., Tolochko, P., & Burscher, B. (2017). From Newsworthiness to Shareworthiness: How to Predict News Sharing Based on Article Characteristics. Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly, 94(1), 38-60.
