# Margaret Sullivan — News Judgment Critique from Inside the Industry

Former NYT public editor and WaPo media columnist Margaret Sullivan's recurring themes form a rubric.

## Sullivan's Recurring News-Judgment Critiques (= Rubric)
- **Stakes-based ledes** — start with what matters to the reader, not who said what
- **Plain language about lies** — call them lies, not "false claims"
- **Headlines that don't sanewash** — don't smooth incoherence into apparent reasonableness
- **Resist false equivalence** — symmetric framing of asymmetric realities is a choice
- **Hyper-local accountability** — small papers' big watchdog work deserves elevation
- **Press freedom under threat** — should be a recurring beat, not occasional

## Sullivan's Front-Page Tests
- "Would a thoughtful citizen learn what they need from this lede?"
- "Is the headline accurate to the article's most important fact?"
- "Are we using euphemism that protects the powerful?"
- "Are we covering this for journalists or for citizens?"

## Examples of Stories Sullivan Elevates
- Local prosecutors' decisions
- Election-administrator threats
- Press-freedom lawsuits
- Newsroom layoffs and their civic cost
- Unreported testimony from House committees

## Stories Sullivan Demotes
- Personality-driven Beltway analysis
- Fluff coverage that normalizes anti-democratic figures
- Process stories with no substantive payoff

## Note
Sullivan's rubric is conservatism in the original sense: protecting earned democratic norms via diligent news judgment.
