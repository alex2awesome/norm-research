# Bell — The Language of News Media

Blackwell, 1991. Sociolinguistic analysis of news language by a linguist who is himself a journalist. Influential in the field of media linguistics.

## Core Claim

"Stories that journalists and editors produce, not articles" — every piece has viewpoint, values, and structure that can be linguistically analyzed.

## Bell's Categories of News Values (post-Galtung & Ruge)

Bell organizes news values into three categories:

### Values Inherent in News Actors and Events
1. **Negativity**
2. **Recency**
3. **Proximity**
4. **Consonance** — fits expectations
5. **Unambiguity**
6. **Unexpectedness**
7. **Superlativeness**
8. **Relevance** to audience
9. **Personalization**
10. **Eliteness**
11. **Attribution** — having a credible source
12. **Facticity** — facts, figures, places, names

### Values in the News Process
13. **Continuity** — story already running
14. **Competition** — outdo other outlets
15. **Co-option** — fits running narrative
16. **Composition** — fills bulletin
17. **Predictability** — scheduled
18. **Prefabrication** — story ready-made

### Values in the News Text
19. **Clarity** — well written
20. **Brevity** — fits space
21. **Color** — vivid prose

## Audience Design

Bell's audience design theory: news language is shaped to address the imagined audience. News selection follows the same logic — what would "our audience" find newsworthy.

## News Style Features (Bell)

- Inverted pyramid
- Lead summarizes key facts
- Quote attribution patterns
- Time/place markers
- Source diversity in citation
- Use of "shocking," "surprised," "first" markers

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria

- Beyond Galtung-Ruge: adds **process values** (continuity, competition, composition) and **text values** (clarity, brevity, color)
- Recognition that newsworthiness is partly a property of the **story-telling**, not just the event
- Audience-oriented selection
