# Cherubini & Nielsen — Editorial Analytics

Reuters Institute report (2016) on how news media use audience data/metrics to inform editorial decisions. Based on 30+ interviews with senior figures in newsrooms.

## Core Argument

Best practice: data **informs** but does **not dominate** editorial decisions. Newsrooms use a mix of:
- Real-time dashboards (clicks, time-on-page)
- Audience research (panels, surveys)
- Editorial judgment (traditional news values)

## Three Stages of Analytics Use

1. **Description** — what happened (clicks, sources)
2. **Diagnosis** — why it happened
3. **Prescription** — what should we do

## Audience Metrics Used to Inform Newsworthiness

- **Page views** — gross attention
- **Unique visitors**
- **Time on page** — engagement depth
- **Scroll depth** — reading completion
- **Sharing** — virality
- **Comments** — engagement intensity
- **Repeat visits** — loyalty
- **Subscriber conversion** — quality signal
- **Source of traffic** — discoverability

## Editorial Strategies Derived

- **Topic prioritization** based on past performance
- **Headline A/B testing**
- **Time-of-day targeting** for posts
- **Format experimentation** (lists, video, longform)
- **Audience segmentation**

## Tensions Identified

- Quantitative metrics (clicks) vs. quality (depth, accountability)
- Short-term performance vs. long-term mission
- Audience demand vs. audience needs
- Freelance/PR-driven topics get clicks but undermine independence

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria from Analytics

- **Predicted clickability** as new news value
- **Predicted dwell time**
- **Predicted social spread**
- **Predicted subscriber retention**
- **Format performance fit**
