# Harvard Kennedy School — How to Write an Op-Ed

The HKS Communications Program guide. Functions as a rubric for what makes an op-ed publishable.

## Op-Ed Newsworthiness Criteria

### Core Requirements
1. **Clearly defined point** — single thesis
2. **Strong, unique voice** — author's authentic perspective
3. **Clarity of thinking** — readable, logical
4. **Compelling subject** — topic readers care about
5. **Narrow focus** — tackleable in 500-1000 words

### Connection to News
- **Connect to current events** — peg to recent news
- People interested in op-eds linked to news cycle
- Timely angle on enduring topic

### Argument Structure
- **Make a call to action** OR
- **Put forward recommendations** OR
- **Illustrate a point of view**
- **One subject only**
- **Briefly mention opposing views with rebuttal**

### Evidence
- Facts, statistics, historical context
- Quotations and citations
- Personal observation
- Source data

## Implicit Op-Ed Newsworthiness Criteria

1. **Authoritative author** — credentials match topic
2. **Original thesis** — fresh perspective
3. **Topical relevance** — peg to current news
4. **Public significance** — beyond personal
5. **Clear conclusion** — what should reader think/do?
6. **Counterargument acknowledgment** — strengthens persuasion
7. **Concision** — packed into short space
8. **Voice / personality** — author distinct
9. **Specific examples** — anchored in concrete cases
10. **Civic value** — contributes to public debate

## Forms of Opinion (Distinctions)

- **Editorial** — Publication's stance, board-written
- **Op-ed** — Outside contributor, named author
- **Column** — Recurring named writer
- **Letter to editor** — Brief reader response

Each form has different newsworthiness expectations.
