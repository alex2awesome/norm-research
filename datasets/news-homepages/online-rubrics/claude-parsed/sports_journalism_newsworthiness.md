# Sports Journalism — What Makes a Sports Story Newsworthy

Specialist newsworthiness criteria for sports journalism, adapted from the seven core news values.

## Sports-Specific News Values

### 1. Timeliness — Most Critical for Sports
"If your provincial team won a national title, the media wants to hear about it immediately, not two weeks later."
- Live game updates
- Same-day post-game analysis
- Trade deadline coverage

### 2. Conflict
- Championship finals
- Rivalries
- Player vs. management
- Sport-vs-sport
- Tribunal disputes

### 3. Prominence
- Star athletes
- Famous coaches
- Iconic teams
- Olympic / World Cup level
- Hall of Fame milestones

### 4. Extremes / Superlatives
- Records broken
- First-ever achievements
- Statistical anomalies
- Unprecedented streaks
- Championship history

### 5. Impact
- Local team success → community joy
- Closure of community sports facilities
- Health implications (concussions)
- Civic pride
- Economic impact (stadium deals)

### 6. Human Interest
- Personal struggles overcome
- Rivalries with backstory
- Underdog comebacks
- Behind-the-scenes dynamics
- Athlete activism / personal causes

### 7. Proximity
- Local teams covered most
- Regional rivalries
- Hometown angles for nationally-known athletes

### 8. The Unusual
- Bizarre plays
- Weird records
- Strange weather affecting games
- Animal interventions

## Sports-Distinct News Values

- **Statistical milestones** — numerical thresholds (1000 goals, 100 wins)
- **Streak / consecutive games**
- **Comeback narratives**
- **Greatness debates** (GOAT discussions)
- **Calendar events** (Super Bowl, Olympics)
- **Off-field news of athletes** (legal, personal life)
- **Team transactions** (trades, drafts)
- **Coaching changes**
- **Sports betting / fantasy implications**

## Sports Reporting Skills (Implicit Criteria)

- Narrative power (personal struggles, rivalries)
- Behind-the-scenes access
- Game knowledge
- Stat literacy
- Timely production
- Multimedia integration
