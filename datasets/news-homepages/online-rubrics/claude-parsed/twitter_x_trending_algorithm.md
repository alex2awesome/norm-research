# X (Twitter) — Trending Topics Algorithm

X's trending algorithm functions as a real-time newsworthiness rubric powered by user behavior.

## Core Signals

### Volume + Velocity (Key)
- **Sharp spikes** prioritized over gradual growth
- A topic generating 10,000 tweets in 1 hour will trend faster than 50,000 over days
- Velocity matters more than absolute volume

### Engagement Quality
- Retweets, quote tweets, replies weighted higher than mentions
- Diversity of users discussing it
- Cross-network spread (not single-cluster)

### Personalization
- Based on:
  - Who you follow
  - Your interests (inferred from behavior)
  - Your location
- Trends are local AND personalized

### Manipulation Detection
- Bot networks penalized
- Coordinated inauthentic behavior detected
- Artificial boosting filtered
- Fake trending removed

### Recency
- Topics popular NOW, not historically
- Decay function on older trends
- Real-time prioritization

## Newsworthiness Implications

### What Trends ≈ What's News (in real-time crowd judgment)
- Breaking events (shootings, disasters)
- Sports moments
- Celebrity events
- Political controversies
- Cultural memes
- Hashtag campaigns

### What Trends ≠ Quality News
- Manipulated trends
- Outrage-bait
- Algorithm-gamed hashtags
- Coordinated promotion

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria from Trending

- **Velocity** of conversation
- **Cross-cluster spread**
- **Engagement intensity** (replies > likes)
- **Geographic concentration** (local trend) or **global spread**
- **Authenticity signals**
- **Real-time relevance**
- **User network diversity**

## Recent Integration (2025)
Grok AI provides "Grok Analysis" — AI summaries explaining why a topic is trending, pulling context from thousands of posts.

## Use by Newsrooms
- Trending topics drive editorial decisions about coverage
- Tweetdeck-style monitoring of trends
- Tip source for breaking events
- Audience-feedback signal

## Implications for Editorial Newsworthiness
- Stories trending on X get coverage attention
- Risk of tail wagging the dog (covering things because viral, not because important)
- Verification challenges for viral claims
- Information disorder risk
