# Hanitzsch & Mellado (2011) - What Shapes the News around the World? How Journalists in 18 Countries Perceive Influences on Their Work

Cross-national survey of journalists in 18 societies measuring how they perceive different influences on their news decisions. Published in *International Journal of Press/Politics* 16(3): 404–426. Based on data from the Worlds of Journalism Study (later expanded to 67 countries).

The contribution: First quantitative cross-national mapping of the relative weights journalists themselves assign to different newsworthiness-shaping forces. Empirically operationalizes the Shoemaker & Reese hierarchy-of-influences model.

## Six Dimensions of Influence on News Decisions

### 1. Political Influences
- Censorship.
- Government officials.
- Politicians.
- Pressure from politicians.
- Political parties.

### 2. Economic Influences
- Profit expectations.
- Advertising considerations.
- Audience research.
- Owners.

### 3. Organizational Influences
- Editorial supervisors (immediate boss).
- Editorial policy of the news organization.
- Owners of the news organization.
- Managerial considerations.

### 4. Professional Influences
- Personal values and beliefs.
- Professional ethics and standards.
- Time limits.
- Availability of news-gathering resources.

### 5. Procedural Influences
- Routines and conventions of news work.
- Verification procedures.
- Press laws and regulations.

### 6. Reference-Group Influences
- Peers in the newsroom.
- Other newsrooms / competitors.
- Sources.
- Public relations.
- Friends, family, acquaintances.

## Cross-National Findings

1. **Political and economic influences** are the most important denominators of cross-national differences. Countries cluster on these axes.

2. **Procedural and professional influences** are most universally reported; routines and ethics are the most cross-nationally stable.

3. **Western liberal democracies** report low political influence and high professional / economic influence.

4. **Authoritarian and transitional regimes** report high political influence and lower professional autonomy.

5. **Reference-group influences** (peers, competitors) are intermediate and modestly cross-nationally varied.

## Implicit News-Selection Criteria Differentiated by Country Type

### Liberal-Democratic Outlets
- Audience demand relevance.
- Professional norms (objectivity, ethics).
- Competitor coverage.
- Advertiser sensitivity.

### Polarized Pluralist (e.g., Italy, Spain) Outlets
- Political alignment with owner / outlet politics.
- Government source access.
- Party-affiliated framing.

### Transitional / Authoritarian Outlets
- State-permission framing.
- Self-censorship of sensitive topics.
- Loyalty to political patrons.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Cross-national validity bound on news-values frameworks**: Newsworthiness criteria from US/UK research generalize partially; political and economic influences vary so much that the same event has different selection probabilities across countries.

- **Empirically operationalizes Shoemaker & Reese**: Hanitzsch & Mellado quantify what the hierarchy-of-influences model described qualitatively.

- **Predicts coverage divergences**: Outlets in different media-system clusters should select differently on the same events; testable on cross-national content data.

- **Operationalizable**: Country-level scores on each of the six dimensions can be used as covariates in cross-national news-placement models.

- **Bridges Hallin & Mancini system typology with empirical journalist responses**: Each country's location on the influence map roughly aligns with Hallin & Mancini's three-models classification.
