# Carey (1989) - Communication as Culture: Essays on Media and Society

James W. Carey's foundational essays on communication theory, including the most-cited statement of the *ritual view* of communication. Published by Unwin Hyman, 1989; revised edition Routledge, 2009.

The contribution: Distinguishes two views of communication and shows that the *ritual view* better captures what newspapers do for readers. Newsworthiness, on this account, is not about transmitting information across space but about confirming a shared world across time.

## Two Views of Communication

### Transmission View

- Communication = sending information across geography for control.
- Origin: telegraph, mass media as common-carriers.
- Quality measure: accuracy, fidelity, reach.
- Ideology: instrumental rationality.

### Ritual View

- Communication = "the maintenance of society in time" through "the representation of shared beliefs."
- Origin: religious worship, communal storytelling.
- Quality measure: confirmation, reaffirmation, communal binding.
- Ideology: meaning-making, identity-confirmation.

## The Newspaper as Ritual

Carey's famous example: reading the morning newspaper is not (primarily) information acquisition. It is "attending mass" — participating in a daily ritual that:

- Confirms a shared world picture.
- Affirms social membership ("we are the kind of people who care about these things").
- Performs civic identity (informed citizenship).
- Reproduces cultural categories (what counts as important, who deserves attention).

> "Attending mass [is] a situation in which nothing new is learned but in which a particular view of the world is portrayed and confirmed."

## Six Newsworthiness Implications of the Ritual View

1. **Confirmatory Stories Premium** - Stories that confirm the shared world picture (pillars of society, predictable rhythms) are systematically preferred over stories that challenge it.

2. **Daily Rhythm Compatibility** - News must arrive on schedule and in expected form; the ritual is disrupted if the morning paper is empty or chaotic.

3. **Ceremonial Coverage** - Anniversaries, holidays, transitions of power, deaths, season-openers all have inherent news value because they ritually reaffirm communal time.

4. **Community-Marking Topics** - Local high-school sports, obituaries, weather are massively over-represented relative to information value because they perform community membership.

5. **Reassurance Premium** - Disorder stories must include closure or restoration narrative ("the situation is being addressed") to fit the reassurance function.

6. **Audience Self-Recognition** - Stories about readers' own demographic / community most newsworthy; readers want to see themselves represented.

## Predictions for Modern News

1. **Why local news survives**: Even in the digital era, local news performs ritual identity-affirmation that national news cannot replace.

2. **Why morning routines persist**: News-consumption rituals (morning newspaper, evening broadcast, Sunday talk shows) survive because they perform the ritual function.

3. **Why sensational outliers persist**: Events that puncture the shared world picture (mass shootings, natural disasters) require *more* coverage, not less, because they demand collective ritual processing.

4. **Why obituaries matter**: Death notices perform communal acknowledgment of membership and loss — high ritual value, low information value.

5. **Why repetition is a feature, not a bug**: Re-narrating the same touchstone events (anniversaries, commemorations) is ritual work; from the transmission view it looks like information waste, from the ritual view it is the point.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Two-axis newsworthiness**: An event can be high or low on transmission-view newsworthiness (information utility) AND high or low on ritual-view newsworthiness (community-identity affirmation). Front-page selection often privileges the latter even when the former is weak.

- **Predicts ceremonial-event premium**: Events with strong ceremonial structure (inaugurations, championships, holidays) get coverage exceeding their information value.

- **Operationalizable**: Stories can be coded for ritual markers (community references, identity-affirming framing, ceremonial structure) as features in placement-prediction models.

- **Bridges journalism and cultural studies**: Carey's framework grounds news selection in cultural anthropology, complementing economic and political analyses.
