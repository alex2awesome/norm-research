# Garbage Day (Ryan Broderick) — What Counts as News on the Internet

Broderick covers the internet as a beat; his selection criteria emphasize cultural-significance over hard-news significance.

## Garbage Day's Criteria for "Internet News"
- **Platform behavior changes** that affect how meaning circulates
- **Subculture eruptions** — niche communities producing widely felt artifacts
- **Format shifts** — new content forms (TikTok genres, Discord patterns)
- **Memetic vector tracking** — where ideas originated
- **Online-to-offline events** — when posts cause real-world action
- **Influencer drama** with structural significance (not just gossip)

## Broderick's Tests
- "Is this a new pattern or just an instance of an old one?"
- "Does this tell us something about the platform / algorithm / culture?"
- "Will the people not online care eventually?"
- "Is this a story we missed because it didn't look like 'news'?"

## Demoted Categories
- One-off viral tweets without context
- "Brand X did Y" PR events
- Established creator beefs without new dynamics

## Elevated Categories
- Telegram / Discord / private-platform reporting
- Right-wing internet ecology shifts
- AI slop / generative content reshaping platforms
- Algorithmic injustice case studies
- Subculture moral panics

## Note
Broderick's framework expands the definition of news to "patterns of cultural production that mainstream news systems are too slow to catch."
