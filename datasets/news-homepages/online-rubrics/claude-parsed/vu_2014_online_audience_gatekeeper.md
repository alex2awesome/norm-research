# Vu (2014) - The Online Audience as Gatekeeper: The Influence of Reader Metrics on News Editorial Selection

Hong Tien Vu's survey study of 318 online newspaper editors examining how audience web metrics (page views, time-on-page, click-through) influence editorial gatekeeping decisions. Published in *Journalism* 15(8): 1094–1110.

The contribution: First large-N empirical mapping of US editors' actual self-reported use of audience metrics — provides a quantitative complement to Welbers et al.'s European content analysis.

## Survey Findings

1. **58% of editors actively monitor audience behavior** when checking web traffic — making audience metrics a routine input to selection.

2. **Editors who attach high readership to economic benefit** are significantly more likely to base selection decisions on metrics.

3. **Journalism training reduces metric reliance** - Editors with more journalism education and experience are *less* inclined to use metrics as the primary selection criterion.

4. **Outlet-level variance** - Local outlets and digital-native outlets more metric-driven than legacy national outlets.

5. **Selection effects are real** - Editors confirm that watching metrics changes their decisions about which stories to promote, repost, push to social media, and run as follow-ups.

## Operational News-Value Criteria from Vu's Findings

1. **Demonstrated Click-Through** - High-performing stories influence selection of related/follow-up coverage.

2. **Time-on-Page** - Stories where readers stick around longer signal quality and are favored for promotion.

3. **Click-Through Rate** - Headlines that draw clicks shape future headline-writing and topic selection.

4. **Social-Sharing Volume** - Highly shared stories pull in additional related coverage.

5. **Search Traffic** - SEO-strong stories direct subsequent reporting toward search-discoverable topics.

6. **Real-Time Trending** - Real-time analytics (Chartbeat, Parse.ly) can shift homepage placement minute-to-minute.

## The Six Influences on Editor Metric Use

1. **Outlet's economic model** (subscriber vs. ad-revenue dependent).
2. **Editor's role identification** (entrepreneurial vs. traditional).
3. **Journalism training** (more training, less metric reliance).
4. **Years of experience** (more experience, less reliance).
5. **Outlet size** (smaller outlets more reliant).
6. **Beat / topic area** (lifestyle / entertainment more metric-driven than politics / foreign).

## Theoretical Contribution

Vu argues for a **revision of the hierarchy-of-influences model** (Shoemaker & Reese 1996) to add the *audience* as a more active level of influence — not just an inferred imagined audience but a measured, real-time signal.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Audience as fifth gatekeeper level** - Joins individual / routine / organizational / institutional / system levels in the hierarchy.

- **Metric-driven re-prioritization**: Real-time analytics platforms enable continuous, sub-cycle re-ordering of front-page placement; newsworthiness is no longer a one-shot judgment but a feedback-driven scoring.

- **Operationalizable**: Click-through, dwell time, share rate are all measurable; predict homepage placement and pushback to traditional editorial values.

- **Predicts outlet stratification**: Outlets segregate along a spectrum from traditional/editorial to metrics-driven; can be mapped empirically.
