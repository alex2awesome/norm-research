# Woodward & Bernstein — 4 Principles of Investigative Journalism

Lessons from Watergate, codified by Bob Woodward. Functions as a rubric for investigative newsworthiness and method.

## Principle 1: Be Patient

- "It often takes weeks, months, or years"
- Watergate took 26 months from break-in to Nixon resignation
- Stories develop in stages
- Don't publish prematurely
- Persistence over scoops

## Principle 2: Build Trust with Sources

- "People agree to be sources because they want their side to be heard"
- Make lists of everyone connected to the case
- Approach sources at home, off-hours
- Listen, don't just question
- Protect anonymity when promised
- Cultivate relationships over years

## Principle 3: Verify Everything

- Confirm with at least one other source
- Documents > human sources where possible
- Re-interview repeatedly
- Even Deep Throat (Mark Felt) confirmed via second source
- Question your own assumptions

## Principle 4: Independence from Authority

- "All good work is done in defiance of management"
- Reporter's instincts > corporate caution
- Pursue stories the establishment would prefer buried
- Hold power accountable regardless of position

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for Investigations

1. **Hidden truth** that powerful actors want concealed
2. **Multiple sources** willing to corroborate
3. **Document trail** that can be independently verified
4. **Public interest** of fundamental constitutional weight
5. **Patience-tolerant** story (won't expire if delayed)
6. **Scalable** — initial tip leads to broader pattern
7. **Verifiable** through paper records (financial, legal, governmental)
8. **Significance** — affects nation, not just niche
9. **Reform potential** — exposure could change something

## Method Innovations from Watergate

- "Follow the money"
- Reverse-pyramid investigation (start from suspicion, work to proof)
- Door-to-door interviewing of low-level staffers
- Using deep-background sources to test theories
- Cross-referencing financial records with political activities
