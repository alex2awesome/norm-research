# IPSO Editors' Code of Practice — Public Interest Definition

The Independent Press Standards Organisation (UK) Editors' Code provides a non-exhaustive list of what counts as "public interest" — the central criterion for justifying intrusive or sensitive reporting. Functions as a working definition of newsworthiness for the UK press.

## Public Interest Includes

1. **Detecting or exposing crime**, or the threat of crime, or serious impropriety
2. **Protecting public health or safety**
3. **Protecting the public from being misled** by an action or statement of an individual or organisation
4. **Disclosing failures to comply with obligations** (regulatory, legal, professional)
5. **Disclosing a miscarriage of justice**
6. **Raising or contributing to a matter of public debate**, including serious cases of impropriety, unethical conduct or incompetence concerning the public
7. **Disclosing concealment, or likely concealment, of any of the above**
8. **There is a public interest in freedom of expression itself**

## Burden on Editors

When invoking the public interest exception, editors must demonstrate that they:
- Reasonably believed publication would serve the public interest
- Believed it would be proportionate to the public interest
- Can explain how they reached that decision at the time

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria

- **Crime exposure** — fundamental
- **Public safety / health threat**
- **Misleading public** — accountability
- **Hidden wrongdoing** — exposure value
- **Justice failures** — wrongful convictions
- **Civic debate enrichment** — democratic value
- **Concealment exposure**
- **Free expression value** itself

## Special Considerations
- Children: editors must demonstrate exceptional public interest to override normally paramount interests of the child
- Subterfuge / undercover reporting requires demonstrable public interest justification
