# Boyd — Broadcast Journalism: Techniques of Radio and Television News

Andrew Boyd's classic textbook (multiple editions, with co-authors Stewart and Alexander). Defines news values for broadcast.

## 6 Core Broadcast News Values (Boyd)

1. **Proximity** — Geographical/cultural closeness
2. **Relevance** — How story affects audience
3. **Immediacy** — Just-happening, real-time
4. **Interest** — Audience curiosity
5. **Drama** — Conflict, tension, narrative arc
6. **Entertainment** — Engaging beyond hard news

## Boyd's Additions for Broadcast Distinctiveness

### Radio
- **Strength is immediacy** — must constantly update
- **Voice quality** — interviewable subjects
- **Sound potential** — atmospheric audio
- **Brevity** — tight scripts
- **Pronounceable** names/places
- **Active voice** for drive-time clarity

### Television
- **Visual potential** — must have video
- **Action over static** — movement matters
- **Identifiable people** on camera
- **Bites** — quotable interview clips
- **Live capability** — being there now
- **Graphics-friendly** — chart-able data
- **Tease-worthy** — promo-able clip

## Broadcast Story Selection Filters

- Can we get there?
- Can we get an interview?
- Can we shoot/record it?
- Can we tell it in 30s-2min?
- Will viewers/listeners stay tuned?
- Does it fit the "rundown rhythm"?

## Quality Markers in Broadcast

- Sound quality
- Image quality
- Anchor delivery
- Reporter on-scene
- Fresh material (vs. file footage)
- Multiple voices in story
- Original reporting visible

## Implicit Newsworthiness for Broadcast

- **Story can be heard / seen** — not just read
- **People available** for interview
- **Visuals exist** (TV) or **sound exists** (radio)
- **Brief enough** for format
- **Dramatic enough** to retain attention
- **Live moment** when possible
- **Promotable** for tease
