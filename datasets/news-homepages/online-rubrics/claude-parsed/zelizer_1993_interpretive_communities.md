# Zelizer (1993) - Journalists as Interpretive Communities

Barbie Zelizer's influential framework treating journalists as an *interpretive community* — a collective whose shared narratives, key cases, and discursive practices shape what counts as legitimate journalism and therefore what counts as news. Published in *Critical Studies in Mass Communication* 10(3): 219–237.

The contribution: Reframes "professional norms" not as formal rules but as the *living narratives* journalists tell each other — about the Kennedy assassination, Watergate, the Vietnam draft, etc. Newsworthiness emerges from these shared narratives.

## Key Concepts

1. **Interpretive Community** - Journalists are bound together not by formal certification but by shared interpretations of key past events that anchor professional identity.

2. **Key Cases / Touchstones** - Specific historical events serve as touchstones (Watergate, the Pentagon Papers, the Kennedy assassination) that journalists invoke to make sense of new situations.

3. **Discursive Authority** - Stories are valid (newsworthy) when they fit recognized interpretive patterns established by these touchstone cases.

## Five Mechanisms by Which the Interpretive Community Defines Newsworthiness

1. **Touchstone Activation** - New stories become news partly by analogy to established touchstone cases ("the next Watergate," "another Pentagon Papers"). The analogical link confers newsworthiness.

2. **Narrative-Template Application** - Templates from past stories (the lone reformer, the cover-up, the heroic source) are applied to new stories; events fitting templates are easier to report.

3. **Heroic-Reporter Mythology** - Stories that valorize investigative journalism (echoing Woodward & Bernstein) get amplified within the profession because they affirm community identity.

4. **Discursive Boundary Maintenance** - Journalists police what counts as "real" journalism vs. partisan, advocacy, opinion, entertainment work. Boundary disputes shape selection.

5. **Memorial Reaffirmation** - Anniversary coverage (5/10/20 years since X) re-narrates touchstone cases, sustaining the interpretive community.

## Implicit News-Selection Criteria from the Interpretive-Community Frame

1. **Touchstone Resonance** - Events that map onto canonical touchstone cases (e.g., a new whistleblower compared to Daniel Ellsberg) get coverage frame inheritance.

2. **Heroic-Narrative Fit** - Stories admitting heroic-reporter or heroic-source narrative templates are more reportable.

3. **Professional-Identity Reaffirmation** - Stories that confirm journalists' self-image as truth-tellers / accountability-watchdogs are pursued enthusiastically.

4. **Inter-outlet Discursive Convergence** - Major stories generate cross-outlet interpretive convergence; deviation from the consensus interpretation is rare.

5. **Award-Worthiness** - Stories that align with what wins Pulitzers / Polks / Murrows are pre-selected for fitting professional definitions of excellence.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Newsworthiness is a community judgment**: Beyond Galtung-Ruge structural factors, what counts as news depends on whether it fits the interpretive community's narrative repertoire.

- **Predicts coverage cycles**: Once a story gets framed via a touchstone case, subsequent stories activate the same framing; produces predictable coverage cycles.

- **Explains historical specificity**: Different generations' touchstones (Civil Rights, Vietnam, Watergate, 9/11, Iraq War) produce different newsworthiness patterns.

- **Bridges Tuchman/Gans with cultural sociology**: Adds a *cultural-narrative* layer above the organizational and structural layers.

- **Operationalizable**: Stories can be coded for touchstone-case references (textual analogies to Watergate, Pentagon Papers, etc.) as predictors of placement and follow-up coverage.
