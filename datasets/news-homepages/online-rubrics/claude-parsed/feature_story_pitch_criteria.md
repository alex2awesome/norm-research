# Feature Story Pitch — Editor Selection Criteria

What lifestyle / magazine editors look for in feature pitches. Functions as feature-newsworthiness rubric.

## Pitch Format Standards

- **3-4 paragraphs** — concise
- **Story, not a topic** — angle, not subject
- **Written in story tone** — sample voice
- **Tailored to publication** — read first, mimic style

## What Editors Want (Implicit Newsworthiness)

### Core Story Elements
1. **Characters** — interesting people with names
2. **Twists and turns** — narrative tension
3. **Suspense** — mystery to resolve
4. **Color** — vivid scene-setting
5. **Beautiful writing** — prose quality
6. **Original angle** — fills coverage hole
7. **Unique perspective** — fresh take

### Practical Considerations
- **Visual potential** — photo opportunities
- **Sidebar potential** — packaged elements
- **Infographic angle** — data visualization
- **Audio/video** — multimedia
- **Source list** — who you'll interview
- **Research backing** — studies, data
- **Proposed deadline** — reliability signal

### Audience Fit
- **Reader interest** in topic
- **Outlet's coverage gap** filled
- **Tone match** to publication
- **Section fit** (front-of-book, feature well, etc.)

### Author Credibility
- **Past clips** demonstrating ability
- **Subject expertise**
- **Reportorial capacity**

## Implicit Feature Newsworthiness Criteria

1. **Compelling protagonist**
2. **Narrative arc** — beginning, middle, end
3. **Sensory detail potential** — sights, sounds, smells
4. **Timely peg** OR evergreen relevance
5. **Counterintuitive angle** — challenges assumption
6. **Universal theme** — speaks to wider experience
7. **Unique access** — author can get the story
8. **Visual storytelling potential**
9. **Reader takeaway** — what will they learn/feel?
10. **Service value** — practical use OR
11. **Inspirational value** — emotional uplift OR
12. **Cautionary value** — warning

## Genre Distinctions

### Service / Utility Feature
- Practical advice
- How-to focus
- Reader benefits

### Narrative Feature
- Story-driven
- Character-led
- Scene-rich

### Trend Story
- Multiple examples of pattern
- Why now?
- Cultural significance

### Profile
- Single subject deeply explored
- Defining moment / question
- Why this person now?

### Investigative Feature
- Wrongdoing or hidden truth
- Document trail
- Multiple sources
