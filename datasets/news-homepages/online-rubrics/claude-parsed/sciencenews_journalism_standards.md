# Science News — Journalism Standards and Practices

Editorial standards for science journalism at one of the leading specialist outlets.

## What Makes a Science Story Newsworthy (Science News)

- **New findings** — original research published in peer-reviewed journals
- **New techniques** — methodological breakthroughs
- **Surprising statistics** — counterintuitive data
- **Latest science trends** — emerging fields

## Key Practices

### Sourcing
- Authors of scientific papers
- Experts whose work is cited
- Independent experts (cross-checking)
- Trusted contacts cultivated over years
- Recommendations from other experts

### Embargo System
- Mandate forbidding publication until set time
- Allows time for thorough reporting (interviews, expert consultation)
- Gives all journalists fair access
- Critics: makes science journalism reactive vs. enterprising

### Quality Standards
- Peer-review status checked
- Sample size reported
- Limitations acknowledged
- Conflicts of interest disclosed
- No "single study" hype without context

## Implicit Science Newsworthiness Criteria

1. **Novelty** of finding
2. **Methodological rigor** of underlying study
3. **Statistical significance** + practical significance
4. **Replicability** assessment
5. **Surprising / counter-expected** results
6. **Health relevance** for non-specialists
7. **Policy implications**
8. **Beautiful / elegant** experiments
9. **Reputable journal / institution** publishing
10. **Author reputation**
11. **Diversity of voices in science** (gender, race, geography)
12. **Independent expert validation**

## Notable Pitfalls Avoided
- Overhyping single studies
- Implying causation from correlation
- Premature health advice
- Press-release stenography
- Echo chamber of "knowns"
