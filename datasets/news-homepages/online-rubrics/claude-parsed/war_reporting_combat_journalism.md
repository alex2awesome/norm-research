# War Reporting / Combat Journalism — News Selection Criteria

Specialist newsworthiness for conflict zones. Distinct ethical and practical considerations.

## Why Conflicts Are Heavily Covered

- **Conflict** is core news value (Galtung-Ruge)
- **Drama** is built-in
- **Magnitude** of impact (death, displacement)
- **Geopolitical significance**
- **Visual potential** (images of war)
- **Audience interest**
- **Historical significance**

## Selection Criteria for Conflict Stories

### Macro Factors
1. **Strategic significance** of the conflict
2. **National involvement** of audience country
3. **Civilian casualties** scale
4. **Refugee flows**
5. **Historical resonance** (same region, related conflict)
6. **Resource implications** (oil, food)
7. **Nuclear / WMD concerns**
8. **Genocide / war crimes** allegations

### Micro Factors
1. **Civilian story** (named victim/survivor)
2. **Soldier story** (identifiable serviceperson)
3. **Atrocity exposure**
4. **Heroic act**
5. **Photographer's iconic image**
6. **Frontline movement** (territory taken/lost)
7. **Negotiation / ceasefire**
8. **High-level visit** (diplomat, leader)

## Embedding vs. Independence

- **Embedded reporting** — assigned to military unit
  - Pros: access, safety, intimate view
  - Cons: restricted movement/conversations, framing bias toward unit
- **Unilateral / independent** — separate from military
  - Pros: independent perspective
  - Cons: extreme danger, limited access

## Ethical / Practical Constraints

- Physical safety (kidnap, killing)
- Source safety (locals interviewed at risk)
- Verification difficulty (propaganda from all sides)
- Censorship (military, government)
- Trauma / PTSD of journalists
- Targeting of journalists (rising in modern wars)

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for War

- **Verifiable atrocity** vs. propaganda claim
- **Civilian face** of military events
- **Identifiable named subjects** (vs. anonymous casualties)
- **Photographic / video evidence**
- **Multiple-source confirmation** (especially for atrocities)
- **Geopolitical resonance**
- **Pattern of conflict escalation**
- **Diplomatic intervention possibility**
- **Humanitarian crisis** dimensions

## Coverage Disparities

- US/UK conflicts heavily covered
- African / Asian conflicts under-covered
- Casualty count alone insufficient (1000 in country X = front page; 10000 in country Y = brief)
- "Worthy victim" framing (per Chomsky/Herman)
