# The New York Times — Guidelines on Our Integrity

The flagship NYT ethics document. Includes implicit newsworthiness criteria embedded in standards for what should and shouldn't be published.

## Core Purpose

"Enhance society by creating, collecting and distributing high-quality news and information." Producing content of the highest quality and integrity is the basis for reputation and fulfilling public trust.

## Key Standards (Newsworthiness-Relevant)

### Verification Standards
- Writers are their own principal fact-checkers
- Concrete facts (addresses, titles, distances, phone numbers) must be verified
- Standard references required (directories, official sites)

### Rumors and Newsworthiness
"Rumors must satisfy The Times's standard of newsworthiness, taste and plausibility before publication, even when attributed."
- The Times's three-pronged test: **newsworthiness, taste, plausibility**

### Conflicts of Interest
- Real or apparent conflicts (with sources, advertisers, advocacy groups)
- Family/spouse civic activities considered

### Corrections
- Explicit, prompt correction of all errors
- Prominent, consistent location

### Rebuttals & Fairness
- When criticism is serious, NYT has "special obligation" to describe scope and allow subject to respond
- Multi-source confirmation for damaging claims

### Anonymous Sources
- Last resort, not first
- Editor approval required
- Reasoning explained to readers

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria
- **Quality / craft** — Highest editorial standards
- **Integrity / verifiability** — Facts must check out
- **Newsworthiness** as standalone test
- **Taste** — restraint on sensational/sex/violence
- **Plausibility** — implausible claims excluded
- **Fairness to subjects** of negative coverage
- **Source diversity**
- **Public significance**
- **Service to public trust**
