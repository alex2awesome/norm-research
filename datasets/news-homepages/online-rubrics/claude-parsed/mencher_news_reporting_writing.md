# News Reporting and Writing — Melvin Mencher

The most widely used American journalism textbook for decades. Mencher identifies a set of "news determinants" plus essential journalistic qualities.

## 8 Core News Determinants

1. **Timeliness** — Current events priority. Online deadlines occur every minute. Examples: breaking news, assassinations.

2. **Impact** — Events affecting many people. Example: deaths of 9 Charleston firefighters affected a whole community.

3. **Prominence** — Well-known individuals and institutions. Michael Jackson's death vs. local musician.

4. **Proximity** — Geographic closeness increases newsworthiness. BTK case dominated Wichita; minimal attention elsewhere. "The closer to home, the bigger the story."

5. **Conflict** — Disputes (physical, ideological, legal). Sports, court challenges, cultural controversies.

6. **The Unusual** — Extraordinary situations. Sinkholes, unexpected rescues.

7. **Currency** — Dormant issues becoming newsworthy through new circumstances. Example: fingerprint scanning in schools gains attention through privacy concerns.

8. **Necessity** — Investigative reporters pursue stories exposing injustice/danger to vulnerable populations. Public service journalism.

## Essential Journalistic Qualities

- **Accuracy** — Fundamental moral requirement
- **Clarity** — Reader understanding
- **Brevity** — Concise presentation
- **Attribution** — Crediting sources
- **Verification** — Confirming facts before publication
- **Fairness and Balance** — Multiple perspectives
- **Human Interest** — Emotional connection

## Five W's and H

Who, What, When, Where, Why, How.

## News Judgment

Effective journalism requires discerning which events merit coverage and which deserve prominence. Multiple determinants often apply to single stories. Judgment develops through experience.
