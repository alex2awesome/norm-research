# Schlesinger (1978/1987) - Putting Reality Together: BBC News

Philip Schlesinger's pathbreaking ethnography of BBC newsrooms in the 1970s, the first sustained organizational sociology of broadcast news in the British context. Originally Constable, 1978; reissued by Methuen/Routledge, 1987.

Schlesinger's central contribution to news-values research is the systematic enumeration of *operational constraints* (logistics, time, format, professional ideology) as determinants of newsworthiness — frequently grouped under "logistics" in subsequent literature.

## Key Operational Constraints Acting as News Values

1. **The Stop-Watch Culture (Time-Slavery)** - News production is organized around clock-deadlines. Events that fit deadlines achieve newsworthiness; events that develop on incompatible timescales are demoted or held over.

2. **Logistics / Resource Deployability** - A story is newsworthy if a crew/reporter can be physically dispatched in time. Geographic concentration of staff biases coverage.

3. **Format Compatibility** - Stories must fit broadcast formats (90 seconds, 2-minute package). Stories that don't compress lose news value.

4. **Bulletin Slot Architecture** - The bulletin has fixed slots (lead, second story, regional, sports, weather). Story selection is shaped by slot availability.

5. **Picture/Visuals Availability** - For TV, no usable picture means low news value, even for important stories.

6. **The Imperative of "Coverage"** - Editors must fill the bulletin every day. This produces a *demand-pull* on stories, lowering thresholds when supply is short.

7. **Professional Self-Image** - Reporters internalize the BBC's "impartiality" and "responsible journalism" ethos, which filters out stories that would compromise these values.

8. **Wire-Service Dependency** - Even the BBC depended heavily on Reuters and PA for international news, importing wire-service news values.

9. **The Hierarchy of Credibility** - Officials/establishment sources are accorded automatic credibility; alternative sources must establish theirs.

10. **The Avoidance of Trouble** - Editors avoid stories that risk libel, government complaint, or audience backlash; newsworthiness includes a defensibility component.

## Schlesinger's Critique of Pure News-Values Approaches

Schlesinger argued that abstract news-value lists (Galtung & Ruge) understate the role of these *production-side* constraints. The same event would be evaluated very differently in a wire-rich beat-rich newsroom vs. a stretched regional outlet. News values are inseparable from the means of production.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Logistics as a master constraint**: Logistics (Schlesinger's term, later codified in Bednarek & Caple) is now standard in news-values lists.

- **News is co-determined by event and outlet capacity**: A model of newsworthiness must include outlet-side variables (resources, format, deadlines), not just event properties.

- **Professional ideology operates as a filter**: The BBC's commitment to impartiality systematically demoted stories incompatible with two-sided framing — predicts British coverage patterns of, e.g., Northern Ireland.

- **Empirically validates Tuchman's typifications**: Schlesinger's UK ethnography found the same hard/soft/spot/developing/continuing typology Tuchman 1973 found in US.
