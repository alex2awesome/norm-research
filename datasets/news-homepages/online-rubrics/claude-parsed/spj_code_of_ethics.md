# SPJ Code of Ethics — Four Pillars

The Society of Professional Journalists' core ethical framework. Not a set of rules, but a guide for journalistic responsibility. While not a strict newsworthiness rubric, it includes several criteria for what should be reported.

## 1. Seek Truth and Report It
"Journalists should be honest, fair and courageous in gathering, reporting and interpreting information."

Key directives on what should be reported:
- Tell the story of the diversity and magnitude of the human experience boldly, even when unpopular
- Give voice to the voiceless; official and unofficial sources can be equally valid
- Recognize a special obligation to ensure public business is conducted in the open and government records open to inspection
- Examine your own cultural values and avoid imposing them on others
- Avoid stereotyping; report differences explicitly
- Consider the long-term implications of decisions
- Distinguish news from advertising and entertainment

## 2. Minimize Harm
"Ethical journalists treat sources, subjects and colleagues as human beings deserving of respect."
- Balance the public's right to know against potential harm
- Show compassion for those affected by news coverage
- Be sensitive when seeking or using interviews/photos of those affected by tragedy
- Recognize that legal access to information differs from ethical justification to publish

## 3. Act Independently
"Journalists should be free of obligation to any interest other than the public's right to know."
- Avoid conflicts of interest, real or perceived
- Refuse gifts, favors, fees, free travel, special treatment
- Distinguish news from sponsored content

## 4. Be Accountable and Transparent
"Journalists are accountable to their readers, listeners, viewers and each other."
- Explain ethical choices and processes to audiences
- Encourage civil dialogue with the public
- Acknowledge and correct mistakes promptly and prominently
- Expose unethical conduct in journalism

## Newsworthiness-Relevant Themes
- The "public's right to know" — a foundational criterion for what to publish
- Public business / government accountability
- Diversity of human experience
- Voice for the voiceless
- Long-term consequences vs. short-term clicks
