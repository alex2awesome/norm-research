# Local News Lab — Community-Centered Newsworthiness

A movement (LMA Lab for Journalism Funding, Trusting News, Solutions Journalism Network) reframing local newsworthiness around community service and trust.

## Core Principle

"Ground work in community need, not your own. The best way to earn community support is to focus on serving and solving community needs."

## Newsworthiness Criteria for Community-Centered Local Journalism

1. **Responds to identified community need** — Listening informs coverage
2. **Practically useful** — Information you can act on
3. **Locally relevant** — Affects this specific community
4. **Solutions-oriented** — Not just problems
5. **Voices of community members** — Centered, not "sources"
6. **Underserved communities prioritized**
7. **Trust-building** — Builds rather than burns
8. **Civic engagement enabling**
9. **Newsroom acts as community member** — beyond traditional boundaries

## Trust-Building Methods

- **Listening sessions** — interview community as customers, not sources
- **Pitch Days** — community presents needs to journalism funders
- **Community advisory boards**
- **Solutions reporting** — what's working
- **Transparency about process**
- **Acknowledging journalism's mistakes**
- **Showing up** at community events outside reporter role

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria (Community-Centered)

### What Counts as News
- Issues raised in community listening
- Practical concerns of residents
- Solutions to local problems
- Stories of resilience and agency
- Voices not previously heard
- Bridging across community divides

### What's Devalued
- Stories driven by national press themes (when local issues exist)
- Crime over-coverage
- Helicopter journalism (parachute in/out)
- Engagement-bait
- Negativity for negativity's sake

## Distinct from Traditional Newsworthiness

Traditional: Conflict, prominence, drama → covered
Community-centered: Civic need, solutions, voice → covered

Traditional: Episodic coverage of crisis
Community-centered: Sustained coverage with community
