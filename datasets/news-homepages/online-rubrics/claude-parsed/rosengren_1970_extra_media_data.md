# Rosengren (1970, 1974) - The Extra-Media Critique of Galtung & Ruge

Karl Erik Rosengren's two influential papers ("International News: Intra and Extra Media Data" 1970 in *Acta Sociologica*; "International News: Methods, Data and Theory" 1974 in *Journal of Peace Research*) launched the methodological critique that reshaped the news-values tradition.

The argument: Galtung & Ruge studied only intra-media data — what newspapers printed — and inferred news values from it. But you cannot say a newsroom *prefers* personalized, negative, elite stories without a benchmark of *what events were actually available*. Without "extra-media data" (independent statistics about events), you cannot distinguish news selection from world supply.

## Rosengren's Methodological Demands

1. **Extra-Media Benchmark** - Compare news coverage of an event class to an independent, statistical measure of how often that class actually occurs. Bias is the *deviation* between the two, not coverage frequency itself.

2. **Population Definition** - Define the universe of potential news events before coding, not from the news itself.

3. **Event-Type Operationalization** - Code events by attributes whose distribution in the world is independently knowable (elections, country wealth, military spending) so that coverage can be benchmarked.

4. **Multiple Outlets, Multiple Countries** - Compare coverage across outlets and countries to separate idiosyncratic editor choice from systematic news values.

## Empirical Application: Election Coverage

Rosengren studied how newspapers in three countries covered foreign parliamentary elections. The extra-media benchmark was the actual list of elections held. He found:

- **Disproportionate coverage of elite nations** even controlling for whether elections were happening.
- **Geographic bias** beyond what proximity alone explains.
- **Treaty/alliance-system bias** — elections in allied countries got more coverage than elections in non-allied countries with similar populations.

## Updated News-Selection Criteria Implied by the Critique

5. **Real-World Event Frequency** - Story selection should be benchmarked against the frequency of relevant event classes; over-representation indicates a value.

6. **Country Salience Independent of News** - Population, GDP, treaty memberships, geographic proximity should be measurable independently and treated as covariates.

7. **Source Country's Media Capacity** - Coverage flows from countries with developed news infrastructure (and English-language wires); this is supply, not demand.

8. **Cultural/Political Affinity** - Net of size, allied/like-system countries get more coverage than rivals — even though news theories predict the opposite.

## Implications for Newsworthiness Research

- **Single-outlet, single-country studies are insufficient** to identify news values; benchmarks are required.
- **News values are conditional on supply**: Reported "negativity" may reflect the world's supply of negative events, not a newsroom preference, unless extra-media data say otherwise.
- **Bias measurement requires baselines**: Any modern audit (homepage placement, recommender systems) inherits Rosengren's demand for an independent reference distribution.
- **Anticipates causal-inference critiques**: Modern computational journalism studies routinely cite Rosengren when constructing counterfactual baselines.
