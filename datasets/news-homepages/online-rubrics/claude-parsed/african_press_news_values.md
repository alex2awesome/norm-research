# African Press News Values — Ghana, Nigeria, and Pan-African Patterns

Empirical research on what shapes news selection in African newsrooms, with focus on Ghanaian and Nigerian outlets.

## Common Coverage Patterns of Africa (in African press)

- War, crime, killings, crises, terrorism dominate
- Omission of progress / development stories
- Reliance on Western news agencies (BBC, Reuters, AP, AFP)
- Limited cross-African correspondence
- Reproduction of Western framing

## News Selection Pressures (Distinct from Western)

### Technical Constraints
- Congested networks
- Slow internet
- Malfunctioning computers
- Lack of multimedia workstations
- Affects what stories can even be reported

### Editorial Pressures
- Owners' political/business interests
- Restrictions on criticizing owner-aligned figures
- "Killed" stories on certain topics
- Political pressures from government
- Self-censorship

### Source Dependencies
- Heavy reliance on international news agencies
- BBC World Service universally consulted
- Limited indigenous foreign correspondence
- AFP, Reuters, AP central

## African News Values (per scholarship)

In addition to standard news values, African newsrooms weight:

1. **Tribal / ethnic identity considerations**
2. **Religious significance** (Christianity, Islam)
3. **Pan-African solidarity** vs. nation-specific
4. **Diaspora connections**
5. **Development progress** (sometimes underweighted)
6. **Anti-colonial framing** opportunities
7. **Major political figures** (often overweighted)
8. **Donor / international agency news** (NGOs, UN)
9. **Sports** (football centrally important)
10. **Religious leader pronouncements**

## Innovations in African Newsrooms

- AI adoption in Nigerian newsrooms (copy-editing, ad targeting)
- Mobile-first news (most readers via phone)
- WhatsApp / SMS news alerts
- Local language broadcasting
- Citizen journalism (Sahara Reporters model)

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria

- **Owner safety** — what won't get killed
- **Government pressure tolerance**
- **Donor relevance**
- **Pan-African vs. national**
- **Mobile-friendly format**
- **Ethnic/religious balance** required
- **Available wire content**
- **Local correspondent reach**

## Critique

African scholars argue Western news values inadequately capture African newsroom realities. Need for development-oriented, peace-oriented, indigenous frameworks.
