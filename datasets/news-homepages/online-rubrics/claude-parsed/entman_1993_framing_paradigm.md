# Entman (1993) - Framing: Toward Clarification of a Fractured Paradigm

Robert Entman's enormously cited synthesis of framing theory. Published in *Journal of Communication* 43(4): 51–58. Provides the canonical definition and the four-function model that organizes most subsequent framing research.

> "To frame is to select some aspects of a perceived reality and make them more salient in a communicating text, in such a way as to promote a particular problem definition, causal interpretation, moral evaluation, and/or treatment recommendation."

## The Four Functions of a Frame

Frames simultaneously perform up to four operations on an event. Each function serves as an implicit newsworthiness criterion — events that admit clear framing in any function are more newsworthy.

1. **Problem Definition** - Identifying what the issue is. Newsworthy events are those that can be cleanly named as a problem (e.g., "drug epidemic," "border crisis," "housing shortage").

2. **Causal Interpretation** - Explaining what causes the problem. Events with attributable causes (a person, a policy, a specific factor) outrank events with diffuse causation.

3. **Moral Evaluation** - Assessing right/wrong, good/bad. Events that license clear moral judgment (a villain, a victim, a hero) fit the form better than morally ambiguous events.

4. **Treatment Recommendation** - Suggesting remedies. Events tied to actionable solutions (legislation, donations, individual choices) gain coverage incentive.

## How Framing Operates: Four Loci

1. **The Communicator** - Frames stem from journalists' beliefs about the audience, the source, and the story.
2. **The Text** - Frames are realized through keywords, metaphors, sources cited, image choice.
3. **The Receiver** - Audiences may decode frames differently than intended.
4. **The Culture** - Common stock of frames in a society shapes which framings are publicly available.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Frame-availability as a news value**: An event must be reducible to one or more of the four functions for it to be reportable. Events without an obvious problem-definition or villain are systematically harder to cover.

- **Salience selection**: Framing increases the *salience* of selected aspects — making them "more noticeable, meaningful, or memorable." News selection is thus inseparable from frame selection.

- **Predicts which controversies get coverage**: Issues that map onto culturally available frames (e.g., crime, terrorism, scandal) become newsworthy faster than novel issues that lack a frame template.

- **Frame contestation = continuing news**: When competing frames vie over an event (e.g., "freedom fighter" vs. "terrorist"), the dispute itself becomes news.

- **Bridges Galtung-Ruge tradition with cognitive science**: Frames operate at the intersection of journalist cognition, text affordances, and audience interpretation — explains *why* news values like personalization and unambiguity work.
