# Iyengar & Kinder (1987) - News That Matters: Television and American Opinion

Shanto Iyengar and Donald Kinder's experimental confirmation of agenda-setting *and* the discovery of *priming*. Published by University of Chicago Press, 1987 (updated 2010).

Used controlled lab experiments unobtrusively altering the order and emphasis of stories in TV news segments shown to participants — providing the first strong causal evidence for media effects on (a) which issues people consider important and (b) which issues people use as criteria when evaluating political leaders.

## Two Effects Documented

1. **Agenda Setting** - Issues that received extended coverage became more important to viewers; issues that were ignored lost credibility. Confirms Cohen 1963 / McCombs & Shaw 1972 with experimental controls.

2. **Priming** - Issues prominent in the news stream become *evaluation criteria* citizens use when judging leaders. If economic stories run, viewers judge the president on economic performance. If foreign-policy stories run, foreign-policy performance dominates the evaluation.

## Newsworthiness Implications of Each Effect

### From Agenda Setting

- **Repetition Confers Importance** - The same story across nights / outlets builds salience.
- **Lead-Story Premium** - Opening segment of a broadcast carries amplified weight.
- **Length Premium** - Longer stories signal more importance.
- **Visual Treatment Premium** - Stories with strong visuals achieve disproportionate salience.

### From Priming

- **Performance-Relevant Stories** - Stories on issues that connect to current officeholders' performance get extra newsworthiness because they directly bear on political accountability.
- **Issue-Coupling** - Stories that can be coupled to other prominent themes (e.g., a domestic crime story coupled to ongoing crime-policy debate) gain coverage.
- **Credit/Blame Hooks** - Stories that present clear opportunities to credit or blame officials gain coverage.

## Five Conditions That Strengthen Priming Effects

1. **High personal relevance** of the issue to viewers.
2. **Existing partisan attachments** that make blame attribution natural.
3. **Frequent repetition** of the story across days.
4. **Affective tone** (negative stories prime more strongly).
5. **Single-source dominance** (when most networks tell the same story, priming is stronger).

## Methodological Contribution

Previous agenda-setting studies relied on correlations between media content and survey responses. Iyengar & Kinder's experiments — randomly assigning subjects to view newscasts containing extra coverage of one issue or another — provided the first *causal* evidence and silenced reverse-causation critiques.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Salience-engineering responsibility**: News selection is not just selection but *priming* — choosing which criteria voters will use to judge leaders. This makes coverage choices politically consequential beyond what the agenda-setting literature alone implies.

- **Operationalizable signals**: Story position, length, repetition, visuals — all measurable on a homepage — function as causal levers of public salience.

- **Why morning/evening news cycle matters**: Stories encountered earlier "anchor" subsequent evaluation; first-mover coverage carries disproportionate priming weight.
