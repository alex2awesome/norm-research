# McCombs & Shaw (1972) - The Agenda-Setting Function of Mass Media

The Chapel Hill study, the empirical foundation for agenda-setting theory. McCombs and Shaw surveyed 100 undecided voters in Chapel Hill, NC during the 1968 presidential election and content-analyzed five newspapers, two television networks, and two news magazines. They found a near-perfect rank-order correlation between the issues media emphasized and the issues voters reported as "important."

Published in *Public Opinion Quarterly* 36(2): 176–187. Operationalized Cohen 1963's aphorism into a measurable theory.

## Core Newsworthiness Implications

The agenda-setting function specifies how news selection translates into public salience. The factors that drive *agenda placement* are themselves implicit news values:

1. **Repetition** - Issues repeated across outlets and across days achieve top-of-mind status. Repetition is itself a marker of newsworthiness.

2. **Position / Display** - Front-page placement, lead-story status, and long-form treatment all signal "this is important." Editors' display choices function as newsworthiness markers.

3. **Length** - Story length contributes to salience; longer treatments mark higher news value.

4. **Time of Coverage** - Stories covered earliest in a campaign / news cycle anchor the agenda for the rest of the cycle.

5. **Cross-Outlet Convergence** - Issues covered by *all* major outlets achieve maximum agenda weight; idiosyncratic coverage is less effective.

## Three Subsequent Models in the Agenda-Setting Tradition

1. **Awareness Model** - Media coverage simply makes audiences aware that an issue exists.
2. **Priorities Model** - Media coverage shapes the *ranking* of issues in importance.
3. **Salience Model** - Media coverage shapes how *prominent* an issue feels in audience minds.

## Two Levels of Agenda Setting

- **First-Level Agenda Setting** - Media transfer the salience of *objects* (which issues, candidates, events to think about).
- **Second-Level Agenda Setting** (later extension) - Media transfer the salience of *attributes* (which features of those objects to think about).

## Five Implicit Selection Criteria for Agenda-Worthy Stories

1. **Pre-existing Public Concern** - Issues already on the public mind get amplified.
2. **Cross-Source Confirmation** - Issues with multiple sources flagging them rise faster.
3. **Visual Newsworthiness** (TV) - Issues with strong visuals reach larger TV audiences.
4. **Compatibility with Election Cycle** - Issues that map to candidate positions get more campaign coverage.
5. **Persistence** - Issues sustainable over multiple news cycles dominate the agenda.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Display = newsworthiness signal**: Where and how a story is placed encodes editorial newsworthiness judgments. A homepage-positioning model can use placement as a target variable for newsworthiness.

- **Aggregate-level effects**: Agenda-setting operates at the population level — even individual stories that lack high news value can become agenda-shaping if reproduced across outlets.

- **Re-coverage justification**: Once an issue is on the public agenda, additional coverage is justified as "responsive to public interest" — creating a feedback loop with reader metrics (anticipates Welbers et al. 2016, Vu 2014).
