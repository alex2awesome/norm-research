# Nieman Lab — Journalists' Perception of Newsworthiness by Source

Survey-based study of 1,510 American newspaper journalists testing how publication source affects perceived newsworthiness.

## Key Findings

### Source Bias in Newsworthiness Judgments
- **National newspaper** publication: same as unpublished
- **Mid-sized paper** publication: same as unpublished
- **Local newspaper** publication: rated **less** newsworthy than unpublished

### Twitter and Newsworthiness
- Inexperienced journalists / heavy Twitter users rated **anonymous, context-free tweets** as newsworthy as AP headlines
- Suggests "profound disconnect between newsworthiness and other significant news values such as credibility, objectivity, and context"

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria (Revealed Bias)

- **Source prestige** influences perceived newsworthiness, controlling for content
- **Local stigma** — Stories that have appeared in local outlets are devalued
- **Twitter as a news driver** despite poor verification
- **Contextlessness** does not disqualify in social media culture
- Newsworthiness divorced from credibility for some journalists

## Implications

- "Newsworthiness" is socially constructed — same content can be more or less newsworthy depending on prestige of prior publisher
- Algorithmic news distribution may amplify these biases
- Local journalism systematically undervalued
