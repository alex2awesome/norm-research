# Jack Shafer — Iconoclastic Newsworthiness Standards

Long-running press criticism columns at Slate / Reuters / Politico provide a contrarian rubric.

## Shafer's Newsworthiness Heuristics
- **Skepticism of "trend pieces"** — three anecdotes do not make a trend
- **Source diversity demand** — single-source bombshells warrant doubt
- **Anonymous sources require justification** in print
- **Counter-intuitive doesn't mean true**
- **Beware "the story everyone is missing"** — usually missed because not real
- **Distrust the access-rich beat reporter** who never breaks bad news about their source

## Shafer's Tests for Front-Page Worthiness
- "What's the evidentiary base?"
- "Could a competing reporter falsify this?"
- "Is the analyst quoted truly independent?"
- "Is the piece structured around what's known or what's wished?"
- "Does the lede over-promise relative to the body?"

## Stories Shafer Demotes
- "Crisis" / "epidemic" framing of normal phenomena
- Polling-driven narratives
- Tea-leaf readings of Fed / SCOTUS
- Conspiracy-curious framing
- "Sources close to" laundering

## Stories Shafer Elevates
- Document-based reporting
- Reporting that names sources
- Old-fashioned beat work
- Stories that puncture popular narratives
- Coverage that names other reporters' errors

## Note
Shafer's rubric is essentially "don't be credulous": newsworthiness must survive evidentiary stress-testing.
