# Schudson (1978, 2001) - Discovering the News & The Objectivity Norm

Two key works by Michael Schudson establishing the historical sociology of how "objectivity" became the central professional norm of American journalism — and what that norm dictates is and is not newsworthy.

- *Discovering the News: A Social History of American Newspapers* (Basic Books, 1978)
- "The objectivity norm in American journalism" *Journalism* 2(2): 149–170 (2001)

## What Objectivity Demands of News Selection

Schudson identifies five operational norms that define how the objectivity ideal shapes news output:

1. **Cool, Detached Tone** - Reports must be "cool, rather than emotional, in tone." Stories that cannot be told without affect are downgraded.

2. **Even-Handed Treatment** - Reports must "represent fairly each leading side in a political controversy." Stories with no oppositional framing get reframed to introduce one.

3. **No Authorial Comment** - The journalist "reports something called 'news' without commenting on it, slanting it, or shaping its formulation in any way." Stories requiring interpretation are riskier than stories that "speak for themselves."

4. **Trustworthy via Procedure** - "Facts here are not aspects of the world, but consensually validated statements about it." Newsworthiness depends on procedural defensibility, not metaphysical truth.

5. **Distrust of the Self** - Schudson calls objectivity an "ideology of the distrust of the self." Reporters' own observations and inferences are systematically discounted unless they can be externally certified.

## Five Functions of the Objectivity Norm

1. **Group Solidarity Function** - Provides a shared professional identity distinguishing journalists from PR writers, partisans, and entertainers.

2. **Hierarchical Control Function** - Allows editors to standardize and supervise reporters' work.

3. **Articulating Profession to Outsiders** - Defends journalism's claim to authority against partisan, market, and government pressures.

4. **Risk-Mitigation Function** - Reduces libel exposure and political retaliation against the outlet.

5. **Cultural Authority Function** - Legitimates news as the trusted record of public life.

## Conditions for Norm Emergence

Schudson identifies four conditions whose joint presence in late-19th / early-20th century US enabled the objectivity norm to crystallize:

1. **Self-conscious internal solidarity pursuit** - Journalists organizing as a profession.
2. **Need for hierarchical control** of reporters by editors.
3. **Need to articulate ideals** to outsiders (advertisers, regulators, audiences).
4. **Generational transmission requirement** - Codifying practice for new entrants.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Procedural eligibility filter**: Events lacking the right procedural shape (named sources, two-sided dispute, factual specifics) are demoted.

- **Bias toward institutional events**: Because objectivity requires authoritative source citations, news skews toward events covered by named officials.

- **Contested-fact aversion**: Stories where the underlying fact is contested without an authority adjudicating tend to be deferred or framed as "controversy" rather than reported as fact.

- **The American specificity**: Schudson notes this norm is uniquely strong in US journalism, where European traditions remain more openly political — predicts cross-national variance in newsworthiness criteria.
