# Community Journalism — Rural / Small-Town News Values

Distinct newsworthiness criteria for community / hyperlocal newspapers. A genre of its own.

## Definition

"Locally-oriented, professional news coverage that focuses on city neighborhoods, individual suburbs or small towns, rather than metropolitan, state, national or world news."

## Coverage Focus (Hyperlocal Newsworthiness)

- City council meetings
- School board meetings
- Community events
- Honor roll students
- Local high school sports
- Vandalism and minor crime
- Zoning issues
- Births, marriages, deaths
- Local business openings/closings
- Church news
- Volunteer recognition
- Letters from local readers
- Local opinion / editorials

## Distinguishing News Values

### Connection-Focused (Distinct from Standard News Values)
- **Civic role** — Build community welfare
- **Affirm community** — Strengthen "us-ness"
- **Celebrate locals** — Positive coverage of neighbors
- **Hold local power accountable** without destroying relationships
- **Participate in community life** — Editor goes to the same church/school

### Personal Connection
- Coverage hits close to home for journalist too
- Different from traditional norm of distance
- Reporter is community member
- Bias toward local pride balanced with truth

## Implicit Local Newsworthiness Criteria

1. **Hyperlocal geography** — Within commuting distance
2. **Naming neighbors** — Specific identifiable people
3. **Civic action enablement** — What can locals DO?
4. **Identity-affirming** — Who "we" are
5. **Relationship-aware** reporting
6. **Practical service** — Closures, weather, events
7. **Local milestones** — Anniversaries, retirements
8. **Local economic implications** — Jobs at major employer
9. **Local governance** — Council meetings, school board
10. **Local sports** — Team identity

## Tensions

- **Critical reporting** vs. **community relationships**
- **Professional distance** vs. **community membership**
- **Hard news** vs. **boosterism**
- **Independence** vs. **economic dependence on locals**

## Threats to Community Journalism

- News deserts (closures of weekly papers)
- Loss of advertising revenue
- Aging readership
- Professional pressures from corporate ownership
