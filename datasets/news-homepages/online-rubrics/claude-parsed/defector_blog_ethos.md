# Defector — Newsworthiness as Distinctive Voice

Defector's "we don't do anything stupid" tagline implies a strong rubric of editorial restraint and voice.

## Defector's Stated and Implied Criteria
- **Distinctive voice over neutrality** — no piece should be interchangeable with another outlet's
- **Reporter-driven assignments** — bottom-up beat ownership rather than chasing trending topics
- **No SEO-bait** — refuse stories whose only justification is search traffic
- **No outrage-cycle posts** unless the writer has something genuinely additive
- **No celebrity-statement aggregation** without reporting value
- **Sports as social phenomenon**, not just box scores

## Tests Defector Applies
- "Is this story we'd write even if it got zero traffic?"
- "Would we be the only outlet writing it this way?"
- "Is the writer the right person for this piece?"
- "Does it deserve the reader's time?"

## Demoted Categories
- Hot takes about discourse
- Listicles without reporting
- Coverage of social-media micro-controversies
- "Brands tweeted X" content

## Elevated Categories
- Long-form on overlooked subjects
- Personal essays grounded in specific knowledge
- Sports labor / business / governance reporting
- Investigations of mid-level institutions
- Cultural criticism with stakes

## Note
Defector's worker-ownership produces a distinctive rubric — newsworthiness is partly defined by what is worth a writer's labor and a reader's attention, independent of traffic metrics.
