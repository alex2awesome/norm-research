# Hicks et al. — Writing for Journalists

Routledge / Media Skills series. UK-style journalism writing textbook covering print, digital, broadcast.

## Topical Coverage

- News writing across formats
- Features writing
- Reviews
- Digital publication (shortform)
- Digital publication (longform)
- Columns and blogs
- Style and its impact
- Glossary of journalism terms

## Hicks's News Selection Criteria (from English for Journalists, Writing for Journalists)

The standard British set:

1. **Topicality** — Recency, currency
2. **Significance** — Impact on people
3. **Proximity** — Local relevance
4. **Prominence** — Notable people
5. **Conflict** — Disputes, drama
6. **Human interest** — Emotional connection
7. **Novelty** — Unusual, surprising
8. **Sex / scandal** (for tabloid context)
9. **Money** — Financial implications

## Quality Criteria for News Writing

### Lead Paragraph
- Most important fact first
- Active voice
- 25 words or fewer (often)
- Captures essence

### Story Structure
- Inverted pyramid (hard news)
- Narrative structure (features)
- Q&A (interviews)
- Listicle (some online)

### Writing Style
- Clear, simple language
- Short sentences
- Avoid jargon
- Active voice
- Concrete > abstract
- Specific > general
- Show > tell

### Verification Standards
- Multiple sources
- Named where possible
- Document trails
- Quotes accurate

## Hicks on Online Newsworthiness Adaptation

- **Shortform**: more news values applied per word
- **Longform**: narrative depth required, character-driven
- **Headlines**: search and social-friendly
- **Mobile-first** considerations

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria (per Hicks)

- **Story-shape** — fits available format (200 words, 800 words, 2000 words)
- **Headline-able** — captures interest in 8-10 words
- **First-paragraph compelling** — hooks reader in first line
- **Quote-worthy sources**
- **Visual potential** for accompanying media
- **Reader-resonant** — speaks to audience life
- **Verifiable** within deadline
