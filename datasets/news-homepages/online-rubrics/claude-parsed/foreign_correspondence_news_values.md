# Foreign Correspondence — News Values for International Reporting

Specific newsworthiness criteria for foreign news, with attention to how distance and unfamiliarity reshape standard news values.

## What Foreign Correspondents Cover

- National and regional security
- Foreign policy
- Elections in covered country
- Conflict and wars
- Sporting events
- Cultural developments
- Development and living standards
- Diaspora connections

## News Values for Foreign News (per Galtung & Ruge tradition + practice)

1. **Relevance to home country** — How does it affect "us"?
2. **Cultural proximity** — Familiar values, religion, language
3. **Geographic proximity** to bureau
4. **Eliteness of nations** — Major powers covered more
5. **Negativity** — Coups, disasters, violence dominate
6. **Magnitude** — Death tolls, scale
7. **Conflict** — Wars, protests, political crisis
8. **Surprise** — Unexpected developments in stable regions
9. **Diaspora connections** — Audience identifies with people there
10. **Long-term trend significance** — Bigger geopolitical pattern
11. **Visual potential** — Dramatic images for TV

## Biases of Foreign Coverage (per critics)

- **Over-coverage of bureau-adjacent countries** — Stories near where reporters are stationed
- **Under-coverage of "global south"** unless catastrophic
- **Episodic vs. thematic** — Events covered, structures not
- **Western-frame interpretation**
- **Parachute journalism** — Limited time, surface coverage
- **Coup-and-crisis only** in some regions

## Hannerz's Concept (Foreign News, 2004)

Hannerz studied foreign correspondents and identified:
- "**Worldview**" of correspondents shapes coverage
- **Networks** of correspondents pass tips
- **Stringers** vs. **staff correspondents** different incentives
- **Hub bureaus** dominate (London, Hong Kong, etc.)

## News Selection Pressures Specific to Foreign

- Visa / access restrictions
- Translation challenges
- Logistics and safety
- Bureau location bias
- Editor's distance from context
- Audience interest assumptions
