# Donsbach (2004) - Psychology of News Decisions: Factors Behind Journalists' Professional Behavior

Wolfgang Donsbach's psychological framework for news decisions, addressing what he saw as a gap in the news-values literature: existing approaches (Galtung & Ruge, Tuchman, Gans) describe *what* gets selected but not the *cognitive processes* by which individual journalists make selections. Published in *Journalism* 5(2): 131–157.

The argument: News selection is fundamentally a *psychological* act of judgment under uncertainty. Two universal cognitive needs explain the patterns observed:

## Two Master Psychological Needs Driving News Decisions

1. **Need for Social Validation of Perceptions** - Journalists, like everyone, are uncertain about reality and seek confirmation that their perceptions are shared. They achieve validation by:
   - Checking what other journalists are covering ("pack journalism").
   - Citing authoritative sources whose definition becomes "the" definition.
   - Following established beats and topics where consensus exists.

2. **Need to Preserve Existing Predispositions** - Journalists prefer information consistent with their pre-existing beliefs and avoid information that would require updating priors. Selection is shaped by:
   - Confirmation bias in story pursuit.
   - Source preferences for those who confirm worldviews.
   - Frame inheritance that perpetuates initial interpretations.

## Five Sources of Influence on News Decisions

1. **News Factors (Object Properties)** - Galtung-Ruge-style structural features of events.

2. **Institutional Objectives** - Outlet's commercial, political, public-service mission.

3. **Manipulative Power of PR Sources** - Strategic communicators who pre-package newsworthy material.

4. **Subjective Beliefs of Journalists** - Personal political attitudes, ideological priors, professional norms.

5. **Underlying Psychological Processes** (Donsbach's contribution) - Validation-seeking and predisposition-preserving cognition that shapes how the other four factors are processed.

## Implicit News-Selection Criteria from the Psychological Framework

1. **Pack Convergence** - A story being covered by other outlets is itself a news value (validates the reporter's perception).

2. **Source Authority** - Stories with named, institutionally-backed sources are favored because they reduce reporter uncertainty (anticipates Tuchman 1972's objectivity ritual at the cognitive level).

3. **Predisposition Compatibility** - Stories that confirm rather than challenge journalists' worldviews are easier to compose.

4. **Frame Familiarity** - Stories that fit recognized narrative templates feel more "real" and reportable.

5. **Reporter Self-Image Compatibility** - Stories that match the reporter's professional identity (investigative, beat, soft-news) are pursued more readily.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Cognitive foundation for news values**: Donsbach explains *why* the empirically observed news values work — they map onto general psychological needs.

- **Predicts pack journalism / herd behavior**: Outlets converge on the same stories not just for competitive reasons but because covering stories others cover validates the reporter's reality-judgment.

- **Predicts confirmation bias in source selection**: Journalists return to sources that confirm priors and avoid those that challenge them — a major source of structural bias not captured by news-values frameworks.

- **Operationalizable via cross-outlet correlation**: Story selection can be modeled as partly driven by what *other outlets* are covering — testable on homepage data.

- **Explains norm-behavior gap**: Editors verbally reject metric-driven selection (Welbers et al. 2016) because it conflicts with professional self-image; nevertheless behavior is shaped by the same psychological needs that metrics tap.
