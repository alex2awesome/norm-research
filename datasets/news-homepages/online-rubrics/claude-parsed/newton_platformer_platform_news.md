# Platformer (Casey Newton) — Newsworthiness for Platform / Tech Coverage

Casey Newton's daily framing of what counts as a "must-read" tech-and-democracy story.

## Newton's Front-Page Tests
- **Trust & safety implications** — does this affect how billions experience platforms?
- **Power shift** — who gains / loses control?
- **Precedent setting** — first-of-its-kind action by platform / regulator
- **Internal dissent** — leaks revealing employee unease
- **Content moderation tensions** — speech vs. safety vs. revenue
- **AI-and-society** — model behavior with downstream user impact

## Newton's Stated Selection Criteria (from intros to issues)
- "What did everyone in tech wake up thinking about?"
- "Which decision today will be cited in five years?"
- "Where did the consensus shift?"
- "Where did a platform act like a state?"

## Stories Platformer Demotes
- Product launches without behavior change
- Incremental quarterly earnings (unless thesis-breaking)
- Personality drama without policy stakes
- Tech-press groupthink takes

## Stories Platformer Elevates
- Researcher findings on platform harm
- Internal documents (Facebook Papers style)
- Whistleblower disclosures
- Court rulings reshaping platform liability
- New regulatory frameworks (EU DSA, UK OSB)
- Election-integrity related platform decisions

## Note
Newton's rubric implicitly asserts: tech news is most newsworthy when it intersects with democratic, civic, or rights-bearing infrastructure.
