# NYT Book Review — What Books Get Reviewed and How

The NYT Book Review's selection criteria for books and the standards for reviews.

## What Gets Reviewed (Selection Newsworthiness)

NYT receives ~1000+ books per week. Selection factors include:

1. **Major publisher** — established imprints prioritized
2. **Established author** — known names get reviewed
3. **Debut buzz** — much-anticipated first books
4. **Prize potential** — likely award contender
5. **Cultural moment** alignment
6. **Subject significance** — important topic
7. **Original argument / approach** — fresh angle
8. **Cross-genre crossover** appeal
9. **Translation events** — major international author into English
10. **Posthumous releases** of significant figures
11. **Genre-defining** works
12. **Diverse voices** — increasing focus
13. **Bestseller / commercial significance**

## Review Standards

### Good Reviews Include
- **Engagement** — emotional + intellectual
- **Depth and context** — what else is on the subject; what writer's done before
- **Specific examples and quotations** from the book
- **Style description** — author's voice
- **Specific judgment** — what works, what doesn't
- **Worth-it determination** — should reader read this

### Common Mistakes
- Conflating review with book report
- Plot summary substituted for analysis
- Spoilers
- Generic praise
- Generic criticism without examples

## Implicit Book Review Newsworthiness

- **Author prominence** drives placement
- **Topical importance** of subject
- **Originality of argument** in nonfiction
- **Craft / style** in fiction
- **Cultural conversation contribution**
- **Place in genre / oeuvre**
- **Reviewer qualifications** matter
- **Length matched to significance**

## Reviewer Selection
- Subject matter expertise
- Quality of past reviewing
- Independence from author (no friends)
- Diverse voices among reviewers

## Format
- Long lead reviews (front of book)
- Briefer reviews (sections)
- Capsule reviews (paperback row)
- Editor's Choice list
- Year-end lists
