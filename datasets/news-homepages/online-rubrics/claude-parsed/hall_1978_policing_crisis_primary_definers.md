# Hall, Critcher, Jefferson, Clarke & Roberts (1978) - Policing the Crisis: Mugging, the State and Law and Order

The classic Birmingham CCCS analysis of how a moral panic over "mugging" was constructed in 1970s Britain. Argues that news selection systematically reproduces the worldview of authorized institutional spokespersons — the "primary definers" — and that this is itself a structural source of newsworthiness bias.

Published by Macmillan, 1978. The single most influential statement of the *primary definer* concept in news sociology.

## The Primary Definer Concept

A "primary definer" is an actor whose institutional position licenses them to be the *first* to define a situation in the news — police chiefs, judges, government ministers, recognized experts. Reporters' objectivity rituals (Tuchman 1972) require named, authoritative sources; primary definers reliably supply them. The result:

- The primary definer's framing becomes the *initial frame* of the story.
- Subsequent voices appear as *secondary* responses to that frame.
- Counter-frames must labor to displace the established frame; the primary definer's framing wins by default.

## Five Newsworthiness Implications of the Primary-Definer System

1. **Institutional Anchoring** - Events become reportable when an authorized institutional actor speaks about them. Events not yet processed by primary definers struggle to enter the news.

2. **Frame Inheritance** - Once a primary definer labels an event (e.g., "mugging," "riot," "extremist attack," "epidemic"), that label propagates across coverage; alternative labels look partisan.

3. **Source Hierarchy** - Police > activists; ministers > NGOs; CEOs > workers. The hierarchy of who can speak first directly determines whose perspective shapes news.

4. **Statistical Authority** - Numbers from primary definer institutions (police statistics, agency reports) get reported as fact; non-institutional counts must justify themselves.

5. **Moral Panic Amplification Cycle**:
   - Primary definers identify a "problem" (e.g., a few muggings).
   - Media report it using primary-definer frames.
   - Public concern rises; politicians demand "action."
   - Police prioritize the issue, generating more recorded cases.
   - Media report the rise in cases as confirmation of the problem.
   - Loop continues, manufacturing a "crisis."

## Six Stages of Moral Panic (Cohen-Hall Synthesis)

1. **Identification** - Primary definers name a new threat.
2. **Stylization** - Media stereotype the threat actors ("muggers," "delinquents").
3. **Symbolization** - Specific symbols come to stand in for the threat.
4. **Distortion** - Statistics and incidents are reported in inflammatory ways.
5. **Amplification** - Ongoing coverage amplifies the perceived threat.
6. **Demand for Action** - Public/political pressure for state response.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Crime/risk story bias**: Topics with active primary-definer apparatus (police, public health, security agencies) generate steady news flow; topics without such apparatus do not.

- **Counterhegemonic stories pay an evidentiary tax**: Reporting that contradicts primary-definer framing requires extra sourcing, slowing or suppressing it.

- **News-cycle entrenchment**: Once a frame is established, subsequent coverage reproduces it; novel frames are filtered out.

- **Empirically testable on homepage data**: Stories using primary-definer terminology in headlines (police-supplied labels, agency-supplied statistics) should achieve higher placement than stories using alternative terminology.
