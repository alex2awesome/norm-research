# What Makes News? 7 Key Criteria for Newsworthiness

## 1. Timeliness
How recently an event occurred. The more recent a story, the more interesting to readers.
*Example*: A traffic accident is newsworthy immediately but becomes outdated within days.

## 2. Proximity
Location-based relevance operating on two levels:
- **Geographical**: Local events directly affecting an audience's daily life
- **Psychological**: Cultural or emotional closeness that transcends geography
*Example*: A water main break disrupting traffic in your city vs. the same incident in a distant country.

## 3. Impact
Scale of effect measured by how many people are affected.
*Example*: A pothole affecting dozens vs. a collapsed bridge affecting hundreds of thousands.

## 4. Prominence
Status of people or organizations involved.
*Example*: Throwing a shoe at a private citizen goes unreported; throwing one at the President becomes international news.

## 5. The Unusual
Events breaking from expected patterns; "If a dog bites a man, that's not news. But if a man bites a dog, that is news."
*Example*: Winning the lottery twice; record-defying achievements.

## 6. Conflict
Strife and power struggles creating natural narrative structure with opposing parties and outcomes.
*Example*: Wars, elections, legal battles, protests, sports.

## 7. Human Interest
Stories connecting through emotion and empathy rather than scale or status.
*Example*: Rags-to-riches stories, overcoming adversity, reunions, acts of generosity. Can succeed despite lacking timeliness or proximity due to emotional resonance.
