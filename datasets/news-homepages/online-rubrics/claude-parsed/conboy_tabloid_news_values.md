# Tabloid Britain — Conboy's News Values for Popular Journalism

Examines how British tabloids construct community identity through distinctive news values that diverge from broadsheet conventions.

## Tabloid News Values / Rhetorical Conventions

1. **Anti-establishment slang** — Linguistic register signaling skepticism of elites
2. **Familiar first names** — Calling subjects by first names (vs. broadsheet "Mr./Mrs./surname")
3. **Rhetoric of violent encounter** — Depicting events as battles, clashes
4. **Binary good vs. evil framing** — Lists, juxtapositions, moral polarities
5. **Cross-page headlines** — Visual dominance
6. **Salacious storylines** — Sex, scandal, transgression
7. **Hyperbolic clashes of personalities** — Conflict centered on individuals
8. **Outsiders vs. insiders** themes
9. **Women as a thematic focus** (often objectified)
10. **Celebrity** — Heavy use of famous names
11. **Nostalgia** — Reference to British past
12. **Reactionary moralism** in working-class rhetoric
13. **Imagined community of readers** — Stories serving collective identity

## Tabloid vs. Broadsheet Newsworthiness

Tabloids weight: human emotion, transgression, celebrity, moral judgment, vivid drama.
Broadsheets weight: institutional importance, policy implications, complexity, analysis.
