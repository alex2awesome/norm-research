# Yamamoto — Community Newspaper Use Promotes Social Cohesion

Newspaper Research Journal, 2011. Identifies what local/community newspapers cover and why such coverage matters for social cohesion.

## Core Finding

Community newspaper reading correlates with respondents' sense of social cohesion. Local newspapers play a unique role in binding communities.

## Implicit Local/Community Newsworthiness Criteria

### Civic-Building Stories
- Local government meetings
- School board decisions
- Local elections
- Tax/budget issues
- Public health updates

### Community-Identity Stories
- Local festivals, parades
- High school sports
- Hometown person profiles
- Local milestones (anniversaries, retirements)
- Births, weddings, obituaries

### Local Economic Stories
- New businesses
- Layoffs at major employers
- Real estate
- Local agriculture / industry

### Practical Service
- Weather affecting locals
- Road closures
- Power outages
- School closings
- Crime in the neighborhood

### Hyperlocal Pride
- Achievements by locals (national recognition)
- Hometown sports team
- Local heroes

## Why These Matter (per Yamamoto)

- Increases interpersonal trust within community
- Enhances belonging
- Promotes participation in local civic life
- Reduces social isolation
- Strengthens "we" feeling

## Implicit Local Newsworthiness Criteria

- **Geographic specificity** — Hyperlocal
- **Civic-action enabling** — Information citizens need to participate
- **Identity-affirming** — Stories of "us"
- **Trust-building** — Naming neighbors, profiling locals
- **Practical utility** — Information you can act on
- **Community-celebrating** — Positive coverage of locals

## Implications

Community newspapers should be evaluated by different criteria than national outlets. Newsworthiness must include cohesion-promoting and identity-affirming stories that may seem trivial to national editors.

## Citation
Yamamoto, M. (2011). Community Newspaper Use Promotes Social Cohesion. Newspaper Research Journal, 32(1), 19-33.
