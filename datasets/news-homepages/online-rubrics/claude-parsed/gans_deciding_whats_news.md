# Gans — Deciding What's News

Pantheon Books, 1979. Ethnographic study of CBS Evening News, NBC Nightly News, Newsweek, and Time over 10 years.

## Eight "Enduring News Values" (Gans)

Gans identified eight enduring values that shape American news selection:

1. **Ethnocentrism** — American-centric viewpoint; foreign coverage filtered through US interest

2. **Altruistic Democracy** — Implicit support for democratic ideals; corruption as the central evil

3. **Responsible Capitalism** — Market system valued, but with responsibility to public; greed framed negatively

4. **Small-Town Pastoralism** — Nostalgia for small communities; suspicion of urban modernization

5. **Individualism** — Heroes/villains as individuals; institutional analysis less common

6. **Moderatism** — Centrism; suspicion of ideological extremes (left or right)

7. **Social Order** — Disorder and disruption framed as bad; crime/protest depicted negatively

8. **National Leadership** — Reverence for the presidency and national institutions

## Plus Operational News Values

- **Importance** of an event
- **Interest** to audience
- **Impact** on people
- **Government action** as default newsworthy
- **Conflict / threat** to social order

## Sources of News (Hierarchy)

Gans identified that journalists rely heavily on:
- Government officials (especially executive branch)
- Industry spokespeople
- Established think tanks
- Already-recognized experts
- "Knowns" over "unknowns"

## Implication

News content reflects **journalists' middle-class values** plus **institutional source dominance**. Journalists "respect authority, want to be liked, see the unfamiliar as vaguely threatening."
