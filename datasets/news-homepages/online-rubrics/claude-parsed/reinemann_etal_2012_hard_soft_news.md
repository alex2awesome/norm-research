# Reinemann, Stanyer, Scherr & Legnante (2012) - Hard and Soft News: A Review of Concepts, Operationalizations and Key Findings

Influential review article in *Journalism* 13(2): 221–239 that resolved 30 years of conceptual confusion about "hard" vs. "soft" news by proposing a three-dimensional measurement scheme.

The contribution: Hard/soft is not a binary distinction but a continuum on three orthogonal dimensions. Provides the first standardized operationalization that makes the construct comparable across studies.

## Three Dimensions of Hard vs. Soft News

### 1. Topic Dimension

Whether the matter covered is *politically substantive*:
- **Harder**: Politics, government, policy, economy at the macro level, foreign affairs, public administration.
- **Softer**: Lifestyle, entertainment, sports (mostly), celebrity, human interest, oddities.

### 2. Focus Dimension

What aspect of the event is emphasized:
- **Harder Focus**: Societal relevance, collective consequences, structural causes, policy implications.
- **Softer Focus**: Individual/personal aspects, emotional impact, private lives, personal experiences.

### 3. Style Dimension

How the topic is presented:
- **Harder Style**: Impersonal, distanced narration; technical/policy language; statistics; multiple expert sources; inverted pyramid.
- **Softer Style**: Personalized, narrative, emotional tone; everyday vocabulary; anecdotes; single character focus; storytelling structure.

## A Story's Position on the Hard-Soft Continuum

Each story can be coded independently on each dimension, producing a 3-vector. A "hard news story" need not be hard on all three dimensions; conversely, a story about politics (hard topic) can be presented in a soft style (personalization of a politician's family life).

## Operational News-Selection Criteria from the Framework

1. **Hard Topic Bias** - Outlets oriented toward serious / quality journalism prefer politically substantive topics.

2. **Soft Focus Premium** - Even within hard topics, soft-focus framing (individual case, emotional angle) often dominates because it satisfies engagement and personalization news values.

3. **Style-Topic Mismatch as Strategic Choice** - "Soft style on a hard topic" (e.g., explaining policy via a single family's experience) is increasingly common — combines accountability with engagement.

4. **Trajectory Effects** - Many studies show secular drift toward softer focus and style over time (tabloidization), even within elite outlets.

5. **Outlet Identity Anchor** - Outlets brand themselves on a hard-soft profile; selection reproduces brand identity.

## Cross-Reference Note: Hard vs. Soft News in Earlier Tradition

- **Tuchman 1973**: Identified hard/soft as one of five typifications, but ambiguously defined.
- **Patterson 2000**: "Doing Well and Doing Good" — measured soft-news drift in US TV news.
- **Boczkowski 2010**: Found soft-news has higher imitation rate online.
- **Welbers et al. 2016**: Audience metrics influence soft-news selection more than hard.

Reinemann et al. provide the integrative framework that allows these previously incompatible findings to be compared.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Operationalizable for homepage models**: A story can be scored on the three dimensions and used as a feature in placement-prediction models.

- **Predicts placement asymmetries**: Hard-topic stories tend to receive higher placement on broadsheet front pages even if engagement is lower; soft-focus stories dominate sidebars and lower-fold positions.

- **Resolves "tabloidization debate"**: The three-dimensional model lets researchers test which specific dimensions are softening (often style softens while topic remains hard).

- **Standardized measurement** enables cross-national and cross-outlet comparison; previously each study used idiosyncratic codings.
