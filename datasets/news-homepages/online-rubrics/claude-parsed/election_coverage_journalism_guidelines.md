# Election Coverage — Newsworthiness Criteria for Political Journalism

Synthesis of guidelines from Thomson Foundation, OSCE, Carter Center, RSF, Council of Europe, and other election observation/journalism resources.

## Three Foundational Rights

1. **Voters' right to be informed** about candidates and platforms
2. **Contestants' right to communicate** platforms with non-discriminatory media access
3. **Media's right** to inform and express opinion on public-interest issues

## Coverage Criteria

### Equality vs. Equity Frameworks
- **Equality**: each party/candidate receives same time/space
- **Equity**: proportional access based on uniform criteria (parliamentary seats, candidate count)

### Newsworthiness Factors for Election Stories
1. **Voter relevance** — affects voting decision
2. **Candidate viability** — likely to win
3. **Issue salience** — voter-important topics
4. **Newsworthy event** — debate, scandal, gaffe, endorsement
5. **Platform clarity** — explaining what candidates promise
6. **Voting logistics** — when, where, how
7. **Voter suppression / fraud allegations**
8. **Polling significance**
9. **Money in politics** — fundraising disclosure
10. **Down-ballot impact**

## Editorial Standards

### Distinct Categorization
- News stories
- Analyses
- Opinions / editorials
- Paid political advertisements (clearly labeled)

### Independence Standards
- Journalists avoid personal political activity (donations, petitions)
- Disclose any past political work
- Editorial firewall from owner's preferences

### Truth-and-Fairness
- Fact-check claims (especially candidate statements)
- Don't amplify lies as both-sides
- Provide context for claims
- Update when facts emerge

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for Politics

- **Power transitions** — who's gaining, losing
- **Conflict / horse race** (often over-covered)
- **Personality drama** (often over-covered)
- **Policy substance** (often under-covered)
- **Process integrity**
- **Suppression and access**
- **Money flows**
- **Polling shifts**
- **Endorsements**
- **Scandal / opposition research**
- **Voter sentiment**
- **Local race significance**

## Critique
Election coverage often privileges horse-race and gaffes over substantive policy. Reform calls for:
- Issue-driven coverage
- Voter-needs-first framing
- Avoid amplifying disinformation
- Focus on what matters for governance
