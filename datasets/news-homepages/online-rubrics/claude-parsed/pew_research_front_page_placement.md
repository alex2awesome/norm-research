# Pew Research — Front Page Placement Patterns

Empirical analysis of how stories framed differently end up on front pages of US newspapers.

## Findings

### Competitive Frames Receive Prominent Placement
- Stories emphasizing **conflict, winning/losing, and wrongdoing** comprise **35% of lead stories** despite representing only 31% of all stories.
- Implication: Conflict-framed stories disproportionately reach front pages.

### Explanatory Frames Get Lower Placement
- Stories using **historical, process, and trend frames** appear in lead position just **8% of the time**, though they are 13% of all stories.
- These frames occupy 17% of lower-page positions.
- Implication: Explanatory journalism is undervalued in placement.

## Two Possible Explanations

1. **Importance-based selection** — Editors view competitive framing as more urgent.
2. **Audience appeal** — "A competitively framed story is more likely to appeal to readers and therefore should be near the top."

## Key Conclusion
"Stories framed around competitiveness ... are placed in such a way as to convey greater news value."

## Implicit Newsworthiness Insights
- Story frame matters as much as topic
- Conflict, contest, wrongdoing frames are systematically privileged
- Trend/process/explanatory stories systematically de-prioritized for placement
- Editors equate framing with reader interest
