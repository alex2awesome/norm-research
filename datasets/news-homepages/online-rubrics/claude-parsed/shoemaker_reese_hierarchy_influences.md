# Shoemaker & Reese - Hierarchy of Influences Model

From "Mediating the Message: Theories of Influences on Mass Media Content" (1991, 1996, 2014). One of the most cited frameworks for understanding what shapes news content. Less a list of news values than a meta-framework for what determines them.

## The 5 Levels of Influence (Macro to Micro)

### 1. Individual Level
- Personal characteristics, beliefs, values, and roles of journalists
- Demographic background, education, religion, political leanings
- Professional values internalized through socialization
- Personal ethics

### 2. Media Routines Level
- Practices and norms developed to manage workload
- News values themselves are routines (Galtung-Ruge factors)
- Beat structures
- Reliance on official sources
- Inverted pyramid structure
- Deadlines and news cycles
- Source-reporter relationships

### 3. Organizational Level
- Internal newsroom structure, ownership, business model
- Editorial policy of the outlet
- Position of journalist within hierarchy
- Newsroom culture and routines
- Resource allocation

### 4. Social Institutional Level (formerly "extramedia")
- External institutions: government, advertisers, audiences, interest groups, PR
- Sources external to the newsroom
- Market pressures
- Legal/regulatory environment

### 5. Social System Level
- Ideology, culture, political/economic system
- Society-wide power structures
- National media system traits

## Implications for Newsworthiness

A given story's selection cannot be explained by news values alone. It is jointly determined by:
- The reporter's individual judgment (Level 1)
- Routinized news values like timeliness/proximity/conflict (Level 2)
- Editorial agenda and resource constraints (Level 3)
- Source pressure, market demand, official statements (Level 4)
- Ideology/culture defining what counts as a problem (Level 5)

## Citation

Shoemaker, P. J., & Reese, S. D. (1996, 2014). Mediating the Message: Theories of Influences on Mass Media Content. New York: Longman / Routledge.
