# Weather News Coverage — NOAA Severe Weather Criteria

NOAA's classification system functions as a rubric for when weather becomes news.

## Severe Weather Categories That Drive Coverage

### Tornado
- **Tornado WATCH** — Storm Prediction Center: conditions favorable
- **Tornado WARNING** — Local NWS: tornado reported or radar-indicated
- **Tornado EMERGENCY** — Highest threat: powerful tornado confirmed, catastrophic damage

### Thunderstorm
- "Severe" requires:
  - Hail ≥ 1 inch diameter
  - Wind gusts ≥ 58 mph
  - Tornado
- Categories: marginal / slight / enhanced / moderate / high

### Hurricane
- **Tropical Storm Watch / Warning** (39+ mph)
- **Hurricane Watch / Warning** (74+ mph)
- Saffir-Simpson categories 1-5

### Flooding
- Flash Flood Watch / Warning / Emergency
- River Flood Watch / Warning

### Winter Weather
- Winter Storm Watch / Warning
- Blizzard Warning
- Ice Storm Warning
- Wind Chill Watch / Warning

## Implicit Weather Newsworthiness Criteria

### Threshold Triggers (Magnitude)
- Tornado of any strength
- Hurricane Cat 3+ (major)
- 1+ inch hail
- 58+ mph winds
- Snow > 6 inches in 24h (varies)
- Wind chill < -20°F
- Heat index > 100°F sustained

### Geographic Factors
- Population in path
- Major cities affected
- Power grid impact
- Agricultural impact
- School / business closures

### Impact Factors
- Loss of life
- Property damage
- Displacement of people
- Economic disruption
- Infrastructure damage (bridges, power)
- Crop loss
- Coastal erosion

### Visual / Drama Factors
- Dramatic visuals (funnel cloud, storm surge)
- Live cam footage
- Driver POV
- Damage photos
- Hero rescues

### Anniversary / Comparison
- "Worst since...", "first time...", "X years since..."
- Comparison to historical events

## When Weather Becomes Front-Page News
- Watches/warnings active for major metro
- Loss of life
- Mass power outages
- Coastal evacuations
- Visual spectacle
- Disrupted major event (game, election)
- Climate context (record-breaking)
