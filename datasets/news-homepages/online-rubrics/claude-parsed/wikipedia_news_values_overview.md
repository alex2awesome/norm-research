# News Values - Wikipedia Comprehensive Overview

A consolidated overview of all major academic frameworks for newsworthiness.

## Core News Values Across Frameworks

### Galtung & Ruge (1965) - 12 Factors
Frequency, Threshold, Unambiguity, Meaningfulness, Consonance, Unexpectedness, Continuity, Composition, Reference to Elite Nations, Reference to Elite People, Personalization, Negativity.

### Process-related Values (later additions)
- **Continuity**: Ongoing stories gain momentum through media infrastructure
- **Composition**: Stories compete for space; editors balance content
- **Competition**: Commercial rivalry between outlets
- **Co-option**: Marginal stories gain coverage if related to a major narrative
- **Prefabrication**: Pre-written marginal stories may be selected
- **Predictability**: Pre-scheduled events more likely to be covered
- **Story Impact**: Shareability metrics, reader engagement
- **Time Constraints**: Deadline pressures
- **Logistics**: Staff/technical resources determine coverage feasibility
- **Data/Facticity**: Stories backed by raw data and figures favored

### Harcup & O'Neill (2001) Additions
- Prominence (well-known figures, celebrities)
- Good News (positive stories)
- Newspaper Agenda (organization's own priorities)

### Harcup & O'Neill (2017) Digital-Era Additions
- Shareability (clicks, social spread)
- Immediacy (real-time coverage)
- Audio-visuals (arresting visual content)

### Bednarek & Caple - Discursive Approach
- News values as constructed through discourse
- Relevance, Impact, Meaningfulness as primary categories

## Common Underlying Categories

1. **Impact** - effect on people's lives
2. **Timeliness** - recency
3. **Prominence** - status of involved actors
4. **Proximity** - geographic/psychological closeness
5. **The unusual / Bizarreness / Novelty**
6. **Conflict**
7. **Currency**
8. **Human interest**
9. **Negativity / Bad news**
10. **Visualness** (in digital/TV)

## Critiques and Caveats

- "There is no end to lists of news criteria"
- Multiple competing frameworks with overlap and disagreement
- News values are culturally and organizationally variable
- Audience-driven metrics are reshaping editorial values in the digital age
