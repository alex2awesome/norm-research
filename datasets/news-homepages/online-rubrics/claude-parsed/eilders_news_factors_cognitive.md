# Eilders — News Factors and News Decisions

Communications, 2006. German research tradition on news values, drawing on cognitive psychology. Reframes news factors as relevance indicators that work on both journalists and audiences.

## Core Argument

News factors are **collective relevance indicators**. They work because:
1. Journalists use them to select
2. Audiences use them to attend
3. Both are using the same psychological mechanisms

## Three Psychological Explanations for News Factor Effectiveness

### 1. Evolutionary Perspective
- Humans have learned more or less automatic reactions to stimuli that might be threatening
- News factors like negativity, conflict, surprise tap evolved attention systems
- Threat detection bias

### 2. General Psychological Mechanisms
- Familiar objects, persons, or events recognized faster
- Mental matrix already exists for known entities
- News factors like consonance, eliteness, personalization tap recognition

### 3. Social Relevance
- Reach (number affected) signals social significance
- Controversy signals collective concern
- Audience attributes relevance to widely-relevant events

## The German Research Tradition

Building on Galtung-Ruge, German scholars developed:
- **Relevance theory** of news factors
- Quantitative content analysis methods
- Cross-national comparisons
- Audience-side studies (do audiences also weight these factors?)

## Key News Factors (German Tradition)

1. **Frequency**
2. **Threshold**
3. **Personalization**
4. **Eliteness** (status of involved persons)
5. **Reach** (geographic and demographic)
6. **Relevance** (consequences)
7. **Controversy** / Conflict
8. **Aggression** (use of force)
9. **Damage**
10. **Success**
11. **Surprise**
12. **Continuity**
13. **Establishment** (institutional involvement)
14. **Influence** (power of actors)
15. **Ethnocentrism** (own-country bias)
16. **Topical relevance**

## Implications

- News factors are **dual-process** — work on journalists AND audiences
- Aligns selection with audience expectation
- Explains why news values are remarkably stable across cultures
- Newsworthiness has cognitive-psychological foundations
