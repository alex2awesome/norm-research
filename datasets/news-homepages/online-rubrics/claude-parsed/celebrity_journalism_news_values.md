# Celebrity / Entertainment Journalism — News Values

The rise of celebrity coverage as a distinct news values cluster, increasingly cross-cutting all media tiers.

## Two Core Harcup-O'Neill Categories

1. **Celebrity** — "Stories concerning people who are already famous"
2. **Entertainment** — "Stories concerning sex, showbusiness, human interest, animals, an unfolding drama, or offering opportunities for humorous treatment, entertaining photographs or witty headlines"

## Celebrity News as Hybrid Genre

- Combines entertainment characteristics with journalism conventions
- Pervaded all types of news outlets
- Even quality press now runs celebrity content
- Tabloids highest celebrity ratio (The Sun leads UK)

## Distinct Newsworthiness Criteria for Celebrity News

### Subject Selection
1. **A-list celebrity** event (any event)
2. **B-list scandal** (mostly scandal that drives coverage)
3. **Reality TV / influencer** events
4. **Dating / relationship** news
5. **Birth / death / marriage / divorce** of famous
6. **Public meltdowns** / arrests
7. **Red carpet / event** appearances
8. **Social media drama**
9. **Cultural product launches** (music, film, book)
10. **Brand / endorsement** changes
11. **Political activity** by celebrities
12. **Charitable / philanthropic** activity

### Story Treatment
- **Personalized framing** — emotional close-up
- **Visual prominence** — paparazzi photos, red carpet
- **Quote heavy** — celebrity in own words
- **Speculation tolerance** — gossip framed as news
- **Anonymous source acceptance** — "friends say"
- **Body, fashion, appearance** focus
- **Family / relationship** details
- **Dramatic narrative** structure

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for Celebrity Stories

- **Fame level** of subject (A/B/C list)
- **Audience identification** with celebrity
- **Photo availability** (especially paparazzi)
- **Conflict / drama** dimension
- **Surprise** factor (famous person doing unexpected)
- **Cultural moment** alignment
- **Social media trending** signal
- **Relationship updates** (couples, breakups)
- **Body / appearance** changes (weight, age, surgery)
- **Personal life** revelations
- **Brand / product** tie-ins
- **Political / activist** statements

## Critique
- Crowds out substantive news
- Personalizes systemic issues
- Reinforces gender stereotypes (women's bodies focus)
- Encourages parasocial relationships
- Ethical concerns (privacy, mental health)

## Defense
- Reaches mass audiences
- Cultural significance not trivial
- Celebrities are public figures
- Reflects audience interest
