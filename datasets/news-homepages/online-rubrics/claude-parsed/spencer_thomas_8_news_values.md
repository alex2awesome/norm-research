# News Values — Owen Spencer-Thomas

Compiled by Owen Spencer-Thomas, former BBC and ITV journalist. A widely-cited industry-friendly summary used in journalism training.

## 8 Typical News Values

1. **Impact** — "The more people involved in an event, the stronger the story." Examples: protests, tax increases affecting most citizens.

2. **Timeliness** — Recent events are newsworthy. Time horizon depends on the medium: TV news = past half-hour; monthly magazine = past 30 days.

3. **Prominence** — Well-known people and famous locations attract media attention even for trivial actions; audiences feel personal connections.

4. **Proximity (Closer to Home)** — Local events get more coverage. Regional media prioritize stories within their broadcast areas.

5. **Conflict** — Stories involving disputes, violence, or tragedy generate interest.

6. **Unexpectedness** — Unusual or bizarre events deviating from norms. "Dog bites man is not news. Man bites dog is."

7. **Currency / Trending** — Stories already under public spotlight gain newsworthiness through social media and contemporary relevance.

8. **Human Interest** — "People are interested in people." Stories about emotions, personal concerns (health, housing, work), and relatable experiences resonate strongly.

## Key Principle

Stories satisfying multiple news values have greater newsworthiness than those meeting only one criterion (additivity).
