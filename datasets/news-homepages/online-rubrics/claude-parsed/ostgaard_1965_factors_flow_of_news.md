# Östgaard (1965) - Factors Influencing the Flow of News

Einar Östgaard's foundational paper, published in the same issue of the *Journal of Peace Research* (2.1: 39–63) as Galtung & Ruge's "The Structure of Foreign News." Together the two papers launched the modern empirical study of news values. Östgaard worked at PRIO (Peace Research Institute Oslo) and his framework is often paired with Galtung & Ruge's as the joint origin of news-factor research.

The argument: News flow is distorted by inherent properties of the news process plus extra-news political and economic factors. The result is a systematically biased "news picture" of the world.

## Three "Inherent" Distorting Factors

1. **Simplification** - To be transmitted quickly through the news chain, an event must be simplifiable. The greater the complexity, the lower the news value. Implications:
   - Stories with clear causation > stories requiring background.
   - Discrete events > processes.
   - Single actors > diffuse collectives.

2. **Identification** - Audiences must be able to identify with the event for it to be news.
   - **Cultural proximity** - Events from similar cultures preferred.
   - **Eliteness** - Stories about elite persons/nations help readers locate themselves.
   - **Personification** - Personalizing events makes identification easier.
   - **Status quo orientation** - Events that fit existing audience worldviews preferred over those that challenge them.

3. **Sensationalism** - News competes for attention; dramatic, shocking, conflict-laden material wins. Includes:
   - **Conflict** - Disputes, fights, wars.
   - **Drama** - High-stakes outcomes.
   - **Negativity** - Bad news travels.

## The "News Barriers"

4. **Geographical/Cultural Distance** - Events become less newsworthy as physical and cultural distance increases.

5. **Linguistic Barriers** - Events from outside the audience's language area face friction.

6. **Resource Concentration** - News organizations cluster in capitals/elite cities; events occurring elsewhere face barriers.

## Two "Foreign-to-the-Process" Factors

7. **Political Factors** - Government interest in promoting/suppressing certain events; censorship; alignment with foreign-policy priorities.

8. **Economic Factors** - Costs of newsgathering; advertising/audience pressures; ownership influence; stories that cost less to cover are preferred.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Joint origin of the news-values tradition**: Östgaard and Galtung & Ruge wrote complementary frameworks. Östgaard's *simplification, identification, sensationalism* trio is sometimes preferred for its parsimony.
- **Cumulative bias**: Each factor reduces the news picture's resemblance to reality; the cumulative effect is systematic distortion.
- **Why simple, dramatic, near events win**: Östgaard provides the first explicit theory of why this happens.
- **Predicts elite concentration**: Resource concentration + identification both push coverage toward elite people, places, and issues.
