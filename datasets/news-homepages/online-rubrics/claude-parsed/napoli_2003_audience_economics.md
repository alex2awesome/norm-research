# Napoli (2003) - Audience Economics: Media Institutions and the Audience Marketplace

Philip M. Napoli's foundational integrated framework for understanding how electronic media (TV, radio, Internet) generate and sell audiences to advertisers. Published by Columbia University Press, 2003.

The contribution: First systematic explication of the *dual-product marketplace* — media firms produce content (sold to consumers) AND audiences (sold to advertisers). News-selection logic responds to both markets, with significant implications for newsworthiness.

## The Dual-Product Marketplace

```
                MEDIA FIRMS
                    /   \
          CONTENT MARKET   AUDIENCE MARKET
              |                  |
          CONSUMERS         ADVERTISERS
                                  |
                          AUDIENCE MEASUREMENT
                                FIRMS
```

Selection optimizes simultaneously for: (a) attracting the desired audience demographic, AND (b) creating a measurable audience product to sell.

## Five Key Actors

1. **Media Firms** - Produce content for consumers, audiences for advertisers.
2. **Consumers** - Choose content; produce attention; aggregated into audiences.
3. **Advertisers** - Purchase audience attention from media firms.
4. **Audience Measurement Firms** (Nielsen, comScore, Chartbeat) - Quantify audiences; the metric becomes the operative reality.
5. **Regulators** - Set rules for audience-marketplace transactions.

## Six News-Selection Implications

1. **Audience-Targeting Premium** - Stories that attract advertisers' target demographics (typically affluent adults 18–54) receive priority placement, even if alternative stories serve broader public interest.

2. **Measurement Distortion** - Whatever the measurement firm measures becomes the implicit operational definition of audience; un-measurable engagement is invisible.

3. **Predicted-Audience-Size Bias** - News selection optimizes against audience predictions; stories with predictable large audiences favored over experimental coverage.

4. **Demographic Stratification** - Different sections, programs, and time slots target different demographic clusters; story selection for each is filtered by target audience.

5. **Audience Quality > Audience Size** - Smaller, affluent, attentive audiences command higher CPMs than larger, lower-income, fleeting audiences. Story selection skewed toward attracting "quality" audiences.

6. **Cross-Subsidy Dynamics** - News departments often subsidize entertainment programming (or vice versa); selection cycles reflect cross-subsidy economics.

## The Audience Measurement Loop

1. Stories run.
2. Audience measurement firm reports demographics.
3. Advertisers buy/decline based on report.
4. Media firms adjust selection toward measurement-favorable demographics.
5. Future stories selected to optimize next measurement cycle.

This loop means audience measurement systems *shape* news content as much as they reflect it.

## Two Audience Marketplaces

1. **Predicted Audience** - Editorial guesses about who will watch/read; drives content choices.
2. **Measured Audience** - Actual realized audience post-publication; drives revenue.

The gap between predicted and measured audiences drives editorial learning; persistent under-prediction causes outlets to update toward higher-engagement story types.

## Operational News-Value Predictions

1. **Affluent-Demographic Premium** - Topics/framings appealing to advertisers' target demographics get over-coverage.

2. **Daypart / Time-of-Day Differentiation** - Different newsworthiness criteria operate at morning vs. evening vs. weekend slots based on which audience is likely to be present.

3. **Predictable-Topic Stability** - Outlets cluster around topics with predictable audience response (sports, weather, lifestyle) for revenue stability; experimental topics struggle.

4. **Niche-Audience Outlet Differentiation** - Specialty outlets (Bloomberg, ESPN, fashion magazines) serve niches with concentrated advertiser appeal; their newsworthiness criteria differ accordingly.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Quantitative grounding for advertising-driven news selection**: Provides framework for testing how advertiser-economic structure shapes selection.

- **Predicts modern data-driven publishing**: The audience-measurement loop intensifies in the digital era when measurement is real-time.

- **Operationalizable**: Outlet-level demographic targets and ad-revenue dependencies can be used as covariates in story-placement models.

- **Bridges Hamilton 2004's economic theory with measurement infrastructure**: Where Hamilton models the demand curve, Napoli explains how that curve gets measured and operationalized.
