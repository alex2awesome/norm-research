# ProPublica — Investigative Project Criteria

Nonprofit investigative newsroom. Sets a high bar for what qualifies as a story.

## Mission

"Expose abuses of power and betrayals of the public trust by government, business, and other institutions, using the moral force of investigative journalism to spur reform through the sustained spotlighting of wrongdoing."

## Project Selection Criteria

For projects to be considered, they must be:

1. **Investigative with a clear hypothesis** of what's going wrong
2. Address: **What's wrong?**
3. Address: **Who is harmed?**
4. Address: **Who or what is responsible?**
5. Address: **What needs to be changed?**

## Disinclined to Pursue
- Exploratory or explanatory projects
- Projects still asking "whether" something is happening
- Projects without specific policy/law/regulation focus
- Projects without identified solutions or comparisons

## Preferred Project Types
- Specific policies, laws, or regulations under examination
- Identified victims / harmed parties
- Identified responsible actors
- Possible solutions identified
- Comparative examples (where solved elsewhere)

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria
- **Wrongdoing exposure** — central to selection
- **Harm to identifiable people**
- **Accountability of identifiable actors**
- **Policy/regulatory impact** as path to change
- **Solution-oriented** framing
- **Sustained focus** vs. one-off
- **Original investigation** required
- **Spurring reform** as success metric
- **Public trust** dimension

## Funding-Independent Standard
ProPublica is donor-supported but maintains strict editorial independence.
