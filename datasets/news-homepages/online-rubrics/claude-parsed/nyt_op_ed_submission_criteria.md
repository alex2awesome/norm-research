# New York Times Op-Ed — Submission Criteria

The NYT Opinion desk's selection criteria for op-eds. Functions as a rubric for opinion-newsworthiness.

## Format Requirements

- **Length**: Typically 400-1,200 words; submissions of any length considered
- **Original**: Must not have been published anywhere
- **Exclusive to The Times**

## Editorial Criteria

### Substance
- **Personal insight + broader public relevance** — both required
- **Challenges assumptions** — counter-conventional wisdom
- **Adds something new** to national conversation
- **Specific** — not generic
- **Unique perspective** — author's distinct viewpoint
- **Author's own voice** — not corporate-style

### Timeliness
- **Urgent now** — why this op-ed today?
- Tied to a peg in current news cycle
- Or seasonally appropriate

### Argumentation
- **Clear thesis**
- **Concise** — focused on one idea
- **Evidence-based** where possible
- **Persuasive structure**

## Selection Filters
- Author credibility / expertise
- Diversity of viewpoints
- Coverage gap filling
- Conversation-starting potential
- Voice / craft quality

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for Opinion

1. **Provocation** — challenges status quo
2. **Personal stake** — author has skin in the game
3. **Public significance** — beyond personal
4. **Counter-narrative** — contrarian or fresh angle
5. **Topicality** — current event hook
6. **Original argument** — not warmed-over
7. **Voice** — distinctive prose
8. **Authority** — author qualified to opine
9. **Concision** — argued tightly
10. **Service to discourse** — fills a void

## Note
Pieces can be sent through digital submission portal or to opinion@nytimes.com. Lack of response within 3 business days = pass.
