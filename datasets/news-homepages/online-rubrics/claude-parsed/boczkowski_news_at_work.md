# Boczkowski — News at Work: Imitation in an Age of Information Abundance

University of Chicago Press, 2010. Ethnography of online and print newsrooms revealing how monitoring/imitation drives selection.

## Core Thesis

In a world of information abundance, journalists increasingly **monitor competitors** and **imitate** their selections. This produces homogeneity even when journalists individually dislike it.

## Selection Mechanisms

### Hard News vs. Soft News
- Hard-news journalists: more intense competitive monitoring, more technology use, more imitation
- Soft-news journalists: less imitation
- Result: hard news is most homogenized

### Implicit News Selection Criteria
1. **What competitors are covering** — explicit imitation signal
2. **What's already trending** — pile-on dynamics
3. **Knowns over unknowns** — established sources, predictable beats
4. **Politicians with illiberal politics** — disproportionately covered
5. **Real-time competitive pressures** — dashboards, hourly checks
6. **Audience analytics** — what is being clicked
7. **Established beat sources** vs. new voices

## Consequences

- Homogenization of news content across outlets
- Reduced diversity
- Original reporting reduced as imitation rewarded
- Even small newsrooms feel competitive monitoring pressure
- Audiences receive a less-varied news diet

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria (revealed by behavior)

- **Competitor adoption** — if NYT covered it, others must
- **Real-time virality / clicks**
- **Convenience / easy availability**
- **Source familiarity**
- **Beat fit** — story fits a reporter's existing assignment
