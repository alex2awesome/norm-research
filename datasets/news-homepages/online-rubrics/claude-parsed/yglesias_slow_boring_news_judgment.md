# Slow Boring (Matthew Yglesias) — Substantive Newsworthiness

Yglesias's repeated argument: most "news" is trivia; substantive news is policy with real consequences.

## What Yglesias Claims Should Lead
- **Material policy changes** affecting income / housing / health
- **Quiet bureaucratic decisions** with large welfare impact
- **State and local stories** with national applicability
- **Studies with strong identification** (RCTs, natural experiments)
- **Long-term trends** (housing, fertility, productivity)
- **Bipartisan deals** (under-covered relative to fights)

## What Yglesias Argues Is OVER-covered
- Twitter discourse / online controversies
- Activist letters and open letters
- Process drama in Congress without policy stakes
- Cultural micro-controversies
- Polling without context
- Personality-driven politics coverage

## Yglesias's Editorial Tests
- "Will this matter to someone's wallet, health, or freedom?"
- "Is this story about a real change or about how people are reacting to something?"
- "Could the average voter act differently based on knowing this?"
- "Does the headline reflect the substantive stakes or the discourse stakes?"

## Yglesias's "Boring is good" Principle
Important news is often boring — zoning reform, transit operations, regulatory rule-makings — and the homepage that buries these in favor of culture-war heat is misallocating attention.

## Note
Slow Boring's title is itself an implicit rubric: prefer slow, boring, important to fast, exciting, trivial.
