# BBC Editorial Guidelines — Impartiality, Due Weight, News Selection

The BBC's editorial framework for what to cover and how. "Due impartiality" replaces equal balance with proportional weighting.

## Core Editorial Values

1. **Trust** — foundation of relationship with audience
2. **Truth and Accuracy** — rigorous in establishing truth
3. **Impartiality** — due weight to events, opinion, main strands of argument
4. **Editorial Integrity and Independence** — free of outside interests
5. **Harm and Offence** — balance of expression with avoidance of unjustified harm
6. **Serving the Public Interest** — accountability and value
7. **Fairness** — to contributors and subjects
8. **Privacy**
9. **Children**
10. **Transparency**
11. **Accountability**

## Key Concept: "Due Weight"

- NOT equal weight for all views
- Proportional to evidence and support
- Minority views need not get equal prominence to consensus
- "Reflecting a wide range of subject matter and perspectives across output as a whole and over an appropriate timeframe"
- "No significant strand of thought is under-represented or omitted"

## What Becomes News (Implicit Criteria)
- **Significance** to BBC audiences
- **Interest** — reasonable audience would care
- **Importance** — informational value
- **Unusual / striking**
- **Public interest**
- **Diverse perspectives represented** over time

## Controversial Subjects
- Wide range of significant views must be given due weight and prominence
- Particularly during active controversy
- Reasonable, carefully reached editorial judgment

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria
- Public interest
- Audience significance
- Range of views over time
- Avoidance of unjustified harm
- Audience trust impact
- Independence from outside influence
- Adequate sourcing
- Privacy considerations
