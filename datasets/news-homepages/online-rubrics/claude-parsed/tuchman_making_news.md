# Tuchman — Making News: A Study in the Construction of Reality

Free Press, 1978. Sociological ethnography of newsrooms revealing how journalists routinize unexpected events through "typifications."

## Core Concept: Typifications

Journalists "routinize the unexpected" by classifying events in already-known categories that fit the organizational structure of news work. Events become "events-as-news" when they fit a typification.

## Five Typifications of News Stories

1. **Hard News** — Time-sensitive, government, major crime, disasters. Goes on front page; written quickly.

2. **Soft News** — Human interest, lifestyle. Less urgent; can wait.

3. **Spot News** — Unscheduled events that just happened. Requires fast reaction.

4. **Developing News** — Stories that unfold over time. Allows planned coverage.

5. **Continuing News** — Stories scheduled in advance (legislation, trials). Easiest to plan.

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria from Typifications

- **Predictability of unfolding** — easier-to-routinize events get covered
- **Time horizon** — fits production schedule
- **Geographic compatibility** — within reporter's beat
- **Source availability** — official spokespeople, predictable
- **Resource availability** — staff, photographer, satellite
- **Editorial planning compatibility**

## "News Net" Concept

Newsrooms cast a "news net" through:
- Geographical concentration (capital cities, courthouses)
- Topic-specific specialization (beats)
- Time-based routines (scheduled press conferences)

This determines what events even reach journalists' attention.

## Implications

What gets defined as news is not inherent in events — it's a product of:
1. Organizational routines
2. Available typifications
3. The "news net" of beats and sources
