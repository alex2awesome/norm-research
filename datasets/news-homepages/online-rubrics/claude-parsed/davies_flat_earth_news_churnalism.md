# Davies — Flat Earth News and "Churnalism"

Nick Davies' 2008 critique of news production exposing how PR-driven content shapes news. Cardiff University study tested his claims.

## Core Concept: Churnalism

"The passive processing of material which overwhelmingly tends to be supplied for them by outsiders, particularly wire agencies and PR."

## Cardiff Study Findings

- **80% of stories in Britain's quality press were not original**
- Only **12% of stories** generated by reporters
- Heavy reliance on:
  - Wire services (Reuters, AP, AFP, PA)
  - Press releases
  - PR agency-supplied material
  - Other media's reporting

## Causes (per Davies)

- Staff cuts
- Spending cuts
- Increased output demands per reporter
- Decline of investigative resources
- Rise of PR industry
- Newsroom consolidation

## Empirical Example
- Provincial reporter producing 48 stories/week
- Spent only 3 hours out of office in a week
- Spoke face-to-face with only 4 people

## Implicit "Newsworthiness" Criteria Revealed (Critical)

What actually drives publication in churnalist newsroom:

1. **Already-packaged** content (press release, wire copy)
2. **Quick turnaround** capability
3. **Verification not required** (already "official")
4. **Source supplies it free**
5. **Filling space** more than informing
6. **Following pack** behavior
7. **Avoiding original investigation** (cost)

## Implications for News Selection

- News values become rationalizations of PR-supplied agenda
- "Newsworthy" effectively means "supplied by reliable PR shop"
- Independent reporting underweighted
- Beat reporting → desk-bound aggregation
- Editorial judgment narrows to whether to print, not what to investigate

## Reform Implications

Davies advocates for:
- Restoration of staff levels
- Verification standards
- Original reporting commitment
- Transparency about sourcing
- Disclosure when content is press-release-derived
