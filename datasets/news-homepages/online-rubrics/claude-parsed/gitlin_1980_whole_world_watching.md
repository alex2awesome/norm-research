# Gitlin (1980) - The Whole World Is Watching: Mass Media in the Making and Unmaking of the New Left

Todd Gitlin's classic study of how mainstream US news coverage processed the 1960s antiwar / New Left movements. Drew on Gitlin's own experience as president of Students for a Democratic Society (1963–64), interviews with activists and reporters, and content analysis. Published by University of California Press, 1980.

The contribution: Systematic enumeration of the *frames* (news selection and framing patterns) that mainstream media apply to protest movements — frames that determine which protests become "news" and how they are characterized.

## Six News Frames Used to Process Protest

1. **Trivialization** - Making light of the movement; emphasizing eccentric appearance (hair, dress, drug use), bizarre tactics, comic incidents. Distracts from substantive grievances.

2. **Polarization** - Pitting movement against right-wing counter-protesters; presenting protests as "two extremes" with implicit moderation as the unstated norm.

3. **Emphasis on Internal Dissension** - Highlighting splits, factionalism, infighting within the movement to suggest it lacks coherence and seriousness.

4. **Marginalization** - Casting protesters as deviant, unrepresentative — students vs. workers, fringe vs. mainstream — minimizing perceived popular support.

5. **Disparagement by Numbers** - Using crowd-size estimates and statistics in ways that minimize movement visibility (low estimates, comparisons to small subsets).

6. **Disparagement of the Movement's Effectiveness** - Framing protests as futile or counterproductive; dismissing the very tactics that brought media attention.

## Three Stages of Media Treatment

1. **Initial Ignoring** - New movements fail Galtung-Ruge thresholds; media don't cover.

2. **Discovery via Spectacle** - Movements adopt provocative tactics to break through; media cover them as oddities (Trivialization frame activates).

3. **Co-optation and Burnout** - Media glare elevates leaders to celebrity status, separating them from base; "moderate" alternatives elevated; rhetoric inflated, then dismissed as extreme; movement destabilizes.

## Implicit News Values Operative in Protest Coverage

1. **Drama / Spectacle Premium** - Conventional protests don't generate visuals; only confrontational, dramatic, or violent tactics get coverage.

2. **Personalization** - Coverage organized around named leaders rather than collective demands.

3. **Conflict** - Internal movement conflict and movement-vs-counterprotester conflict both generate news; coordinated protest action does not.

4. **Eliteness** - Movement statements legitimized only when paired with elite responses (politicians, experts).

5. **Negativity** - Disorder, violence, and dysfunction within the movement are amplified.

6. **Authority Source Privilege** - Police estimates of crowd size, official characterization of protesters' demands, become the working definitions.

## Five Selection Criteria for Whether a Protest Becomes News

1. **Visual Newsworthiness** - Photogenic confrontation, large crowds, distinctive tactics.
2. **Celebrity Anchor** - Famous leaders or surprising allies attract coverage.
3. **Establishment Counter-Reaction** - Police response or political condemnation provides the second-source needed for objectivity rituals.
4. **Coupling to Major Story** - Protests pegged to a current major event (election, war anniversary) get coverage; freestanding ones don't.
5. **Disorder Threshold** - Pure peaceful protest is under-covered; disorder at the protest provides the news hook.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Predicts modern protest coverage patterns**: BLM, Occupy, Tea Party coverage all show Gitlin's frames in action.

- **Operationalizable**: Frame analysis of protest coverage can be conducted at scale using Gitlin's six frame categories.

- **Reveals selection-induced distortion**: The very stories that DO get covered (dramatic, conflictual, leader-centered) systematically misrepresent the movement's substantive demands.

- **Newsworthiness is double-edged for movements**: Coverage can both amplify and undermine; the news-values that grant coverage often produce hostile representation.
