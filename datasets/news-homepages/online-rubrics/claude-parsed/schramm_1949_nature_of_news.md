# Schramm (1949) - The Nature of News

Wilbur Schramm's foundational article on news as a constructed reframing of events, predating Galtung & Ruge by 16 years. Published in *Journalism Quarterly* 26(3): 259–269.

The contribution: Schramm distinguished *immediate-reward* news from *delayed-reward* news, providing one of the earliest psychological models of news selection. Underpins later audience-pull analyses of news values.

## Two Classes of News by Reader Reward

1. **Immediate Reward News** - Generates quick gratification: crime, accidents, disasters, sports, social events, human interest. Reader gets emotional payoff at the time of reading.

2. **Delayed Reward News** - Generates eventual benefits: public affairs, economic news, science, education, public health. Reader must invest cognitive effort for later payoff.

Schramm argued newsrooms over-produce immediate-reward news because it's easier to package and reliably engages readers; delayed-reward news requires more effort from both producer and consumer.

## Schramm's Account of News Construction

Schramm distinguished news from events. News is a *reconstruction* of essential frameworks — built from:

1. Eyewitness accounts.
2. Secondhand accounts.
3. Tertiary commentary.
4. The reporter's own knowledge and predispositions.

The reconstruction is then coded by people with no connection to the original event, modifying length, form, emphasis, and interpretation to meet:

- **Mechanical demands of transmission** - Word counts, broadcast slots, page layout.
- **Anticipated needs and preferences of audience** - What readers want.
- **Wishes and demands of buyers of news** - Wire-service customers, editors, advertisers.

## Five Implicit News-Selection Criteria

1. **Reward Type** - Immediate-reward stories preferred at the same Galtung-Ruge factor level.

2. **Mechanical Fit** - Stories that fit established formats (column inches, broadcast slots) win.

3. **Predisposition Compatibility** - Reporters' own beliefs filter source selection (anticipates White 1950).

4. **Audience Anticipation** - Editors' models of audience preferences shape selection.

5. **Customer Demand** - Wire services and outlets cater to subscriber preferences (anticipates modern recommender feedback loops).

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **First explicit psychological news theory**: Pre-Galtung-Ruge, Schramm framed selection in terms of audience reward; complements G&R's structural account.

- **Anticipates engagement-driven journalism**: The immediate-reward bias predicts the social-media era's preference for emotionally arousing content (Berger & Milkman 2012).

- **Predicts soft-news encroachment**: When competition rises, immediate-reward stories crowd out delayed-reward stories — Schramm's framework predicts the late-20th-century soft-news rise.

- **Empirically operationalizable**: Immediate-vs-delayed reward can be coded for any story; provides a content-feature predictor of homepage placement and engagement.
