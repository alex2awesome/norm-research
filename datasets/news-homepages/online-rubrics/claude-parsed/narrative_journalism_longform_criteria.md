# Narrative Journalism / Longform — Story Selection Criteria

A genre with distinct newsworthiness criteria emphasizing scene, character, and meaning over breaking news.

## Tom Wolfe's 4 Techniques (New Journalism Foundation)

1. **Scene-by-scene construction** — not abstract summary
2. **Realistic dialogue** — recorded as spoken
3. **Point of view of specific characters**
4. **Status details** — telling everyday details revealing social reality

## Three-Aspect Structure

1. **Setting** — Context behind the story
2. **Complication** — Main events
3. **Outcome** — Aftermath

## What Makes a Story Suitable for Longform Narrative

### Story Selection Criteria

1. **Complexity** — "So much complexity in themselves that they leave you in uncomfortable places and produce emotions"
2. **Compelling main character** — guides the narrative
3. **Multi-layered access** — sources willing to share over weeks/months
4. **Underlying universal theme** beyond facts
5. **Time-rich subject** — not breaking news
6. **Visual scene potential**
7. **Possible to follow over time**
8. **Emotional resonance**
9. **Stakes** — something matters
10. **Original** — not previously well-covered

### Reporting Demands
- Multiple sources explored in depth
- Weeks or months of reporting
- Rigorous fact-checking
- Returning to subjects multiple times
- Living with subjects when possible

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for Longform

- **Resonance over recency** — emotional depth more important than freshness
- **Character over event** — people drive stories
- **Scene over summary** — show, don't tell
- **Ambiguity over closure** — uncomfortable endings welcomed
- **Pattern over incident** — what does this story illuminate
- **Underdog stories** — overlooked subjects
- **Hidden communities** — worlds readers don't know
- **Process / ritual** — "how things actually work"
- **Identity / belonging** themes
- **Lives in transition**

## 15 Best Practices (LatAm Journalism Review summary)

1. Find the right story (resonance + access)
2. Develop multiple sources
3. Spend significant time
4. Build trust slowly
5. Take rigorous notes
6. Use scene-by-scene construction
7. Capture dialogue accurately
8. Find a character who can carry the story
9. Identify a thematic frame
10. Plan structure carefully
11. Test multiple drafts
12. Strong opening
13. Build to memorable ending
14. Triple fact-check
15. Edit ruthlessly
