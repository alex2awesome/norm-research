# Conboy — Journalism Studies: The Basics

Martin Conboy's introductory textbook (Routledge). Covers history, language, news values, and tabloid vs. broadsheet distinctions.

## Conboy's Definition of Tabloid News Values

"Tabloid" extends beyond format — refers to news values regardless of page size:
- Populism
- Accessibility
- Simplicity
- Personalization
- Sensationalism
- Drama / spectacle
- Familiarity

## Convergence Theory: "Tabloidization" or Dual-Convergence

Conboy and others have argued for **dual convergence**:
- Tabloids becoming slightly more serious
- Broadsheets becoming more entertainment-driven
- Result: middle-ground common product

## News Values Framework (Conboy)

Building on Galtung-Ruge / Harcup-O'Neill, Conboy emphasizes **historical contingency**:

1. **News values vary by era** — what's newsworthy in 1850 vs. 1950 vs. 2025
2. **News values vary by format** — broadsheet, tabloid, digital, broadcast
3. **News values vary by culture** — UK, US, Global South
4. **News values are contested** — not natural, but constructed

## Tabloid Newsworthiness Criteria

1. **Working-class identification** — speaks to "us"
2. **Anti-elite stance** — punches up
3. **Emotional immediacy** — feel before think
4. **Visual prominence** — large photos, headlines
5. **Celebrity** centrality
6. **Sex and scandal** prominence
7. **Crime and punishment** drama
8. **Underdog narratives**
9. **Patriotic appeal**
10. **Common-sense framing** of complex issues

## Broadsheet Newsworthiness Criteria

1. **Public-policy substance**
2. **Institutional accountability**
3. **Analytical depth**
4. **Foreign affairs**
5. **Business / economy**
6. **Cultural commentary**
7. **Long-form investigation**
8. **Complexity tolerance**

## Conboy's Theoretical Contribution

- News values are not universal
- They reflect cultural assumptions about audience
- Tabloid is a value system, not just a format
- Digital media is hybridizing both
