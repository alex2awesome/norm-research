# AHCJ — Association of Health Care Journalists

1500+ member organization. Mission: improve quality, accuracy, visibility of health care reporting.

## Editorial / Ethics Positions (Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria)

- Reject hospital arrangements that improperly influence coverage
- Resist confidentiality agreements with hospitals
- Oppose secrecy clauses in medical-device-manufacturer contracts
- Independent verification of health claims required

## Coverage Areas (Implicit Newsworthiness)

### Topics
- Drug approvals / FDA actions
- Clinical trials
- Public health alerts (outbreaks, recalls)
- Hospital ratings and quality
- Insurance / costs
- Mental health
- Disparities (race, gender, geography)
- Aging and end-of-life
- Pandemics (COVID, etc.)
- Reproductive health
- Substance use
- Pharma industry
- Medical devices
- Telemedicine
- Health policy / Medicare / Medicaid

### Newsworthiness Factors
1. **Public health threat** — magnitude of risk
2. **Number affected**
3. **Severity of outcome** (mortality, morbidity)
4. **Vulnerable populations** disproportionately affected
5. **Policy implications** — coverage decisions, regulations
6. **Accountability** — provider/insurer/pharma misconduct
7. **Research quality** — peer-reviewed, sample size, replication
8. **Cost story** — affordability angle
9. **Disparities exposed**
10. **Patient story** — humanizing data
11. **New treatment** — efficacy, side effects, access
12. **Emergency** — outbreak, recall

## Quality Markers (per AHCJ)

- **Multiple independent expert sources**
- **Patient voices**
- **Numerical literacy** — relative vs. absolute risk
- **Limitations disclosed**
- **Conflicts of interest of sources noted**
- **Plain-language explanation**
- **Avoid hype** — single-study breakthroughs
- **Update health stories** as evidence evolves

## AHCJ Awards Categories
Recognize excellence in health investigative reporting, beat reporting, consumer/feature, public health, business, and online journalism.

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for Health
- Threat scale + immediacy
- Verifiable evidence base
- Patient/community impact
- System accountability
- Equity dimension
- Practical actionability for readers
- Counter-misinformation value
- Reform potential
