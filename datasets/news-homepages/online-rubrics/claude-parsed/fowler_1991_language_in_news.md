# Fowler (1991) - Language in the News: Discourse and Ideology in the Press

Roger Fowler's foundational application of *critical linguistics* to news. Argued that newspaper coverage is not a neutral mirror but a "socially constructed practice" through which ideological commitments are encoded into linguistic structure. Published by Routledge, 1991.

The contribution: Identifies *linguistic markers* of news values — how transitivity, lexical choice, modality, and naming patterns realize newsworthiness in text. Foundation for the discursive news values tradition (Bednarek & Caple).

## Key Linguistic Mechanisms by Which Newsworthiness is Realized

1. **Transitivity Choices** - Who is grammatical agent vs. patient encodes responsibility ("Police shot protester" vs. "Protester died in police clash"). Story selection favors events that admit clear agent-patient grammar.

2. **Lexical Field / Naming** - The choice of words (rebel/freedom-fighter/terrorist; strike/walkout/disruption) embeds ideological framing. Stories using familiar lexical fields are easier to fit into news templates.

3. **Modality** - The hedging ("alleged," "reportedly") signals epistemic uncertainty and source distance. Stories with strong modality fit ill with the news-form's preference for declarative reporting.

4. **Stereotyping** - Reduces complex actors to recognizable categories (the immigrant, the welfare claimant). Stereotype-compatible stories are easier to compose.

5. **Editorial Voice / Consensus Formation** - The "we" of editorial address constructs a presumed consensus; stories that align with it gain newsworthiness.

## News Values Embedded in Linguistic Structure

1. **Negativity** realized via lexical fields of disaster, conflict, crime.
2. **Personalization** realized via named individuals as sentence subjects.
3. **Eliteness** realized via title use ("Sir," "Minister," "CEO").
4. **Conflict** realized via opposition pairs ("clashed," "rivals," "split").
5. **Consonance** realized via familiar narrative templates ("once again," "as expected").

## Topics Treated in the Book

- Gender stereotypes in news (women as victims/wives, men as agents).
- Power and authority (linguistic deference patterns).
- Law and order (criminalization through naming).
- Stereotyping of marginalized groups.
- Editorial voice and consensus.

## Five Operational Newsworthiness Criteria from Fowler's Critical Lens

1. **Lexical Template Compatibility** - Events mappable to standard news vocabularies are more reportable.

2. **Active-Voice Suitability** - Events with clear agents preferred over events whose primary actor is structural / diffuse.

3. **Stereotype Activation** - Events that activate available stereotypes are easier to narrate.

4. **Consensus Compatibility** - Events that confirm presumed shared values gain easy entry; events that challenge them require extra discursive labor.

5. **Naming Convention Fit** - Events about actors with established naming conventions (politicians by office, criminals by category) fit faster than novel actor types.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Linguistic feasibility as a selection filter**: Stories that resist standard news language structures are systematically demoted.

- **Bridges critical linguistics and news sociology**: Fowler shows that what news sociologists called "selection bias" can be operationalized as linguistic bias.

- **Foundation for discursive news values analysis (DNVA)**: Bednarek & Caple's framework formalizes Fowler's approach into measurable categories.

- **Operationalizable for modern NLP**: Transitivity, modality, lexical field can all be extracted from text and used as predictors of newsworthiness.
