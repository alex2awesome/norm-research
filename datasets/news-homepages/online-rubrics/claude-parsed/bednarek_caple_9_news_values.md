# Bednarek & Caple — 9 News Values

The consolidated list of news values from "The Discourse of News Values: How News Organizations Create Newsworthiness" (OUP, 2017).

## The 9 News Values (DNVA Framework)

1. **Timeliness** — The relevance of an event in relation to the current moment (recent, ongoing, seasonal, anniversary).

2. **Consonance** — The compatibility of an event with stereotypes that audiences hold about the people, places, things, etc. involved (fits expectations).

3. **Negativity** — The negative aspects of an event (death, damage, conflict, scandal, crisis, loss).

4. **Impact** — The effects or consequences of an event (causal, ripple effects, predictions of effect).

5. **Proximity** — The geographical or cultural nearness of an event to the audience.

6. **Unexpectedness** — The unexpected, unusual, or surprising aspects of an event.

7. **Superlativeness** — The maximized or intensified aspects of an event (scope, scale, intensity).

8. **Personalisation** — The personal or "human" face of an event (named individuals, ordinary people quoted).

9. **Eliteness** — The high status of the people, organizations, or nations involved.

## How Each Value is Linguistically Constructed

The DNVA framework provides linguistic indicators for each value:
- **Negativity**: words like "tragedy," "disaster," "crisis"
- **Timeliness**: "today," "now," "yesterday," tense markers
- **Proximity**: place names, "here," cultural references
- **Superlativeness**: intensifiers ("massive," "unprecedented")
- **Eliteness**: titles, role labels (CEO, president)
- **Impact**: causal verbs, references to consequences
- **Unexpectedness**: "first," "shocking," "surprise"
- **Personalisation**: names, emotion words, quotations from ordinary people
- **Consonance**: stock phrases, reference to recurring story types
