# Global Investigative Journalism Network (GIJN) — Investigative Journalism Criteria

GIJN's introduction and follow-the-money methodology, used worldwide for training investigative journalists.

## Investigative Story Criteria (per GIJN)

A true investigative story must include:
1. **Original reporting** — facts not previously reported
2. **Public significance** — matters affecting many people
3. **Initiative** — reporter's own work, not handed by sources
4. **Documentation** — paper trail, records
5. **Multiple sources / verification**
6. **Accountability** — identifies wrongdoer or responsible party
7. **Methodology disclosable** — process explainable
8. **Holds power to account**

## Follow-the-Money Methodology

Core investigative approach:
- Trace financial flows
- Identify beneficial owners (vs. shell companies)
- Cross-reference public records (tax, registration, court)
- Identify offshore structures
- Connect dots across jurisdictions

## GIJN Training Topics (Implicit Newsworthiness Areas)

1. Finding sources and backgrounding
2. Public records / FOI
3. Interviewing techniques
4. Deep internet research
5. Following the money
6. Data journalism
7. Fact-checking
8. Digital security (for risky reporting)
9. Collaborations (cross-border)
10. Editing investigations

## Newsworthiness Criteria for Investigations

- **Wrongdoing magnitude** — How serious?
- **Number of victims**
- **Power of perpetrator**
- **Hiddenness** — Was it concealed?
- **Verifiability through documents**
- **Geographic scope** — Local vs. transnational
- **Replicability** of the wrongdoing pattern
- **Reform potential** — Can exposure produce change?
