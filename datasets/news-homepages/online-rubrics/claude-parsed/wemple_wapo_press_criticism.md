# Erik Wemple — A Rubric for Newsroom Self-Accountability

WaPo's media columnist provides recurring criteria for what newsrooms should treat as newsworthy.

## Wemple's Newsworthiness Tests for Media Stories
- **Falsehood-correction speed** — how fast did a network admit an error?
- **Disclosure adequacy** — did the host disclose conflicts?
- **Pattern-not-incident** — recurring problems > one-offs
- **On-air vs. off-air discrepancies** — leaked private comms
- **Personnel decisions revealing standards**
- **Defamation-suit risk surfaced**

## Wemple's Front-Page Tests for News Outlets Generally
- "Did you correct prominently or in a tweet at midnight?"
- "Are anchors held to same standard as reporters?"
- "Has your outlet changed practice based on past errors?"

## Stories Wemple Elevates
- Cable-news fact-pattern accountability
- Defamation litigation outcomes
- Departures of senior anchors / executives
- Editorial-standards documents (newly issued)
- Reporting on the reporting (when it reveals craft norms)

## Stories Wemple Demotes
- Insider gossip without policy implication
- Personality drama without standards angle

## Note
Wemple's rubric extends accountability journalism to journalism itself — newsworthy = consequential for press credibility.
