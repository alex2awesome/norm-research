# Kovach & Rosenstiel — The Elements of Journalism (Nine Principles)

Foundational text from the Committee of Concerned Journalists. Defines normative obligations of journalism that implicitly determine what should be covered.

## The Nine Elements

1. **Journalism's first obligation is to the truth** — News must be accurate and verifiable.

2. **Its first loyalty is to citizens** — Not advertisers, not owners, not sources.

3. **Its essence is a discipline of verification** — Distinguishes journalism from entertainment, propaganda, art.

4. **Its practitioners must maintain an independence from those they cover** — No co-option by sources.

5. **It must serve as an independent monitor of power** — Watchdog role; "guard against tyranny."

6. **It must provide a forum for public criticism and compromise** — Space for civic debate.

7. **It must strive to make the significant interesting and relevant** — Don't bury important news; craft to engage.

8. **It must keep the news comprehensive and proportional** — Coverage should reflect actual importance, not fashion.

9. **Its practitioners must be allowed to exercise their personal conscience** — Professional moral autonomy.

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria

- **Significance** to citizens (not just interest)
- **Verifiability** as a precondition
- **Accountability of power** as priority
- **Forum function** — coverage that enables democratic deliberation
- **Proportionality** — important things should get more space than trivial ones
- **Independence** from sources/advertisers in story selection
- **Interest in service of significance** — making important stories engaging

## Newer (10th) Element (Added Later)
**Citizens have rights and responsibilities when it comes to the news** — Audience as participant, not just consumer.
