# Iyengar (1991) - Is Anyone Responsible? How Television Frames Political Issues

Shanto Iyengar's book-length study showing that the *form* of news framing — episodic versus thematic — has measurable effects on how viewers attribute responsibility for social problems. The study used controlled experiments on edited TV news segments. Published by University of Chicago Press, 1991.

The contribution: Television news's heavy preference for *episodic* framing (illustrating issues through individual cases) rather than *thematic* framing (presenting collective evidence) is itself a structural news-selection bias with downstream political consequences.

## Two Frame Types

1. **Episodic Framing** - Concrete, case-based, often visual. A homeless person, a single victim, a specific crime, a single school shooting. The "passing parade of specific events." High emotional engagement.

2. **Thematic Framing** - Aggregated, contextual, statistical. Unemployment rates, poverty distributions, structural school problems. Lower visual appeal; more abstract.

## Empirical Findings

- **Television news heavily favors episodic frames** (driven by the demands of visual storytelling, time slots, and personalization).
- **Episodic framing → individual attribution** (poverty caused by laziness, low education).
- **Thematic framing → societal/governmental attribution** (poverty caused by economic conditions, policy failure).
- **Effect operates without viewer awareness** — frame choice is invisible to the audience but shapes attribution.

## Implicit News-Selection Criteria Driving Episodic Bias

1. **Visual Availability** - Stories with photographable individual victims/heroes preferred over stories with only statistics.

2. **Emotional Engagement** - Single-case stories generate stronger viewer response, supporting ratings/clicks.

3. **Narrative Closure** - A single case has a beginning, middle, end; structural conditions don't.

4. **Time-Slot Compatibility** - Episodic stories fit 90-second TV slots; thematic stories require longer treatment.

5. **Source Availability** - Individual victims/heroes are easier to interview than aggregated trends.

6. **Personalization Premium** - Episodic stories satisfy Galtung & Ruge's personalization criterion almost automatically.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Form-driven selection**: An event's frameability determines its eligibility for coverage. Stories that resist episodic treatment are systematically under-covered.

- **Diffuse responsibility hidden**: Structural problems get coverage only when they erupt into visible cases — which gives the *case*, not the *structure*, news value.

- **Predicts reform-resistance bias**: Because episodic framing locates blame in individuals, news coverage subtly disfavors policy reform in favor of personal-virtue solutions.

- **Operationalizable for homepage models**: Whether a story is framed episodically vs. thematically is a measurable feature with predictive value for engagement metrics.

## Related Iyengar Work

- Iyengar & Kinder (1987) *News That Matters* established media priming — i.e., that issues prominent in news become criteria for evaluating leaders.
