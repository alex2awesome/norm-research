# McManus — Market-Driven Journalism: Let the Citizen Beware?

Sage, 1994. Foundational text showing how market forces (consumers, advertisers, investors, sources) shape news selection.

## Four-Market Model

News selection is influenced by four markets simultaneously:
1. **Consumers** — Audience attention
2. **Advertisers** — Reach desired demographics
3. **Investors** — Maximize shareholder return
4. **Sources** — Information providers shape stories

## Implicit News Selection Criteria (Market-Driven)

### Cost-Driven
- **Low cost to produce** — Press releases, official events, predictable beats
- **High audience appeal** — Sensational, easy to consume
- **Avoiding offense** to advertisers
- **Quick and easy to verify**

### Audience-Driven
- **Sensationalism** — Drives ratings/clicks
- **Light entertainment** over heavy public affairs
- **Personalization** of complex issues
- **Conflict and drama** — Entertainment value
- **Familiar formats** — Easy comprehension

### Investor-Driven
- **Maximize ROI** per story
- **Resource efficiency** — Avoid expensive investigations
- **Repurpose / wire content**
- **Use freelancers vs. staff**

### Source-Driven
- **Reliance on official sources** (cheap, predictable)
- **Press release journalism**
- **PR-driven story ideas**

## Quality Concerns

McManus argues market-driven news fails minimum economic conditions for markets to benefit society:
- Information asymmetry (audiences don't know what they're missing)
- Externalities (uninformed citizenry harms democracy)
- Public good nature of news
