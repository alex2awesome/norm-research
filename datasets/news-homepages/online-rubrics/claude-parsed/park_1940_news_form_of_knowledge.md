# Park (1940) - News as a Form of Knowledge: A Chapter in the Sociology of Knowledge

Robert E. Park's foundational sociological essay on what news *is* and what makes something newsworthy. Published in the *American Journal of Sociology* 45(5): 669–686. Park, the Chicago School sociologist and former newspaperman, was the first major American academic to theorize news selection as a topic for sociology of knowledge.

The contribution: Park gave the first analytic definition of news as a distinct form of knowledge — neither the formal "knowledge about" of science nor the informal "acquaintance with" of common sense, but a third category with its own selection criteria.

## Park's Definition of News

> "News is something that will make people talk."

News is characterized by:

1. **The Character of a Public Document** - Open, communicated, capable of widespread circulation.

2. **Limitation to Sudden, Decisive Changes** - News is the report of *events* (discrete happenings), not conditions. Slow-developing situations are not news.

3. **Transient and Ephemeral Quality** - News loses value once it ceases to be talked about.

4. **Orienting Function** - News exists to orient persons and societies in an "actual world."

## Park's Implicit News-Selection Criteria

1. **Talkability** - Will people talk about it? An item with high talkability is news.

2. **Sudden / Decisive Change** - Events bringing abrupt change qualify; gradual change does not.

3. **Public Relevance** - The event must matter beyond the actors involved.

4. **Recency** - News is, by Park's definition, perishable. Old happenings are not news.

5. **Orienting Value** - Does the event help readers locate themselves in their world?

6. **Continued Conversation** - News that sustains discussion across the news cycle outlives one-shot reports.

## Knowledge-Type Continuum

Park placed news on a continuum:

```
ACQUAINTANCE WITH .... NEWS .... KNOWLEDGE ABOUT
(experiential common sense)    (formal scientific)
```

News occupies an intermediate position — more systematic than common sense but less systematic than science; more time-bound than either.

## Newsworthiness Implications

1. **Conversation-Generation** - Stories that produce social discussion (water-cooler talk, social-media chatter) are newsworthy by Park's criterion.

2. **Sudden-Change Bias** - News systematically over-covers discrete events and under-covers structural conditions — anticipates Iyengar's episodic/thematic asymmetry.

3. **Public-vs-Private Filter** - Items with public-document character outrank private/intimate matters (unless those matters reveal public significance).

4. **Anti-Continuity Bias** - News must be fresh; long-running situations need a new "peg" to re-enter coverage.

5. **Orientation Function** - News selection reflects what audiences need to make sense of their world; this filters out the trivially novel.

## Why It Matters

- **First sociological definition** of news that is still cited as canonical.
- **Direct ancestor of conversation-driven news models**: Park's "talkability" criterion anticipates the social-media era's shareability research (Trilling et al. 2017, Berger & Milkman 2012).
- **Foundation for the Chicago School's sociology of news**: Influenced Hughes, Janowitz, and the entire institutional ecology approach.
