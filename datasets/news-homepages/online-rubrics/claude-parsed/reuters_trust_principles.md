# Reuters Trust Principles

Created in 1941 during WWII to preserve Reuters' independence, integrity and freedom from bias. Built into the Reuters charter (1984) and Thomson Reuters founding documents (2008).

## The Five Trust Principles

1. **Integrity, independence and freedom from bias of Thomson Reuters shall at all times be fully preserved.**

2. **Thomson Reuters shall supply unbiased and reliable news services** to newspapers, news agencies, broadcasters, and other media subscribers, and to businesses, governments, institutions, individuals and others with whom Thomson Reuters has or may have contracts.

3. **No effort shall be spared to expand, develop and adapt** the news and other services and products of Thomson Reuters in order to maintain its leading position.

4. **Thomson Reuters shall not pass into the hands of any one interest, group or faction.**

5. **Management shall act at all times with proper regard to the maintenance of these principles.**

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **Accuracy** is sacrosanct
- **Balance and freedom from bias** required in every story
- **Images and stories must reflect reality** (no manipulation)
- **Transparency about errors**
- **Worldwide reach** prioritized — global beats and significance
- **No single owner** can dictate editorial direction

## Editorial Style Standards (Derived)

- "Show, don't tell" — let facts speak
- Multi-source verification
- Wire-style speed
- Original reporting prioritized over commentary
- Source attribution mandatory
- Avoid emotive language

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria
- **Significance to global business / governments**
- **Speed** — wire service tradition
- **Breaking news** as core
- **Accuracy over scoop**
- **Independence from source pressure**
- **Wide audience relevance** (not niche)
- **Verifiability**

## Citation
Reuters Trust Principles, established 1941, codified in Thomson Reuters Corp. Memorandum and Articles.
