# Glasgow University Media Group - The "Bad News" Series

The Glasgow Media Group, formed at the University of Glasgow in 1974, conducted the first systematic content analysis of British TV news bulletins (BBC and ITN) in the late 1970s. The "Bad News" series — *Bad News* (1976), *More Bad News* (1980), *Really Bad News* (1982), *War and Peace News* (1985), *Bad News from Israel* (2004) — established standard methodology for measuring structural bias in broadcast news.

## Core Argument

Despite broadcaster claims of impartiality, news output systematically privileges institutional / managerial / governmental perspectives over labor, dissenting, and grassroots perspectives. This is achieved not through overt editorializing but through patterned selection of what counts as news.

## Methodology

- Recorded, transcribed, and coded entire news bulletins from BBC and ITN over months.
- Counted source appearances, speaking time, story selection, visual treatment, terminology.
- Compared coverage to independent statistics (strikes per industry, casualty figures, official reports).

## Key Empirical News-Selection Findings

1. **Source Asymmetry** - Industrial-dispute coverage allotted dramatically more speaking time to managers/government than to union representatives, even controlling for actor relevance.

2. **Disruption Frame for Strikes** - Strikes covered as "disruption to the economy/public" rather than as labor grievances; the framing itself narrows newsworthy angles.

3. **Selection Bias in Strike Coverage** - Some industries (e.g., car manufacturing) over-covered relative to actual strike days; others under-covered, even with comparable disruption.

4. **Visual Coding Bias** - Camera angles, music, lower-thirds systematically presented strikers unfavorably (workers shouting at cameras vs. managers speaking calmly behind desks).

5. **Linguistic Asymmetries** - Workers' actions described with stronger verbs ("demand," "threaten"), management actions with neutral verbs ("offer," "respond").

6. **Numerical Distortion** - Statistics about strike costs prominently reported; statistics about wage erosion or workplace injury under-reported.

7. **Pre-strike Cause Omission** - Strike stories typically opened mid-conflict, omitting the originating disputes — implicitly framing strikes as unprovoked.

## News-Value Implications

1. **Disruption Premium** - Events disrupting normal economic/social order are highly newsworthy when the disruptors are non-elite; the *disruption frame* itself constitutes news value.

2. **Asymmetric Source Newsworthiness** - Spokespersons of established institutions are systematically more newsworthy than spokespersons of insurgent groups.

3. **Visual-Friendly Bias** - Stories admitting calm, authoritative visuals (officials at podiums) are easier to package than stories requiring chaotic crowd visuals; selection skews accordingly.

4. **Cost-Centric Framing** - Economic stories framed in cost-to-business / cost-to-public terms rather than benefit-to-affected-group terms.

## Implications for Newsworthiness

- **First systematic empirical evidence** that "objective" news selection produces patterned political bias.

- **Establishes audit methodology**: GMG's coding scheme — source counts, speaking time, terminology — is now standard for content-analysis studies of news selection.

- **Predicts modern findings on labor coverage**: Recent studies (US, UK, EU) replicate the manager/labor speaking-time asymmetry.

- **Operationalizable for homepage models**: Story selection and headline framing on labor disputes can be benchmarked against GMG-style metrics.
