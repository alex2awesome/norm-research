# Tom Wolfe — The New Journalism (Literary Feature Criteria)

The 1960s-1970s movement that brought literary techniques into journalism. Established criteria for what makes a feature/longform story work.

## Wolfe's 4 Devices Borrowed from Fiction

1. **Scene-by-scene construction** — Tell the story through scenes rather than historical narrative summary.

2. **Dialogue in full** — Conversational speech recorded as it actually happened (not just official quotes).

3. **Third-person point of view** — Present every scene through the eyes of a particular character (interior thoughts where possible).

4. **"Status life" details** — Recording everyday details of behavior, possessions, friends, family that indicate a character's social position and identity.

## Reporting Demands (Wolfe / Talese)

- "Depth of reporting and attention to the most minute facts and details that most newspapermen, even the most experienced, have never dreamed of"
- Talese: interior monologue from subjects (what they were thinking)
- Hours of observation, not just interviews
- Total immersion in subject's world

## Newsworthiness Criteria for New Journalism

1. **Subject open to extended observation** — willing to be followed
2. **Vivid scene potential** — places that can be described
3. **Character-driven story** — main subject carries narrative
4. **Social significance** beyond the personal
5. **Status detail richness** — material culture matters
6. **Dialogue-rich** — people willing to speak fully
7. **Underlying theme** — about something larger
8. **Time-investment-worthy** — pays back hours of reporting

## Examples / Practitioners

- Gay Talese ("Frank Sinatra Has a Cold")
- Tom Wolfe ("The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test")
- Joan Didion (essays / Slouching Towards Bethlehem)
- Hunter S. Thompson (Gonzo journalism)
- Norman Mailer (Armies of the Night)
- Truman Capote (In Cold Blood)
- Jimmy Breslin

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria

- **Specificity over typicality** — one specific person reveals universal
- **Process over event** — show how things actually work
- **Ambiguity tolerance** — don't simplify to fit news template
- **Original characters** — vs. official spokespeople
- **Subcultures** — rarely covered worlds
- **Scene access** — can the journalist be present?

## Rules Maintained from Traditional Journalism
- Strict factual accuracy
- No composites
- No invented dialogue
- Source attribution
