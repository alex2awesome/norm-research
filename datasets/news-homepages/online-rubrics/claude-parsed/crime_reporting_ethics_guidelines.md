# Crime Reporting Ethics — Newsworthiness vs. Harm

Guide for journalists balancing crime newsworthiness with victim protection.

## Core Tension

Crime might be newsworthy and the community should know — BUT reporting certain details might cause harm to victims or interfere with investigation/prosecution.

## Ethical Considerations for Newsworthiness

### Identification Decisions
- Generally identify suspects after charges filed
- Sexual assault victims: typically NOT named unless they consent
- Juveniles: typically NOT named (laws vary)
- Domestic violence: protect identity
- Witnesses: weigh safety vs. transparency
- Family members of deceased: notify before reporting death

### Newsworthiness Factors for Crime
1. **Severity of crime** — Magnitude (homicide > theft)
2. **Location** — Proximity to audience
3. **Identity of victim/suspect** — Prominence
4. **Pattern significance** — Part of trend
5. **Police response significance**
6. **Community impact** — Fear, displacement
7. **Justice implications** — Trial, sentencing
8. **Policy implications** — Calls for reform

### Anti-Bias Considerations
- Avoid disproportionate coverage of:
  - Crime by minority groups
  - White female victims (vs. proportional reality)
- Provide context (statistics, trends)
- Avoid mug shot prominence for non-violent crimes
- Avoid criminal history details unless directly relevant
- Use neutral language

## Harm Reduction Principles

- Show compassion to victims
- Be sensitive in approach
- Recognize emotional toll of repeated questioning
- Avoid graphic details when not necessary
- Update stories with positive outcomes
- Allow victim agency in narrative

## Implicit Newsworthiness Criteria for Crime

- **Severity**
- **Public safety implications**
- **Pattern or systemic issue**
- **Public-figure involvement**
- **Investigative angle**
- **Justice system intersection**
- **Policy / reform potential**
- **Visual evidence available** (with care)
- **Resolved vs. unresolved**
- **Anniversary / continuing significance**

## Sentencing Project's 10 Dos and Don'ts (Excerpts)
- DO contextualize crime statistics
- DON'T generalize from single incidents
- DO consider racial/ethnic representation
- DON'T disproportionately use mug shots of people of color
- DO interview reform advocates
- DON'T center "fear" framing
- DO acknowledge structural causes
- DON'T conflate suspects with convictions
