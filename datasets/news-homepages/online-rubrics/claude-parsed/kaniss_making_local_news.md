# Kaniss — Making Local News

University of Chicago Press, 1991. First comprehensive study of how local news is made, focused on Philadelphia. Pioneer of local news studies.

## Core Question

Why crimes and accidents earn more local news coverage than development and policy issues affecting thousands of people?

## Forces Shaping Local Newsworthiness

### Economic Motives of Media Owners
- Cost-efficiency
- Audience size in target geography
- Suburban audience priorities (not just city core)
- Advertiser preferences

### Professional Motives of Journalists
- Career advancement / making a name
- Reliance on official sources (police, courts, mayor)
- Beat structure
- Daily deadline pressures

### Strategies of Media-Wise Politicians
- Stagecraft (press conferences, photo ops)
- Pseudo-events for cameras
- Spin operations
- Source-reporter relationships

## Implicit Local Newsworthiness Criteria

1. **Crime and accidents** — easy to cover, dramatic, official sources
2. **Local geography** — neighborhoods readers identify with
3. **Local elites** — mayor, council, police chief
4. **Visual potential** — fires, crashes for TV
5. **Pseudo-events** scheduled for media
6. **Sports** — local teams
7. **Weather**
8. **Fitting suburban audience** — drawing readers from rings around city
9. **Avoiding deep policy / urban issues** that lack drama
10. **Story already picked up by other local outlets** (pack journalism)

## Key Insight

Coverage choices have urban policy consequences: under-coverage of housing, transit, education depresses public engagement on critical issues.
