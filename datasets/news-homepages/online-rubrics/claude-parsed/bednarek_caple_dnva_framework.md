# Bednarek & Caple - Discursive News Values Analysis (DNVA)

A constructivist framework where news values are seen as values that exist in and are constructed through discourse (language and image). Developed across multiple publications including "News Discourse" (2012) and "The Discourse of News Values" (2017, OUP).

## DNVA News Values

1. **Negativity** - Negative aspects of an event (death, damage, conflict, scandal, crisis). Constructed through evaluative language ("disaster," "tragedy"), images of destruction, etc.

2. **Timeliness** - Relevance to the present (immediacy, currency, seasonality). Marked by "today," "now," "yesterday," recent-tense verbs, references to anniversaries.

3. **Proximity** - Geographic, cultural, or psychological nearness. Marked by place names, references to "here," cultural touchstones, national references.

4. **Superlativeness** - Maximization, intensity, scope. Marked by intensifiers ("massive," "unprecedented"), quantifications, comparatives, superlatives.

5. **Eliteness** - High status of news actors/sources. Marked by titles, role labels (CEO, president), credentialing.

6. **Impact** - Effects/consequences of an event. Marked by causal language, references to consequences, predictions of effects.

7. **Novelty** - Unexpectedness, unusualness. Marked by "first," "unprecedented," "shocking," "surprise."

8. **Personalization** - Human face/personal angles. Marked by named individuals, quoted ordinary people, emotional details.

9. **Consonance** - Stereotypicality, fitting expectations or familiar narratives. Marked by stock phrases, references to recurring story types.

10. **Aesthetic Appeal** - For images: composition, color, framing, dramatic visual qualities (added in their photograph analysis).

## Two Perspectives

- **Cognitive**: news values as criteria journalists apply when selecting stories.
- **Discursive**: news values as constructed properties expressed in/through the text.

## Key Contribution

DNVA provides systematic linguistic and multimodal analysis tools to study how reported events are "sold" to audiences as news (made newsworthy). The first book-length treatment of discursive construction of news values through words and images.
