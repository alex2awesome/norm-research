# Press Watch (Dan Froomkin) — Implicit Newsworthiness Rubric

Froomkin's running critique of Beltway press judgment doubles as a normative rubric for what "should" lead the homepage.

## Stories That MUST Be Above the Fold
- **Threats to democracy** — anti-democratic actions by elected officials, especially patterns rather than one-offs
- **Direct quotes from the powerful** that reveal intent (not laundered through "sources say")
- **Policy consequences for ordinary people** (not Beltway insider reaction)
- **Hypocrisy / contradiction** between stated principle and action
- **Pattern recognition** — connect today's story to last week's, last year's

## What Mainstream Outlets Wrongly Elevate
- **Horse-race polling** at expense of stakes
- **Both-sides framing** of asymmetric situations
- **Process stories** (who's up / down) over substance
- **Personality and access journalism**
- **Anonymous-source spin** from operatives
- **Stenography of official statements** without fact-check

## Froomkin's Implicit Test
"Does this story leave the reader better able to act as a citizen?" — if not, it doesn't belong on A1.

## Anti-Patterns Calling for Demotion
- Stories where the lede buries the lede
- Headlines that euphemize
- Coverage that treats lying as an interesting strategic choice rather than a fact

## Note
Press Watch is meta-criticism — its rubric is what mainstream homepages SHOULD prioritize, by inverting common errors.
