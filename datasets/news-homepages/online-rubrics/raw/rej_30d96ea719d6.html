[edit]
Main article: Fake news in India

Indian Chronicles, dubbed by the EU Disinfo Lab, is an influence operation initiated by Srivastava Group starting in 2005 and enabled by Asian News International in order to promote Indian interests and disparage Pakistan and China. The operation created a network of at least 500 fake news websites and had targeted 116 countries. Srivastava Group had co-sponsored a trip of 27 Members of European Parliament to visit Kashmir and meet with Narendra Modi in 2019.[55][56]

Philippines

[edit]
Main article: Fake news in the Philippines § List of fake news websites

Fake news sites have become rampant for Philippine audiences, especially being shared on social media.[116] Politicians have started filing laws to combat fake news[117][118] and three Senate hearings have been held on the topic.[119][120][121]

The Catholic Church in the Philippines has also released a missive speaking out against it.[122]

Vera Files research at the end of 2017 and 2018 show that the most shared fake news in the Philippines appeared to benefit 2 people the most: Former President Rodrigo Duterte (as well as his allies) and President Bongbong Marcos, with the most viral news driven by shares on networks of Facebook pages.[123] Most Philippine audience Facebook pages and groups spreading online disinformation also bear "Duterte", "Marcos" or "News" in their names and are pro-Duterte.[124] Online disinformation in the Philippines is overwhelmingly political as well, with most attacking groups or individuals critical of the Duterte administration.[125] Many Philippine-audience fake news websites also appear to be controlled by the same operators as they share common Google AdSense and Google Analytics IDs.[124]

According to media scholar Jonathan Corpus Ong, Duterte's presidential campaign is regarded as the patient zero in the current era of disinformation, having preceded widespread global coverage of the Cambridge Analytica scandal and Russian trolls.[126] Fake news is so established and severe in the Philippines that Facebook's Global Politics and Government Outreach Director Katie Harbath also calls it "patient zero"[127] in the global misinformation epidemic, having happened before Brexit, the Donald Trump nomination and the 2016 United States elections.[128]

Radical Economic Transformation media network

[edit]
See also: Bell Pottinger § Activities in South Africa

A network of websites, dubbed the Radical Economic Transformation media network by the African Network of Centers for Investigative Reporting[131] and described by journalists as "disinformation"[132] and "propaganda",[132][133] promoted the concept of white monopoly capital and repeatedly and anonymously attacked Cyril Ramaphosa, journalists, opposition politicians and other critics of the Gupta family, Jacob Zuma and their associates.[132] The sites were registered by CNET Infosystem, an Indian reputation management firm that is linked to the Gupta family, who had close ties with Zuma.[132] As of 2017, they received nearly all of their web traffic from users in India, and were linked to pro-Gupta and pro-Zuma Twitter accounts whose posts were retweeted by bot accounts.[132] This network of websites and social media assets also included media outlets owned by the Gupta family: ANN7 and The New Age.[133] The overall social media strategy was analyzed by Bell Pottinger, who was contracted by the Gupta family.[133] The New York Times noted that, due to the disinformation campaign, "racial tensions [in South Africa] rose to levels that had not been felt since apartheid" and asserted that the campaign significantly damaged the cause of advancing economic equality for Black South Africans.[133]
