10 "Canceled" Comedians Who Are Still Releasing Specials and Touring

By  CD Nuckols  &  Adam Symchuk
Updated  Jan 10, 2026, 6:00 PM EST

Here is a fact-based summary of the story contents:
Try something different:

Having a comedy special is a massive opportunity for comedians. It can help propel a comic's career to new heights. A perfect example of this is stand-up comedian Taylor Tomlinson. Her first Netflix special, Quarter Life Crisis, became so popular that she has since released multiple follow-up specials and branched into hosting and other projects, including being chosen to host the late-night show @fter Midnight. Some of the best stand-up comedians in the world have a comedy special to thank for their impact on their careers.

But just because you're funny doesn't mean you can say whatever you want. Whether it was a joke in their comedy special, behind-the-scenes behavior, or a social media post, some comedians have fallen victim to the phenomenon known as "cancel culture." This trend saw many comedians being outted for both current and past behavior, with the intent to limit their reach through word-of-mouth and censorship. Still, within the context of being "canceled," there is further nuance, whether it be through personal actions and allegations or through a joke that offended a particular group.

Sometimes, cancellations can have a more permanent effect, lasting several years or permanently, while others manage to find work once again or even thrive post-cancellation.

10 Shane Gillis

Latest Comedy Special – 'Shane Gillis: Beautiful Dogs' in 2023 on Netflix

In an era where it can be polarizing for a comedian to make fun of either the left or the right, Shane Gillis found a way to be neutral in his Netflix comedy special, Beautiful Dogs. Notably, Gillis took equal punches at President Joe Biden and former President Donald Trump, showing how humor can help find common ground amid division. Yet, back in 2019, Gillis saw his chance to be a cast regular at Saturday Night Live taken away. After a successful audition process, SNL announced he would be joining the cast for its 45th season. But not long after, a video clip from a podcast he used to host started trending. The video showed Gillis talking in an East Asian accent while using ethnic slurs.

Days after the video was released, Gillis was canned from SNL. On paper, this is a significant setback in his career. But it may have only been a speed bump. In 2020, he took his talents online and found success with the web series Gilly and Keeves. In 2021, he released a comedy special on YouTube. In 2023, he released Beautiful Dogs on Netflix, and in 2024, he would find success with his sitcom series on Netflix, Tires. Gillis would finally get his chance to be a part of SNL as a host, rather than a cast member.

9 Joe Rogan

Latest Comedy Special – 'Burn The Boats' in 2024 on Netflix

Sometimes, it's easy to forget that one of the top dogs in podcasting used to be a stand-up comedian. Joe Rogan's Burn the Boats was the podcast host's first comedy special in six years. He was a little rusty, to say the least, but fans got exactly what they expected from a Joe Rogan—cracking jokes about the COVID-19 pandemic, aliens, and hot-button topics.

Joe Rogan's podcast, The Joe Rogan Experience, came under heavy fire during the COVID-19 pandemic. In many of the show's episodes, Rogan was accused of spreading misinformation about the vaccines on top of using racial slurs. Health officials and organizations were pleading with Spotify, which held exclusive rights to The Joe Rogan Experience, to remove the podcast from its streaming service. It may be a hard pill for some readers to swallow, but by the time the pandemic hit in 2020, The Joe Rogan Experience was basically too big to fail. Spotify stuck it through with Rogan amid the calls for cancellation, though today the podcast is no longer exclusive to Spotify.

8 Ellen DeGeneres

Latest Comedy Special – 'Ellen DeGeneres: For Your Approval' in 2024 on Netflix

Ellen DeGeneres took the stage for what has been deemed her final stand-up special. 2024's For Your Approval dived into the controversies and heartache following the end of The Ellen DeGeneres Show in 2022. Some comedy fans viewed this special as an hour-long ramble about how her show ended and a plea for emotional validity. They aren't far from the truth. But it does offer some fun, cathartic laughs, even if the comedian tiptoed around the specifics of her cancellation.

Back in 2020, a group of former employees of The Ellen DeGeneres Show came forward, accusing her of creating a toxic work environment, failing to address sexual harassment towards female employees, and wrongfully firing employees for taking sick or bereavement leave, among other issues. Ultimately, Warner Bros. Entertainment conducted an investigation and found that The Ellen DeGeneres Show had a toxic culture. After the 2021 investigation, Ellen announced she would not return to the show when her contract expired in 2022.

7 Kevin Hart

Latest Comedy Special – 'Kevin Hart: Reality Check' in 2023 on Peacock

In his 9th comedy special, Kevin Hart reminds us what makes a good comedian: authenticity. Kevin Hart: Reality Check is genuinely authentic with its tone, comedic timing, and stories. One minute, Kevin Hart is taking shots at NBA legend LeBron James; the next, he is honoring his dad, who died ten days before the special was filmed. The latter is one of the more relatable comedy special moments in recent years. Despite the safe approach of Hart's comedy in the special, according to Kevin Hart, he has been canceled "three or four times."

A notable cancellation came in 2018 as he was preparing to host the Oscars. That's when online users went digging into the depths of Hart's Twitter feed. They found several homophobic tweets of Hart using derogatory slurs towards the LGBTQA+ community. After the tweets resurfaced, Kevin Hart stepped down as the host of the Oscars. However, this did little to slow his career, as Hart remains an in-demand actor who continues to build an impressive filmography, with stand-up now taking a back seat to his acting.

6 Roseanne Barr

Latest Comedy Special – 'Roseanne Barr: Cancel This!' in 2023 on Fox Nation

Roseanne Barr: Cancel This! was not the triumphant return the She-Devil star had hoped for. Occasionally, some of Roseanne's jokes will bring out a devilish laugh. Still, overall, her leaning into the anti-cancel-culture talking points presented what many critics thought was a limited and messy comedy special. Still, her previous comedy special came out in 2006, and the comedian certainly had a lot to get off her chest after being removed from her own television show.

In 2018, Roseanne Barr's self-titled sitcom, Roseanne, had a successful revival. However, Roseanne's Twitter feed caused issues when she criticized Valarie Jarrett, a senior advisor to the president, for her appearance. The tweet was viewed as extremely racist. In the fallout, Roseanne lost her job, and her show was canceled despite being one of ABC's biggest hits of the year. However, less than a month later, ABC announced plans to rebrand the show as The Connors, which has become one of the network's most popular shows.

5 Dave Chappelle

Latest Comedy Special – 'Dave Chappelle: The Unstoppable' (2025) on Netflix

Dave Chappelle has become a staple on Netflix, with the comedian releasing eight Netflix Original specials, the most recent of which was Dave Chappelle: The Unstoppable in 2025. Unfortunately, the latest special continues a trend as Dave, once again, goes after the LGBTQA+ community. But the special focuses mostly on current pop culture and personal stories, from defending his appearance at the controversial Saudi Arabian Riyadh Comedy Festival to the events surrounding Charlie Kirk.

While tackling controversial material throughout his career, Chappelle faced the most backlash through his poking fun at the trans community. His 2021 comedy special, The Closer, included jokes that many people considered to be transphobic. The backlash was bad enough to inspire a walkout by Netflix employees, who demanded that the streaming service take down the special. Yet, the hard push to cancel Chapelle seemed to fall flat as he earned Emmy nominations and went on to win a Grammy Award in 2021 for his work on The Closer, followed by The Dreamer in 2023 and Unstoppable in 2025.

4 Ricky Gervais

Latest Comedy Special – 'Ricky Gervais: Mortality' in 2025 on Netflix

Mortality is Rick­­y Gervais’s most recent Netflix comedy special, arriving at the end of 2025 after his widely discussed 2023 special Armageddon. While Armageddon would attract heavy criticism for its shocking material and contested language, Mortality would represent a more introspective and thoughtful special, as the British stand-up reflected on life and death, blending humor with existential observations. The special has been well received and considered an evolution in Gervais's career, compared with Armageddon, which many felt was a step back and was controversial.

Indeed, some of Gervais's jokes in Armageddon did not sit well with a general audience. Particularly, those regarding inappropriate behavior at a child's funeral and people seeking asylum. The most controversial moments were his jokes about terminally ill children, referring to some as “baldy” and claiming he encourages them through the Make-A-Wish Foundation to wish to get better rather than meeting him in person. Despite the controversy, Armageddon won a Golden Globe for Best Stand-Up Comedy Performance on Television.

3 Louis C.K.

Latest Comedy Special – 'Louis C.K. at The Dolby' in 2023 on louisck.com

Louis C.K. decided to ditch the streaming services and release a duo of comedy specials exclusively on his website. At The Dolby is a classic Louis C.K. comedy. The special was primarily made to reach out to existing fans rather than bring more into the fold, a fact that made sense given the controversy he had previously faced. It is far from his best work, but the framing of it makes sense.

This cancellation was unsurprising to many, as rumors about the comedian's personal life began surfacing as early as 2015. Then, in 2017, The New York Times published a story with five women as sources claiming that C.K. masturbated in front of them or offered to do so. C.K. later admitted that the article was genuine. Afterward, his self-titled show, Louie, was ditched by FX, with Netflix and HBO also cutting ties with the comedian. Louis has spent years slowly working his way back to the good graces of comedy fans, touring, directing, and even releasing a novel. Still, public opinion seems to be split on C.K. and his return.

2 Aziz Ansari

Latest Comedy Special – 'Aziz Ansari: Night Club Comedian' in 2022 on Netflix

He's one of the stand-ups who helped make Netflix one of the top distributors of comedy specials. In 2013, Aziz Ansari released the first Netflix comedy special, Aziz Ansari: Buried Alive. In his last special for the streaming giant, Nightclub Comedian, Ansari delivers a genuinely authentic comedy routine that reminds fans why they love his shows. While he does hit a couple of modern issues, like COVID-19 and vaccinations, the best moments are when he talks about how quickly the world has changed and personal observations on the importance of family.

Following the #MeToo Movement in 2018, a woman accused Ansari of sexual misconduct. The original report stated the woman told Ansari she didn't feel comfortable with his advances, to which Ansari responded with an apology, noting that the encounter was consensual. Still, the report was enough to put his career on hold, with his hit show Master of None being canceled and Netflix suspending plans for future stand-up specials. While Ansari has not made another special since 2022, he has been touring and made his feature directorial debut with Good Fortune in October 2025.

1 Kathy Griffin

Last Comedy Special – 'Kathy Griffin: A Hell of a Story' in 2019 by Fathom Events

Kathy Griffin has no problem picking which side she is on when it comes to Donald Trump, with A Hell of a Story digging pretty deep into her discontent with the commander-in-chief, who at the time of the special was the ex-president. Still, Griffin threw punches at other politicians and the Kardashians while also blending documentary-style elements to illustrate how conservative America has treated her over the years. In 2025, it is outdated in many ways, but at the time of its release, it was a bold statement from the veteran comedian with the very apt title of A Hell of A Story.

In 2017, Griffin posed for a picture holding a mask that looked like former President Trump's decapitated head. This picture was met with backlash, which was the catalyst for Griffin to make A Hell of a Story, without the backing of a TV network or major streaming service. In 2022, Griffin faced further controversy when her Twitter account was temporarily suspended for impersonating Elon Musk. While far removed from 2019, Griffin may yet get another special with the comedian actively touring into 2026 and working on new material.
