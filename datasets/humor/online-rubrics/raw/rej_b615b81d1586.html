Ex-Twitter safety chief stands by banning Babylon Bee for 'misgendering' Rachel Levine

by ALEC SCHEMMEL | The National Desk

Fri, December 2, 2022 at 3:21 PM
Updated Fri, December 2, 2022 at 3:24 PM

CORAL GABLES, Fla. (TND) — The former head of trust and safety for Twitter, who admitted this week that it was a mistake for Twitter to suppress the Hunter Biden laptop story, took a different stance on Twitter’s decision to suspend Christian satire outlet The Babylon Bee for misgendering a public official.

The Babylon Bee was banned from Twitter earlier this year prior to entrepreneur Elon Musk taking over Twitter. Since Musk’s takeover, The Babylon Bee has been allowed to return.

READ MORE:“Censoring Hunter Biden laptop story was a ‘mistake,’ ex-Twitter safety chief admits”

Yoel Roth, who left the company amid the company's ownership change, indicated during a Knight Foundation panel that he agreed with kicking contentious conservative accounts off the platform because they violated Twitter policies.

“You can like the policies or you can dislike the policies, but it’s the same rules for everyone,” Roth told host Kara Swisher. “When you repeatedly tweet violations of a policy there are consequences, including account timeouts, and ultimately they can lead to suspension. And they did.”
“You can change the law if you want to,” Roth continued, “and Mr. Musk clearly did this week. But there was a written policy and it was enforced as written.”

Swisher then specifically asked Roth his thoughts on The Babylon Bee's account.

The satire outlet lost its account for violating the platform’s rules on“misgendering,” the act of identifying someone with pronouns other than those they prefer, or that don’t align with their chosen gender.

READ MORE: “Twitter suspends The Babylon Bee after it named Rachel Levine ‘Man of the Year’”

“I want to start by acknowledging that the targeting and the victimization of the trans community on Twitter is very real, very life-threatening, and extraordinarily serious,” Roth told Swisher in response to her question about The Babylon Bee. “We have seen from a number of Twitter accounts, including Libs of TikTok notably, that there are orchestrated campaigns that particularly are singling out a group that is already particularly vulnerable within society.”
“Not only is it not funny, but it is dangerous, and it does contribute to an environment that makes people unsafe in the world. So, let’s start from the premise that it’s f***ed up.”

As Roth noted in his discussion with Swisher, the act of “misgendering” was – and still remains – a violation of Twitter’s hateful conduct policy, but some users are concerned things won’t remain that way.

Alejandra Caraballo, a Harvard Law School Cyberlaw Clinic instructor and a transgender rights activist, told NBC News she is fearful Musk will remove the policy.

“There’s a plethora of ways that he can just make life hell for trans people on sites like this,” Caraballo reportedly said. “And there will be no way to really moderate or keep it in check.”

Before taking over Twitter, Musk said in a tweet that when he touts “free speech” for Twitter, he only means “that which matches the law.”

“I am against censorship that goes far beyond the law,” Musk continued in the tweet. “If people want less free speech, they will ask government to pass laws to that effect. Therefore, going beyond the law is contrary to the will of the people.”
