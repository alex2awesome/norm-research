The NBA Trade Deadline has passed.

Giannis Antetokounmpo is still in Milwaukee (and Ja Morant in Memphis) but there were a few unexpected blockbusters: James Harden to the Cavaliers, Jaren Jackson Jr. to the Jazz, Anthony Davis to the Wizards. To help you stay on top of all of it, the NBCSports.com NBA crew was on it, putting updates on everything worth knowing in this one place. Just scroll down and get recaps on all the action.

TRADE: Celtics send Xavier Tillman Sr., cash to Hornets

A transaction slipped in after the 3:00 PM Eastern deadline, as the Celtics sent the seldom-used Tillman and $3.5 million to Charlotte. The deal was first reported by ESPN’s Shams Charania.

The purpose of the deal was for Boston to get out of the luxury tax. Tillman had been out of the rotation, averaging 7.9 minutes per game in 14 appearances. And the outlook for him in Charlotte isn’t promising, either.

Nets to waive Cam Thomas

Anyone need a bucket getter?

The Brooklyn Nets held on to Cam Thomas in the hope he could be part of a trade deadline move. That didn’t happen, so the Nets are waiving the scoring guard, making him a free agent, reports Shams Charania of ESPN.

Thomas becomes the first player on this year’s buyout market. Thomas battled injuries this season and has only played in 24 games, averaging 15.6 points per game, but on just 39.9% shooting. Thomas has the reputation around the league of a volume scorer, a guy who can put up points but is not a great playmaker for others, not efficient enough as a scorer, and not a great defender. He has a role in the NBA with the right team, but he’s not for everyone.

TRADE: Bulls get center in deal for Nick Richards

Just after the news came down that the Milwaukee Bucks were not going to trade Giannis Antetokounmpo at the deadline, they made a small trade, sending Cole Anthony and Amir Coffey to the Phoenix Suns for Nick Richards and Nigel Hayes-Davis.

That trade has grown into a three-team deal that brings a new center to Chicago to replace Nikola Vucevic: Nick Richards. Shams Charania had the details.

TRADE: Philadelphia sends Eric Gordon to Memphis

The Philadelphia 76ers needed to create a roster spot so they could convert two-way player Dominick Barlow to a standard contract.

The 76ers have done that by sending Eric Gordon and a 2032 second-round pick to Memphis in a trade. Expect the Barlow contract announcement in the next 24 hours.

TRADE: Hornets trade Tyus Jones Mavericks for Branham

Just before the trade deadline there are always a flood of small trades, usually made for financial reasons but sometimes just because of fit. This is one of those trades, the Hornets made a deal to get a trade exception they can use later, via Shams Charania of ESPN.

The Charlotte Hornets are trading Tyus Jones to the Dallas Mavericks for Malaki Branham, sources tell ESPN. Mavericks have had interest in Jones this season and get him today. Hornets open up a $7 million trade exception.

TRADE: Clippers trade Ivica Zubac to Pacers

Indiana has been going hard after a center (after watching Myles Turner walk in free agency), and they have landed on the most underrated big man in the game.

The LA Clippers are sending center Ivica Zubac and Kobe Brown to Indiana for Bennedict Mathurin, Isaiah Jackson, two first-round picks and one second-round pick, a trade long rumored but broken by Shams Charania of ESPN.

Zubac is averaging 14.4 points and 11 rebounds a game this season, numbers that are slightly down from last season but a key part of that is other teams have focused more on slowing him (especially before Kawhi Leonard got healthy).

From the Clippers’ perspective, they have sold high on Zubac — this is as high as his value has ever been. Part of that value comes from his real connection with James Harden. Zubac could set a pick or be in the dunker’s spot, and his skills were highlighted by Harden’s elite passing. But Harden is now in Cleveland, and Darius Garland is going to be the Clippers’ new point guard (and he looks to score first, although he is an underrated passer).

Next season, when Zubac is setting picks for Tyrese Haliburton in the halfcourt, his value will shine again (even if he is not the floor spacer that Turner was). Zubac may not have the highlight blocks of Turner, but he is a better overall paint defender — he knows how to use his massive frame to clog the lane and makes life difficult for shooters.

The first-round picks the Clippers are getting are good ones: the Pacers 2026 first-rounder (protected 1-4 and 10-30) and the 2029 Pacers unprotected first-rounder. With the 2026 pick, the Pacers currently have the third-worst record in the league and would enter the lottery with a 52.1% chance to keep the pick, 47.9% it goes to the Clippers. If the Pacers keep this pick, it converts to a 2031 unprotected first from Indiana.

Mathurin was a part of the Pacers’ run to the Finals last season, but has been injured this season. When he’s played, he’s averaged 17.8 points a game, shooting 37.2% from beyond the arc.

Combined with the Harden trade, we are watching the Clippers begin a pivot to whatever comes next at the Intuit Dome. Kawhi Leonard loves being back in Southern California, but his future with the team will be a topic of discussion this offseason.

TRADE: Denver sends Hunter Tyson to Brooklyn, teams swap second-round picks

In a move that was about the Nuggets clearing a roster spot to convert two-way player Spencer Jones to a standard contract, Denver is trading trading Hunter Tyson to Brooklyn. As a sweetener, Denver also is sending a 2032 second-round pick to Brooklyn for the least favorable of the Clippers or Hawks 2026 second-round pick.

TRADE: Knicks land Jose Alvarado from the Pelicans

Despite not making the big-swing type of deal at the deadline, the Knicks remain active, trading Dalen Terry and two second-round picks to the Pelicans for Jose Alvarado. The Knicks have been linked to Alvarado for months, and he gives them a tremendous on-ball defender to help back up Jalen Brunson and provide more point of attack defense for a team that desperately needs it, especially with Deuce McBride now out for the remainder of the regular season following sports hernia surgery.

Alvarado is from Queens and went to Christ the King High School, so this is a homecoming for him. He has a player option for next season, so if he were to opt into that and extend his deal, the Knicks could enter the offseason below the second apron, which would give them more flexibility for a big move.

For the Pelicans, this trade was simply about moving a player they no longer had big minutes for in exchange for more draft capital. - Eric Samulski

Bucks tell teams they are not trading Giannis Antetokounmpo at deadline

Giannis Antetokounmpo is staying with the Milwaukee Bucks. At least until the end of the season.

The Bucks have formally told suitors they will not be trading Giannis Antetokounmpo at the trade deadline, reports Shams Charania of ESPN. That was pretty clearly where things were headed when two of the primary suitors for Antetokounmpo — the Golden State Warriors and Minnesota Timberwolves — pivoted in the last 12 hours and started making other trades.

After finally agreeing to listen to offers for Antetokounmpo, the Bucks spent the last couple of weeks taking calls and surveying the market, but often gave other teams the impression this was more of a fact-finding mission, trying to gauge the market for Antetokounmpo, rather than a genuine desire to trade him, league sources told NBC Sports.

These trade conversations will pick up again in the offseason, when more teams — specifically the New York Knicks, Miami Heat and Los Angeles Lakers — would have more draft assets they can trade and more flexibility to make moves. If maximizing their return was the goal, waiting until the offseason to trade Antetokounmpo was always the logical play for the Bucks. However, these kinds of blockbuster deals gain their own momentum and logic sometimes flies out the window.

It will be a little awkward in Milwaukee the rest of the season. Not because Antetokounmpo will be disruptive or a bad teammate, but because it’s in the best interest of the organization to tank and get as high a draft pick as they can. Milwaukee will have the second-best pick of the ones owned by the Pelicans, Bucks and Hawks — right now the Pelicans have the second-worst record in the league and the Bucks the ninth (Atlanta is 14th). Milwaukee should want to tank its way into a better pick, but when Antetokounmpo’s calf gets healthy, he will want to play and win games, not sit out. Those could be interesting conversations.

The Bucks have already moved on, trading Cole Anthony and Amir Coffey to the Phoenix Suns for Nick Richards and Nigel Hayes-Davis, Charania reports.

Warriors plan to keep Draymond Green

The idea of Draymond Green in any uniform other than the Warriors just seems weird. And wrong.

But for the first time, that seemed possible at the trade deadline, with his name figuring prominently in the Warriors’ pursuit of Giannis Antetokounmpo. But with that trade off the table (at least for now), so is Green, who Golden State is keeping, reports Anthony Slater of ESPN.

They have been on a persistent search for a rim-protecting center who can hit 3s and have long believed Porzingis would fit well in the frontcourt next to Draymond Green, whom they now plan to keep despite dangling him in trade talks, sources said.

Maybe Kristaps Porzingis — who the Warriors received as part of the Jonathan Kuminga trade — and Green can pair well together, but that requires Porzingis to be on the court. Illness and injury have limited Porzingis to 17 games this season and 42 last season. It was really KP’s expiring $30.7 million that the Warriors front office wanted.

Golden State made a pick-heavy offer to Milwaukee for Antetokounmpo, four first-rounders, but the player package of Green, Jonathan Kuminga, and Brandin Podziemski was not enough to impress the Bucks, who understandably want a massive haul for the two-time MVP.

Minnesota out of Giannis Antetokounmpo chase (for now)

Much like with Golden State and Jonathan Kuminga, a trade signals that the Minnesota Timberwolves are out of the Giannis Antetokounmpo trade chase. At least until this summer.

Scroll down a little in this tracker, and you can see Minnesota traded Rob Dillingham, Leonard Miller, and four second-round picks to Chicago for Ayo Dosunmu. Dillingham, and likely some of those picks would have been part of any trade with Milwaukee for Antetokounmpo, and this is the kind of trade the Timberwolves had in their back pocket, ready to go if things were not happening with Antetokounmpo.

This is another sign of what we have been reporting here at NBC Sports for weeks — the Giannis Antetokounmpo trade saga was very likely going to drag out into the offseason.

With Golden State also out of the mix, only Miami remains a suitor for Antetokounmpo, but they don’t have enough first-round draft picks to satisfy Milwaukee. It would take a complex three-team construction with Portland to make it happen, and Brian Windhorst of ESPN reports that’s what the Heat are trying to do. That trade remains a long shot.

It makes things a little awkward in Milwaukee the rest of the season when the Bucks will want to tank, and Antetokounmpo will want to play and win, but the potential returns for the Bucks this summer are better from teams like New York, Miami and the Los Angeles Lakers (each would have more draft picks to trade and more financial flexibility). Maybe the Bucks even get lucky in the NBA Draft Lottery and can make a move or two that keeps Antetokounmpo in Milwaukee (unlikely at this point, but not impossible).

Anything can happen in the hours leading up to the deadline, but the smart money remains on Antetokounmpo being a Buck for the rest of this season.

TRADE: Lakers add shooting, trade Gabe Vincent for Luke Kennard

The Lakers went into the trade deadline with clear needs: Wing defense and shooting. Ideally, both of those things in one player, but there are 29 other teams looking for players like that, so at least get one or the other.

The Lakers got shooting, trading Gabe Vincent and a 2032 second-round pick to Atlanta for veteran sharpshooter Luke Kennard, reports Shams Charania of The Athletic.

Kennard is shooting 49.7% from 3-point range this season on 3.2 attempts per game — 62.1% of his shot attempts are from beyond the arc this season. Expect his volume to go up with the Lakers, he should thrive as a kick-out option for Luka Doncic, Austin Reaves and LeBron James. Kennard, however, is a defensive liability.

Atlanta gets depth at point guard, which it needed after trading Trae Young away. Vincent is a solid rotation point guard in the league, but has struggled to stay healthy the past couple of seasons in Los Angeles. —Kurt Helin

TRADE: Bulls trade Ayo Dosunmu to Wolves

A day after sending Coby White to the Hornets, the Chicago Bulls traded away another guard, sending Ayo Dosunmu and Julian Phillips to the Minnesota Timberwolves for Rob Dillingham, Leonard Miller, and four second-round picks.

A trade like this was anticipated, especially after the Bulls acquired guards Jaden Ivey, Anfernee Simons, and Collin Sexton yesterday. Dillingham is another guard to add to the mix. The 21-year-old has produced just 3.5 points, 1.7 assists, and 1.2 rebounds in under 10 minutes per game with the Timberwolves, but he was the 8th overall pick in the 2024 NBA Draft. As the Bulls look to build out their roster for the future, Dillingham joins Ivey and Giddey as early-round draft picks that the team hopes it can build a backcourt around. Leonard Miller should also get more playing time in Chicago than he was getting in Minnesota, where he only appeared in 19 games. At 6'10", Miller is now the tallest player on the Bulls roster, but the wing is certainly not a big body down in the block, so it remains to be seen how Chicago sorts out their rotation.

On the other end, Dosunmu was a sneaky Six Man of the Year candidate in Chicago. He’s averaging 15 points, 3.6 assists, and 3.0 rebounds in 26.4 minutes per game while shooting 45.1% from beyond the arc and 51.4% overall. Dosunmu will likely continue to come off the bench in Minnesota, but adds a much-needed second ballhandler to the team after Mike Conley was traded earlier this week. - Eric Samulski

Warriors out of Giannis Antetokounmpo chase (for now)

Golden State was going to trade Jonathan Kuminga somewhere at the deadline — he demanded a trade and the Warriors were more than ready to move on.

The fact that the Warriors traded him to Toronto is a clear sign Golden State is out of the Giannis Antetokounmpo sweepstakes, according to multiple reports. And just basic logic. Any Warriors trade for Antetokounmpo would have included Kuminga, both because of his salary ($22.5 million) and because he was a young player with potential that the Bucks would theoretically want to take a chance on. The fact that the Warriors pivoted to send him to another team is a sign they are out of the Antetokounmpo sweepstakes. At least until this summer.

Golden State took Milwaukee’s lack of communication as a sign it was not in the mix right now, so the Warriors pivoted to their next move. Also, the Warriors’ package of Green, Kuminga, and Brandin Podziemski was not enough of a player return for the Bucks (although the four first-round picks were the real selling point).

While the Timberwolves and Heat are reportedly still in the chase to try to trade for Antetokounmpo before the deadline, neither of those teams has enough first-round draft picks and good young players to interest the Bucks. Maybe a third team gets looped in, but it gets very complex.

Maybe we get surprised, but it looks like the Giannis Antetokounmpo trade saga will continue into the summer, which was always the most likely outcome. Still feels a little disappointing. —Kurt Helin

Trade: Knicks send Guerschon Yabusele to Bulls for Dalen Terry

Signed by the Knicks to a two-year deal with a player option last summer, Yabusele was expected to provide added frontcourt depth off the bench. Things did not go as planned, with the power forward ultimately falling out of Mike Brown’s rotation. And Yabusele’s time in the Big Apple ended a few hours after Wednesday’s double-overtime win over the Nuggets.

First reported by ESPN’s Shams Charania, the Knicks are trading Yabusele to the Bulls for Dalen Terry.

Yabusele played in 41 games for the Knicks, averaging 2.7 points and 2.1 rebounds in 8.9 minutes. The Bulls are lacking frontcourt depth due to injuries and the Nikola Vučević trade, so the change of scenery may do Yabusele some good.

Terry, a 2022 first-round pick, also struggled to carve out a role for himself, appearing in 34 games this season and averaging 3.5 points, 1.9 rebounds and 1.3 assists in 11.1 minutes. He can provide athleticism and defense off the bench, but Terry’s chances of earning rotation minutes are slim, even if Miles McBride is sidelined for an extended period due to his left ankle injury.

Trade: Warriors send Trayce Jackson-Davis to Raptors

The Toronto Raptors have been looking for a big man, they have landed at least one worth taking a look at for next to nothing.

The Golden State Warriors have traded Trayce Jackson-Davis to the Toronto Raptors for a second-round pick, a deal broken by Shams Charania of ESPN.

Jackson-Davis, the son of NBA player Dale Davis, has played a limited role for the Warriors this season, averaging 4.2 points a night in the 36 games he’s gotten into. The Raptors take a flyer on him, and he’s on a minimum contract with a team option for next season if things work out. —Kurt Helin

TRADE: Warriors trade Kuminga, Hield to Hawks for Porzingis

The five-year roller coaster ride of Jonathan Kuminga in Golden State ends... or just moves on to Atlanta.

The Golden State Warriors have agreed to send Kuminga and sharpshooting wing Buddy Hield to Atlanta for the $30.7 million expiring contract of Kristaps Porzingis, a story broken by Shams Charania of ESPN and confirmed by NBC Sports Bay Area’s Monte Poole.

Kuminga had clashed with coach Steve Kerr and back in January had demanded a trade.

Coach Quin Snyder and the Hawks have a couple of months to see how Kuminga fits alongside Jalen Johnson, Dyson Daniels, and Nickeil Alexander-Walker. Snyder, like Steve Kerr, prizes tempo, ball and player movement, something Atlanta has done more of this season as Trae Young was out (and then was traded). Kuminga was in Kerr’s doghouse, racked up a lot of DNP-CDs, and averaged just 12.1 points per game when he did play. Snyder will give Kuminga a clean slate, we will see what he does with it.

Kuminga has a $24.3 million team option for next season.

The theory of Porzingis would be a great fit for the Warriors — a floor-spacing big man who can protect the rim for Golden State. However, he has played just 17 games this season due to illness and injury, and he has a long. He is on an expiring $30.7 million contract, which is what the Warriors were really focused on. —Kurt Helin

Offseason rumors: LeBron to Cleveland? Draymond Green to Lakers?

With the NBA trade deadline less than 24 hours away, some of the trade and player-movement speculation starts to shift to the offseason. Here are a couple of examples.

The expectation in league circles is that LeBron James will play one more season in the NBA, kind of a farewell tour, but it will not be with the Lakers. If LeBron does play another season, “sources close to him said Cleveland would be an obvious destination,” reports Jason Lloyd and Joe Varden of The Athletic. This echoes what has been the buzz around the league for a while, that LeBron going home to a team that — with Donovan Mitchel and now James Harden should be a threat in the East — makes the most sense. However, LeBron is going to have to take a steep discount from the $52.4 million he is making this season.

A crazier idea was floated: Draymond Green to the Lakers this summer.

This is highly unlikely, and as the guy who floated it — Brian Windhorst of ESPN on “Get Up” — noted, there are a lot of “ifs” here. That didn’t stop him from saying that if the Warriors trade for Giannis Antetokounmpo at the deadline, Green would be part of that trade going back to Milwaukee (which is true, according to multiple reports, the Bucks want Green over Jimmy Butler). However, Green will not want to be on a rebuilding team. If all this happens and Green is looking to force a trade, “the Los Angeles Lakers would be a team he would have interest in, and they would have interest in him,” Windhorst said.

Never say never in the NBA but... this is the long shots of long shots. Let’s ignore for a second the fact that most league sources do not expect Antetokounmpo to be traded before the deadline. If this happens and Green wanted to go to Los Angeles, the Lakers would have to step back and say, “Is an older, undersized five who can’t space the floor as a shooter the big man we want to pay $27.7 million (Green’s salary next season) to be next to Luka Doncic?” Green brings defense, but he’s not a natural fit next to Doncic. —Kurt Helin

Mavericks do not plan to trade Kyrie Irving

This is not a fire sale in Dallas (insert your “that was the Luka Doncic trade” joke here).

Dallas may have traded Anthony Davis, but they have no plans to do the same with Kyrie Irving, reports Christian Clark at The Athletic.

Dallas can go into next season with a core of Flagg, Irving — whom the Mavericks don’t plan on trading before Thursday’s deadline, a team source said — Dereck Lively II, Christie, Naji Marshall and whoever Dallas adds with its first-round pick in June. That is a potentially fun nucleus that isn’t so old and expensive that it will keep hindering the team-building efforts around Flagg.

Charlotte controls Dallas’ first-round pick next season (top two protected), so they are a winner in the trade, too. But with Irving and Flagg, Dallas is going to win some games and be very entertaining doing it. —Kurt Helin

TRADE: Magic trade Tyus Jones to Hornets, duck tax

Welcome to the portion of trade deadline week where the moves are more about financial concerns than basketball or players.

The Orlando Magic have agreed to trade point guard Tyus Jones to the Charlotte Hornets for a 2028 second-round pick and cash considerations, reports Shams Charania of ESPN. The Timberwolves had been ready to part with Julius Randle for Giannis Antetokounmpo, but it likely wouldn’t take as much to land Harden, and the Clippers are focused on maintaining their cap flexibility from 2027 on. — Eric Samulski

Lonzo Ball could join brother in Charlotte

Now that Dennis Schröder and Keon Ellis are in Cleveland, the Cavaliers have a surplus of guards that they can trade from. While shipping Darius Garland to the Clippers for James Harden has been discussed, Marc Stein also reports that the Cavaliers have discussed a trade that would send Lonzo Ball to Charlotte to play alongside his brother, LaMelo.

“Sources say there has been some preliminary conversation between the Cavaliers and Hornets about Ball trade constructions that could furnish Charlotte with second-round draft capital and thus enable the Hornets to pair the elder Ball with his younger brother LaMelo. Charlotte, Brooklyn and Utah, according to league sources, are all teams that have been looking to facilitate multi-team trades or take on unwanted salary in exchange for draft pick compensation.”

Despite injuries to Darius Garland and Max Strus, Lonzo Ball hasn’t really emerged as a key piece in Cleveland’s rotation. He’s averaging 4.6 points, 4.0 rebounds, and 3.9 assists in 21 minutes per game. He’s also shooting a career-worst 30% from the field and 27.2% from beyond the arc with just a 41.5% effective field goal rate. While he has reportedly dreamed of playing in the NBA with his brother, it’s unclear why Charlotte would risk adding a player who is not producing on the court when they are arguably the hottest team in basketball right now. — Eric Samulski

Clippers players react to Harden report

A couple of weeks ago, Tyronn Lue said he expected this to be a quiet trade deadline for the Clippers. He was asked that same question Monday night — just after his team lost to the 76ers.

“I’m not sure,” Lue responded this time.

That’s because news has broken during the game that James Harden wants out and the Clippers are looking to trade the 11-time All-Star before the Feb. 5 trade deadline.

Lue’s Clippers — without Harden, who missed the game for “personal reasons” — came out Monday night and looked distracted in falling behind 16-2 early to the 76ers and never getting within single digits the rest of the night. That said, to a man the Clippers players said postgame that they were shocked by the news and didn’t learn about this until after the game.

“It’s a surprise…" Kawhi Leonard said. “Respect his decision, or whoever’s decision it is. That’s still gonna be my boy. Trust the front office.”

“It definitely was shocking,” John Collins said. “Obviously, I don’t know too much right now. Just gotta continue to see how it unfolds.”

Harden is a Southern California guy who went to Artesia High School just 20 miles from the Intuit Dome, and his coming home to the Clippers was and is a big deal.

“He means a lot, yeah, he means a lot to our team as we’ve seen in the last three years. So he means a lot,” Lue said.

Is Harden the kind of guy Lue would want on his roster for a playoff run?

“Who wouldn’t have James Harden?” he asked.

As for how to deal with the distraction, Lue was pretty old-school and straightforward.

“You gotta be professional, no matter what the rumors may be and what it is,” Lue said. “We got to come out and be professional and play.” —Kurt Helin

Harden for Garland trade in “advanced” talks

James Harden was out for the second straight game for the Clippers Monday night, due to what coach Tyronn Lue said pregame was for “personal reasons,” but didn’t elaborate beyond that. Turns out it was something else.

The Clippers and Cavaliers are in “advanced talks” of a James Harden for Darius Garland trade, a swap of point guards, reports Chris Mannix at Sports Illustrated.

The deal would represent an exchange of former All-Star guards. Harden, 36, has been a workhorse for the Clippers this season, averaging 25.4 points in 35.4 minutes per game. Garland, 26, has battled injuries this season. He missed Cleveland’s first seven games recovering from offseason toe surgery and has been sidelined since Jan. 16 with an ankle sprain. On the season, Garland is averaging 18 points per game.

This is not the only trade the Clippers and Harden have looked at, the two sides are working together to find a trade that works for both sides and are talking to multiple teams, reports Shams Charania of ESPN. The conversation between the Clippers and Harden had been going on for a couple of weeks.

The Cavaliers are asking for a first-round draft pick — or at least a first-round swap — as part of this trade but the Clippers are balking at that price, Mannix reports.

This is all about money, league sources told NBC Sports. Harden has a player option for $42.3 million next season, but only $13.3 million of that is guaranteed (it was a dual option contract). Harden wanted assurances that the Clippers were not going to exercise their option and that he was going to get his full salary next season, plus he wanted to talk extension next summer (Harden is not extension-eligible during the season). The Clippers are looking to pivot out of the Harden/Kawhi Leonard era and did not want to extend him beyond next season. That was what sparked the disagreement.

Cleveland has already given Evan Mobley a max extension, and this summer it can and will extend Donovan Mitchell for up to five years, $273 million. This may be a signal that Garland is the person left standing without a chair when the music stops, plus, due to his ongoing toe injury issues, he has given them very little this season. Harden serves as a shorter-term answer to keep the Cavaliers in contention.

Harden and Garland can be traded for each other straight up under the CBA, despite the Cavaliers being over the second apron because Harden makes about $300,000 less than Garland. Garland has two years and $87 million left on his contract. While the Clippers have not wanted to take back money in a trade that extends beyond next season, they might be willing to make an exception for a 26-year-old All-Star guard (but are they willing to blow up their 2027 cap space plan).

Cleveland is in Los Angeles right now and will take on the Clippers at the Intuit Dome on Wednesday, which makes the timing interesting. Both the Clippers and Cavaliers have been playing their best basketball of late. That said, the Clippers understand they are not a contender in the West and are looking beyond the Harden/Leonard era to what is next.

Cleveland entered the season thinking it should be a contender in the East, but then got off to a sluggish start, and Garland has missed much of the season dealing with a toe injury that dates back to last postseason. That said, the Cavs have climbed up to fifth in the East and in recent weeks have looked as good as any other team in the conference. They have done that without Garland.

How would the ball-dominant Harden and Donovan Mitchell mesh as a backcourt? Harden has meshed with Kevin Durant and other high-volume scorers in the past, but this would not be good for the Cavaliers’ defense. —Kurt Helin

Will Bucks trade Antetokounmpo at deadline?

It’s the question everyone in the NBA is asking: Will the Bucks trade Giannis Antetokounmpo before the Feb. 5 deadline, or will they hold off until the summer?

Nobody knows. The Bucks probably don’t even know — this isn’t a general manager question, this is an ownership question. While sources NBC Sports has spoken to lean heavily toward a summer trade (“maybe 10-20% chance” it happens before the deadline, one front office person told me), other people are saying this is closer to a coin toss. Situations like this have their own momentum, their own logic, and factoring into all of this is the question of what the locker room in Milwaukee would be like the rest of the season if Antetokounmpo is still there and walks back in. The Bucks should be in tank mode, but Antetokounmpo will return and want to play — and this is basically a .500 team this season with him on the court. What happens if the Bucks want to shut him down?

Where do things stand with the Bucks? Here is how ESPN’s Shams Charania summed it up on SportsCenter Monday evening.

“The Bucks are sifting through proposals specifically from the Heat, Wolves, Knicks and Warriors. And throughout the whole process, the Bucks have wanted a young blue-chip talent and or a surplus of draft picks. And so my understanding is the Warriors have made a pick-heavy offer. Minnesota is more of a player-centric offer, and Miami is somewhere in the middle, somewhere between players and picks. And so the Bucks are going to have to choose. Do we take one of these offers?”

Other reports say the Knicks are not being that aggressive at the deadline. Both the Heat and Timberwolves are trying to find three or four-team deals that are more enticing to the Bucks. That pick-heavy offer — Golden State can offer up to four first-rounders and a swap — is considered the frontrunner by many, reports Sam Amick of The Athletic.

None of that answers the question: Will the Bucks pull the trigger on a trade before the deadline? The only answer right now is nobody knows.

While so few transactions have made the going rate for Bulls guard Coby White difficult to gauge, one league source indicated that Chicago has struggled in early attempts to net a first-round pick in a deal that might involve the seventh-year guard. Any reluctance stems from his expiring contract, the potential figure on his next deal and his nagging calf issues this season.

Denver’s deadline plan: Duck the tax

Denver may be the team — once fully healthy — best equipped to push (and maybe beat) Oklahoma City in a seven-game series. Does that mean at the trade deadline they are looking to add that one more player that puts them over the top?

No. Instead, look for them to make a money-saving trade to get below the luxury tax, Bennett Durando reports at the Denver Post.

The team’s aim, as I’ve reported in recent weeks, will be to duck the luxury tax with a minor deal and convert Spencer Jones to a standard contract so he can continue playing, as long as his money aligns with [team owning family] Kroenkes’ end goal. The Nuggets would be able to treat Jones as an upgrade to their power forward depth for the stretch run and the playoffs. Aaron Gordon’s ongoing injury woes have increased the sense of urgency at that position...

The Nuggets have paid the luxury tax three consecutive years, meaning if they finish either of the next two seasons with a payroll that exceeds that threshold, they’ll trigger the repeater tax. This is basically a more dramatic tax penalty imposed on teams based on four-year windows, incentivizing owners not to spend excessively over the salary cap for prolonged periods of time.

Denver is just $400,000 over the tax line right now. They can get under that number with a small trade that does not impact them on the court. Look for that to happen.

(This summer, keep an eye on Denver and its spending when the tax is still an issue and both Aaron Gordon and Christian Braun both see their salaries spike with their extensions, plus Peyton Watson is owed a raise.)

Timberwolves aggressive in Antetokounmpo pursuit

Minnesota has emerged as the most aggressive suitor in pursuit of a Giannis Antetokounmpo trade before the deadline, according to Brian Windhorst of ESPN. Here is what he said on the network’s “Get Up” show Monday:

“The most aggressive team in the West right now is the Minnesota Timberwolves. They are in the game for Giannis. I don’t believe that their offers can outweigh the other offers and I’m not even sure that Giannis is getting traded before Thursday. The Timberwolves are being aggressive. If they don’t make what I would call a Hail Mary Giannis deal, I think they are aggressively looking to upgrade their backcourt.”

Minnesota’s problem is simple: Milwaukee wants a lot of draft first-round picks, and the Timberwolves don’t have any. Straight up, Minnesota’s best offer is something like Julius Randle, Jade McDaniels, Rob Dillingham, and some first-round pick swaps. That’s not going to get it done, which is why the Timberwolves are trying to loop in a third and maybe fourth team — likely Brooklyn and Portland — to secure the picks they need. At some point, Minnesota needs to step back, ask, “We have beaten the Thunder already twice this season, do we really need to blow up our roster and depth for this trade?”

The backcourt move by Minnesota seems more likely. They have been linked to Chicago’s Coby White, among others.

On that same episode, Windhorst said the Knicks “are not showing that aggression to get Giannis right now. And I think that’s because they like this team.” Same as the Timberwolves, the only way the Knicks make this trade work by the deadline is to bring in a third and maybe fourth team to get more picks, and in doing so completely blow up their roster. As evidenced by their win over the Lakers on Sunday on NBC, the Knicks may not need to do that to win the East, given how they are defending lately.

One other Antetokounmpo trade note: The Bucks have made counteroffers to some of the teams that have called about the Greek Freak, reports Shams Charania of ESPN.

Knicks deadline decisions

“The Bucks are looking for a premier young player(s) and multiple, good draft picks in exchange for one of the three best players in the NBA. The Knicks have neither of those, and to get close to what the Bucks are asking for, New York would need to trade two or three players in its starting lineup, most likely OG Anunoby, [Mikal] Bridges and/or [Karl-Anthony] Towns.”

If the Knicks are not going to get Antetokounmpo before the deadline, they have other priorities.

Either man would just be bench help for now, although Missi — just a second-year player — could grow into a larger role. —Kurt Helin

Doc Rivers thinks Giannis stays a Buck. For now.

On ESPN’s NBA Countdown over the weekend, the Milwaukee Bucks coach said what he has always said, that Giannis Antetokounmpo is saying all the right things to his teammates, and that Rivers expects him to be with the Bucks past the deadline.

“Giannis has said everything that we need to hear, that he wants to be a Buck, he loves the city and that’s all I can go by as a coach right now. Has it been difficult? Yeah. My favorite day of the year this year will be the day after the trade deadline. That’ll be my favorite day. I think everyone will be here.”

Rivers may well get his wish, with league sources continuing to tell NBC Sports they expect the Antetokounmpo trade saga to drag out past the Feb. 5 deadline and into the offseason.
