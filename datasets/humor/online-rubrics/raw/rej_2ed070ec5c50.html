'SNL' Stars Talk About Getting Fired From The Show

Ann Casano
Updated July 11, 2025 13 items
Voting Rules

Vote up the Saturday Night Live firing stories that hurt the most.

Lorne Michaels does not like to give bad news. The famed Saturday Night Live executive producer has become known for not directly telling cast members that they have been fired, not even the big stars who had been on the sketch comedy staple for years.

Adam Sandler said he and Chris Farley were both fired and kind of quit at the same time. Chris Parnell got canned not once but twice. Robert Downey Jr. may have been a movie star when he signed on to be a cast member in 1985, but he admits that he was “ill-suited” to be a "Not Ready for Prime Time Player.”

Who else was unceremoniously terminated from SNL? Whose firing story is the most memorable?

    Adam Sandler And Chris Farley Were Both Fired Despite Being Breakout Stars

    Lorne Michaels doesn’t like to give his actors bad news. In fact, sometimes not-ready-for-primetime players do not know when they’ve been fired.

    Despite becoming 1990s SNL legends, both Chris Farley and Adam Sandler were dismissed after five ratings-challenged seasons.

    Sandler talked about getting the axe, along with Farley, in a 2014 interview with The Daily Beast:

    Yes, we were [fired]. We kind of quit at the same time as being fired. It was the end of the run for us. The fact that me and him got fired? Who knows. We were on it for a few years, had our run, and everything happens for a reason. We kind of understood because we did our thing. It hurt a lot at the time because we were young and didn’t know where we were going, but it all worked out.

    Joan Cusack Said She Was 'Miserable' During Her Only Year On 'SNL'

    Joan Cusack was also part of the new revamped cast of actors who joined SNL in 1985. Like Robert Downey Jr. and Anthony Michael Hall, Cusack’s skill set was not in the world of sketch comedy.

    The Sixteen Candles actor was fired from the show after one season. In a 2000 interview with Terry Gross on NPR's Fresh Air, she talked about her negative experience working on the show:

    It wasn't working, and it wasn't working for me too. I was miserable.

    I think I wound up in the hospital, actually. I had, like, some surgery, and it's, like, horrible.

    Anthony Michael Hall Called His Year On 'SNL' A 'Shaky-Legs Season'

    Anthony Michael Hall was another famous actor brought in to revive SNL in 1985. Hall was 17 years old when he signed on and was already a big movie star with memorable performances in The Breakfast Club and Sixteen Candles. 

    Like Downey and Cusack, Hall's acting abilities did not into translate into sketch comedy. He was let go after just one season. 

    Hall gave his honest assessment of his time at SNL in a 2021 interview:

    To be very honest and candid with you, it was not great — it was a shaky-legs season. I'm grateful for the experience, but I didn't have a breakout season. I didn't even have any breakout characters or anything like that.

    Robert Downey Jr. Admits He Was 'Ill-Suited' To Be An 'SNL' Cast Member

    In the mid-1980s, it looked like SNL had lost its mojo. Lorne Michaels returned to serve as executive producer in 1985 after a five-year hiatus. In an effort to shake things up, he hired new established actors like the then 20-year-old Robert Downey Jr. and teenage Brat Packer Anthony Michael Hall.

    However, despite Downey’s immense talent as a dramatic actor, he was ill-suited for the late-night sketch show.

    The Academy Award winner admitted in 2019:

    I learned so much in that year about what I wasn't. I was not somebody who was going to come up with a catchphrase. I was not somebody who was going to do impressions. I was somebody who was very ill-suited for rapid-fire sketch comedy. I was not of that ilk of The Groundlings. I'd never been part of any improv group, so I was kinda like, 'Wow, this seems really hard and like a lot of work.' But I would still say that, to this day, there's not a more exciting 90 minute you can have, whether you are any good or not.

    Gilbert Gottfried Found Out He Was Fired From A Fan Letter

    Gilbert Gottfried’s one-season run on SNL came during a transitional period. The sketch show was in its sixth season, and Michaels had just left along with many of the show’s original cast members.

    Gottfried was an established stand-up comic in 1980 when he signed on. He told Joe Rogan on a 2021 episode of his podcast that he had a “horrible experience” working at SNL.

    He said:

    The season I was on, Lorne Michaels left, and the original cast left. So, people hated the show before it even got on the air.

    I didn’t like the writers, and the writers hated me. One time, to prove how much they hated me, they wrote a funeral sketch where I was the dead body. So, I just had to lay there in the coffin.

    Gottfried revealed that he found out that he was fired when he read a letter from a fan.

    I don’t know [how she knew].

    Jay Pharoah Said 'SNL' Puts 'People Into Boxes'

    Jay Pharoah was one of the best impressionists in SNL history. He expertly crafted repertoire included spot-on impressions of Barack Obama, Jay-Z, and Denzel Washington. The talent was fired in 2016 after six seasons.  

    During a 2017 interview with Hot 97 show "Ebro In the Morning," Pharoah talked about how the show did not adequately use his talent:

    You go where you’re appreciated. If you have multiple people on the cast saying things like, ‘You’re so talented, and you’re able, and they don’t use you. It’s unfair, and it’s making us feel bad because they don’t use you, and you’re a talent…'

    They put people into boxes and whatever they want you to do, they expect you to do.
