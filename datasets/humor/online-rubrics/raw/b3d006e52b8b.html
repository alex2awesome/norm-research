How to Create a Winning Humorous Speech for the Toastmasters Contest

September 1, 2025September 1, 2025

The Humorous Speech Contest is one of the highlights of this Toastmasters year, and for Ozark Orators, the journey begins soon. Our club contest will be held on January 30, 2026, with the winner advancing to the Area Contest and then possibly on to the Division A Speech Contest in March 2026 (date and location to be announced). From there, the top contestant has the chance to move on to represent us at the District level.

To help our members prepare, I’ve created a three-part article series:

  1. How to Create a Winning Humorous Speech – Contest rules, judging criteria, and insider tips.
  2. A Sample Humorous Speech Outline – A practical example to help you structure your own story.
  3. From Practice to Polished – How to rehearse and refine until your speech is contest-ready.

Whether you plan to compete, support a fellow Toastmaster, or simply sharpen your own humor skills, this series is designed to give you the tools, inspiration, and confidence to succeed.

Laughter isn’t just fun. It’s a powerful leadership tool. A well-timed story can break down barriers, build trust, and leave an audience feeling both lighter and inspired. That’s why the Toastmasters Humorous Speech Contest is one of the most anticipated events of the year.

On January 30, 2026, Ozark Orators will host our club-level Humorous Speech Contest. The winner will advance to the Area Contest, with the possibility of continuing on to the Division contest, and possibly even on to the District contest. That means you have months to prepare, refine, and practice. And every Pathways project between now and then can double as practice for your contest delivery.

Here’s your complete guide to creating a humorous speech that conforms with contest requirements and gives you the best chance of winning.

1. Understand the Rules

Toastmasters contests run on clear guidelines. Before writing a single word, know the key rules for the Humorous Speech Contest:

  • Length: 5–7 minutes (plus a 30-second grace period on either end). Timing starts with your first sound or action and ends with your last.
  • Originality: The speech must be substantially original. Quoting others is fine in small doses, but the majority must be your own content.
  • Content: Humor must be central, but speeches are judged on value and structure, not just laughs.
  • Eligibility: You must be a member in good standing of your club to compete.

Knowing the rules helps you aim your creativity in the right direction.

2. Craft a Story, Not a String of Jokes

The contest isn’t stand-up comedy. It’s about a humorous speech: a structured story flavored with humor. To conform with requirements:

  • Open strong: Grab attention with something funny or surprising.
  • Build the body: Tell a story that naturally creates opportunities for humor.
  • Land the ending: Tie your story back to a clear message or lesson. Judges are instructed to reward speech value, not just entertainment.

3. Focus on the Judging Criteria

Every Humorous Speech Contest is judged using the official Judges’ Guide and Ballot. Here’s how the points break down:

  • Speech Development (25%) – Structure, organization, flow.
  • Effectiveness (25%) – Audience engagement, message clarity.
  • Speech Value (15%) – Meaning, originality, contribution.
  • Delivery (15%) – Physical presence, body language, eye contact.
  • Language (10%) – Clarity, grammar, appropriateness.
  • Appropriateness of Humor (10%) – Relatable, well-timed, fits the audience.

Notice that “humor” itself is only part of the scoring. A hilarious speech that’s disorganized or has no point won’t win. To compete, you need the whole package.

4. Use Humor Tools That Work

Humor in speeches thrives on these techniques:

  • Exaggeration: Blow everyday situations out of proportion.
  • Surprise twists: Set up an expectation, then flip it.
  • Callbacks: Refer back to an earlier joke or phrase for bonus laughs.
  • Contrast: Juxtapose serious tone with absurd content.
  • Physicality: Use gestures, expressions, or movement to amplify the humor.

These tools should always serve your story, not distract from it.

5. Keep It Clean, Inclusive, and Appropriate

The rulebook is clear: speeches must be appropriate for all Toastmasters audiences. Avoid:

  • Offensive jokes or stereotypes.
  • Inside jokes that only a few will get.
  • Language that could alienate or embarrass.

Winning humorous speeches are inclusive. They invite everyone in the room to laugh along.

6. Practice With Pathways

Your Pathways projects between now and January 30 are the perfect test bed:

  • Try humorous openings in an Ice Breaker–style speech.
  • Focus on vocal variety and pauses in a project on delivery.
  • Polish your gestures and stage presence in a project about body language.
  • Test entire sections of your contest speech at meetings.

By contest night, you’ll not only have a polished speech, you’ll have practiced each skill that judges will evaluate.

7. Perfect Your Timing

In a humorous speech, timing has two layers:

  • Contest timing: Stay within 5–7 minutes (plus the 30-second grace). Go under or over, and you’re disqualified.
  • Comedic timing: Pause after punchlines. Let laughter land before continuing. Rushing kills humor.

Practice with a timer and also with a live audience. Both will teach you where adjustments are needed.

8. Test and Refine

Laughter is immediate feedback. If people don’t laugh where you expected, refine your setup or delivery. If they laugh in places you didn’t expect, lean into it.

Practice in front of your club, family, and friends. Each time, track what works and what doesn’t. Winning speeches are crafted through iteration.

9. Close With Heart

The best humorous speeches don’t just make us laugh, they leave us with something to think about. Wrap your speech in a takeaway: a moral, a reflection, or a lesson.

Judges are looking for speech value. A laugh without meaning fades fast. A laugh tied to insight is remembered long after the contest.

The Journey to January (and Beyond)

The Humorous Speech Contest isn’t just about earning a trophy. It’s about:

  • Growing as a communicator.
  • Sharpening your leadership voice.
  • Learning how to connect through humor and story.

With the Ozark Orators contest on January 30, 2026, you have a generous runway. Use Pathways projects to practice, refine, and gain feedback. By contest night, you’ll be ready not only to make people laugh, but to make them feel something, too.

And if you advance to Division… and then District? All the better. Every stage is a bigger audience, and a bigger chance to bring joy through your words.
