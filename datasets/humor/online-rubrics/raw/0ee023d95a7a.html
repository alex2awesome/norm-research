Introduction

The Columbia University Rare Book & Manuscript Library (RBML) collects archives of comics creators, editors, and publishers, with an emphasis on New York-area professionals, as well as materials that document the history of comics publishing, in keeping with RBML’s larger focus on the history of publishing.  RBML also collects materials related to comics fan culture, especially convention material, fanzines, and fan mail.  Materials of interest include manuscripts, drafts, original art, roughs and tracings, correspondence, contracts, records, fanzines, artifacts, and other formats in support of research and teaching in comics studies as an interdisciplinary field.

Creator archives

  • Charles Saxon papers This link opens in a new window
    Noted illustrator and cartoonist. Charles David Saxon (1920-1988) graduated with a B.A. from Columbia in 1940. He began his professional career as an editor at Dell Comics; after the war he left Dell and joined the staff of "The New Yorker." His drawings were used in advertisements for a number of corporations. His cartoons and illustrations appeared in such magazines as "Architectural Record," "McCall's Magazine," "Sports Illustrated," "Newsweek," "Town and Country," "Woman's Day," and "Gourmet Magazine."
    more... less...
    Sketches, sketchbooks, scrapbooks, tearsheets, photographs and printed material. The collection includes more than 900 drawings and watercolors. The material covers much of Charles Saxon's professional career. His work was collected in three volumes, OH, HAPPY HAPPY HAPPY! (1960), ONE MAN'S FANCY (1970), AND HONESTY IS ONE OF THE BEST POLICIES (1984).
  • Amram Scheinfeld papers This link opens in a new window
    Journalist, author, professional cartoonist. Scheinfeld (1897-1979) worked as a journalist in Milwaukee, Baltimore, and New York, and was a professional cartoonist in the 1920s and 1930s, before leaving comics to conduct research on heredity.
    more... less...
    The collection includes original art, tearsheets, correspondence, correspondence school exercises, and more.
  • Chris Claremont papers This link opens in a new window
    Chris Claremont is the internationally recognized comic book writer and novelist best known for his work on Marvel's X-Men comic books and, in particular, his 16 year stint writing the book Uncanny X-Men.
    more... less...
    The Claremont collection consists of Claremont's professional papers, including his comic book work and drafts and manuscripts of novels, short stories and screenplays. Claremont's comic-related files include project proposals, scripts, and story ideas; his work with the X-Men franchise and his independent, creator-owned series Sovereign Seven are particularly well represented in the collection. The papers also include Claremont's business records, many of which relate to publicizing and promoting his work, and mail from his readers and fans.
  • Wendy and Richard Pini papers (unprocessed but accessible)

The Wendy and Richard Pini papers include original art for the first 20 years of Elfquest, the complete Elfquest publishing history, materials from Wendy Pini's adaptation of Stormbringer and Masque of the Red Death, drafts of scripts and novelizations, legal records, fanzines, fan art, fan mail, and Wendy Pini's 1976 "Red Sonja" costume.

  • Jerry Robinson papers, 1940-1964 This link opens in a new window
    The Jerry Robinson collection contains a half dozen Batman scripts given to Robinson to illustrate, including one by Bill Finger. These include scripts from the Batman book, but also a script for World's Finest Comics #16 which included Batman as a featured character. In addition to the scripts, the collection includes coursework from high school and Robinson's time as a student at Columbia, as well as notebooks, script ideas, and examples of Robinson's illustrating work outside of the comics. There are also a number of tearsheets for Robinson's newspaper features, and source material for his 1974 book on the history of the comic strip.
  • Al Jaffee papers This link opens in a new window
    Papers contain books by comic artists that have been inscribed to Al Jaffee. Still to be processed: materials for "Snappy Answers to Stupid Questions," examples of Fold-In art and tracings, original illustrations for his biography, advertising work, and more.
  • Howard Cruse papers This link opens in a new window
    The papers of cartoonist, activist, and pioneer Howard Cruse consist of story drafts, process materials, original art, correspondence, and much, much more. This archives complements the Kitchen Sink Press records, which holds a great deal of Cruse's correspondence, as well as editorial records for GAY COMIX.
  • MoCCA Arts Fest Awards of Excellence collection, 2013- This link opens in a new window
    Winners of the MoCCA Arts Fest Award of Excellence, instituted in 2013, when the Society of Illustrators aquired the assets of the former Museum of Comic and Cartoon Art, and assumed organization of the annual comics festival.
  • Pulitzer Prizes collection, 1917-2011 This link opens in a new window
    The bulk of the collection consists of prize-winning exhibits. In many cases, the exhibit is accompanied by an Entry Form, a Letter of Support, Photograph of Winner, Biography of Winner. Subseries II.7 of the Journalism series contains original art, tearsheets, and photostats for the Editorial Cartooning category, 1922-present.
  • S. Clay Wilson papers This link opens in a new window
    The papers of influential underground cartoonist S. Clay Wilson consist of original art (dating back to the age of four), correspondence, contracts, Wilson's own comix library, collaborations with William S. Burroughs, MTV, and other artists, and much more.
  • Stephen DeStefano papers, 1982-2018 This link opens in a new window
    Stephen DeStefano is a Queens-born comics and animation artist who got his first job with DC Comics when he was just 15 years old. His work has been nominated for Eisner Awards (the "Oscars of the Comics Industry") and he has been involved with noted animated series such as "Venture Bros." The archive includes original comic art from his time at DC Comics, including the series he created, "'Mazing Man;" his Eisner-nominated graphic novel, "Lucky in Love;" and storyboards for "Venture Bros.," "Ren & Stimpy," etc., along with early comic art, sketchbooks, minicomics, and more. This collection reveals a working cartoonist's process, and also relates to the Danny Hellman, Bob Fingerman, and New York Comics Community collections. (This collection has not yet been processed.)
  • Danny Hellman papers, 1980-2017 This link opens in a new window
    Danny Hellman is a Queens-born cartoonist and illustrator, and a graduate of the High School of Art & Design. He has drawn for both mainstream and alternative publications, and has drawn and edited comics and comics anthologies. The collection will eventually encompass his original art, sketchbooks (dating back to high school), correspondence, and the legal records of the lawsuit lodged against him by political cartoonist Ted Rall.
  • Comic Art Collection This link opens in a new window
    A teaching collection of original comic art, much of which sheds light on various historical styles or techniques. It includes newspaper strip art, comic book art, comic books, and ephemera.
  • New York comics community collection This link opens in a new window
    A cumulative collection of original art and process materials, creating a snapshot of the work of the comics community in the NYC metropolitan area. It will allow researchers to track who was working here, when, and how.

Other archives

  • Kitchen Sink Press This link opens in a new window
    Kitchen Sink Press, founded by Denis Kitchen in 1969, was one of the pre-eminent publishers of underground comics for over a quarter of a century. It also republished classics of early comic strips and comic books, most notably ernie Bushmiller's Nancy and Will Eisner's The Spirit. The archives consist of over 40,000 letters from every important cartoonist of the time, as well as business records, mechanicals, and more.
  • Dennis Ryan editorial cartoon art collection This link opens in a new window
    Dennis Ryan amassed a collection of nearly 2,200 pieces of editorial cartoon art, dating back to Thomas Nast in the 1880s, up through KAL in the 2000s. Work by giants of the medium, such as Bart, Shoemaker, and Herblock, mingles with that by stars of the funny pages like Frederick Opper and Winsor McCay.
    more... less...
    This collection is not yet processed for use.
  • Jonathan Zeitlin comic books collection This link opens in a new window
    A collection of nearly 1500 comic books, chiefly although not entirely from the 1960s, donated by a School of Library Service alumnus in 1973.
  • John Koukoutsakis collection of comic-con programs, 1959-2013 This link opens in a new window
    Collection contains programs, bulletins, and other promotional material related to comics conventions, primarily in the United States. The San Diego Comic-Con is particularly well represented.
  • Larry Tye papers, 2008-2012 This link opens in a new window
    This collection consists of a range of materials, relating primarily toTye's well-received 2012 book on Superman. The files include, correspondence, subject files, and legal files related to litigation between Superman's creators and Warner Communications, DC Comic's parent company.
  • Mark Estren papers, 1970-1993 This link opens in a new window
    Collection contains research material, notes, and drafts of Estren's 1970 Columbia Journalism School masters essay on underground comics, and his book A History of Underground Comics. The research material includes a number of comic books, both underground and mainstream.
  • Charles Kochman editorial archive
    The papers of Charles Kochman, a long-time editor at Abrams ComicArts, consist of book pitches, drafts, corrected proofs, some original art, and more.
