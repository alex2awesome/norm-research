Bowen Yang (Chinese: 杨伯文; born November 6, 1990)[2] is an American comedian, actor, podcaster, and writer. Yang was a staff writer for the NBC sketch comedy series Saturday Night Live in 2018, and a cast member for seven seasons from 2019 until his departure in 2025.[3][4][5] He became the first SNL featured player to be nominated for a Primetime Emmy Award (in the Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Comedy Series category) in 2021, the same year he was promoted to repertory status.[6][7]

Yang has appeared in the television series Girls5Eva, Ziwe, and The Other Two, and was recurring on Awkwafina Is Nora from Queens. He is also known for his appearances in the LGBTQ romantic comedies Fire Island and Bros, both released in 2022, and in the musical fantasy films Wicked (2024) and Wicked: For Good (2025). He co-hosts a comedy pop-culture podcast, Las Culturistas, with Matt Rogers.[8][9] In January 2019, he was named to Forbes magazine's 30 Under 30 Hollywood & Entertainment list.[10]

Early life and education

[edit]

Yang was born in Brisbane, Queensland, Australia, to a family that had emigrated from China in 1986.[8][11][12][13] His father, Ruilin, was raised in a rural part of the Inner Mongolia region of mainland China, growing up in a straw and mud hut.[14] Yang's mother, from Shenyang, is a retired physician who was an obstetrician-gynecologist in China before working in diagnostics.[13][14][3] The couple moved to Brisbane so Ruilin could earn his doctorate in mining explosives.[14]

Yang has an older sister.[13] The children spoke Mandarin in their home, and attended Chinese Sunday school.[14] When Yang was six months old, the family moved to Canada and eventually settled in Brossard, Quebec,[15] where Yang first discovered Saturday Night Live (SNL). As a child, he was drawn to late-night comedians and hosts David Letterman and Conan O'Brien.[3]

When he was nine, the family moved to the United States, settling in Aurora, Colorado.[11][14][16] At Smoky Hill High School, Yang's calculus teacher, Adrian Holguin, was also his coach for the school's improvisational comedy group, Spontaneous Combustion.[17] He was named homecoming king and also voted "Most Likely to Be a Cast Member on Saturday Night Live".[18][19] Yang graduated in 2008.

When he was seventeen, Yang's father found out his son was gay from an "open chat window" on the family's computer.[3] His parents were not receptive to the news, stating that such things "did not happen in China".[20] Yang's father cried often over the revelation and, being non-religious but wanting to "solve problems", arranged for him to attend eight sessions of gay conversion therapy.[3][20] He agreed to attend the sessions to appease his parents, who said they would otherwise not allow him to move to New York to attend New York University, which his sister was already attending.[13] He was immediately alarmed by the counselor's mix of religion and use of pseudo-scientific reasoning to explain away positive homosexual manifestations.[14][3] In an interview for The New York Times, Maureen Dowd questioned why his parents, both scientists, did not see the disconnection.[14] Yang said, "It was a cultural thing for them, this cultural value around masculinity, around keeping the family line going, keeping certain things holy and sacred." He also said "It was me wanting to meet them halfway but realizing it had to be pretty absolute. It was an either-or thing."[14]

In 2008, Yang moved to New York City and joined his sister at NYU.[13] His father assigned her to chaperone him during this period as Bowen tried "straightness on for size and fail[ed] miserably".[20][21] He came to accept being gay, incorporating it into his comedy, and hoped his parents would learn to accept that aspect of him.[20] They have since found a truce and enjoy a "great relationship'.[3] At NYU, he was in the improv group Dangerbox, and he occasionally performed with Stephanie Hsu, a member of the school's sketch comedy group.[22][23]

Yang was inspired by Sandra Oh's character Cristina Yang on Grey's Anatomy for her neurotic and relentless pursuits, and aspired to be a doctor.[14][24] He went to pre-med classes and graduated from NYU with a bachelor's degree in chemistry.[14][25][17] After realizing he was actually inspired by Oh for her acting ability, he decided to pursue a career in comedy instead.[24] At NYU, he met Matt Rogers, with whom he started Las Culturistas, a weekly comedy podcast where Yang "unapologetically expresses his personality, story and himself by sharing his experiences as a member of the LGBTQ community".[12][24]

Career

[edit]

Early career

[edit]

Yang taught himself Adobe Photoshop and graphic design software and later worked at One Kings Lane, a luxury interior and home design website, from 2013 to 2018 as a graphic designer.[14][26] The company was flexible with Yang's time-off needs for comedy.[14] Yang has also performed improv at the Upright Citizens Brigade.[27] During this time, Yang designed graphics for his own shows and for his friends' comedy shows.[26]

The podcast that Yang co-hosts with Matt Rogers, Las Culturistas, is described by Vulture as both "delightfully screwy" and a "two-headed snark routine".[28] The podcast premiered in 2016 and as of September 2019[update], has over 300 episodes.[29] Each one opens with an interview with a pop culture guest, then goes to one-minute rounds of "I Don't Think So, Honey!" (IDTSH) where the hosts and guests each expound on pet peeves.[28] IDTSH has also morphed into its own live show.[28] Yang credits the Las Culturistas podcast with Rogers for building his fanbase. In 2018 it was nominated for a Shorty Award recognizing the best in social media.[8][30][31]

Yang appeared in shows such as Comedy Central's Broad City, a Vimeo web series The Outs, and the HBO web comedy High Maintenance.[10][32][33] He was a supporting cast member in the 2019 film Isn't It Romantic. Yang performed stand-up on HBO's 2 Dope Queens.[32] He played fashion designer Alexander Wang in a sketch series on Comedy Central, Up Next.[34] In 2019, Yang received press coverage for his viral Twitter posts consisting of "expertly-timed lip-sync videos of famous movie scenes", in which he "reproduces dialogue from diva scenes" and well-known moments in popular culture. Past videos featured a monologue by Miranda Priestly in The Devil Wears Prada, Tyra Banks yelling at contestant Tiffany Richardson on America's Next Top Model, and a viral video of Cardi B talking about the 2019 government shutdown.[14][24] Each garnered thousands of likes and retweets.[9][35]

Saturday Night Live

[edit]

Staff writer: 2018

[edit]

In 2018, Yang was hired as a staff writer on Saturday Night Live for the show's 44th season.[3] He said he "always loved SNL growing up, but had trouble imagining himself on the show, because he'd never seen people who looked like him associated with the series".[36] Yang has writing credits on 21 episodes of the show for the 2018–2019 season.[29] His writing included: "GP Yass", a play on a vehicle's GPS navigation device that utilizes drag queens to deliver driving directions; and two sketches co-written with Julio Torres, which features Yang's talent for infusing "drama, tension, and exquisite backstory" into an everyday activity like paying bills in "Cheques" with Sandra Oh, and an actress doing a cameo in a gay pornography film, "The Actress" with Emma Stone.[32] "The Actress" was hailed by Out as the "gayest SNL sketch of all time", and featured Stone as an earnest method actress taking her role as a cheated-on housewife too seriously alongside real-life gay porn actor Ty Mitchell.[37][38] The pre-tape—so-called as it is filmed days ahead rather than acted live—was championed by Stone to be included on air.[37][38] SNL creator Lorne Michaels knew Yang would be an on-air talent but wanted him to be comfortable on their stage first.[3] Yang made a cameo appearance during the Sandra Oh/Tame Impala episode as North Korean leader Kim Jong-Un while Oh played his translator.[39]

On-air cast: 2019–2025

[edit]

In September 2019, Yang was promoted to featured player for the 45th season, alongside improviser Chloe Fineman, both of whom were promoted to repertory status at the start of the show's 47th season in September 2021.[4][7] Yang is the show's first ever Chinese-American cast member, and third gay male cast member after Terry Sweeney and John Milhiser.[40][41]

SNL has had "little representation from Asian actors, as cast members or hosts" over several decades.[24][39] Up until Yang's promotion, there had been only three cast members,[a] and six hosts who were of Asian descent.[b][24][36] A 2016 study of SNL revealed that 90% of 1975–2016's show hosts (826 total) were white, 6.8% were black, 1.2% were Hispanic, and 1.1% were labelled "other".[42] Similarly, SNL has had comparatively low representation of LGBTQ on-air cast and guest hosts since the series started in 1975. Yang is the third gay male, and sixth LGBTQ cast member.[c] The announcement of Yang's casting on Saturday Night Live was reported internationally, and within hours was overshadowed by revelations that comedian Shane Gillis, who had been hired at the same time, aired homophobic and anti-Asian jokes.[48][49] Gillis issued an apology, but within days was fired by SNL.[50][49]

Yang's first episode as a regular cast member was the season's opening episode September 28, 2019, with host Woody Harrelson.[51] Notably he was included in the show's cold open playing Kim Jong-un giving advice to Trump on handling the Ukraine controversy including the whistleblower who helped trigger the 2019 impeachment hearings.[51] In other sketches, he portrayed Democratic presidential candidate Andrew Yang in a parody town hall debate, and was an extra in a mock movie trailer for Downton Abbey.[51] In October 2019, Yang made his debut on Weekend Update (WU), as Chinese trade representative Chen "Trade Daddy" Biao in a segment about Donald Trump's trade war that was "brief, funny and took some clever satirical shots".[52][14] Yang's Biao character returned to WU as the newly appointed health minister for the COVID-19 pandemic which he unconvincingly tries to assure China has in control.[53] Perhaps his "filthiest" sketch, also co-written with friend Julio Torres, was for guest host Harry Styles as an incompetent Sara Lee Corporation social media manager who mixes up his own gay BDSM account on Instagram with the company's "wholesome bread brand".[14] He was listed No. 2 in Variety's Power of Pride list of most influential queer artists in Hollywood in 2021.[54] The same year, Yang was nominated for a Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Comedy Series. He is the first featured player to ever be nominated. He also played NBA hall-of-famer Yao Ming in January 2022.[55] Yang also played embattled Congressman George Santos in multiple sketches in which he received critical praise.[56][57]

In 2021, Yang was applauded for speaking out on the recent surge in violence against Asian-Americans[58] during a Weekend Update segment. He told audiences to "fuel up" (using the Chinese cheer Jiayou) and do more for Asian Americans. Also in 2021, he appeared on the Time 100, Time's annual list of the 100 most influential people in the world.[59]

Yang departed the SNL cast in the show's 51st season,[5] with a farewell sketch during the December 20, 2025 Christmas episode.[60]

Other work

[edit]

Yang plays Nora Lin's mobile app-developer cousin in the Comedy Central sitcom Awkwafina Is Nora from Queens, which premiered in January 2020 and was renewed for a third season in 2022.[61][62][63][64] Yang is a writer on the Apple TV+ musical comedy series Schmigadoon!, which stars his former SNL castmate Cecily Strong.[65] In September 2021, he appeared as a guest on the NPR news quiz Wait Wait... Don't Tell Me! In 2022 he starred in Fire Island, written by and co-starring his close friend Joel Kim Booster, whom he met at the beginning of his comedy career.[66] He appeared in the 2022 film Bros and is part of the cast of Dicks: The Musical with Aaron Jackson, Josh Sharp, and Megan Thee Stallion.[67] Yang appears as Pfannee in the first part, released in 2024, of the two-part film adaptation of the musical Wicked.[22] He reprised the role in its sequel, Wicked: For Good, the following year.[68][69]

Yang is a signatory of the Film Workers for Palestine boycott pledge, which was published in September 2025.[70]
