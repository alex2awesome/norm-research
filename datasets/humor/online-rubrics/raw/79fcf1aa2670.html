The Best Stand-Up Specials of 2025 (Part 2)

PLUS The Golden Globes & What Didn’t Make the Cut

John McDermott

The Best Stand-Up Specials of 2025 (Part 2):

Welcome back. In Part 1, I ran through my Top 16 and the Middle Tier specials. Now let’s talk about the Golden Globes nominations, what didn’t make my list, and what’s still coming.

The Golden Globes Nominations

The Golden Globes nominees for Best Performance in Stand-Up Comedy on Television are:

  • Bill Maher (Is Anyone Else Seeing This?)

  • Brett Goldstein (The Second Best Night of Your Life)

  • Kevin Hart (Acting My Age)

  • Kumail Nanjiani (Night Thoughts)

  • Ricky Gervais (Mortality)

  • Sarah Silverman (Postmortem)

Here’s my dilemma: Kevin Hart’s special is funnier than Sarah Silverman’s, but Sarah’s is better art. Of course, the right answer is Marc Maron should have been nominated instead of any of them, but here we are.

If I had to pick from this list? I’m struggling. Kevin Hart made me laugh more consistently, but Sarah’s craft is undeniable. Nanjiani isn’t out yet, and good luck getting anyone from Hulu to return an email, a filled out form, or a Linkedin connection.

The Globes continue to be the Globes - nominating the names they know rather than necessarily the best work. Maron’s “Panicked” was head and shoulders above everything on this list, but I guess HBO didn’t campaign hard enough or maybe not at all knowing Maron.

Not On The List

These are the specials that people talked about but didn’t make my list. That doesn’t mean they’re terrible - they’re just... not for me, or not good enough to crack the top tier. In no particular order:

Bill Maher: Is Anyone Else Seeing This? (HBO) - Maher continues to do his “I’m the only one brave enough to say this” energy when he’s really just saying what half the country is already thinking. There’s brave comedy, and then there’s just being predictable while calling yourself brave.

Andrew Schulz: LIFE (Netflix) - Schulz’s pregnancy/IVF special filmed at the Beacon Theatre didn’t work for me - the storytelling felt off and I couldn’t get into the whole journey.

Rosebud Baker: The Mother Lode (Netflix) - The pregnant/not-pregnant gimmick of cutting between two performances months apart is clever editing, but the format distracted more than it added.

Chris DiStefano: It’s Just Unfortunate (Hulu) - Hack. That’s my review of this special. Overall, I found the Hularious brand to be where comedians go when they are on their way down.

George Lopez: Muy Católico (Amazon) - Horrific. I can’t believe George released this and I say that as someone who might have George on my list of best stand-ups on the century (so far), a list I am still working on.

Liza Treyger: Night Owl (Netflix) - Like the energy, didn’t love the material.

Iliza Shlesinger: A Different Animal (Amazon) - Watch this one and pay attention to how she recites her act with no regard whatsoever to how the crowd reacts. It’s like a robot performing comedy.

Bill Burr: “Drop Dead Years” (Hulu) - Even Burr has off nights. We were supposed to come away impressed by how enlightened Bill has become. Instead, it’s the main example of how Hilarious is where you guy once you’ve been to the peak.

Steph Tolev: Filth Queen (Netflix) - Title says it all. Filth isn’t my thing.

Devon Walker: LOL Live (Hulu) - Had one good Chris Rock joke, that was about it.

Ralph Barbosa: Planet Bosa (Hulu) - I sometimes say of specials, “Civilians will like it.” I’m a comedy snob and I never felt this one. I like Barbosa so in his case I hope I am wrong about my Hulu Specials Theory.

Joe List: Small Ball (YouTube) - I didn’t believe a word was true, and I need believability from my comedians. I understand all of these are a performance, and I understand exaggerating for comedy, but I need some real in there somewhere.

David Spade: Dandelion (Amazon) - Started too slow and I lost interest.

Sebastian Maniscalo: It Ain’t Right (Hulu) - The trailer for this radicalized me. Sebastian, enough with the stupid faces. The actual special made me realize that Sebastian’s act is Old Man Dice Clay At 60.

Jim Gaffigan: Bourbon Set (YouTube) - Not his best work, but over 3.5 million views on Youtube suggest I’m a loser who does a podcast in the basement so Jim wins this round, even if he’s going to sell you the round to promote his bourbon.

The Rest Of The Year

There are still huge specials coming in December. The nature of these end of year lists is that they need to go out on time so my publicist can do something with them before everyone breaks for the holidays, so that’s why I jumped in the pool this weekend.

I’ll likely do a revised version of this the first week of January when I’ve actually seen everything, and I think it’s likely most of these make the revised list in some form.

Sarah Sherman: Live + in the Flesh (HBO Max) - this actually came out Friday night and I watched it last night! I was wondering if casual SNL fans would be ready for Sarah Squirm. Based on the reaction of my podcast’s Facebook Group they are not. I was really enjoying it for the first twenty minutes, although it was dirtier than I usually like my comedy, and my brain felt like a LOT was being thrown at me. Then she went into a bit that dragged and my finger hit that exit button on the remote. Might make it into that gray region between the Top 25 and today’s “Not On List” list.

Robby Hoffman: Wake Up (Netflix) - Robby is really funny, and this is produced by John Mulaney. Out today.

Kumail Nanjiani: Night Thoughts (Hulu) - December 19, 2025. Vulture has this as their best of the year!!! But I keep seeing the word Hulu and the trailer wasn’t great. Also nominated for a Golden Globe. Hmmm.

Tom Segura: Teacher (Netflix) - Christmas Eve release. Won’t get the media coverage it might at a different time of year.

Ricky Gervais: Mortality (Netflix) - December 30th. Expect the usual debate about Ricky saying things he “shouldn’t” and Ricky doubling down. Also expect it to be funny.

Final Thoughts

2025 was a solid year for stand-up, even if it wasn’t spectacular. We got some genuinely great hours from Maron, some crowd-pleasing stuff from Hart and Rife (regardless of what critics think), and a bunch of solid middle-tier work from comedians who know what they’re doing.

The other trend? Comedians going to YouTube. Gaffigan got over 3.5 million for his Bourbon set. Gianmarco Soresi putting “Thief of Joy” straight to YouTube rather than waiting for a streamer deal is smart - he owns it, he controls it, and with 1.3+ million YouTube subscribers, he’s got the audience. I suspect we’ll see more of this in 2026.

As for the streamers - Netflix is still king, but they’re clearly pivoting toward populist comedy (Rife, Hart, Gillis) over prestige stuff. HBO/Max continues to make smarter picks (Maron, Goldstein, Sherman), even if they don’t get the same viewership numbers. Amazon has money but no taste. And Hulu….be afraid when your favorite comedian takes their special to Hulu.
