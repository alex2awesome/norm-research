Ah, Just For Laughs New Faces. It’s one of several accolades I covet (alongside Don’t Tell Comedy Taping Recipient and Starting 3rd Basemen for the New York Mets).

Each year, thousands of comedians submit to be considered for the illustrious industry showcase in Montreal. Last year, talented folks (and alumni of my monthly show Hot Gossip) like Conor Janda, Maggie Crane, Dylan Adler, and newly minted SNL writer Jo Sunday got tapped. Other past recipients include Shane Gillis, Kevin Hart, Amy Schumer, and many other stars of our time. But if you read this newsletter, you probably already know this.

So, the instructions for stand-up comedians who are submitting are as follows:

“Please submit a recent 6-minute set (ideally something from the past 12 months). Note: Do not submit an edited reel, or ‘best of’ collection. We are looking for one continuous 6-minute, uninterrupted set. Note that comedians will be free to perform other material at the audition if they so choose.”

Simple enough, but I have a large swath of material to choose from. So what’s the best distillation of me? Is it the jokes only I can tell? Is it the material I’m currently highest on?

I considered this all and more before eventually settling on a set that, if it doesn’t get me an audition, is at least a set I am proud to have submitted.

Studying Others’ Sets

In preparation, I watched a handful of sets from other comedians who were selected for JFL or other similar industry showcases, just to see if I could pick up on any trends. There were a handful of comedians for whom this exercise was not even remotely helpful, like Ryan Goodcase’s Netflix set (that dude is one of one), but for the others, there were definitely some overlapping themes.

Lots of people talking about their family. Lots of people talking about where they’re from, how many siblings they have, their mom’s weird quirk, etc. Quite a few people talking about their sexuality. People talking about dating, of course.

Truthfully, the biggest takeaway from watching these sets was that 1) You’ll never go wrong by being unabashedly yourself. Do whatever is your truest identity. 2) If you’re going to do a joke about Hinge, it better be the best joke about Hinge I’ve ever heard. 3) There are a LOT of funny people in the world and only a handful of them get selected for Just For Laughs.

What Material to Include

So when I headline on the road, my setlist ~typically~ boils down to these six sections.

  • Intro/travel (~5 minutes)

  • About me (10 minutes)

  • Local material (7-10 minutes)

  • Fox News/MSNBC/politics (~10 minutes)

  • Public school (~10 minutes)

  • Arrest story (~10 minutes)

Bada bing, bada boom. Factoring in a little crowdwork, that gets me to 60 minutes, easy peasy. But how do I distill that down to my six best minutes?
