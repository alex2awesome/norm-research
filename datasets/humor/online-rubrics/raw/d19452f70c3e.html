Free Comedy Writing Templates

May 7, 2026by Tinsel Vandergraph

Free Comedy Writing Templates

Because even your chaos needs a format.

Welcome to the Joke Architect’s Toolbox

So, you’ve got the funny. Great. But do you have the structure?
Because while comedy is art, writing it is blue-collar labor with a word processor and a god complex.

That’s where templates come in—your scaffolding, your invisible foundation, your excuse to scream “I’m being productive!” while wearing pajama pants at 2 p.m.

This guide includes downloadable templates (or replicable formats) for:

  • Stand-up comedy sets

  • One-liner joke sheets

  • Sketch scripts

  • Sitcom scene breakdowns

  • Improv character bios

  • Roast writing outlines

  • Satirical article structures

We also include instructions, jokes, and real-world satire strategies used by fake writers who have achieved real success.

1. Stand-Up Set Blueprint (Tight 5 Format)

TEMPLATE:

Title: “This is Why I’m Emotionally Unavailable”
Persona: Awkward yet oddly confident millennial
Opener: [One-sentence intro joke]
Joke Cluster #1 – Topic: Dating Apps

  • Setup

  • Punchline

  • Tag

  • Callback prep
    Joke Cluster #2 – Topic: Family Trauma (Funny!)
    Joke Cluster #3 – Topic: Self-hatred vs Retail Therapy
    Callback to Joke #1
    Closer: [Big laugh line + emotional twist]

How to Use:

  • Group your bits by theme

  • Build toward emotional vulnerability or total chaos

  • Test each cluster separately at open mics

“A tight five is like a first date. Leave them laughing and wanting more—not crying in a Red Lobster.” — Ron White

2. One-Liner Writer’s Grid

For writing jokes like Mitch Hedberg if he microdosed anxiety instead of mushrooms.

Setup Punchline Tags
I joined a gym… …but only to access the vending machine. “Now I curl protein bars.”
I tried online dating… …but the CAPTCHA asked if I was emotionally ready. “I failed.”

Hot Tip:

Do 10 per day. Only 1 will be funny. That’s the rule.
Comedy = Rejection x Volume + Repetition².

3. Sketch Comedy Script Template

vbnet

TITLE: “The Emotional Support IRS Agent”

INT. DMV – DAY

Characters:
– Agent Johnson (straight man)
– Rita (deranged tax therapist)

JOHNSON:
Please state your business.

RITA:
I’m here to deduct my ex-husband as a dependent. Emotionally, he’s still mine.

[Game established: Rita uses tax code as emotional therapy. Heighten.]

BUTTON:
JOHNSON (crying): Fine. I’ll file under “weirdly specific trauma.”

END.

Sketch Structure:

  1. Premise: Real setting, one absurd twist

  2. Game: Repeating the absurdity

  3. Escalate

  4. End with a bang, not a sigh

4. Sitcom Scene Breakdown Sheet

Scene Planner:

Episode: “I Didn’t Mean to Buy a Llama”
Setting: Kitchen, late night
Characters:

  • DANIELLE: Rational but sleep-deprived

  • JAMAL: Delusional online shopper

Conflict: Jamal bought a llama during a sleepwalking episode
Goal: Danielle needs Jamal to return it before HOA fines them again
Turns:

  • Jamal argues llama brings “emotional balance”

  • Llama licks toaster, shorts out kitchen
    Resolution: They agree to rebrand the llama as an “emotional appliance”

Bonus:

Include a B-plot about a haunted Keurig machine for emotional layering.

5. Improv Character Bio Card

Name: Sharon Dinglesworth
Age: “Thirtysomething in wombat years”
Profession: Ferret hypnotist
Status: Just got dumped by a sock puppet
Objective: Prove to her hometown that raccoons can vote
Quirk: Only speaks in IKEA instruction metaphors

Use This To:

  • Jump into any improv scene with more flavor than your roommate’s cooking

  • Confuse your teammates into brilliance

“In improv, character is everything. Especially when plot goes missing like your dad in 2008.” — Amy Schumer

6. Roast Writing Scaffold

Roasts must be mean, clever, and just barely legal.

Template:

Target Name: Todd “The Human Vape Cloud” Jenkins
Intro Joke: “Todd’s here tonight, fresh off his shift as a failed NFT.”
Physical Jab: “Todd looks like what happens when a beard gives up.”
Career Insult: “He once got fired from Fiverr.”
Callback Joke: “If Todd were a drink, he’d be a lukewarm IPA with daddy issues.”
Closer: “We love you, Todd. Without you, there’d be no one here to make me look humble.”

Rules:

  • 60% insults

  • 30% jokes

  • 10% plausible deniability

7. Satirical Article Format (Ahem…)

Title: “Florida Appoints Alligator as Education Secretary”
Subtitle: “Critics claim he eats too many students; supporters say he’s efficient.”
Byline: Written by a possum wearing glasses.

Article Flow:

  1. Absurd Headline

  2. Fake but grounded facts

  3. Public reaction (quotes from fake sources)

  4. Policy analysis via parody logic

  5. Quote from someone named Chad

  6. Final irony twist + callback to headline

“If it reads like The Onion but tastes like legal trouble, you’re doing it right.” — Groucho Marx (ghost)

What the Funny People Are Saying

“Writing without a template is like skydiving without pants—you’ll survive, but it won’t be pretty.” — Jerry Seinfeld
“Every joke needs bones. Just make sure they’re not your own.” — Larry David
“I wrote my first special using a napkin and anxiety. Now I use Google Docs and more anxiety.” — Sarah Silverman
“Templates are the adult coloring books of comedy.” — Billy Crystal
“The greatest jokes were written on cocktail napkins. The second greatest were formatted in Helvetica.” — Groucho Marx

Final Thoughts: Use These. Abuse These. Then Write Your Own.

Comedy is math with feelings. Templates help organize the chaos before the muse shows up high and late.

Once you get the rhythm, you’ll build your own formats that fit your voice like a sweaty glove of laughter and trauma.

And when someone says, “But comedy isn’t supposed to be structured,” show them your sketch outline, your beat sheet, and your mental breakdown notes.

Then punch them in the timing.

Auf Wiedersehen, and happy formatting.

Whether you’re laughing with us or at us, we’re doing our job. Because in a world that’s already a punchline, someone needs to write the setup.
