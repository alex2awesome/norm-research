• Abbi Jacobson and Ilana Glazer

    Popular Broads
    IMPACT: The inaugural season of “Broad City,” Jacobson and Glazer’s hit scripted comedy series drew more than just laughs — the oddball odd-couple pairing of the two women gained raves and was one of the most well-reviewed shows on Comedy Central in the network’s history.
    NEXT: The second season of the series launches in January.

  • Adam DeVine, Blake Anderson, Anders Holm and Kyle Newacheck

    The “Workaholics”
    IMPACT: DeVine, Anderson, Holm and Newacheck, who have worked as the Mail Order Comedy sketch troupe since 2006, co-created the Comedy Central series “Workaholics,” which has built a strong young-male audience since its 2011 debut. Breakout DeVine parlayed the series into a recurring role last year on “Modern Family,” as well as Comedy Central’s “Adam DeVine’s House Party.”
    NEXT: The series has been renewed through its fifth season. DeVine will appear in “Pitch Perfect 2” in May, and Holm has a pivotal role in Chris Rock’s “Top Five.”

  • Amy Poehler and Tina Fey

    Bosom Buddies
    IMPACT: Former “SNL” stars recently completed filming “Sisters,” and now prep for a third consecutive stint co-hosting the Golden Globes. Poehler’s memoir “Yes Please” hit No. 1 on the New York Times bestseller list (following in the footsteps of Fey’s “Bossypants,” still selling strong).
    NEXT: Poehler’s run on NBC’s “Parks and Recreation” ends next year, and her Paper Kite Productions shingle boasts Comedy Central’s “Broad City” and NBC’s “Welcome to Sweden.” Fey begins filming “The Taliban Shuffle” and exec produces Netflix’s “Unbreakable Kimmy Schmidt.”

  • Amy Schumer

    Dirty Girl
    IMPACT: It’s enough that Schumer has a thriving standup career and is one of the few female comics with her own sketch show, “Inside Amy Schumer,” on Comedy Central. But a 2014 Primetime Emmy nom for her series and a second season that was better reviewed than the first is keeping her self-deprecating, uninhibited humor on top.
    NEXT: She scripted and stars in the 2015 release “Trainwreck” for director Judd Apatow, and “Inside Amy Schumer” has been renewed for a third season.

  • Andy Samberg (Alumnus)

    ‘Brooklyn’ Boy
    IMPACT: The former “Saturday Night Live” cast member has already earned a Golden Globe for his first season as Detective Jake Peralta on Fox’s “Brooklyn Nine-Nine,” on which he also serves as producer. He also wrote the awesome “Everything Is Awesome” song for this year’s “The Lego Movie” with his Lonely Island cohorts.
    NEXT: He’ll star in and produce HBO’s sports mockumentary “7 Days in Hell” about a fictional tennis rivalry that results in a weeklong match between two opponents.

  • Armando Iannucci and Julia Louis-Dreyfus

    Executive Team
    Impact: Iannucci’s biting, cringey and LOL satire has found the perfect vessel in Julia Louis-Dreyfus as “Veep.” HBO’s political send-up is dripping with awards for writing and acting, and Louis-Dreyfus has won three Emmys for her portrayal of Selina Meyer. She earned stellar reviews for her turn in “Enough Said” last year. Iannucci returned to Brit screens in 2013 with the “Alan Partridge” movie, the latest iteration of the character co-created by Steve Coogan.
    NM: In 2015 we’ll see another season of “Veep,” which continues to attract top tier comic talent — Hugh Laurie is one of the guest stars in the coming season.

  • Bill Lawrence

    Showrunner Extraordinaire
    IMPACT: The wine-swilling gang of Lawrence’s “Cougar Town” may be drinking its final round this season, but he’s left behind a legacy of in jokes, from oversized wine glasses to the penny can. The “Scrubs” creator also exec produces TBS’s “Ground Floor” and NBC’s “Undateable,” both renewed for second seasons — that is, when he’s not dispensing questionable advice via Twitter.
    NEXT: He’s got five projects in development via his overall deal with Warner Bros: one at NBC, one at ABC, and three at CBS, including “Rush Hour,” an hour-long comedy based on the hit feature franchise.

  • Billy Crystal

    Comic Legend
    IMPACT: As busy as ever, Crystal reprised the beloved Mike in “Monsters University” in 2013, and was nominated for an Emmy for his HBO special “700 Sundays,” which was adapted from his Broadway hit and bestselling 2006 book. His latest tome, “Still Foolin’ ’Em: Where I’ve Been, Where I’m Going, and Where the Hell Are My Keys?,” came out in paperback in September.
    NEXT: Crystal will star in “The Comedians” with Josh Gad for FX, his first series regular role since “Soap” ended in 1981.

  • Billy Eichner

    Lighting up Fuse
    IMPACT: A scene-stealing appearance opposite host Seth Meyers at this year’s Emmy awards boosted the profile of the cult favorite mastermind behind the Funny or Die gameshow “Billy on the Street,” which airs on Fuse, as did a recurring role on NBC’s “Parks and Recreation.” Eichner penned an uproarious musical ode to pop tart Taylor Swift, entitled “Glitter and Ribs,” that he recently performed live on “Conan.”
    NEXT: More “Billy on the Street,” more “Parks” (in its final season) and more TV guest spots including “New Girl.” Plus, starring in his very own scripted series for Hulu, “Difficult People,” exec produced by Amy Poehler.

  • Channing Tatum and Jonah Hill

    ‘Street’ Kids
    IMPACT: Tatum and Hill bested their box office performance with the “22 Jump Street” sequel, which has earned more than $330 million worldwide since its June release — a full $130 million more than the first. Their off-screen friendship translates into an on-screen buddy-cop chemistry that keeps the R-rated laughs coming.
    NEXT: Tatum and Hill remain established dramatic actors, but no need to worry about them taking themselves too seriously. There’s a “23 Jump Street” on the way.

  • Chelsea Handler

    Showgirl
    IMPACT: Since her E! talk show, “Chelsea Lately,” debuted in 2007, Handler has been the singular female voice in late night, augmenting her TV career with standup and a series of bestselling books, through her own Borderline Amazing imprint. Before she ended the show in August, she was among the many rumored as a replacement for David Letterman on “Late Show.” Instead, in June Handler announced a multi-tiered deal with Netflix, including a talk show in 2016.
    NEXT: Her Netflix partnership launched with her October standup special, “Uganda Be Kidding Me,” and includes four docu specials in 2015.

  • Chelsea Peretti

    To the Nines
    IMPACT: Peretti has contributed to comedy behind the scenes as a writer for years on series like “Parks and Recreation,” “Saturday Night Live” and “The Sarah Silverman Program.” However, playing the self-absorbed police chief’s assistant with the deadpan drawl Gina Linetti on “Brooklyn Nine-Nine” has taken her to a new level.
    NEXT: Peretti’s Netflix special, “One of the Greats,” premiered last month, and she continues to work as a writer and actress on Comedy Central’s “Kroll Show.”

  • Chuck Lorre

    Smallscreen King
    IMPACT: Lorre currently has more series on the air than any other showrunner in primetime: “Two and a Half Men,” which is in its final season; “The Big Bang Theory”; “Mike & Molly”; and “Mom,” all of which routinely pull in a high volume of viewers. In February, he achieved the unusual feat of having all four hit the top 20.
    NEXT: Lorre is two years into a four-year production deal with Warner Bros., which, when it was inked, included broadcast and cable drama and longform projects.

  • Chris Rock

    ‘Top’ Comic
    IMPACT: After a somewhat controversial appearance on “Saturday Night Live” last month, Rock is back in theaters with a comedy he wrote, directed and stars in, “Top Five,” about a standup comic who attempts to craft a serious acting career. Paramount picked up distribution rights at this year’s Toronto Intl. Film Festival, and Questlove serves as the film’s music producer. In the wake of such devastating tragedies as the shooting death of Michael Brown in Ferguson, Mo. and the chokehold death of Eric Garner by a NYPD cop, Rock has also been a ubiquitous presence on social media, summing up the pain and anguish of the Garner incident in one concise, but powerful, tweet: “This one was on film.”
    NEXT: Rock has talked about going back on the road with his “Black Plague” tour, though no dates have been scheduled.

  • Gina Rodriguez

    Like a ‘virgin’
    IMPACT: From the moment she stepped on to the stage at last summer’s TV Critics Press tour, it was clear that Rodriguez, 29, was going to be the breakout star of the TV season. Smart, well-spoken and comfortable in her own skin, she’s also won over audiences with her portrayal of the title character of the CW’s hilariously heartwarming telenovela “Jane the Virgin”. As for playing a character who stands by her own convictions, she told critics, “I think that’s a really awesome thing to put out into the world right now.”
    NEXT: Rodriguez is taking a dramatic turn with a role in the upcoming feature “Sticky Notes.”

  • Hannibal Buress

    The Standout Stand-up
    IMPACT: A longtime comedy-circuit fave, Buress bizarrely saw his biggest impact of the year come when comments in his standup routine almost singlehandedly reignited the national conversation over Bill Cosby rape allegations. But he had plenty of successes in his own right, limning memorable roles in the Seth Rogen comedy “Neighbors” and Comedy Central skein “Broad City,” as well as shooting a Comedy Central special, “Hannibal Buress Live From Chicago.”
    NEXT: Buress is slated to co-star in the Will Ferrell-Mark Wahlberg comedy “Daddy’s Home,” currently in production.

  • Ice Cube

    Mr. Cool
    IMPACT: Two decades ago, “family friendly comic actor” would have been one of the least likely roles anyone would have imagined rabble-rousing rapper Ice Cube inhabiting. And yet, 2014 saw the L.A. native notch two massive hits with buddy-comedy “Ride Along” ($153 million) and sequel “22 Jump Street” ($331 million). He also contributed a voice role to the animated movie, “The Book of Life,” and even appeared on “Sesame Street.”
    NEXT: The success of “Ride Along” all but ensured a sequel, which is slated to arrive in 2016. Meanwhile, Cube is plotting to follow up previous comic franchises with a third installment of “Barbershop” and a fourth “Friday.”

  • Jason Bateman (Alumnus)

    The ‘Boss’ Man
    IMPACT: Although “Horrible Bosses 2” hasn’t exactly wowed the critics, Bateman appeared in “The Longest Week” and “This Is Where I Leave You,” in which he turned in one of his most compelling performances to date. Bateman also continues to pursue directing and producing projects through his Universal-based Aggregate Films shingle.
    NEXT: Bateman is directing and starring in his next project, “The Family Fang,” a drama starring Nicole Kidman and Christopher Walken. As a producer, he’s in pre-production on the TV movie “Two to Go,” directed by Craig Zisk.

  • Jeff Dunham

    The Puppeteer
    IMPACT: The ventriloquist, who lets the dummies he builds by hand get all the laughs, ranks as Forbes’ third-highest paid comic, netting more than $20 million this year through books, DVD, merchandising and a busy stand-up career. His YouTube videos, particularly Achmed the Dead Terrorist, have had close to half a billion hits.
    NEXT: His Las Vegas show, “Not Playing With a Full Deck,” launched in November, and he’s developing additional film and TV projects.

  • Jeffrey Tambor

    Wonder Woman
    IMPACT: Tambor is nothing short of a revelation as Maura, a 70-year-old Jewish man transitioning to womanhood in Amazon’s critically hailed dramedy “Transparent”. The actor, heretofore best known for modern cult classics “Arrested Development” and “The Larry Sanders Show,” plays Maura with equal parts poignancy and aplomb. The performance is groundbreaking, not just for the way it normalizes the transgender experience but for the ease with which Tambor tackles such a complicated, nuanced role. When Tambor, teary-eyed, announces in episode one, “All my life I’ve been dressing up like a man,” his chances for an Emmy nod skyrocket.
    NEXT: Season 2 of “Transparent” is slated for 2015.

  • Jerry Seinfeld (Alumnus)

    Front Seat Driver
    IMPACT: What does a top-earning comedian with “Seinfeld”-type residuals do to keep himself busy? He interviews comedians in cars. His Crackle Web series “Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee” has attracted some of the best comedic talent around, including Sarah Jessica Parker, Howard Stern, Larry David and Mel Brooks (with pal Carl Reiner), all with the promise of coffee and a ride in one of Seinfeld’s classic cars. The fifth season debuted in November with Amy Schumer and Fred Armisen, among others.
    NEXT: He plays himself in Chris Rock’s “Top Five” this month, and he’s producing and appearing in the web series pilot “Cop Show” with Colin Quinn starring and directing.

  • Jill Soloway

    The Transformer
    IMPACT: Soloway, a three-time Emmy nominee for her work writing and producing HBO’s “Six Feet Under,” created, far and away, this season’s most spellbinding comedy in Amazon Studios’ “Transparent.” The writer-director also turned heads at the 2013 Sundance Film Festival, where she won the U.S. Dramatic Directing Award for her first feature film, “Afternoon Delight.” An ardent feminist, Soloway also recently founded Wifey.Tv, a channel/brand for women.
    NEXT: Season 2 of “Transparent” arrives in 2015.

  • John Oliver

    The Newsman
    IMPACT: Across the TV landscape, ratings-driven news operations have fallen into the trap of packaging entertainment as news — and have lost credibility as viewers have gotten wise to their game. On HBO, John Oliver has gone the other way, disguising in-depth explainers and investigative reporting as comedy. “Last Week Tonight” is to America what the fool is to Shakespeare’s kings, except Willy never thought of a break-dancing Lincoln or viral video of the Supreme Court justices as adorable dogs.
    NEXT: Oliver is going on tour and then will start work on season 2 of “Last Week Tonight.”

  • Josh Lieb

    ‘Show’ Man
    IMPACT: Jimmy Fallon tapped former “The Daily Show” showrunner Josh Lieb to take the same position on the “The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon” while still working from the “Late Night” desk, but the move has paid off since his transition. Fallon debuted to strong ratings in February, and has continued dominating the timeslot, improving substantially even on Jay Leno’s ratings from last year. Pairing inventive and Internet-friendly comedy bits with Fallon’s monologues and guest interviews, “The Tonight Show” is as strong as ever in Lieb’s hands.
    NEXT: Already a bestselling author for his young adult novel “I Am a Genius of Unspeakable Evil and I Want to Be Your Class President,” which he is scripting for a feature, Lieb will debut his second book, “Ratscalibur,” in May.

  • Kathy Griffin

    Leading Lady
    IMPACT: The redheaded spitfire scored a career high when, after being nominated in the category five years in a row, she finally won the Grammy for comedy album for “Calm Down Gurrl,” making her the first female to win the award in 30 years (and joining the ranks of Lily Tomlin and Whoopi Goldberg, who each won the award in 1973 and 1986 respectively).
    NEXT: Griffin has been tapped to replace Joan Rivers on E!’s “Fashion Police,” the catty commentary series that was made famous by Rivers, Griffin’s longtime friend and mentor. The first installment of the newly formatted show will air in January and focus on gowns and glad rags at the Golden Globes.

  • Keegan-Michael Key and Jordan Peele

    The Dynamic Duo
    IMPACT: Duo earned an Emmy nom this year for writing their critically hailed Comedy Central sketch series “Key and Peele,” in which they parody everything from the Oscar-winning musical “Les Miserables” to the pronunciation of Liam Neeson’s name, and stole scenes as hapless FBI agents on FX’s limited series “Fargo.”
    NEXT: The 22-episode fourth season of “Key and Peele” is now airing, and a transition to the big screen is in the works with New Line comedy “Keanu.” They’re also set to produce a reboot of “Police Academy.” Separately, Peele plans to direct horror pic “Get Out,” while Key co-stars in “Tomorrowland” and “Vacation.”

  • Kevin Hart (Alumnus)

    The Moneymaker
    IMPACT: Arguably the hardest-working comic in showbiz, Hart has quickly become one of its most profitable as well, as his first three starring roles of the year, “Ride Along,” “Think Like a Man Too” and “About Last Night” (a remake of the 1986 classic rom-com) grossed a combined $272 million before the Fourth of July. He continues to anchor BET’s “The Real Husbands of Hollywood,” and appears in Chris Rock’s Toronto fave “Top Five” alongside Jerry Seinfeld, Whoopi Goldberg and Adam Sandler.
    NEXT: Roles in Screen Gems’ action-comedy “The Wedding Ringer” and Will Ferrell starrer “Get Hard” beckon at the start of 2015. Hart has also wrapped production on “Ride Along 2,” the sure-to-be hilarious sequel to the 100 million dollar box office juggernaut.

  • Kumail Nanjiani

    Geek Treat
    IMPACT: The Pakistan-born comic joined the cast of HBO’s “Silicon Valley,” playing computer programmer Dinesh, after three seasons as agoraphobic attorney Pindar Singh on TNT’s now defunct “Franklin and Bash.” He also hosts “The Meltdown With Jonah and Kumail” with Jonah Ray, which started as a live standup show in the back room of Meltdown Comics on Sunset Boulevard and now airs on Comedy Central.
    NEXT: He’s finished four features, including “Goosebumps” and the animated comedy “Hell and Back,” and he’s working on “Hello, My Name Is Doris.”

  • Kenya Barris, Anthony Anderson, Laurence Fishburne

    The ‘Black-ish’ Gang
    IMPACT: “Black-ish” creator and executive producer Barris’ series takes a straightforward comedic look at one man’s quest to educate his children on the cultural identity he grew up with. Anderson is placed squarely between the conflict of his old-school father (Fishburne) and his children, who have grown up in suburban luxury, in the highly relatable and frequently hilarious skein.
    NEXT: After scoring the highest-rated new comedy premiere of the season, “Black-ish,” which Anderson and Fishburne also exec produce (along with showrunner Jonathan Groff), got a back-nine order in October, giving it a full season to find its footing following established hit “Modern Family.”
     

  • Lena Dunham

    Funny Girl
    IMPACT: The multi-hyphenate, who published a moving account of her friendship with the late Nora Ephron in the New Yorker, continued to break ground through her Golden Globe-winning portrayal of the neurotic, OCD-stricken Hannah Horvath on HBO’s “Girls”. But it was the release of her witty and self-depreacting coming-of-age memoir, “Not That Kind of Girl,” for which Dunham earned a whopping advance of $3.7 million, that launched a battalion of tweets and other social media responses.
    NEXT: Season 4 of “Girls” premieres January.

  • Lorne Michaels

    Mr. ‘Saturday’ Night
    IMPACT: The “Saturday Night Live” creator has been impacting late-night laughs for four decades — the iconic sketch comedy show celebrates its 40th anniversary with a special primetime episode in February — and has demonstrated a keen eye for discovering talent. He also exec produces “The Tonight Show starring Jimmy Fallon” and “Late Night With Seth Meyers.”
    NEXT: He has three films on his 2015 slate, including “Masterminds,” starring Zach Galifianakis, and the new FXX comedy “Man Seeking Woman.”

  • Louis C.K.

    The Chameleon
    IMPACT: C.K. has enviable freedom in writing, directing and editing his semi-autobiographical FX series, “Louie,” the result of which is a singular voice that moves between comedy and drama sometimes imperceptibly. He controls ticket sales and album downloads through his website, giving him unprecedented control of the revenue he brings in.
    NEXT: New episodes of “Louie” air on FX in the spring. The cabler also picked up 10 episodes of “Baskets,” a new series written and produced by C.K. and starring Zach Galifianakis.

  • Luke Greenfield and Nicholas Thomas

    Big ‘Cops’
    IMPACT: Greenfield (producer, director, co-writer) and Thomas (co-writer, exec producer) are responsible for one of the year’s most profitable features, the $17 million “Let’s Be Cops,” which has grossed $125 million worldwide. The comedy is based on Greenfield’s real-life experience of getting busted while impersonating a police officer.
    NEXT: Greenfield will produce and direct the suspense-thriller “Holiday Club” for Fox and the sci-fi film “Slingshot” for Gulfstream Pictures and Warners. Thomas is penning the features “Gamers” for Fox, “Fake Kidnap” for Tri-Star and a comedy pilot for Fox/20th.

  • Melissa McCarthy

    Heat Missile (Alumna)
    IMPACT: Her sitcom “Mike & Molly” is now in its fifth season, but McCarthy hasn’t slowed down on the feature side either. She starred in her husband, Ben Falcone’s, directing debut, “Tammy,” and played opposite Bill Murray in the comedy “St. Vincent.” She recently announced plans to partner with Sunrise Brands and design her own “all-size” casual sportswear clothing line, set to launch in the fall 2015.
    NEXT: The actress-cum-comedian has two features in 2015 — “Spy” and toon “B.O.O. — Bureau of Otherworldly Operations” — plus Falcone’s sophomore effort “Michelle Darnell” which is slated to come out in 2016.

  • Michael Patrick King and Lisa Kudrow

    The ‘Comeback’ Kids
    IMPACT: The first season of HBO’s “The Comeback” achieved cult classic status — and has now earned critical raves for its long-awaited return, nine years later. The co-creators wickedly skewer every aspect of the television business — and Kudrow shines as the sitcom star who oh-so-desperately craves the spotlight.
    NEXT: King’s also juggling exec producer duties on CBS’s “Two Broke Girls,” while Kudrow’s delivering spot-on improv on Showtime’s “Web Therapy.” She’s also EP on “Who Do You Think You Are,” which returns to TLC early next year.

  • Mike Judge and Alec Berg

    ‘Valley’ Guys
    IMPACT: Judge, best known for his work creating and starring in MTV’s animated ode to stupidity, “Beavis and Butt-Head,” and Berg, a former writer-director-executive producer on HBO’s “Curb Your Enthusiasm,” scored big with their freshman HBO series “Silicon Valley,” which nabbed four primetime Emmy nominations out of the gate. Judge, creator and executive producer of the satirical play on Nor Cal’s technology sector, earned a directing nom for the pilot episode while Berg, executive producer, nabbed a writing nom for the season finale and has 4 WGA noms to his credit.
    NEXT: Judge and Berg are working on the second season of “Silicon Valley.”

  • Mike Schur

    The Sitcom Whiz
    IMPACT: Mike Schur is quietly cultivating a small television empire, as he continues to see critical and commercial success with his back-to-back co-created hit sitcoms, “Parks and Recreation” and “Brooklyn Nine-Nine.”
    NEXT: “Brooklyn Nine-Nine” seems poised for sustained success, so the future looks bright for Schur. He also owns the rights to David Foster Wallace’s seemingly unfilmable “Infinite Jest.”

  • Nick Stoller

    Friendly ‘Neighbor’
    IMPACT: Multihyphenate Nick Stoller is perhaps best known for his collaborations with Jason Segel including directing his 2008 comedy “Forgetting Sarah Marshall,” writing “The Muppets,” and “Sex Tape” and writing, directing and producing “The Five-Year Engagement.” The London-born, Harvard-educated Stoller fortified his comedy credentials by directing one of the year’s biggest and raunchiest comedies, “Neighbors,” starring Seth Rogen and Rose Byrne.
    NEXT: Stoller’s slate includes the animated adaptation of Dav Pilkey’s “Captain Underpants” books, with a voice cast that so far includes Kevin Hart, Ed Helms, Nick Kroll and Jordan Peele.

  • Ning Hao

    The Big ‘Breakup’
    IMPACT: An alum of the Beijing Film Academy, director Ning Hao has pursued an active film career since his graduation in 2003, helming six feature films in that time. His latest, “Breakup Buddies,” premiered at the 2014 Toronto Intl. Film Festival and has pulled in $188 million at the B.O. The road trip comedy follows a divorced man and his heavy-drinking friend in a night of delirious binge drinking, and became the latest in Hao’s long line of hits in China.
    NEXT: Hao says his next film will be a big-budget science fiction adaptation of Chinese sci-fi writer Liu Cixin’s “The Rural Teacher.”

  • Phil Lord and Chris Miller

    Builders of Blockbusters
    IMPACT: The pair had massive 2014 hits in both the animated world (“The Lego Movie,” as writer-directors) and in the live-action arena (“22 Jump Street,” as directors). They are also executive producers—and directed the pilot—for the Fox hit “Brooklyn Nine-Nine.”
    NEXT: We can expect inevitable “Lego” sequel and spin-offs, which Lord and Miller will be involved with at least as producers. And a likely “Jump Street” sequel as well. They also directed the pilot for the new Will Forte comedy “The Last Man on Earth,” premiering next year on Fox, on which they serve as executive producers.

  • Philippe de Chauveron

    The Frenchman
    IMPACT: De Chauveron is the director and co-writer of France’s massively successful comedy “Qu’est-ce qu’on a Fait au Bon Dieu?” (aka “Serial Bad Weddings”), which has done more than 12 million admissions in France, and gone on to fare well in several other European countries.The hit comedy revolves around a racist dad and his four daughters and how he goes to pieces when the youngest daughter announces her engagement to a black man.
    NEXT: The writer-director is currently at work on several projects in his native France.

  • Sarah Silverman

    Miracle Woman
    IMPACT: Silverman nabbed a primetime Emmy for her hour-long HBO solo show “Sarah Silverman: We Are Miracles,” in which the ballsy standup star regales an intimate crowd at L.A.’s Largo nightclub with sharp-tongued observations on everything from her elderly dog to cell-phone porn. She proved her versatility in her recurring role as lovesick Helen on Showtime’s “Masters of Sex.” She also also hosted “Saturday Night Live” and upped her visibility in the political arena, igniting the political right while campaigning for women’s health issues.
    NEXT: Upcoming projects include indie pics “Ashby”and “I Smile Back.”

  • Seth Rogen, Evan Goldberg (Alumni) and James Weaver

    Shades of Grey
    IMPACT: Seth Rogen and Evan Goldberg have turned longtime friendship into a collaborative team capable of producing monster Hollywood successes, including co-writing “Superbad” and “Pineapple Express,” and taking the helm together for last year’s “This Is the End.” The duo formed production company Point Grey Pictures in 2011, named after their Canadian high school, and named producer James Weaver president in 2013. Weaver’s producing credits include “50/50,” “This Is the End” and “Neighbors.”
    NEXT: Rogen and Goldberg return as co-writers and co-directors this Christmas in North Korean assassination-comedy “The Interview” with Weaver producing, and in 2016 the group plans to release adult animated comedy “Sausage Party.”

  • Shane Dawson

    YouTube Sensation
    Impact: A popular YouTube star since 2008 with his YouTube channel ShaneDawsonTV, Shane Dawson began expanding to more mainstream projects this year, winning the first season of Starz’s competition reality series, “The Chair.”
    Next: Dawson’s $250,000 will go to an undisclosed future project, per “The Chair” contest rules.

  • The Farrelly Bros.

    Rhode (Island) Scholars
    Impact: Brothers Bobby and Peter Farrelly were the undisputed kingpins of gross-out comedy in the 1990s, creating ribald comedy classics like “There’s Something About Mary” and “Me, Myself and Irene.” Returning to their roots has already proven a wise move, as sequel “Dumb and Dumber To” notched the highest opening frame of the brothers’ career, letting them laugh all the way to the bank to the tune of $36 million.
    NEXT: The native east coasters have projects in the works but are keeping mum on specifics.
