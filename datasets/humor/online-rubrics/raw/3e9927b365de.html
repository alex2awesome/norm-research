Where to Submit Your Humor Writing: 20+ Market Opportunities

Publications where you can submit satire, literary humor, and humorous essays

Julie Vick
May 23, 2024

Have a humor piece that you want to submit? The good news is that there are lots of publications that run humor pieces.

I’ve been writing and publishing humor for over 15 years, and I compiled these markets based on places currently open to submissions. I’ll periodically update this list as markets come and go.

I’ve broken these markets down by the following categories:

  • Satire/Conceptual Humor Markets

  • Medium Humor Markets

  • Satirical News Sites

  • Funny Personal Essay Markets

  • Literary Humor Markets

Categorization is a little tricky, as some markets would fit in more than one spot, but I tried to break them down in a way that would be useful for what writers might be looking for.

Hopefully, this goes without saying, but before you submit to a publication, you should spend some time reading it to make sure your piece is a good fit.

There are many more markets than these, but I tried to include ones that have been around for a while, are accepting submissions, and publish consistently.

Satire/Conceptual Humor Markets

These markets accept satire and conceptual humor pieces in most formats except the satirical news format (see below for more info on satirical news). These types of pieces depart from reality in some way, and if you aren’t already familiar with this type of short humor piece, spending time reading the sites below can help you get a better sense of them.

Points in Case: A site with up to 500k monthly readers that takes a variety of humor pieces and jokes. Submission Guidelines.

McSweeney’s Internet Tendency: A well-regarded humor website open to short conceptual humor and satire. You can find their submission guidelines here, and I wrote a piece with additional tips for submitting here.

New Yorker Shouts: The New Yorker Shouts section publishes satirical pieces (other sections of The New Yorker run humorous essays, but this is not the place for nonfiction essays). They can take several months to respond, but will get back to you eventually. Submission guidelines.

Chortle is a Substack publication that accepts short (600 words or fewer) humor pieces.

Weekly Humorist:  A humor website that accepts cartoons and satirical humor pieces under 1500 words. Submission Guidelines.

The American Bystander: This print and online publication runs humor writing and cartoons. Submissions can be sent to: submissions@americanbystander.org

Medium Humor Markets

There are several humor-focused pubs on Medium, a site that hosts publications in addition to providing a space for writers to publish pieces on their own. Unless you already have a following on Medium, it’s typically better to submit to one of the publications that accept humor rather than just publishing on your own, because that can help you reach a wider audience.

If you put your piece behind the Medium paywall, you have the potential to get paid for your work. In my experience, the pay range per piece for humor pieces varies pretty widely (i.e., less than $1 to over $100, with the upper end being less common).

If your article gets selected for Boost, then it tends to make more, so submitting to publications that have Boost can be worth it.

Here are just some of the Medium humor pubs:

The Belladonna: A site for satirical and conceptual humor pieces written by women and nonbinary authors. Submission Guidelines.  

Frazzled: A parenting humor-focused publication that runs satirical pieces, satirical news, and humorous essays. Submission Guidelines.

Slackjaw: One of the biggest (if not the biggest) humor pubs that is open to most humor formats except satirical news. Submission Guidelines.

Jane Austen’s Wastebasket: Open to satirical and conceptual humor (that doesn’t necessarily have to be literature or history-themed). Submission Guidelines.

Satirical News Sites

The most well-known satirical news site is The Onion, and you are likely familiar with their pieces, which are formatted like newspaper articles (and sometimes just headlines).

The Onion does not take submissions on an open basis, and because they and other sites like them are dedicated to satirical news, a lot of the general satire sites do not take pieces formatted in this parody of a newspaper article style.  

However, these are some publications that do take submissions in the format:

Reductress: Reductress opens for submissions quarterly. To get info on the next opening, email the address at the bottom of this page.

The Hard Times A satirical news site relating to punk music. Follow the directions here to submit

Hard Drive This site focuses on satirical video game news. Learn more about submitting here.

The Needling: A Seattle-based satirical news site that lists submission guidelines here.

Broadway Beat: A spot for your theater-based satirical news submissions.

If you want more tips for writing satirical news headlines, check out this piece from my newsletter.

Funny Personal Essay Markets

It’s a little hard to recommend a list of these, as there are so many out there, and the short answer is that any market that accepts personal essays is also probably open to funny personal essays (with some caveats, which I go into more detail about here).

However, these are just some of the places I’ve seen run personal essays with humor in them:

Business Insider – This pub runs short (600-word) essays that can sometimes have humor elements in them. Submission Guidelines.

  • Example: I Booked a Multigenerational Vacation to Bring My Mom and Kids Closer and, Wow, Did it Backfire by Liz Alterman

Huff Post Personal: Huff Post runs personal essays and has sometimes run ones with humorous elements. Pitch guide.

  • Example: I Had a Baby at 40, And it Was Awesome by Jessica Delfino

Slate: Slate will also run essays with humorous elements. Pitch Guide.

  • Example:” I Put Everything in My Planner—Even Showering” by Elissa Bassist  

Scary Mommy: Scary Mommy is a parenting site that frequently uses humor in their pieces. Reading through the site should give you several examples of essays that blend humor in. Submission guidelines.

  • Example: “I Was Awful At Sports. Then Came My Kid” by Janine Annett

Newseek’s “My Turn”: My Turn runs personal essays, often with timely hooks.

  • Example: “At 49, I Became an Accidental Influencer—For a Very Intimate Reason” by Caren Lissner

Regional Magazines:  Regional magazines in the U.S. will sometimes run short, lighter personal essays. You can often pick up a copy of these in grocery stores or other spots that have racks of local publications. You can find more about submitting to them here, too.

  • Example: “Take Your Child to Work Day is April 25” by Shannon Carpenter

I also wrote some tips (and highlighted a couple of other essay markets) in this post about writing humorous personal essays.

If you are looking for more essay markets, I’ve also got this guide available for paying subscribers (which you can get access to with a $5/month monthly subscription, and yes, it’s okay to just subscribe for a month or two to get it).

Literary Humor Markets

Some of the publications in other sections are probably also classified as literary, but here is a list of just some of the markets that fall into the “Lit Journal” category that run pieces with humor in them.

The line between what makes something more literary humor vs. not differs, but reading what these sites have run before should give you an idea of the type of style they are looking for.

Literary journals sometimes only open for submission at certain times of year, and response times can be longer (so look out for those in the submission guidelines)

The Offing’s “Wit Tea” section. This lit journal accepts literary-leaning humor pieces in this section. Submission Guidelines.

Defenestration: A literary magazine dedicated to humor that accepts many formats (including poetry, which a lot of other humor sites do not take). Submission Guidelines.

Bonus leads: This old Tweet thread may give you some leads on other lit journals that are open to humor, and Chill Subs can be a good place to look for potential leads.

If you are looking for more markets, I recommend following humor writers you enjoy and seeing where they are publishing (this is how I found many of the examples above).
