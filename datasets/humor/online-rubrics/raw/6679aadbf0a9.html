The 20 Best Podcasts of 2025

This year’s selection balances humor with existential dread, snark with sincerity, and intellectual rigor—all while hangin' out, of course.

By Brendan MenapacePublished: Dec 22, 2025 11:32 AM EST

Let’s be honest: even the term “podcast” is antiquated at this point. Ever think about how the “save” icon is still a floppy disc? Or that the Instagram logo is an old camera? Well, kids these days probably don’t even know that the podcast was originally pod … as in iPod.

The art of the podcast has come a long way since then. It has broken free of its 32GB constraints to become a mainstream—if not the mainstream—medium. To call podcasts even an audio medium would be selling it short, because “podcast” now refers to just about anything with recorded speech. And there is no barrier to entry—for better or worse. Anyone from a dude with a laptop in his basement to an A-list celebrity (backed by professional lighting arrangements and a staff of producers) looking to stay in the spotlight can call themself a podcaster. Podcasts are a great equalizer in a way. And that’s good, right? Maybe? It’s still hard to tell.

Podcasting now looks a lot like the music, TV, and movie worlds. There are studios with stables of shows. There are scrappy indie upstarts. There are waves of influence and innovation. And here I was thinking that it was weird that Kevin Smith made a movie about a podcaster in 2014. Surely this isn’t the cultural thing he thinks it is, right? I thought. Wrong.

A lot has changed in the podcasting space since then, and will certainly continue to change. Right now, though, here are the most entertaining, inspirational, educational and noteworthy podcasts of 2025—a list that will no doubt seem quaint somewhere down the road when all meals are delivered in podcast form.

WTF with Marc Maron

Okay. This is cheating a little, considering Marc Maron is a trailblazer of interview podcasting (just ask him). But with Maron hanging up his mic this year after a truly prolific and iconic run, it deserves a place on this list. And it’s not just for sentimentality’s sake. Despite Maron’s 16-year run of nearly uninterrupted releases, this past year seems like as close to a final, definitive “season” as you’d get from him, which started with his June episode featuring John Mulaney, when Marc announced the end of the show.

Like most celebrated legacy TV shows, Maron might not have perfectly stuck the landing. That’s life. Endings are rarely perfect. WTF wasn’t created on the idea of impeccability—Maron knew he wasn’t perfect. Listening to Maron in 2025, compared to older episodes, feels like experiencing two different shows. Or maybe even three. Maron’s evolution was plainly visible through his monologues and interviews, which ran the gamut of guests in entertainment, politics, and beyond. Others have written plenty on the importance of his show, myself included. Just as Maron was a pioneer in the medium when he started, he was a pioneer in ending it the way he did.

Listen on Spotify

Panic World

Panic World—the podcast hosted by “Garbage Day” writer Ryan Broderick—does one thing especially well. It never gives into exhausting nihilism that less talented and more obnoxious presenters might give in to when talking about the most awful things about internet culture and how they shape our reality. In Panic World, Broderick, and occasionally his producer Grant Irving (when given permission to speak), dives headfirst into internet ephemera that ranges from silly (see: AI music, Etsy witches, and folks who are just a little too into superhero movies) to the legitimately dangerous and scary (online forums that coerce vulnerable people into harming themselves or others). It feels necessary to have someone point out the darkest pits of the internet and where it all leaks into the real world right now. To manage to make it not feel insanely depressing feels like magic.

Listen on Spotify

Staying Alive

As we get older, our conversations with our friends tend to go from “Dude, last night was so crazy,” to “Last night all I did was eat two slices of pizza at 11 P.M. and now I think I need to go to the hospital because my heartburn is so bad.” Each episode, hosts/comedians/real-life pals John Gabrus and Adam Pally invite a guest to tell them what they’re doing to stay alive. The answers include rigorous gym routines, yoga, meditation, and even just being nicer to yourself on a daily basis and allowing yourself that cheat meal or night out because life is short. Staying Alive delivers a wellness and motivational conversation without any of the wellness or motivation grift that unfortunately is plastered all over the podcasting world nowadays. It’s friends talking about what works for them, what hasn’t worked, and what brings them joy—even if they know that it’s going to make ‘em hurt a little in the morning.

Listen on Spotify

It's Called Soccer

To quote one of my favorite Simpsons bits: Open wide for some soccer, America! With what will surely be an … interesting World Cup on the horizon, now is as good a time as ever to brush up on the culture. NBC Sports anchor Rebecca Lowe—known to early risers as the host of NBC’s Premier League coverage—is flanked by former Premier Leaguer footballer (and “IT Crowd” punchline) Theo Walcott and American soccer player Taylor Twellman. It’s Called Soccer is a nice salve for the bro-y, scream-fest of most American sports broadcasts, even when Twellman gets a little loud. Lowe, especially, is one of the sport’s most knowledgeable and engaging broadcasters. And the three cover both the headlines from European leagues, the impending World Cup from the American perspective, and host guests from around the world.

Listen on Spotify

Where Everybody Knows Your Name With Ted Danson & Woody Harrelson (Sometimes)

With the position of “America’s Dad” currently sitting vacant, I am petitioning to fill the role with Ted Danson. Yes, Woody pops in from time to time—increasingly less so, it seems. And that’s fun. But this is really Ted Danson’s show. And boy does he shine in that role. Each episode is delightful. It’s calming. Danson is so clearly in heaven talking to people about all manner of things, and he imparts just enough of a certain age wisdom that his podast feels comforting in a way that talking to a trusted relative does. Sometimes the formula for a successful podcast is to find someone who seems genuinely nice and funny, and stick them in a room with other interesting people, and hit record. This is absolutely one of those cases.

Listen on Spotify

Bandsplain

You remember that person in school who seemed to know every cool band? And not only do they know them, but they have deep knowledge of all their songs, and music history in general, and they’re also clever and funny and smart, and you wonder how they had all the time to cultivate this personality? That’s Yasi Salek. She is the coolest girl in all of our schools, and each week she tackles the story of a great band or artist. Make no mistake: Bandsplain isn’t some pretentious, high-falutin podcast only talking about the most obscure bands you’ve never heard of. It’s extremely accessible and Salek makes no bones about her love of music—all kinds of music—and leads with that. She’s just really cool.

Listen on Spotify

Good One

The thing about explaining jokes is that it often makes any particular gag terminally unfunny. And the thing with a podcast about comedians is that there are now so many that they all sort of blend together. Often they are used just as vehicles to plug something—or worse, a stage for performing improv bits. Good One, hosted by Jesse David Fox, writer and author of Comedy Book, really sticks to the topic at hand: comedy. Fox focuses on the craft of comedy without it feeling like a James Lipton interview. Fox’s encyclopedic knowledge allows for insightful questions and contextual explorations of comedy. And yes, Good One is funny, too.

Listen on Spotify

Tough Cookie: The Wally ‘Famous’ Amos Story

I never really thought too much about Famous Amos cookies. Who knew that there was such wild behind-the-scenes tale behind the little snacks? Well, every family has its drama. When you throw wealth and ambition into the mix—and the patriarch in your family is, to put it mildly, sometimes prone to the ambitious spontaneity required to start a cookie empire—things can get messy. As reported by Amos’s daughter, Sarah Amos, Tough Cookie follows her father’s path from the segregation-era South where he was raised by a strict mother, continuing through a trailblazing career in entertainment, multiple marriages, and a tumultuous career in business full of the highest highs and lowest lows.

Listen on Spotify

If Books Could Kill

Have you ever come across a book, most likely a self-help or motivational book, flipped through a few pages and thought, There’s no way people are falling for this crap, right? And then you find out that the author of that book just appeared on a podcast or nine. Turns out they’re the star of the moment, and their grand theory about whatever pop psychology du jour is making the media rounds. But, seriously, no one’s falling for this, right? Right? Thankfully, If Books Could Kill tactfully takes down these books that are often heralded as works of genius when in reality they are, to put it diplomatically, maybe not as deep as they think they are. And it’s not just mean-spirited hating. The hosts, Michael Hobbes and Peter Shamshiri, are sure to back up their claims with thoughtful research for counterarguments. But, sometimes, you also just have to call out bullshit when you see it.

Listen on Spotify

Only If You Get Caught

There are so many ways to cheat in the world. You can cheat in sports, you can cheat in your relationship, you can cheat in business. Hosted by Defector staffer Patrick Redford, Only If You Get Caught is an exploration of famous examples of cheating, and what they meant. Topics range from the people who go on The Bachelor or The Bachelorette “for the wrong reasons” and cheat the game for their own fame and financial gain, or the ways that WNBA franchises found ways to pay their athletes more while avoiding salary caps and what it says about the larger conversations in women’s professional sports right now. In an era where podcasts run long thanks to enormous ad breaks or lack of self-editing, each episode is a very respectable length, too. Only If You Get Caught is nicely commute or workout-sized.

Listen on Spotify

The Adam Friedland Show

There are very few shows where every week you have absolutely no idea who the guest is going to be. It could truly be anyone. Friedland made a name for himself as part of the quote-unquote “dirtbag left” podcast scene as one third of Cumtown alongside Stavros Halkias and Nick Mullen. While Halkias focused on standup, giving advice and landing in Yorgos Lanthimos flicks somehow, Friedland (and Mullen at first) did their own thing, recreating the Dick Cavett Show backdrop and asking politicians, actors, comedians, athletes and public faces of all kinds often uncomfortable questions. It’s hard to tell when Friedland is playing a character or when he’s being himself. Friedland’s best quality is that he has no problem making his guest uncomfortable, even if it’s just for a laugh about NBA players who stink. This is perhaps because if you ask him, The Adam Friedland Show is not a podcast, it’s a talk show.

Listen on Spotify

Guys: With Bryan Quinby

There are so many types of guys. It’s endless. Hosts Bryan Quinby and Chris James explore the wide world of guys and how they became that type of guy. Knife guys, Eminem guys, Funko Pop guys, the list goes on and on. Each episode is also a look into the inner lives of its hosts, with new information coming out all the time about what kind of guys they are (like Quinby being something of a LEGO guy). Thanks to the fact that guys are not shy about interacting with other guys about their hobbies online, each episode has plenty of material to draw from. It’s like a post-internet culture David Attenborough documentary.

Listen on Spotify

Good Hang

The celebrities have podcasts. It’s just how it is now. Yes, the celebrity podcast is often a big ol’ pat on the back. But, you know, sometimes that’s still fun. It’s fun to hear (and see) familiar faces talk about things we like. Amy Poehler hasn’t broken the podcast mold with Good Hang, but she proves a very likable host. The guests are great. The conversation is a blast. Good Hang is funny, obviously. Yes, yes, it’s all very saccharine. Allow yourself the candy. There are plenty of murder podcasts out there.

Listen on Spotify

What’s Our Podcast? With Beck Bennett and Kyle Mooney

As I stated above, everyone working in entertainment must legally have a podcast. With that in mind—and having moved on from SNL—Kyle Mooney and Beck Bennett are scrambling. They know they want a podcast and they even have an idea of which guests they want, but they don’t know what the podcast is about. That’s up to the guest each week. The bit is just good enough stay fresh each week without ever devolving into a game of improv. And for those of us who grew up in the heyday of YouTube when Mooney and Bennett’s “Good Neighbor” videos were quoted in school hallways and dorm rooms, it’s fun to see the duo doing their own thing together again.

Listen on Spotify

What Are We Even Doing?

Kyle MacLachlan feels ageless. Even still, he’s just like the rest of us in asking: What are the kids even into these days? Which is exactly what he does in What Are We Even Doing? There’s something uniquely cool and disarming about Kyle MacLachlan, despite his Lynchian past. He’s an old soul brimming with youthful exuberance. The guy was the original Lisan al-Gaib, but he was also the mayor on Portlandia. His mix of smart, sophisticated, and silly makes him a great conversation partner with younger guests to find out what the youth of today has going on.

Listen on Spotify

Hardcore History

Dan Carlin has been doing this for a long time. But in January 2025, he dropped the almost four-hour-long second part of his series on Alexander the Great. Carlin is the history teacher we all wish we had. He is passionate, sharp, and makes our very real history sound like outrageous mythology or Shakespearean drama. In “Mania for Subjugation,” Carlin explores what made Alexander the leader he was, from his background to the psychology of someone trying to conquer and maintain a vast empire.

Listen on Spotify

No Such Thing

The best podcasts often feel like you’re listening to your own friends having a conversation. That’s why so many people try their hand at making their own. In reality, it’s a little bit like watching Olympic ski jumping and thinking, I could do that, even though you’ve only gone skiing a few times in your life. They’re pros for a reason. Perhaps no one is better at the podcast-and-chill format than the three hosts of No Such Thing, Devon Joseph, Manny Fidel, and Noah Friedman, who sometimes tackle big topics like Kanye West’s downfall. But other times, it’s observations about why so many statues look so dumb, or griping about why Tesla car handles are so needlessly hard to open. All of this is, of course, meticulously researched. It’s not all just: What’s the deal with airline food?

Listen on Spotify

The Lonely Island and Seth Meyers Podcast

On the surface, this podcast is just four guys who worked on SNL talking about the good ol’ days. More than just talking about the behind-the-scenes of The Lonely Island’s iconic digital shorts, though, it’s a look at how the entire landscape of comedy in film and TV changed in the past decade or so. “Dick in a box.” “I’m On a Boat.” The Natalie Portman rap. “Jizz in My Pants.” Insane run. Oh, and you want to feel old? The first Lonely Island digital short to run on SNL—”Lazy Sunday”—dropped 20 years ago this month. Since then, the Lonely Island guys became some of the most reliable comedic voices of a generation and Seth Meyers turned into a stellar late-night host. It’s well worth your time to revisit the Loney Island videos that birthed a whole wave of comedy. It’s a bonus that the retrospective is delivered by four really funny guys who are clearly still great friends.

Listen on Spotify

Oh Gatekeeper

Joe Talbot of the post-punk band IDLES has become one of the most outspoken forces of aggressive positivity and inclusion in music. Through IDLES, he’s done his damndest to make the album title Joy As an Act of Resistance a reality through activism, while being very loud about the evils of homophobia, misogyny, and xenophobia. On his podcast, Talbot is a much more soft-spoken figure, but his conversations still touch on big feelings like self-confidence as an artist, substance abuse, and freedom in creativity.

Listen on Spotify

Against the Rules

Michael Lewis, author of enormously popular books like Liar’s Poker, The Big Short, and Moneyball knows a thing or two about taking risks. Season 5 of Lewis’s podcast explored the sports gambling world—which some might call an epidemic—through the eyes of his teenage son who, like most teenagers, think they know best. Lewis gives his son all of the tools he thinks he needs to find out how stacked the deck is against him, and documents every step of the process. It’s not as simple a process as Lewis had hoped as a father, and it’s a decent look into what we’re in for now that every phone is a casino and betting is more linked to live sports (and other societal happenings) than ever before.

Listen on Spotify
