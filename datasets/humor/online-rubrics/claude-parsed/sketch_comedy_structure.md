# Sketch Comedy Writing

## 5-Point Structure

### 1. Setup / Exposition
- Answer audience questions ASAP.
- Typically 5 lines of dialogue or less.
- Establish the world.

### 2. Introduce the Premise
- Set up reality of your world.
- Add ONE crazy/wacky/funny thing.
- This is the engine.

### 3. The Game / Middle Section
- 95% of length comes here.
- Best jokes are here.
- Heightening of the game.

### 4. The Button / Ending
- Goofiest, most fun part.
- Final joke that lands the sketch.
- Leave them laughing.

## Beats and Heightening
- 3 beats heighten the game until you reach a breaking point.
- Each beat: more ridiculous, more specific, more-er.
- Build: setup → reinforcement → absurd climax.

## Premise Development
- Take a nugget of an idea.
- Phrase it as "What if?"
- This becomes the game of the scene.
- Get game moves by answering the "what if" in escalating ways.

## What a Sketch Is
A short, self-contained scene focused on **one unusual thing** that heightens to comedic absurdity.

## Big Challenge
Sustain tension between reality and absurdity over several minutes WITHOUT shifting to a new premise OR becoming predictable.

## Common Sketch Structures
- **Heightening sketch**: same premise gets crazier.
- **Reveal sketch**: builds to a dark/surprising reveal.
- **List sketch**: SNL "Weekend Update" style examples.
- **Character sketch**: established character in new situation.
- **Parody sketch**: imitates a known format.

## Rubric
1. Is there one unusual thing (clear game)?
2. Does each beat heighten?
3. Is the button satisfying?
4. Does it sustain without losing the premise?
5. Is the world established quickly enough?
