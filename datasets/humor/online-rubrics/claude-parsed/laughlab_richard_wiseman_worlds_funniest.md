# Richard Wiseman: LaughLab and the Science of the World's Funniest Joke

## Project Background
LaughLab (2001-2002) was a one-year online experiment where users submitted jokes and rated jokes from others. ~40,000 jokes submitted; ~2 million ratings collected. Wiseman analyzed the patterns.

## The Winning Joke (consensus)
A hunters-in-the-woods joke involving a 911 dispatcher's miscommunication. Won because:
- Universally accessible (no cultural insider knowledge).
- Two stages of misdirection (unusual structure).
- The dim character is sympathetically dim (we laugh WITH, not AT).
- A "feel good" superiority that requires no malice.

## Patterns in Top-Rated Jokes
- **Animal protagonists** (especially ducks): higher ratings, possibly because of K-sound, possibly cultural absurdity.
- **Length**: 103 words appears to be the optimal joke length.
- **Time of day**: jokes rated highest at 6pm; lowest in early morning.
- **Country differences**:
  - Brits/Irish/Aussies preferred wordplay.
  - Americans/Canadians preferred superiority jokes.
  - Germans rated everything more positively (no clear preference).
  - Smaller cultural variation than expected.

## The Three-Class Theory
Wiseman concluded jokes broadly cluster into:
1. **Relief jokes** — release tension (sex, death, taboo).
2. **Superiority jokes** — laugh at someone else's failure.
3. **Incongruity jokes** — surprise at unexpected logic.
The world's funniest jokes pull from incongruity + a touch of superiority that doesn't sting.

## On Cross-Cultural Universals
Despite local variation, jokes that crossed cultures shared:
- A clear two-character setup.
- A misunderstanding rather than a deception.
- A sympathetic protagonist.
- A punchline relying on a CONCEPTUAL twist, not wordplay.

## LaughLab Working Rubric
1. Is the joke universally accessible (no cultural insider knowledge)?
2. Is there a sympathetically dim character?
3. Is the misdirection conceptual, not just verbal?
4. Is the joke length close to 100 words?
5. Does the joke provide superiority WITHOUT malice?
6. Could it be re-told across countries with minimal translation?
