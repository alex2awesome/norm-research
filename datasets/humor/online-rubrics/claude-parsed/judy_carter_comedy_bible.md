# Judy Carter — The Comedy Bible

## Core Approach
Judy Carter turned comedy writing into a "mathematical formula comprehensible to 4th graders" — a systematic, accessible joke-writing process.

## Key Joke Components

### Setups
- Setups are usually very serious and authentic.
- They have a ring of truth and honesty.
- They establish a foundation for the audience.

### Attitude (Critical)
- Jokes without attitude end up sounding too literary — humorous but not funny enough to get big laughs.
- Attitude words are the tools to get really big laughs.
- A funny attitude for a joke = the comedian's emotional perspective on the topic.

### Authenticity
- Find your "authentic voice" — the true source of comedy.
- Embarrassing truths about oneself are gold.
- Reluctant admissions reveal genuine comedy.

## Key Exercises
- Coming up with embarrassing truths about oneself
- Reluctant admissions
- Half-truth mashups combining different identities
- Redefining industry terms humorously
- Transforming acronyms

## Rubric for Evaluating a Joke
1. Does the setup feel authentic and serious (truth-grounded)?
2. Is there clear attitude (emotional POV) on the topic?
3. Does the comedian sound like an authentic voice (not generic)?
4. Is there an embarrassing truth or reluctant admission underneath?
5. Are the words sharp enough to capture attention quickly?

## Audience Engagement
- The audience makes up its mind quickly.
- Every joke is important to capture attention, then make them laugh.
