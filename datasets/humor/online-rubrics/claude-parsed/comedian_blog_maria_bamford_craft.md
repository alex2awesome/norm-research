# Maria Bamford: Craft Notes

## On Voice Work as Joke Engine
- Bamford's signature is voice characters embedded in narrative bits.
- The character voice DELIVERS the joke; the comic acts as narrator.
- The character can say things the comic cannot — the voice creates ethical distance.

## On Mental Illness as Material
- The bit is not "look at how broken I am."
- The bit is "here is the absurd specific texture of how broken I am."
- Specificity transforms confessional into comedy; vagueness stays in confessional.

## On Family Material
- The family is impersonated, not described.
- The voice impressions confer love; the audience hears the affection in the mimicry.
- This is what permits even hard family material to land warmly.

## On Performing Vulnerability Without Exploiting It
- Bamford never asks the audience to pity her.
- She invites them to recognize themselves in her specifics.
- The audience laughs because they recognize, not because they feel superior.

## On the Small Room
- Bamford has performed entire shows for one person.
- The small room is honest in ways the big room isn't.
- Joke writing improves under the discipline of intimate audiences.

## Bamford-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Could a CHARACTER deliver the line better than you?
2. Is the voice impression carrying affection?
3. Have you made vulnerability specific, not vague?
4. Are you inviting recognition, not pity?
5. Does the joke survive an audience of one?
6. Are you using ethical distance to say the unsayable?
