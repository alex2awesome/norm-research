# Comedy of Manners

## Definition
A genre of realistic, satirical comedy that questions and comments upon the manners and social conventions of a sophisticated, artificial society. Witty, cerebral. Depicts and satirizes the manners and affectations of a contemporary society.

## Key Characteristics

### Wit and Dialogue
- Plot (often a scandal) is secondary.
- Social commentary delivered through witty dialogue.
- Fast-paced repartee.
- Showcases characters' intelligence and social acumen.

### Focus on Upper Classes
- Satirizes the behavior of the upper class.
- Exposes superficiality and pretension of social interactions.
- Hypocrisy as the central comedic engine.

### Stock Characters
- The cynical wit.
- The foolish suitor.
- The scheming lady.
- The pompous fool.
- The jealous husband.

## Source of Humor
- Conflict between public personas and private motivations.
- Misunderstandings.
- Characters' attempts to navigate complex social situations.
- The gap between what is said and what is meant.

## Examples
- Wycherley *The Country Wife*
- Sheridan *The School for Scandal*
- Wilde *The Importance of Being Earnest*
- Coward *Private Lives*
- Modern: *Frasier*, *The Crown*-adjacent comedies.

## Rubric
1. Is there a sophisticated social setting with rules?
2. Are characters violating the rules in revealing ways?
3. Is dialogue the primary comic engine?
4. Are public/private gaps exploited?
5. Is the satire targeting pretension?
