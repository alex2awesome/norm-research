# Benign Violation Theory of Humor

## Core Definition
Humor emerges when three simultaneous conditions are met:
1. A situation is a violation
2. The situation is benign
3. Both perceptions occur simultaneously

## What Constitutes a Violation
A violation occurs when circumstances contradict someone's expectations about how reality should function. McGraw describes this as something that "threatens the way that you believe the world 'ought' to be." Examples span diverse categories—from physical experiences like tickling to breaches of linguistic standards evident in wordplay.

Violations include:
- Physical threats (tickling, play fighting)
- Psychological threats (insults, sarcasm)
- Norm violations: social, cultural, linguistic, logical, and moral

## Three Mechanisms That Make Violations Benign

1. **Norm Detachment / Alternative Norms**: The violation appears harmless when someone lacks genuine commitment to the violated standard (e.g., non-religious individuals finding humor in a church distributing an SUV). Or alternative interpretations make sense (as in puns).

2. **Psychological Distance**: The violation feels safer through separation in time, space, social distance, or reality (past events, situations affecting others, fictional scenarios). "Comedy is tragedy plus time."

3. **Reinterpretation**: An alternative framework redefines the violation's meaning, as with playfighting where laughter signals "a threatening situation is okay."

## When Humor Fails
A situation can fail to be funny because:
- It depicts a violation that does not simultaneously seem benign (too aggressive/threatening)
- It depicts a benign situation that has no violation (too safe/boring)

## Application Rubric (for evaluating a joke)
- Is there a violation present (norm/expectation/moral/social/linguistic)?
- Is the violation simultaneously benign (alternative norm available, weak commitment, distance)?
- Are both perceptions held at once?
- Does the audience have appropriate distance from the violation?
