# Act Outs in Stand-Up Comedy

## Definition
An act-out is stand-up jargon describing a comic's shift from narrating a joke to **acting it out**. Examples: dramatizing a conversation, impersonating an animal, vegetable, or anatomical part.

## Key Techniques

### You Don't Need a Perfect Impression
- You don't have to be a great actor or do a perfect impression.
- You can deliver a line in your own voice and still get solid laughs.
- An act out isn't always an impression.

### Commitment
- Fully commit to the emotions, punchlines, choices.
- Half-hearted acting won't sell the performance.
- Correct commitment is key to credibility on stage.

### Show, Don't Tell
- Whenever you have an opportunity to introduce information, show, don't tell.
- Showing brings the audience into the present moment — urgent and alive.

### Be Specific with Characters
- Avoid the "ghost mannequin" — a vague imaginary character.
- Give the character qualities, characteristics, identity.
- Specificity adds dynamic and dimensions.

### Emotional Variety
- Experiment with different emotional filters for the same line.
- Record yourself saying lines with many emotions.
- Sometimes confusion lands harder than surprise.

## Why Act Outs Work
- Visual + auditory channel deliver more information than words alone.
- Embodied delivery creates immediacy.
- Surprise: shifting modes (narration → embodied) is itself an incongruity.

## Rubric for Act Outs
1. Is there a clear shift from narration to embodiment?
2. Is the character/voice specific (not a ghost mannequin)?
3. Is the comic fully committed?
4. Does the act-out show rather than tell?
5. Is the emotional choice the funniest available?
