# Viola Spolin — Improvisation for the Theater

The "Bible" of improvisational theater. 130+ theater games + exercises.

## Core Principles

### Present Moment Awareness
- Games make actors feel grounded in the present.
- Action and character development arise organically.
- With scene partners and even the audience.

### Freedom from Self-Consciousness
- Encourage freedom of expression.
- Discourage self-consciousness.
- Enable fearless exploration onstage.

### Take Emphasis Off "Acting"
- Focus attention on the game itself.
- Diminish self-consciousness.
- Unleash creativity.
- Act more naturally.

### Problem-Solving as Theater
- Improvisation = solving problems in the moment.
- Solving them so by-product is theater.

### Democratization
- Removes judgment between teacher/student and performers/audience.
- Choices are never "right" or "wrong."

## Sample Games

### Contact
For every line you say to someone on stage, you must make physical contact with that person consistent with the intent of the line.

### Where, Who, What
Establish setting, character, and activity through specific actions, not exposition.

### Mirror
One person leads, another mirrors exactly — develops attentiveness.

### Gibberish
Communicate without language — relies on emotion, intent, physicality.

### Status Game
Play scenes at high or low status — reveals power dynamics.

## Why This Matters for Comedy
- Spolin techniques produce alive, reactive performers.
- Comedy depends on liveness; canned doesn't laugh.
- Specificity of "Where/Who/What" → specific comedy.

## Rubric
1. Are the players in the present moment?
2. Are they reacting to each other (not pre-planned)?
3. Are they specific in setting/character/action?
4. Are they free of self-consciousness?
5. Is the game (constraint) generating the comedy?
