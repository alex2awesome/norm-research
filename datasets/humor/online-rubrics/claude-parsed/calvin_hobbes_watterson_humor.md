# Calvin and Hobbes — Watterson's Approach

## Philosophical Foundations
- **Calvin** named after John Calvin (Swiss Protestant Reformer).
- **Hobbes** named after Thomas Hobbes (political philosopher).
- Two opposing worldviews in dialogue.

## How the Humor Works

### Childhood + Philosophy
- Six-year-old grappling with adult philosophical questions.
- The mismatch (gravitas in a child) generates absurd insight.

### Imagination as Reality
- Stuffed tiger as living best friend (only Calvin sees him alive).
- Spaceman Spiff, Stupendous Man, Dinosaurs in Ferraris.
- Reader inhabits Calvin's POV; reality intrudes when others arrive.

### Suburban Mundanity vs. Cosmic Stakes
- Tree forts, Snowmen sculptures, family car trips.
- Treated with epic-scale framing.

### Slapstick + Wisdom
"Equal parts slapstick and philosophy, a perfect blend of wit, warmth, and wonder."

### Watterson's Restraint
- No merchandising.
- Refused to commercialize.
- Maintained Sunday format integrity.
- Quit at peak (1995).

## Themes
- Temporality of life, relationships, hobbies, memories.
- Tension between freedom and structure (Calvin vs. Hobbes' rationalism).
- Wonder at nature.
- Critique of modern life.

## Recurring Comic Engines
- Calvin's misadventures with snow goons, Susie, Moe.
- Calvin's dad explaining the world fictionally.
- Calvin at school (deeply bored).
- Calvin & Hobbes in nature, philosophizing.

## Rubric
1. Is there a child's POV grappling with adult themes?
2. Is imagination treated as real for the protagonist?
3. Are mundane stakes reframed as cosmic?
4. Is wisdom delivered alongside slapstick?
5. Does the humor avoid cynicism (warmth + wit)?
