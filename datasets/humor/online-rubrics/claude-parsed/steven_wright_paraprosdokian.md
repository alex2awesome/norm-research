# Steven Wright — One-Liner Technique

## Structure
- Non-sequitur observational jokes.
- Often achieve the joke without explicit setup.
- Observation = setup AND punch in one beat.

## Key Techniques

### Paraprosdokian
A figure of speech in which the last part of a sentence is so unexpected that it makes you reinterpret the first part.

Examples:
- "I'm writing a book. I've got the page numbers done."
- "I have a large seashell collection, which I keep scattered on beaches all over the world."
- "Right now I'm having amnesia and déjà vu at the same time. I think I've forgotten this before."

### Idiom Reformation
Take an idiom or cliché and reform the ending.
- "I went to a restaurant that serves 'breakfast at any time,' so I ordered French toast during the Renaissance."

### Premise as Punch
- Some jokes are just absurd statements with no setup.
- The audience does the imaginative work.
- "Right next to my house there is a vacant lot — but it's never vacant when I look in."

## Delivery
- Distinctly lethargic voice.
- Slow, deadpan delivery.
- Never looks to audience for approval.
- Mystery character behind the words.

## Content Categories
- Ironic / philosophical
- Sometimes nonsensical
- Non sequiturs
- Anti-humor
- Contrived situations

## Rubric (Steven Wright Style)
1. Does the joke fit one sentence?
2. Is there a paraprosdokian (last part reframes first)?
3. Is delivery deadpan / no-approval-seeking?
4. Is the premise itself absurd (no need for explicit setup)?
5. Does it require imaginative work from the audience?
