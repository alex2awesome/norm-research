# Shaggy Dog Story / Anti-Joke

## Definition
An extremely long-winded anecdote characterized by extensive narration of typically irrelevant incidents and terminated by an anticlimax. After long, involved narration, the punch line is absurd or irrelevant.

## Why It's Funny
The humor works through expectation-subversion:
- The audience's expectations are built up by elaborate presentation.
- Then the structure is intentionally subverted — punchline is anticlimactic, irrelevant, or absurd.
- Comedy reveals human folly: more energy invested in the pointless endeavor → more foolishness in the end.

## Classic Example
A character reacts to an exceptionally shaggy dog with: "That dog's not so shaggy." — subverts the entire buildup.

## Anti-Joke Defined
- Joke that subverts the joke form itself.
- Sets up a familiar joke structure, then refuses to deliver the expected payoff.
- Example: "Why did the chicken cross the road? To get to the other side." (the proto-anti-joke).
- Modern: "What did the boy do when he found his mother dead? He cried."

## Sub-types
- **Pointless punch**: 10 minutes of buildup → no payoff.
- **Literalist punch**: subverts by being too literal.
- **Failed escalation**: audience expects a great twist, gets a flat statement.

## Why It Works
- Meta-humor: laughs at the convention of jokes.
- Long form: payoff is the wasted time itself.
- Surprise via withholding payoff is itself a payoff.

## Rubric
1. Is the setup overlong / over-detailed?
2. Does the audience anticipate a big payoff?
3. Is the punchline deliberately anticlimactic?
4. Does the audience laugh at the form, not just content?
5. Is the wasted time itself the joke?
