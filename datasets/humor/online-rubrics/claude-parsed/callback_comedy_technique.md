# The Callback in Comedy

## Definition
A callback is a joke that refers to one previously told in the set. More broadly, a callback is a reference to a previous piece of material — a full joke, syntax, contextual setup, or anything previously mentioned.

## How It Works
The callback functions as a slow-motion joke or comedic foreshadowing. The result is usually a much bigger laugh the second time as the audience is more surprised the longer it's held back.

## "Ah-ha" Moment
Callbacks create an "ah-ha" moment where audience members grasp what you were up to when you set up the callback minutes earlier. They enjoy understanding the clever way you structured the joke.

## Benefits
- Makes a piece feel connected.
- Treats the audience as intelligent.
- Provides invention by way of necessity.
- Shapes performance into a cohesive whole.

## Critical Timing
- A callback is most effective when audiences have forgotten the original reference.
- If a detail is mentioned, then immediately referenced again, it generates little comedy.
- If audiences forget about an early detail, then encounter it again unexpectedly, the surprise creates a bigger laugh.

## Best Practice: Recontextualization
The strongest callbacks recontextualize the original reference. Instead of simply repeating it, the new reference uses it in a completely different context or with new meaning.

## Rubric for Callbacks
1. Is there an earlier setup planted before the callback?
2. Has enough time passed for the audience to forget?
3. Does the callback recontextualize, not just repeat?
4. Does it reward the attentive listener?
5. Does it land at a strategic moment (often near the end)?
