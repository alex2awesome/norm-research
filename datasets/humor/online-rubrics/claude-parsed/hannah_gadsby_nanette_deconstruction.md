# Hannah Gadsby — Nanette

## Central Critique
Gadsby uses *Nanette* to **deconstruct the nature of comedy itself**, exposing how its conventions can harm marginalized people.

## How Jokes Work (per Gadsby)
- Joke = setup creates tension; punchline releases tension.
- Comedians need to be funny to release tension.
- For marginalized people: the tension comes from being marginalized.
- Comedians like Gadsby learned to release tension she represented for OTHERS due to who she was.

## Critique of Self-Deprecation
- Self-deprecating humor is doubly painful for marginalized people.
- It adds another voice to the chorus of insults.
- Inviting the comedian to belittle themselves alongside the bullies.

## The "Half-Told Story" Problem
- Comedy turns trauma into "half-told stories."
- Joke structure truncates traumatic experience.
- Trauma needs more space and complexity than jokes can offer.
- The "easy punchline" replaces the "complicated full story."

## Structural Innovation
*Nanette* breaks the joke contract midway:
- First half: traditional jokes about coming-out experiences.
- Second half: returns to the same stories, telling the rest — the trauma.
- The jokes are no longer funny; they are revealed as wounds.

## Why It's Significant
- Demonstrated comedy can hold tragedy without resolution.
- Shifted what stand-up can be.
- Forced industry to reckon with whose pain becomes whose joke.

## Quitting (or Not)
- Gadsby announced quitting stand-up in *Nanette*.
- Continued performing after.
- The "quit" was rhetorical: announcing a new mode.

## Rubric (Gadsby Approach)
1. Does the comedian use joke form to deliver something more than a joke?
2. Is there a half-told story being completed?
3. Does the work critique its own form?
4. Is marginalized experience handled with full complexity?
5. Does the audience emerge changed (not just relieved)?
