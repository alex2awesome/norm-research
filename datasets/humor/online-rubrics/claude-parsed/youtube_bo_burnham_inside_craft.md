# Bo Burnham: Craft Interviews (compilation)

Synthesis of Bo Burnham's craft observations across podcast appearances and Q&A sessions, with particular attention to material from the Inside production-period interviews.

## The Form-Content Equation
- The form of the joke must do something the content alone can't.
- A song allows you to MEAN two things at once: the lyrics and the music.
- A camera move can underline a joke a microphone can't.
- The question is always: what does THIS medium let me do that another wouldn't?

## On Self-Awareness as Both Tool and Trap
- The comic acknowledging the comic is a tool: it cuts through pretense.
- But meta-awareness used too often becomes its own pretense.
- Use sparingly — once per show, not once per joke.

## On the Density of Inside
Inside packs jokes per minute at roughly 3x the rate of conventional stand-up specials. Burnham attributes this to:
- Editing that removes any moment the audience could disengage.
- Replacing setups with VISUAL information.
- Allowing songs to do double duty as setup AND punch.

## On Why Stand-Up Stopped Working for Him
- The room contracts the comic.
- Live performance demands certain rhythms that constrain other forms of expression.
- Recorded media allows for tonal shifts the stage cannot sustain.

## Craft Rubric From Burnham's Interviews
1. Is the form earning its keep — would another medium do this better?
2. Is meta-awareness used sparingly enough to still be effective?
3. Is the density of jokes high (no dead air)?
4. Are visuals replacing words where they can?
5. Does the piece allow tonal range a single live audience couldn't sustain?
6. Are you in service of the work, not the audience's affection?
