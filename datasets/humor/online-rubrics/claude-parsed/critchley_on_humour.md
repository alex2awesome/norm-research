# Simon Critchley — On Humour

## Central Thesis: Ritual and Subversion
Humour combines ritual and subversion — reproduction and revolution, imbricated and coexisting within humorous structures.

> "Humour both reveals the situation, and indicates how that situation might be changed."

## Incongruity Grounding
Built on Kant, Schopenhauer, Kierkegaard's incongruity theory: necessary congruence between joke structure and social structure, but presenting jokes as liberating/critical practices that "explode the arbitrariness of social rites."

## Humor as Dissensus
Functions as "moments of dissensus communis":
- Defeats expectations.
- Verbal inversions, contortions, explosions.
- Refusal of everyday speech.
- "Lights up the everyday."

## Ethics of Humor
- Acknowledges sexism, racism, dark sides.
- Important for reminding us of "people we would rather not be."
- The **bitter laugh is the ethical laugh** — laughs that critique self/in-group.

## Key Themes
- Boundary between human and animal.
- The gap between being a body and having a body.
- Affinity between humour and philosophy.
- Structural identity of reactionary and revolutionary humour.

## Three Functions
1. **Reactionary** — confirms in-group, mocks out-group.
2. **Revolutionary** — punctures pomposity of in-group.
3. **Existential** — reveals the absurd condition of being human.

## Rubric (Critchley)
1. Does the joke reveal a social situation?
2. Does it indicate how that situation might be changed?
3. Is it reactionary, revolutionary, or existential?
4. Does it punch up or down? Does the laughter critique self?
5. Does it combine reproduction (ritual) with subversion?
