# Martin's Four Humor Styles

Martin and colleagues identified four functionally distinct uses of humor that crosscut content and topic. Each style varies independently across persons.

## The two-by-two structure

Humor styles vary along **two orthogonal dimensions**:

- **Target:** humor directed at *self* vs. humor directed at *others/relationships*.
- **Valence:** *benign/adaptive* uses vs. *harmful/maladaptive* uses.

This produces four cells:

|              | Adaptive       | Maladaptive       |
|--------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Toward self  | Self-enhancing | Self-defeating    |
| Toward other | Affiliative    | Aggressive        |

## The four styles in detail

### 1. Affiliative humor (adaptive, other-directed)
- Saying funny things, telling jokes, witty banter.
- Goal: amuse others, ease tension, strengthen bonds.
- Does not denigrate self or others.
- Correlates with extraversion, cheerfulness, social support.

### 2. Self-enhancing humor (adaptive, self-directed)
- Maintaining a humorous perspective on adversity.
- Amusing oneself even when alone.
- Coping use of humor — closely related to Freud's "humor proper."
- Correlates with self-esteem, optimism, lower depression and anxiety.

### 3. Aggressive humor (maladaptive, other-directed)
- Sarcasm, ridicule, teasing, disparagement.
- Used to manipulate, criticize, or put others down.
- Compulsive teller of offensive jokes.
- Correlates with hostility, neuroticism, lower agreeableness.

### 4. Self-defeating humor (maladaptive, self-directed)
- Excessive self-deprecation, allowing oneself to be the butt.
- Amusing others at one's own expense to gain acceptance.
- Avoidance of real issues through humor.
- Correlates with depression, anxiety, low self-esteem, neediness.

## Functional criteria for placing a humor instance

To classify a particular humor act, ask:

1. **Who is the target?** Self, others, or no one in particular?
2. **What is the social effect?** Does it strengthen or damage relationships?
3. **What is the psychological effect on the user?** Does it support coping/well-being or compensate for deficit?

The same joke text can serve different styles depending on speaker intent, target attachment, and audience response.

## Why content-neutral

A dirty joke is not inherently aggressive. A self-deprecating remark is not inherently self-defeating. The classification is about *function*, not *content*.

- Self-deprecation that is genuinely good-natured = affiliative.
- Self-deprecation as a defense against intimacy = self-defeating.
- Roast humor in a context of mutual love = affiliative.
- The same roast in a context of resentment = aggressive.

## What this excludes

- A pure structural theory: HSQ tells you nothing about *what makes a joke land*, only about *what social purpose the joker is using humor to serve*.

## Diagnostic questions for any humor instance

- Toward whom is the humor directed?
- Does it maintain or damage the dignity of self/other?
- Does it serve coping or evasion?
- Does it serve connection or distance?
