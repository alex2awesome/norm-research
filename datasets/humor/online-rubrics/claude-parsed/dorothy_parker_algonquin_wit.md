# Dorothy Parker / Algonquin Round Table — Wit

## Parker's Distinction
> "Wit has truth in it; wisecracking is simply calisthenics with words."

She distinguished real wit (truth-bearing) from mere wisecracks (verbal exercise without insight).

## Famous Examples
- "You can lead a horticulture but you can't make her think." (pun on "lead a horse to water")
- "That woman speaks eighteen languages and can't say 'no' in any of them."
- Asked to use "horticulture" in a sentence — turned it into a pun in 10 seconds.
- "Brevity is the soul of lingerie."
- "Heterosexuality is not normal, it's just common."

## The Round Table Game
"I can give you a sentence" — multi-syllabic word turned into a pun in 10 seconds. Parker was best at this.

## Techniques

### Pun via Sound Substitution
- Replace a word with a homophone that creates a second meaning.

### Contradiction at the Sentence Level
- Setup + reversal in the same line.

### Truth-Telling Politeness
- Polite phrasing carrying barbed observation.

### Compression
- Single sentences carry full essays of meaning.

## Round Table Style Generally
- Lunchtime quips, often published in newspaper columns the next day.
- Rapid one-liners as competitive sport.
- Insider references.
- Cultivation of public reputation as wit.

## Rubric (Parker / Algonquin Wit)
1. Is there truth beneath the cleverness?
2. Is the sentence brief and quotable?
3. Does it use sound (pun, alliteration, rhyme)?
4. Is it polite-yet-cutting?
5. Could it be re-read and still land?
