# Cundall: Why Incongruity Alone Is Not Enough

Cundall (2007) argues that incongruity is a **necessary but not sufficient** condition for humor. He documents cases where genuine incongruity exists but produces no humor response, demonstrating that additional conditions must be specified.

## The under-determination problem

Many phenomena involve incongruity without being funny:
- **Surprise** (an unexpected loud noise — startles, doesn't amuse).
- **Confusion** (a baffling text — puzzles, doesn't amuse).
- **Awe** (a stunning vista — incongruous with everyday scale, doesn't amuse).
- **Horror** (a person's face suddenly changes shape — incongruous, terrifies).
- **Mystery** (the locked-room puzzle — incongruous, intrigues).
- **Tragedy** (the noble hero brought low by trivial flaw — incongruous, devastates).

Each of these involves the structural feature of incongruity but produces emotions other than amusement.

## What more is needed beyond incongruity

Cundall enumerates additional conditions that must obtain for an incongruity to produce humor specifically:

### 1. Perceptual readiness
The audience must be in a perceptual stance prepared to *play* with the incongruity, not to take it seriously.

### 2. A play frame / safe context
There must be contextual cues (genre, social setting, speaker tone) that signal "this is for amusement," not "this is real."

### 3. Recognizable resolution path
There must be a discoverable way to make sense of the incongruity — not necessarily a clean resolution, but enough cognitive traction that the audience can engage with it.

### 4. Affective tolerability
The content of the incongruity, even when negative or transgressive, must be tolerable to the audience. Beyond a threshold, the incongruity becomes painful rather than funny.

### 5. Appropriate target / object selection
The incongruity must concern an object whose disturbance is acceptable. Sacred objects, beloved persons, deep traumas may resist comic framing.

## The analytical procedure

Cundall recommends, for any candidate humor instance:

1. Identify the incongruity precisely.
2. Verify whether the audience has perceptual readiness.
3. Verify whether play-frame cues are present.
4. Verify whether resolution is achievable.
5. Verify whether the content is affectively tolerable.
6. Verify whether the object is in the comic-acceptable range.

If any condition fails, the same incongruity will produce a different response (surprise, confusion, awe, horror, offense).

## Compatibility with benign violation theory

Cundall's analysis converges with McGraw & Warren's benign violation theory: the additional conditions can be summarized as *benignness* of the violation. But Cundall offers a more granular decomposition into perceptual, contextual, cognitive, affective, and target-selection components.

## Implications for joke construction

A joke designer must ensure not just incongruity but also:
- The audience is primed to play (warm-up matters).
- Stage/medium signals "this is comedy" (genre matters).
- The incongruity is solvable (clarity matters).
- The content is within audience tolerance (calibration matters).
- The targets are acceptably disposable (sensitivity matters).

## Diagnostic questions

- What is the precise incongruity?
- Is the audience in a play-prepared state?
- Are there explicit play-frame cues?
- Is there a resolution path the audience can find?
- Does the content stay within the audience's affective tolerance?
- Are the targets in the comically-acceptable range for this audience?
