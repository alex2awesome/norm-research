# UCB Training Center: Blog Essays on Improv and Sketch

## On the "Game" as the Center of UCB Pedagogy
UCB pedagogy organizes around finding and playing the GAME of a scene. A game is the first specific, repeatable, unusual element identified in a scene. Once found, all subsequent moves heighten that game.

## The Three Kinds of Game
1. **Pattern Game** — a behavior repeats with variation.
2. **POV Game** — a character has an unusual point of view, applied across situations.
3. **Status/Reality Game** — an unusual social structure is treated as normal.

## On If-Then Logic
- IF the world is X, THEN logically Y follows.
- The improviser's job is to find the funniest LOGICAL implication, not the funniest random thing.
- Random escalation breaks the audience's investment.

## On Initiations
- Strong initiations contain a who, where, what, AND a hint of relationship.
- Avoid declaring the game in the initiation; let it emerge.
- A declarative line of dialogue is stronger than a question.

## On Editing Scenes
- Edit when the laugh peaks, not after.
- Sweeps mid-laugh teach the team trust.
- A scene that goes 30 seconds past its peak is unrecoverable.

## On Sketch as Logical Extension of Scene
- Sketches are scenes with the game pre-identified.
- The sketch's job: heighten the game systematically across acts.
- Each beat must complicate, not repeat.

## UCB Working Rubric
1. Have you identified the game in the first 30 seconds?
2. Are you heightening through if-then logic?
3. Is the heightening vertical, horizontal, or thematic?
4. Are initiations declarative and grounded?
5. Are you editing at the laugh peak?
6. Is each beat complicating, not just repeating?
