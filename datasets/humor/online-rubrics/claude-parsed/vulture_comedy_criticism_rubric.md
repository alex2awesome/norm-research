# Vulture: Comedy Criticism (working rubric synthesis)

Vulture's comedy criticism over a decade-plus (Jesse David Fox, Megh Wright, Matt Wilstein, Kathryn VanArendonk) has converged on recurring evaluative criteria. Synthesized below.

## What Vulture Asks of a Comedy Special
1. **Voice** — does this comic sound like nobody else?
2. **Density** — are there enough laughs per minute to justify the runtime?
3. **Architecture** — does the hour have a structure, or is it a string of bits?
4. **Risk** — does the comic try anything that could fail?
5. **Earned weight** — if the special turns serious, has it earned the right?
6. **Originality** — are the premises ones we've heard, or new?
7. **Resonance** — does the special sit with you the next day?

## What Vulture Asks of a Sitcom Episode
1. Character consistency.
2. A-story / B-story interweaving.
3. Joke density per page.
4. Emotional turn that justifies the half-hour.
5. World-building economy (no exposition for its own sake).
6. Surprise within form.

## What Vulture Asks of a Sketch
1. One clear comedic engine.
2. Escalation, not repetition.
3. Strong button.
4. Performances elevate the writing (or vice versa).

## On the Critic's Trap
- Don't praise difficulty for its own sake.
- Don't conflate intent with execution.
- Don't review the comic instead of the work.
- Acknowledge laughter as data even when craft is uneven.

## Vulture-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Voice: distinguishable in 30 seconds?
2. Density: laughs per minute high enough?
3. Architecture: does the work as a whole have shape?
4. Risk: was anything attempted that could have failed?
5. Earned weight: do tonal shifts pay off?
6. Originality: are premises new, or just performed well?
7. Resonance: are you still thinking about it tomorrow?
