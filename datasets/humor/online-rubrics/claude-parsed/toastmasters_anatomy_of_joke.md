# Toastmasters — Anatomy of a Joke

## Two-Part Structure
- **Setup**: furnishes information.
- **Punch line**: surprise twist on that information.

## Key Elements

### Surprise
- Laughter is created when surprise is present.
- A comedic surprise is "a twist without serious repercussions."
- (Echoes Benign Violation Theory.)

### Misdirection
- Audience is led away from the punchline.
- Misdirection statement makes the punch more effective.

### Truth as Foundation
- Always begin with the truth.
- Identify assumptions in your opening statement.
- Explore alternative interpretations until you find a funny twist.

### Concision
- Give listeners all info needed to understand the punch.
- No unnecessary information that weakens the effect.
- Make the joke as concise as possible.

### Punch at the End
- Put the punch as close to the end as possible.
- Don't bury the punch.

### Allow Audience Reaction
- Don't step on the audience's response.
- Allow time to laugh and soak in the humor.

## The Toastmasters "MAP" for Humor
- **M**aterial (truthful, relatable, specific)
- **A**udience (know who you're talking to)
- **P**resentation (timing, delivery, eye contact)

## Six Rules of Humor (Toastmasters)
1. Make it relevant.
2. Be original.
3. Tell the truth.
4. Be self-deprecating (when appropriate).
5. Avoid offensiveness.
6. Practice your timing.

## Rubric
1. Is the setup truthful and relatable?
2. Is there clear misdirection toward the wrong interpretation?
3. Is the punch as close to the end as possible?
4. Is every word necessary (concision)?
5. Does the speaker allow audience reaction?
