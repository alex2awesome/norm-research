# Conan O'Brien: Late Night Writing (interview compilation)

Conan O'Brien's recurring craft observations across his own podcast, SNL Q&As, and college talks.

## The Monologue Joke Anatomy
1. **News-line setup**: state the news factually in 1 line.
2. **Pivot phrase**: "which means," "in other words," "and which is..."
3. **Punch**: angle that recasts the news.

The joke must run under 12 seconds total. Audiences fatigue past that.

## On the Topical Joke Constraint
- The joke must be intelligible to a viewer who saw the news headline 10 seconds ago.
- Anything requiring background context is cut.
- Anything that could've worked yesterday is cut.

## On Writers' Room Velocity
- 7 writers, 50 jokes pitched per topic, 5 chosen.
- The pitch is a sport; the funniest 30 seconds in the room becomes the show.
- Brutal self-editing because the bar is "did the room laugh?"

## On the Late-Night Persona
- The persona must give the joke a HOST — not the writer's voice.
- Conan's persona allowed jokes that combined sincerity, absurdity, and self-deprecation.
- Letterman's persona allowed irony and contempt.
- Leno's persona constrained jokes to broad-appeal observation.
- The persona is a filter through which jokes pass; jokes that don't fit are cut.

## On Bombs as Information
- A bombed monologue joke is data: the writers misread the audience's familiarity, sympathy, or fatigue with the topic.
- Pros aren't surprised by bombs; they diagnose them.

## Conan-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Does the joke run under 12 seconds?
2. Is it intelligible to a viewer who knows only today's headlines?
3. Does it fit the host's persona, not the writer's?
4. Did the writers' room laugh out loud at first read?
5. Is the pivot phrase clean?
6. Will it be obsolete by tomorrow? (For monologue: yes. For show: no.)
