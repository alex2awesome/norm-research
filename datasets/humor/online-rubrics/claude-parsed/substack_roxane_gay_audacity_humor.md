# Roxane Gay's The Audacity: Humor and Power

Roxane Gay's Substack regularly addresses the politics of who gets to be funny about what.

## The Power Differential Test
Every joke takes place in a power relationship. To evaluate a joke ethically:
- Who is the speaker?
- Who is the target?
- What is the audience's relationship to each?
- What does the joke ASK the audience to enjoy?

A joke is not made worse by punching at power; it is made worse by punching at vulnerability.

## On the Limits of "It's Just a Joke"
"Just a joke" is a defensive frame, not an analytical one. Comedy carries arguments. The argument is: this is something we can laugh AT. Pretending otherwise is dishonest about what comedy does.

## On Self-Deprecation and Identity
- Self-deprecation about IMMUTABLE identity (race, body, queerness) often functions as preemptive permission for the audience to laugh AT that identity.
- Self-deprecation about CHOICES (laziness, procrastination, taste) is structurally different — the laugh is at agency, not category.
- The first kind requires an enormous amount of context and trust. The second is universal.

## On Funny Writing About Hard Things
- Acknowledge the weight of the subject.
- Find the absurd small detail inside the heavy frame.
- Let the absurd detail carry the laugh; don't insist on it.
- Trust the reader to feel both at once.

## Roxane Gay's Working Rubric
1. What power differential is operating in this joke?
2. Does it punch up, sideways, or down?
3. If it self-deprecates, is it about category or agency?
4. Does the joke argue something you'd defend in a serious essay?
5. Does it trust the reader to hold seriousness and humor together?
6. Could you make this joke if your target were in the room?
