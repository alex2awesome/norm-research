# Late Night Writers Room (Conan)

## Daily Process
1. **First hour**: writers craft premises from the day's news headlines.
2. Email first batch of jokes to a writers' assistant.
3. Jokes revised for grammar and style.
4. Batch sent to O'Brien for review.
5. He makes edits, approves, identifies topics to keep developing.
6. Second batch crafted and submitted around early afternoon.
7. After rehearsal, selected jokes vetted by research department (factual accuracy) and producers (sponsorship).
8. By 3:40 p.m., third batch submitted.
9. O'Brien, Andy Richter, and head writer Jeff Ross meet to finalize the monologue.

## Joke Quota
- 40 to 50 jokes per writer per day was common.
- High volume needed to find the few that work.

## Anonymous Submission
- Jokes are presented anonymously, by design.
- "The reason for that is so that writers don't feel desperate to come up with things."
- Removes ego, focuses on quality.

## Late-Night Joke Anatomy
- **Topic**: from today's news — must be familiar to mass audience.
- **Setup**: the news premise stated plainly.
- **Punchline**: connects via wordplay, surprising association, or angle.
- **Example**: "President X said Y. He hadn't been this surprised since [Z]."

## The Power of Constraint
- Daily deadline forces working through dry spells.
- News topics provide built-in setups.
- Volume means learning to kill darlings.

## Rubric
1. Is the topic from current news (audience-shared knowledge)?
2. Is the setup short and clear?
3. Is the punchline a surprising angle, not the obvious one?
4. Is it factually defensible (research-vetted)?
5. Could it land with a generic late-night audience?
