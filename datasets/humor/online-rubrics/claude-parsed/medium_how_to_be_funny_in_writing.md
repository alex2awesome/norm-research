# Medium: How to Be Funny in Writing

A consolidated rubric from the most-clapped Writing Cooperative essays on humor in prose.

## The Five Levers of Funny Prose
1. **Specificity** — "a Honda Civic" beats "a car."
2. **Surprise** — the unexpected word at the end of the sentence.
3. **Sound** — sentences with consonant clusters and rhythm scan funnier.
4. **Self-awareness** — the writer's voice noticing what's absurd.
5. **Stakes** — what would normally be tragic, played as banal (or vice versa).

## Sentence-Level Techniques
- **The k-sound rule**: words containing K (kale, Kafka, Kalamazoo) are funnier.
- **The list-of-three with the absurd third**: "He brought wine, cheese, and his ex-wife."
- **The unexpectedly precise number**: not "a lot of" but "31."
- **The tonal whiplash**: an academic register collapses into colloquial.
- **The understated noun**: tragedy described as "an inconvenience."

## Paragraph-Level Techniques
- Open with the most surprising sentence.
- Bury the punchline at the END of the paragraph.
- Use a callback in the next paragraph to the previous joke.
- Short paragraphs. Then. Sometimes. Even shorter.

## What Doesn't Work
- Telling the reader something is funny ("hilariously," "comically").
- Exclamation points!!!
- Italics for emphasis instead of word choice.
- Making yourself the hero of the funny story.
- Quoting your own clever line earlier in the piece.

## Rubric for "Is This Sentence Funny?"
1. Is there one specific, concrete noun?
2. Does the sentence end on the funniest word?
3. Does it use a hard consonant or unexpected register-shift?
4. Could the sentence stand alone in a tweet and get a laugh?
5. Is the tone deadpan rather than nudging?
6. Does it earn the laugh through observation, not insistence?
