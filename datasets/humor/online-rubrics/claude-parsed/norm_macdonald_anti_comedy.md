# Norm MacDonald — Joke Construction

## Approach
"A joke should catch someone by surprise."
- Mission statement.
- Describes not just the punchline but the meandering, straightlaced, sometimes anti-humorous path the joke takes.

## Setup-Heavy Construction
- The setup mattered more than the punchline.
- Long, winding setups that test audience patience.
- Meandering paths to the punch.
- Sometimes the "point" is that there isn't one.

## Quasi-Anti-Comedy
- Not strictly anti-comic — no revealing of artifice.
- But "hyper-exaggerated version of outdated joke format."
- Liberates the comedic force from its relation to the comic.
- Pushes the joke toward its own collapse.

## The Moth Joke
- Iconic example: 6+ minute setup involving a moth at a podiatrist's office.
- Punchline is essentially a children's joke.
- The discrepancy between long setup and corny punch IS the joke.
- Audience laughs at the form being subverted.

## Trademark Techniques
- **Old-Fashioned Premise**: jokes that sound like 1950s formats.
- **Earnest Delivery**: no winking.
- **Sudden Punctuation**: the punch is offhand, not telegraphed.
- **Wink-less Irony**: ironic without seeming ironic.

## Why It Works
- Audience trained on modern comedy is destabilized.
- The joke functions on multiple levels — corny on surface, sophisticated about form.
- Earnestness disarms cynicism.
- The "fail" is the win.

## Rubric (Norm-Style)
1. Does the setup take significantly longer than expected?
2. Is the punchline disproportionately corny?
3. Is the delivery earnest, no winking?
4. Does the joke comment on the joke form itself?
5. Does the audience laugh at form, not just content?
