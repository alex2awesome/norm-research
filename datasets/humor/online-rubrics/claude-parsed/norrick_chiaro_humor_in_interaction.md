# Norrick & Chiaro: Humor as Multifunctional Interactional Tool

The Norrick & Chiaro volume (2009) consolidates a key insight from interactional sociolinguistics: humor in real conversation is not a single phenomenon with a single function. It is a multifunctional interactional resource whose meaning depends on local context.

## Bypassing single-function reductionism

The volume explicitly argues against simplistic theories that reduce humor to one function (just relief, just incongruity-pleasure, just superiority). In actual interaction, the *same* humorous turn can simultaneously:

- Build rapport.
- Test alignment.
- Negotiate identity.
- Soften a face-threatening act.
- Establish in-group boundary.
- Probe an unspoken topic.
- Release tension.
- Display competence.

A complete account of any humor instance must specify which functions are operative in that local context.

## Forms of conversational humor catalogued

### 1. Canned jokes
Pre-formed jokes inserted into conversation. The teller signals "I am about to tell a joke," shifts to the joke frame, delivers, and returns. Often functions to display joke knowledge or fill conversational space.

### 2. Spontaneous wit / wordplay
A turn that exploits something just said for a witty effect. More integrated into conversation than canned jokes; demonstrates quick uptake.

### 3. Anecdotes / funny stories
Personal narratives constructed for comic effect. Often serve self-presentation as much as amusement.

### 4. Banter / mutual teasing
Reciprocal mock aggression between conversation partners. A relationship marker (you can banter with me = we are close).

### 5. Sarcasm / irony
Saying the opposite of what is meant. Establishes shared knowledge of context (only insiders catch the inversion).

### 6. Self-deprecation
Making oneself the target. Functions vary widely: humility, defense, bid for sympathy, social leveling.

### 7. Mock impoliteness
Deliberate violation of politeness norms in a way that signals "this is play, not real." A relationship marker.

## Interactional functions identified

The volume catalogs functions across many interactional types:

- **Solidarity construction** — humor as bonding.
- **In-group affiliation** — humor as boundary-marker.
- **Identity construction** — what kind of person am I, what kind of group am I in.
- **Power negotiation** — humor as asymmetric or symmetric power-play.
- **Politeness work** — humor as face-saving or face-threatening.
- **Topic management** — humor as a way of opening or closing topics.
- **Tension management** — humor as a way of dissipating awkwardness.

## Necessary conditions for interactional humor success

1. **Mutual recognition of the play frame** — both parties must know it's a joke.
2. **Sufficient shared background** for the humor to work.
3. **Compatible interactional goals** — the humor's function must serve both parties' aims.
4. **Politeness calibration** — the humor must respect or skillfully exploit the relevant face concerns.
5. **Uptake** — the response (laugh, smile, return wit) must complete the interactional move.

## Why this matters

A joke can be structurally well-formed yet fail in conversation because of:
- Wrong interactional moment (someone just shared bad news).
- Wrong relationship (banter from a stranger reads as offense).
- Wrong power dynamic (subordinates teasing superiors carries different stakes).
- Wrong cultural context.

Conversely, a structurally weak joke can succeed when its interactional function is well-served.

## Implications for evaluating humor

A complete evaluation requires:

1. Identifying the interactional context (who, where, when, what relationship).
2. Identifying the functions the humor is serving in that context.
3. Assessing whether the joke succeeds at those functions, not just structurally.

## Diagnostic questions

- What forms of conversational humor are in use here?
- What functions is the humor serving in this interaction?
- Is the play frame mutually recognized?
- Are the interactional preconditions (relationship, context, topic timing) right?
- Does the uptake complete the comic move?
