# Daniel Sloss: Building the Hour Special

## On Building an Hour Around a Single Idea
- The strongest hours have ONE thesis (Jigsaw: a partner is not a puzzle piece for completion).
- Every joke either supports the thesis, complicates it, or earns the right to land harder by association.
- Jokes that don't relate are cut, even if they kill in isolation.

## On the Funny-to-Devastating Pivot
- Train the audience to laugh at a register they trust (light, recognizable).
- Use that trust to pivot, late in the hour, into something painful.
- The laughs preceding the pivot become the emotional capital that lets the pivot land.
- Sloss's "Dark" routines do this systematically.

## On Premise Density vs. Story Density
- Open with high JOKE density — establish you can be funny.
- Mid-hour, slow into story; the audience is now patient with you.
- Late-hour, drop the joke density; you've earned the right to be sincere.

## On the Audience's Emotional Contract
- They came to laugh; you must deliver early and abundantly.
- Once delivered, they will follow you almost anywhere.
- Betraying that contract early loses them permanently.

## On Tags and Callbacks
- A tag should hit the same target with a different angle.
- A callback works because of EARLIER investment — never set up the callback, just place the seed.
- The audience must feel they DISCOVERED the callback.

## Sloss-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Does the hour have a single articulable thesis?
2. Does every bit serve that thesis somehow?
3. Is joke density highest at the beginning?
4. Has the audience earned the right to be devastated by the end?
5. Are callbacks discovered, not engineered?
6. Are you willing to cut killer jokes that don't fit?
