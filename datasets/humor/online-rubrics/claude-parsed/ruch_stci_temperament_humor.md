# Ruch's STCI: Cheerfulness, Seriousness, Bad Mood

The STCI (State-Trait-Cheerfulness-Inventory) measures three temperamental dimensions that, together, define a person's "humorous temperament" — the disposition that determines how easily they enter the comic frame.

## The three trait dimensions

### 1. Trait cheerfulness
- Low threshold for amusement.
- Frequent positive affect.
- Pleasure in humorous interaction.
- Habitual smiling and laughing.
- Composure in adversity (cheerful coping).

### 2. Trait seriousness
- Preference for goal-directed, important activities.
- Sober reflective attitude.
- Higher threshold for amusement.
- Lowered intrinsic interest in playfulness.
- *Not the opposite of cheerfulness* — one can be both serious-trait and cheerful-trait.

### 3. Trait bad mood
- Tendency toward sadness/ill humor.
- Sullenness, irritability.
- Reduced reward responsiveness.
- Higher threshold for amusement, similar to seriousness — but driven by negative affect.

## State analogues

Each trait has a state form: a person can momentarily be more cheerful, serious, or in a bad mood than their trait baseline. Humor depends on the *state at the moment of perception*, not the trait alone.

## Why this matters for "what makes a joke funny"

A joke does not have a fixed funniness rating — its funniness depends on the temperament/state of the perceiver. The same stimulus is funnier:
- To high-cheerful audiences than to low-cheerful.
- To audiences in cheerful state than serious state.
- To audiences not in bad mood.

Therefore, evaluating "is this funny" requires specifying the temperament profile of the audience.

## Two virtue-related humor styles (later addition)

Ruch and colleagues added two further humor styles that link humor to character strength:

### Benevolent humor
- Treats human weakness and wrongdoing **benevolently** — with sympathy and acceptance.
- Smile of understanding rather than ridicule.
- Aligns with virtues of kindness and forgiveness.
- "We all err, and there is something endearing about it."

### Corrective humor
- Aims to **correct and improve** wrongdoing through humor.
- The bite of satire turned to ethical purpose.
- Aligns with the virtue of justice.
- "By laughing at this fault, we may discourage it."

## Distinct from Martin's HSQ

Where Martin's four-style framework focuses on social functions (affiliative, aggressive, etc.), Ruch's framework focuses on:
1. Temperamental disposition to enter the humor frame.
2. Virtue orientation (benevolent vs. corrective).

## Diagnostic questions

- What is the audience's trait cheerfulness/seriousness/bad-mood profile?
- What is their current state along these dimensions?
- Does the humor adopt a benevolent posture toward human flaws or a corrective one?
- Does the joke require a low threshold of amusement (high-cheerful audience) or can it land even with a serious/bad-mood audience?
