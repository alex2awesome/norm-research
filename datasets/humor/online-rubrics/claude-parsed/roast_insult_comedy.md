# Roast / Insult Comedy

## Definition
A form of insult comedy in which a specific guest of honor is subjected to jokes at their expense, alongside genuine praise and tributes. The assumption is that the roastee can take jokes in good humor.

## Origins
- New York Friars Club gatherings, early 20th century.
- Private events where members poked fun at each other.
- 1949: first official Friars roast (Maurice Chevalier as roastee).
- 1970s: Dean Martin Celebrity Roast specials televised.
- 2000s: Comedy Central revived format.

## Structure
- **Roastmaster**: host and moderator.
- **Roasters**: friends, colleagues, professional comedians.
- Each roaster delivers their best jabs.
- **Roastee** gets the final word — rebuttal.

## Core Social Contract
- The room understands the insults are part of the game.
- Consent, context, ritual make it thrilling rather than mean.
- Affection underpins the abuse.
- "Genuinely sensitive topics" are avoided.

## Comedic Techniques
- **Personal embarrassment**: career flops, romantic failures.
- **Exaggeration**: take a flaw and amplify.
- **Double entendre**: hint at deeper truths.
- **Cross-roastee jabs**: fellow roasters attack each other.
- **Setup-then-shame**: build up the roastee, then drop the bomb.

## Why Roasts Are Funny
- Forbidden things said in front of the target.
- Acknowledged truths everyone has thought.
- Affection wrapped in venom.
- Audience sees the roastee laugh = permission.

## Rubric
1. Is there a clear roast contract (consent)?
2. Are insults rooted in affection?
3. Are they specific and earned (not generic)?
4. Does the roastee laugh / sign off?
5. Are sensitive topics avoided / handled with care?
