# Garbage Day: How Internet Humor Works

Ryan Broderick's Substack analyzes meme economies and the changing texture of online comedy.

## The Three Layers of an Internet Joke
1. **Surface joke** — what a normal person sees.
2. **Format joke** — recognizing the meme template, getting the meta-laugh.
3. **Posting-context joke** — the joke is partly that someone posted it HERE, NOW.

The funniest posts hit all three; mass-shared posts hit at least two.

## Why Some Memes Spread
- Recognizable template → low cognitive cost to receive.
- Adaptable → users can substitute their own subject.
- In-group flag → posting it signals which corner of the internet you live in.
- Time-bound → the laugh decays after the moment passes.

## Why Some Jokes Die in Group Chats
- The format is too new for half the recipients.
- The format is too old (cringe to post unironically).
- The poster mis-uses the template's structure.
- The post explains itself.

## On Reposting and the Death of Originality
The internet's dominant comedy unit is no longer the joke; it's the SCREENSHOT of someone else's joke. The remixer is the comedian. This shifts craft toward CURATION: which juxtaposition, which crop, which context.

## On Brand Accounts and Posters
- Personas (the Wendy's account era) collapsed because corporations can't sustain edge.
- The shitposter's most underrated skill is restraint — knowing when NOT to post.
- The ratio is the only objective metric.

## Garbage Day Working Rubric for an Internet Joke
1. Does it work on the surface for someone who's never seen the format?
2. Does it reward recognition of the format?
3. Does it signal where you post from?
4. Is the timing tied to a current event or moment?
5. Did you NOT explain the joke?
6. Does the crop or screenshot itself add a layer?
