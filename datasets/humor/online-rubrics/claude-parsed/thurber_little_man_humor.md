# James Thurber — Humor for an Age of Anxiety

## Style
- Concise language laced with irony.
- Humorous undercurrent throughout.
- Transforms everyday life into profound or absurd situations.
- Distinctive cartoons accompany.

## Influence on American Humor
- "First fully achieved style of American literary humor since Mark Twain."
- **Invented the one-liner gag cartoon** as we know it today.
- New Yorker style architecture.

## The "Little Man" Character
A 20th-century protagonist:
- Comic antihero.
- Function: create sympathy, not scorn.
- Brings into question the values and behaviors of society.
- Outwardly competent, inwardly bewildered.
- Examples: Walter Mitty, the average urban man.

## Cumulative Humor
- Thurber's comedy is cumulative — read whole stories to fully grasp absurdity.
- Comic monologues build slowly.
- Not punchy one-liners (in prose).
- Plot-driven absurdity.

## Topics
- Marriage and the battle of sexes (his collected works).
- Office life.
- Domestic rituals.
- Self-doubt.
- Imagination's escapes from drudgery (*Mitty*).

## "The Secret Life of Walter Mitty" Structure
- Daily mundane errand interrupted by daydream sequences.
- Each daydream casts Mitty as heroic.
- Reality returns, deflating.
- Bathos pattern.

## Drawing Style
- Loose, deceptively simple line.
- Characters with rounded heads, off-kilter proportions.
- Often anxious-looking.
- Captures inner state.

## Rubric (Thurber Style)
1. Is the protagonist a recognizable "little man"?
2. Is the absurdity cumulative (story-length)?
3. Does the comedy generate sympathy, not scorn?
4. Are interior states (anxiety, insecurity) the engine?
5. Does it reveal society's contradictions?
