# Defector: Humor Criticism (consolidated rubric)

Defector's writers (Drew Magary, Albert Burneko, Patrick Redford) have written extensively on what makes sports/pop-culture humor work in 2020s digital writing.

## The Magary Voice Principles
- Nothing in the post should sound like a press release.
- Profanity is punctuation, not crutch.
- The funny is in your specific frustration with the specific thing.
- Every blog should contain at least one absurd hypothetical.
- The reader should be able to hear you talking, not writing.

## The Burneko Tonal Model
- Anchor every piece in a small, vivid, true detail (a smell, a sound, a particular insult).
- Build outward into argument; use rage productively.
- The joke is in the OVER-COMMITMENT to the bit.
- Never break to wink. Sustain the bit longer than seems advisable.

## Defector's Comment Section as Audience
Defector writers report tuning their humor to a paying-subscriber comment culture: readers who reward depth, despise condescension, and detect inauthenticity instantly. This means:
- No "as we all know" framing.
- No celebrity sycophancy.
- Acknowledge when the bit is dumb; commit anyway.

## What Defector Considers Hack
- Twitter-thread structure ("the wildest part is...").
- Lists of brand names presented as a joke.
- Sarcasm that requires italics to read.
- Sentences that announce the punch ("and here's where it gets wild").

## Defector Working Rubric for a Funny Blog
1. Does the piece have a real, specific opinion?
2. Is the funniest sentence buried mid-paragraph (not telegraphed)?
3. Have you sustained at least one bit longer than seems wise?
4. Is your voice distinguishable from a generic news blog?
5. Did you avoid all "winking" devices (italics, "lol," ellipses)?
6. Would you say this out loud at a bar without embarrassment?
