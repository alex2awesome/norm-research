# Lawrence Wood's New Yorker Caption Contest Strategy

Lawrence Wood is the record-holder for most New Yorker Caption Contest wins.

## Key Principles

**Word Economy**: Keep captions brief. The 1912 San Francisco Call advised that "the title must not exceed sixteen words" and emphasized "the shorter the better."

## Core Topics (29 chapters in his book)
- "Who's Talking" (correctly identify which character is speaking)
- "What's Happening"
- "Tell a Story" (don't merely describe the cartoon)
- "Choose Your Words Carefully" (eliminate unnecessary words)
- Avoid being "Too Predictable" or "Too Unpredictable"

## Critical Insights

**Originality Matters**: When a cartoon generated over 200 similar submissions, the winning caption "I will give you no quarter" succeeded where variations like "I give no quarter" and "I will give you no quarters" did not — through precision and distinctiveness.

**Manage Expectations**: Wood himself submitted more than 800 gags while losing frequently, yet achieved 8 wins and 15 finalist placements overall.

**Get Feedback**: Test multiple entries with friends and family judges before submitting.

## Bob Mankoff's tips (referenced)
- Surprise is the essential ingredient in humor.
- Surprise violates our expectations — when the unexpected occurs and we realize there's nothing to worry about, we laugh.
- New Yorker cartoons "remove some of the main levers by which we're funny — and they do it intentionally — which are obscenity, aggression and cruelty."

## Practical Rubric for a Caption
1. Correctly identify the character delivering the line.
2. Don't just describe what's happening — tell a story.
3. Accept the premise of the cartoon, no matter how outlandish.
4. Caption must make sense within the parameters of the premise.
5. Choose words carefully — eliminate unnecessary words.
6. Find the unique angle — avoid the obvious read.
7. Aim for surprise without obscenity, aggression, or cruelty.
