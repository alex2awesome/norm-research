# David Letterman's Top Ten List

## Structure
- One setup → 10 punchlines.
- Treat as "a joke with 10 punchlines."
- Counts down from #10 (warmup) to #1 (peak).

## Inherently Funny Words
- Letterman privileges words that bring their own comedy.
- "Pants," "ass," "Regis," "Ed Sullivan" — guaranteed laughs.
- Hard consonants (K-sounds) preferred.
- Specific brand names + ridiculous attributes.

## The Final Joke Strategy
- #1 should be the strongest.
- Often the shortest.
- Should have summation/finality.
- Avoid making it too long or too specific.

## Rhythm and Variety
- "Like a meal or music — you want variety."
- Don't put two long ones next to each other.
- Movement in rhythm matters.
- Mix short punches with longer setups.

## Escalation and Plot Twists
- Numbered structure invites escalation.
- Audience anticipates each next number.
- Plot twists like "8 and a half" subvert numerical expectations.
- Rare meta breaks.

## Common Topical Hooks
- Current celebrity / political figure.
- Recent event.
- Universal fear/embarrassment.

## Writers' Approach (Joe Toplyn, head writer)
- Generate 50–100 candidates.
- Cull to top 10.
- Order: weak → strong → strongest at #1.
- Tag at the very end if applicable.

## Why It Worked for 30+ Years
- Predictable format with infinite variation.
- Audience anticipation built-in.
- Easy to reference / quote.
- Quotable line at end.

## Rubric (Top 10 List)
1. Is the unifying topic specific and clear?
2. Are inherently funny words used?
3. Does the list build (weak to strong)?
4. Is #1 the strongest, shortest, most summary?
5. Is rhythm varied (not all same length)?
