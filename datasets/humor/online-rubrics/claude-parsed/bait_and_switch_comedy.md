# Bait and Switch (Comedy)

## Definition
A type of setup where a character leads the audience or other characters into thinking they're going to say or do something — but says/does something unexpected instead.

## Two-Part Structure
1. **The Bait**: character says something that raises a specific expectation.
2. **The Switch**: character keeps talking; audience discovers the expectation was wrong.

## Why It Works
The effect of surprise is what makes people laugh. The audience commits to interpretation A, the speaker reveals interpretation B.

## Origin of the Name
From a now-illegal advertising practice: store advertised reduced-price item, then "all out" upon arrival, sold customer a more expensive similar item.

## Classic Example (Groucho Marx)
> "He may look like an idiot and talk like an idiot but don't let that fool you. He really is an idiot."

The setup leads us to expect "actually he's smart" — switch reveals "no, really an idiot."

## Sub-Types

### Bait-and-Switch Comparison
- "He's faster than… a slug after a rainstorm."
- Setup expects superlative; punch delivers anti-superlative.

### Bait-and-Switch Comment
- Speaker seems to set up praise; delivers insult (or vice versa).

### Long Bait-and-Switch
- Multi-step setup before reveal.
- Used in shaggy dog stories.

### Visual Bait-and-Switch
- Image hides reveal until camera/frame moves.

## Used Throughout Comedy History
- Groucho Marx
- Woody Allen
- *Airplane!*, *The Hangover*
- Used in basically every joke that has misdirection.

## Rubric
1. Is there a clear setup raising a specific expectation?
2. Does the switch deliver something unexpected?
3. Is the surprise valid in retrospect (not arbitrary)?
4. Is the gap between bait and switch funny (not just confusing)?
5. Is the switch as compact as possible?
