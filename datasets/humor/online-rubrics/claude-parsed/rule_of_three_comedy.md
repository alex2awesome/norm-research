# The Rule of Three in Comedy

## Definition
A simple writing structure where you list three things — the first two set up an expectation, and the third one breaks it in a surprising or absurd way.

## Why It's Funny
- Comedy writing relies on building tension to a comedic release.
- Tension built with the first two items in the pattern, then released with the final item.
- The third item should be the funniest of the three.
- Pattern + surprise: we see patterns and rhythm when three elements are available, so the surprise of a third element is naturally funnier.

## Why Three Specifically
Three is the smallest number that creates a pattern and lets you break it. The rule of three combines brevity and rhythm with the smallest amount of information needed to create a pattern.

## How It Works in Practice
- Set up a pattern of two items.
- Subvert audience expectations by breaking that pattern with the third item.
- The third element creates surprise; it is frequently the punch line.

## Examples
- "Life, liberty, and the pursuit of … happiness." (parallel; not subverted)
- Comedy version: "I love beaches, sunsets, and crippling debt."
- Bart Simpson: "I'm Bart Simpson. Who the hell are you?" (after a long polite intro pattern)

## Rubric for a Rule-of-Three Joke
1. Are there exactly three items in the list?
2. Do the first two establish a clear, expected pattern?
3. Does the third item subvert that pattern?
4. Is the third item placed last (anchored at end)?
5. Is the third item maximally surprising or absurd given the setup?
