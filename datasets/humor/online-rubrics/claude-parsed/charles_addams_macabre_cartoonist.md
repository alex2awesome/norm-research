# Charles Addams — Macabre Cartoon Humor

## Core Approach
> "The goal was never to create fear, but to defuse it."

Infuse horror with playfulness so it appeals to those who prefer daylight to night.

## Suggestion Over Depiction
- "The suggestion of blood, the suggestion of gore, the suggestion of torture is always there but his cartoons never showed it."
- Audience completes the dark in their imagination.
- The unseen is more comic than the seen.

## Style: Morbid + Mundane
Addams discovered comic gold at the crossroads of:
- The morbid (death, gore, horror)
- The mundane (everyday domesticity)

Use everyday domestic activity as a frame for the macabre.

## Famous Cartoon Examples
- Family preparing to pour boiling oil on Christmas carolers.
- A skier bypassing both sides of a tree.
- A husband at the door asking wife if she let the cat out — visible cat-shaped silhouette in cement-mixing tank.

## Visual Techniques
- Delicate ink wash.
- Atmospheric darkness.
- Gothic architecture, gnarled trees.
- Family looks "off" but is fully human.

## How Macabre Becomes Funny
- Treat the morbid AS IF it were ordinary.
- The family doesn't realize they're spooky.
- Visitors are the ones disturbed.
- Inversion: norm-violating behaviors are this family's norms.

## Background Origins
- Played in graveyards as child.
- Cleaned up crime scene photos for *True Detective* magazine.
- Developed visual fluency in macabre.

## Influence
- *The Addams Family* (TV, films).
- Horror-comedy hybrid genre.
- Roz Chast cited him as inspiration.
- Defined "New Yorker" sensibility.

## Rubric (Addams-Style Macabre Cartoons)
1. Is the morbid suggested rather than shown?
2. Is the macabre framed as mundane?
3. Are the "monstrous" characters going about their day normally?
4. Is the surrounding world the one disturbed?
5. Does the cartoon reward second-look interpretation?
