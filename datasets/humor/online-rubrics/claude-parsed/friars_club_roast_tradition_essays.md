# Friars Club: Roast Tradition (consolidated rubric)

## The Roast as Ritualized Insult Comedy
The roast is a controlled-aggression form: roastees agree to be insulted, audience consents to laugh AT them, roasters compete for the harshest landing line. Its ethics depend on:
- The roastee's full consent.
- The targets being CHOICES (career flops, public behaviors), not immutable identity.
- Reciprocity — roasters expect to be roasted in turn.

## The Roast Joke Anatomy
1. **Compliment-pivot setup**: "I've known X for years..." (signal affection).
2. **Specific target**: a real, public, mockable thing.
3. **Punchline**: brutal, surprising, brief.
4. **Optional out**: "but seriously..." rescue at the end of the set.

## On Punching Targets
- Career failures: ALWAYS fair game.
- Public behaviors: fair game.
- Spouse / kids: questionable; needs roastee's pre-clearance.
- Immutable traits (race, body, family of origin): risky, mostly avoided in modern roasts.
- Recent tragedy: never. Even in roasting culture.

## On the Order of Roasters
- Open with a moderate roaster to set the room's level.
- Build toward the most aggressive set.
- The roastee's response set caps the night.
- Heaviest hitter often goes 2nd or 3rd to last (penultimate gets the hardest pre-roastee laughs).

## On Cross-Roasting
- Roasters roast each OTHER as much as the roastee.
- This distributes aggression and signals camaraderie.
- A roast that focuses only on the roastee gets exhausting.

## On the Roastee's Final Set
- Demonstrates self-awareness and good sportsmanship.
- Returns the most aggressive shots back.
- Closes with sincerity (the "but seriously" moment).

## Friars Club Working Rubric
1. Does the joke target a CHOICE, not an immutable trait?
2. Has the roastee consented to this kind of material?
3. Is there a compliment-pivot to signal affection?
4. Is the punchline brutal AND brief?
5. Does the order of the roast build appropriately?
6. Is there room for sincerity at the end?
