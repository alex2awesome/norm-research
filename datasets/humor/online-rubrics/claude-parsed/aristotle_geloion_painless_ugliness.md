# Aristotle on to geloion (the laughable)

Aristotle's surviving definition of comedy depends on a precise condition: the comic is a species of ugliness or error, but one that crosses no threshold of harm.

## The defining formula (Poetics 1449a32-37)

> "Comedy is, as we said, an imitation of inferior people — not, however, in the full sense of the word bad, but the laughable is a species of the base or ugly. It consists in some defect or ugliness which is not painful or destructive."

## Necessary conditions for the laughable

1. **The hamartēma (mistake/blemish) is real** — there must be an actual fault, error, ignorance, or unseemliness present.
2. **It is not painful (anōdunon)** — neither to the agent nor to the spectator at the moment of perception.
3. **It is not destructive (ou phthartikon)** — it does not cause ruin, death, or major harm.
4. **It belongs to the inferior (hoi phauloi)** — the comic mode imitates people worse than ourselves, so we look down without strong negative emotion.

## What collapses the joke

- If the mistake causes visible suffering → it slides into pain or pity (tragedy).
- If the mistake destroys someone or something important → no comic frame.
- If the target is too noble or close to us → the painless distance is lost.

## The well-bred wit (Nicomachean Ethics IV.8)

The *eutrapelos* (the witty person) is the mean between two vices:
- **Buffoon (bōmolochos)** — laughs at anything, says anything for a laugh, no taste.
- **Boor (agroikos)** — neither makes nor receives jokes, joyless.
- The eutrapelos jokes only at things appropriate to a free person, never at what a free person would be ashamed of.

## Tractatus Coislinianus (likely epitome of lost Poetics II)

The Tractatus enumerates verbal and material sources of laughter:
- **Verbal sources:** homonymy, synonymy, garrulity, paronyms (additions/clippings), diminutives, perversion of word, grammar/syntax errors.
- **Material sources:** assimilation (worse/better), deception, the impossible, the possible-but-inconsequent, things contrary to expectation, debasing personae, choice of vulgar dancing, when the powerful pass over great matters and choose the worthless, when the story is disjointed and lacks sequence.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is there a real fault, mistake, or ugliness present?
- Does the audience feel pain or pity at the moment of laughter? If yes, the laughter is suspect.
- Is anyone or anything actually being destroyed?
- Does the joker show eutrapelia (taste) rather than bōmolochia (will to laugh at anything)?
