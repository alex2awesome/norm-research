# Do Androids Laugh at Electric Sheep? Humor Understanding Benchmarks from The New Yorker Caption Contest

By Jack Hessel, Ana Marasović, Jena Hwang, Lillian Lee, Jeff Da, Rowan Zellers, Robert Mankoff, Yejin Choi.

## Three Tasks for Humor Understanding
1. **Matching**: Match a cartoon to a winning caption (vs. distractors).
2. **Quality Ranking**: Rank a finalist caption higher than a low-quality one.
3. **Explanation**: Explain why a cartoon-caption pair is funny.

## Annotation Guidelines (each cartoon is annotated with):
- **Image description**: literal description of the scene, locations, entities.
- **Unusual elements**: what is incongruous about the image.
- **Caption explanation**: why the winning caption is funny — connecting the unusual elements to a punch.

## Findings on Humor
- Captions exploit incongruity in the cartoon image.
- The winning caption typically:
  - Identifies the unusual element
  - Provides a frame that resolves it (often by recasting the unusual as mundane in a new context)
  - Achieves surprise for the reader
- Low-quality captions either describe the image or provide a non-resolving line.

## Rubric
1. Does the caption identify (implicitly) the unusual element?
2. Does it impose a coherent frame that resolves the incongruity?
3. Is the resolution surprising (not the most obvious read)?
4. Does it tell a story rather than describe?
5. Is the language tight and well-chosen?
