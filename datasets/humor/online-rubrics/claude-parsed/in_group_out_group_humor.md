# In-Group vs. Out-Group Humor

## Solidarity Function
Humor denigrating an out-group reinforces in-group solidarity. Jokes invoke imaginary enemies — spouses, mothers-in-law, politicians, bosses — to summon belonging.

## Identity-of-Speaker Effect
Who delivers the joke matters significantly:
- A joke about a group is funnier, less offensive, more acceptable when made by an in-group member.
- Gay joke from a gay person > from a straight person.
- Asian joke from an Asian person > from someone outside.
- Race jokes from same-race comedian considered acceptable; same content from outside considered offensive.

## Trust Relationship (Comedian–Audience)
Comedy is a trust transaction:
- Audience extends benefit of doubt.
- Trust earned through identity, history, persona.
- Trust enables transgression.
- Without trust, the same line is hostile.

## Live Joke Performance Dynamics
- In-group scenarios "haunted by out-group persons."
- Even physically absent, out-groups are present as joke subjects.
- The room defines who's in.

## Strategic Uses
- **Solidarity**: bond in-group via shared mockery.
- **Hierarchy**: establish status within group.
- **Identity indexing**: signal who you are.
- **Critique**: punch up at power.
- **Self-mockery**: reveal in-group flaws to disarm out-group critique.

## Trust + Humor Theory
- Trust is necessary for humor to land.
- Humor reciprocally builds trust.
- Failed humor damages trust.

## Rubric
1. Who is the joke's in-group / out-group?
2. Does the comedian have in-group standing?
3. Has the comedian earned audience trust?
4. Does the joke build or break solidarity?
5. Does the audience identity match the comedian's intended audience?
