# Killing It (David Tate Jenkins): Stand-Up Coaching Rubrics

Synthesis of pedagogical materials from the Killing It comedy coaching project.

## The Beginner's Five-Minute Set Anatomy
1. **Opener (60s)**: establishes who you are, why we should listen.
2. **Bit 1 (60s)**: short, punch-dense bit; demonstrates competence.
3. **Bit 2 (90s)**: longer story; demonstrates range.
4. **Bit 3 (60s)**: punchy, returns to opener's energy.
5. **Closer (30s)**: callback or strong button.

The five-minute set is the unit of stand-up assessment. The shape matters as much as the content.

## On the Premise-First Workflow
1. Write 50 premises.
2. Pick the 5 you most believe.
3. Generate 5 jokes per premise.
4. Pick the strongest joke per premise.
5. Set order them by energy and tone.
6. Stage-test for 10 sets before re-evaluating.

## On Memorization
- Memorize the BEATS, not the words.
- Word-perfect memorization makes you sound canned.
- Beat memorization gives you room to react to the room.

## On Microphone Technique
- Mouth 3–6 inches from mic.
- Don't hide behind the stand.
- Eye contact with three sections of the room.
- The mic is an instrument; learn it.

## On Recording Sets
- Audio at minimum.
- Listen at 1.5x speed for pacing diagnostic.
- Listen at 1x speed for word choice diagnostic.
- Annotate every laugh, miss, and silence.

## Killing It Working Rubric
1. Does the five-minute set have a clear shape (opener, bits, closer)?
2. Are bits sequenced for energy flow, not arbitrary?
3. Are you memorizing beats, not words?
4. Are you commanding the mic and the room?
5. Are you recording and reviewing every set?
6. Are you tracking which jokes have hit in 10+ sets?
