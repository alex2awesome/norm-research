# George Carlin's Method

## The Filing System
- 1,400+ files on his computer with notions, ideas, data, materials over 44 years.
- Paper scraps with words and phrases sorted by category.
- Cross-referenced to build larger routines from independent ideas.

## Word-Play Principles
The "Modern Man" routine ("I'm a modern man, a man for the millennium. Digital and smoke free…") demonstrates:
- **Density of word-play**: piling up jargon and clichés.
- **Self-aware buzzwords**: invokes corporate/marketing-speak ironically.
- **Rhythm of clichés**: stringing them creates rhythm and absurdity.

## The List Routine
Carlin loved list bits. To produce 25 strong list items, he'd write 40–50 candidates, then refine the surviving 25 over many iterations.

## Refinement Process
- Every line reworked many times.
- Material evolved on stage.
- "Better and better" through performance feedback.

## The Carlin Method (paraphrased)
1. Capture every observation/idea immediately, however raw.
2. File by topic / category.
3. Cross-reference categories to find unexpected combinations.
4. Generate way more material than needed.
5. Refine via repeated performance.
6. Don't be precious — ruthlessly cut.

## Themes
- Language and its abuse.
- Hypocrisy of authority.
- The body and its embarrassments.
- Classism and political power.

## Rubric (Carlin Style)
1. Is there a clear topical anger or wonderment driving the bit?
2. Is the language dense and rhythmic?
3. Does the bit reveal hypocrisy or pretension?
4. Are clichés stripped to absurdity?
5. Has the bit been refined through many iterations?
