# Ellis Rosen: Cartoon Craft Essays

Ellis Rosen is a New Yorker cartoonist whose process posts and craft essays focus on the BATCH-PRODUCTION discipline of weekly submissions.

## On the Weekly Batch
- Cartoonists submit ~10 cartoons per week to The New Yorker.
- Most weeks, zero are bought.
- The discipline is to keep producing despite zero feedback.

## On Generating 10 Ideas
- Rosen's working method: brainstorm 30+ premises, draw the strongest 10.
- Premise sources: news headlines, idioms taken literally, animals doing human things, two unrelated tropes colliding.
- Most premises are bad; the discipline is to find the few that aren't.

## On the Drawing
- Skill in drawing is necessary but not sufficient.
- The drawing must serve the gag — overdrawn cartoons distract.
- Negative space gives the eye time to read the caption.

## On Caption-First vs. Drawing-First
- Many cartoonists generate captions in batches, then sketch images.
- Others sketch images and find captions later.
- Rosen advocates caption-first: comedy lives in the language; image executes.

## On the Editor's Role
- The editor (currently Emma Allen) selects ~17 of ~1000 weekly submissions.
- Selection criteria: original, tonal fit, drawable into the magazine.
- Cartoonists develop relationships through repeated submission.

## On Knowing When to Quit a Cartoon
- A cartoon you can't draw clean in three tries probably isn't ready.
- A caption you can't get under 12 words probably isn't ready.
- Move on; the failed cartoon's premise may resurface stronger.

## Rosen-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Did you generate 30+ premises to find these 10?
2. Is the premise original (not seen in this magazine recently)?
3. Does the drawing serve the gag without distracting?
4. Is the caption under 12 words?
5. Does the lock between image and caption work cold?
6. Are you producing weekly enough to stay loose?
