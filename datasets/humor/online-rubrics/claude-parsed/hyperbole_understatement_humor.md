# Hyperbole vs. Understatement

## Hyperbole (Overstatement)
A figure of speech in which statements are exaggerated or extravagant. Reflects/effects strong feelings or impressions.

### In Comedy
- Powerful tool for satire (audiences easily understand exaggeration).
- Generates strong feelings.
- Examples: "I've told you a million times…" / "I'm so hungry I could eat a horse."
- Key in tall tales, shaggy dog stories.

### Why It's Funny
- Deviation from expected magnitude.
- Implicit absurdity in the size.
- Reveals true intensity of feeling.

## Understatement (Litotes when negated)
A figure of speech in which statements are purposely understated. Indicates speaker's nonchalance/obliviousness regarding an important situation.

### In Comedy
- Comic from treating very lightly something quite serious.
- Hinges on absurdity of soft language in inappropriate situations.
- Example (Wilde, *Earnest*): "To lose one parent may be regarded as a misfortune; to lose both looks like carelessness."
- "It's just a flesh wound" (Black Knight, Monty Python).

### Why It's Funny
- The mismatch between language and reality.
- The audience supplies the missing intensity.
- Implies something even bigger than stated.
- Politeness mask over horror.

## Side-by-Side
| | Direction | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Hyperbole | Magnify | Gross-out absurdity, intensity |
| Understatement | Minimize | Politeness/horror gap, dryness |

Both rely on **willful distortion** to redirect focus.

## Rubric
1. Is the magnitude clearly distorted (up or down)?
2. Does the distortion expose a truth?
3. Is the gap between language and reality significant?
4. Does the audience get the unspoken implication?
5. Is the distortion fitted to the topic (not generic)?
