# Surprise / Incongruity / Expectation Violation Research

## Key Definitional Findings (Warren & McGraw 2016)

The literature describes incongruity in at least 4 ways:
1. **Surprise** — unexpected outcome.
2. **Juxtaposition** — two unlike things side by side.
3. **Atypicality** — divergence from norm.
4. **Violation** — threat to "ought" beliefs.

### Important Result
**Incongruity alone does not adequately differentiate humorous from non-humorous experiences.**
- All surprises are not funny.
- All atypicalities are not funny.
- Only violations that are simultaneously benign tend to be funny.

## Historical Lineage
- Aristotle: best way to make audience laugh is to setup an expectation and deliver something "that gives a twist."
- Kant (1790): laughter "arises from a strained expectation suddenly reduced to nothing."
- Schopenhauer: "incongruous interpretation of an object."
- Modern: McGraw & Warren's Benign Violation Theory.

## Temporal Dynamics
Recent research (Timing is Everything, 2026):
- Peak semantic violations matter more than average incongruity levels.
- Pauses systematically lengthen before high-surprise punchlines.
- Strategic coupling distinguishes successful from unsuccessful performances.

## What This Means for Joke Quality
- Surprise is necessary but not sufficient.
- The surprise must violate something important.
- The violation must register as benign.
- Timing scaffolds the surprise (pauses).

## Rubric
1. Is there a clear surprise / unexpected outcome?
2. Does the surprise violate a norm (social, linguistic, moral)?
3. Is the violation simultaneously safe (benign)?
4. Is the violation peak (concentrated at the punch)?
5. Is the timing scaffolded (pause before peak surprise)?
