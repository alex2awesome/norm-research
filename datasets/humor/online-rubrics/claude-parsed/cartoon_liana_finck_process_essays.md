# Liana Finck: Process Notes on Cartoons

## The Diagrammatic Joke
Finck's signature is the DIAGRAM as joke form: a labeled image where the labels reveal an emotional truth the diagram pretends to neutrally describe.

## Why Diagrams Are Funny
- Diagrams promise OBJECTIVITY.
- Filling them with subjective, emotional content violates the form.
- The violation is the joke.

## On Cartoon vs. Caption
- For Finck, the image carries 80% of the meaning.
- The caption (when present) functions as label rather than punchline.
- This inverts the New Yorker tradition where image is setup and caption is punch.

## On Anxious Comedy
- Finck's work normalizes neurotic interior monologue.
- Universalizing the inside-of-the-head experience is the comedic move.
- The reader laughs at recognition: "I do this; I thought I was alone."

## On Daily Practice
- Finck publishes a near-daily Instagram cartoon.
- The practice of constant production prevents preciousness.
- The audience watches the comedic mind in motion.

## On Single-Panel vs. Multi-Panel
- Single panel: maximum compression, requires perfect lock.
- Multi-panel: allows escalation but risks losing the eye.
- For interior-life comedy, single panel forces clarity.

## Finck-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Does the cartoon use a form (diagram, label, list) the joke can violate?
2. Does the image carry most of the meaning?
3. Is the joke about an interior experience the reader will recognize?
4. Have you produced enough recently to be unprecious?
5. Is the format (single, multi-panel) right for the joke?
6. Does the laugh come from "I thought I was alone"?
