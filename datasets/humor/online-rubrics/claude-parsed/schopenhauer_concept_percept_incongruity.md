# Schopenhauer's Concept-Percept Incongruity

Schopenhauer refines Kant by specifying *what* is incongruous: the gap between an abstract concept and the concrete particular subsumed under it.

## The defining formula

> "The cause of laughter in every case is simply the sudden perception of the incongruity between a concept and the real objects which have been thought through it in some relation, and laughter itself is just the expression of this incongruity."

> "The greater and more unexpected this incongruity, in the apprehension of the laugher, the more violent will be his laughter."

## The two species of comical

1. **Wit (Witz)** — when we *start with the concept*, then suddenly find a particular case it absurdly subsumes. The conceptual side is given, the perception is the surprise.
2. **Folly / Absurdity (Narrheit)** — when we *start with the particular*, then realize the concept under which the agent has acted does not really fit. The perception is given, the concept is the surprise.

## The two cognitive operations

- **Humorous bifurcation** — an idea believed to belong to one category turns out to belong to two (puns, double entendres).
- **Humorous subsumption** — two ideas thought distinct turn out to share a category (forced comparisons, "how is X like Y" jokes).

## Necessary conditions

1. **A genuine concept must be in play** — there must be a rule, classification, or generalization governing expectations.
2. **A real particular must be presented** — a specific case with concrete features.
3. **The particular must be paradoxically subsumed** — it does belong under the concept by some thread, but otherwise does not "remotely belong there."
4. **The recognition must be sudden** — gradual perception of the misfit produces no laughter.
5. **Greater discrepancy → louder laughter.**

## Why this matters: the Schopenhauerian insight

The comical is the perception's *triumph over* concept. Reason, with its rigid categories, is suddenly shown to be inadequate to the rich particularity of the world. Laughter is intellectual revenge of perception against abstract thought.

## What this excludes

- Pure word-games with no real subsumption (these are merely clever, not funny).
- Tragic mismatches where the concept's failure causes harm (these provoke pity, not laughter).
- Cases where the misfit is never resolved — there must be a moment where one *perceives* the discrepancy.

## Diagnostic questions

- What concept is the setup invoking?
- What particular is being slipped under it?
- Does the particular technically fit while obviously not really fitting?
- Does the audience experience a sudden *click* of perceiving the gap?
- Would tightening the concept ruin the joke? Loosening it? (If yes to either, the rubric is correctly calibrated.)
