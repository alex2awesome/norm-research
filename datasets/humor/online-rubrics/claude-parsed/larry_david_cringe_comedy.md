# Larry David — Cringe Comedy

## Core Technique: Sabotage Working Consensus
Larry's signature is calling out any behavior that doesn't fit his narrow conception of the way things ought to be — and refusing to let it go.
- He disrupts social tact.
- Refuses to swallow micro-aggressions.
- Pursues petty grievances to absurd extents.

## What Makes It Funny
- Centers the **miser** character.
- Dials cringe to 11.
- Doesn't apologize.
- Protagonist lacks a conscience / compass for social rules.

## Psychological Appeal
- Like a nightmare in dose-controlled exposure therapy.
- Brain practices social discomfort safely.
- Constant friction plays with audience's sense of social acceptability.
- Schadenfreude (seeing him punished by his own behavior).
- Vicarious release (we secretly want to do what he does).

## Improvisation Engine
- No script — basic outline.
- Cast improvises.
- Spontaneity creates authentic cringe.
- Real reactions feel uncomfortable.

## Cringe Comedy Mechanics
1. **Awkward situation**: introduce social rule.
2. **Violation**: character violates the rule.
3. **No deflection**: violator does NOT apologize or downplay.
4. **Compounding**: violation begets new violation.
5. **Audience embarrassment**: we feel for everyone present.

## Sub-Genres
- *Curb Your Enthusiasm* — Larry David
- *The Office* (UK/US) — Ricky Gervais, Steve Carell
- *Peep Show*
- *I Think You Should Leave* — Tim Robinson
- *Nathan For You*

## Rubric
1. Is there a clear social rule established?
2. Does the protagonist violate it without apology?
3. Do violations compound?
4. Does the audience feel secondhand embarrassment?
5. Is improvised, real-feeling reaction present?
