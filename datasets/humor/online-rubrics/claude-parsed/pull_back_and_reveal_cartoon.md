# Pull-Back-and-Reveal

## Definition
A visual comedy technique used especially in cartoons — start with a close-up or partial view of a scene, then expand the frame to show broader context that reveals something unexpected or humorous.

## How It Works
- Initial frame establishes a clear, normal-looking scene.
- Reader interprets it within a familiar frame.
- The "pull back" widens the view.
- The reveal shows the situation is in fact absurd / different.
- Reader's understanding flips.

## Why Cartoonists Love It
- Visual analog of misdirection.
- Single-panel constraint forced creativity.
- Wordless humor possible.
- Reader does the cognitive work.

## Examples
- A figure praying intensely → reveal: praying for an absurd thing.
- A shadowy menacing figure → reveal: it's a small kid in a Halloween costume.
- An office scene → reveal: the building is on Mars.
- A serious meeting → reveal: held in a kindergarten classroom.

## In New Yorker Cartoons
- Many classic single-panel cartoons use it.
- Charles Addams was a master.
- Gary Larson's *Far Side* often deployed it.

## In Multi-Panel Comics
- First panel = bait.
- Final panel = reveal.
- Variants: zoom-out, multiple wider shots, perspective shift.

## In Film / TV
- Slow zoom-out reveal.
- "It's actually a movie set."
- "It's all a dream / hologram."

## Rubric
1. Is there an initial framed scene that primes a normal interpretation?
2. Does the wider view reveal absurdity / surprise?
3. Does the gap between frames create the laugh?
4. Is the wider context itself coherent (not random)?
5. Does the reveal recontextualize without explanation?
