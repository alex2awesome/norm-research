# Farce

## Definition
A form of comedy that relies on exaggerated, improbable, or ridiculous situations. Comic dramatic piece using highly improbable situations, stereotyped characters, extravagant exaggeration, and violent horseplay.

## Key Conventions

### Plot Structure
- Highly improbable situations.
- Rapidly escalating complications.
- Breathless pace.
- Often ends with everything resolved comically.

### Character Development
- Stereotyped, one-dimensional figures.
- Driven by exaggeration.
- Vehicles for comedic situations.
- React in predictable yet overblown ways.

### Setting
- Most action takes place in one precise location.
- Same physical space for all characters.
- Relationships shift; stakes rise.

### Physical Comedy
- Pratfalls, spit-takes, stunts, clowning.
- Actions more important than words.

## Ray Cooney's Rules of Farce
- Every entrance/exit must matter.
- Characters never share information except by misunderstanding.
- The plot escalates continuously.
- Doors are characters in their own right.
- The stakes must feel life-or-death to the characters.
- Violence must be threatened more than performed.

## Why Farce Is Funny
- Audience sees the misunderstandings the characters can't.
- Characters in over their heads.
- Repeated misses generate compound tension.
- Final reveal pays off layered confusion.

## Examples
- Plautus, Molière
- Feydeau (French farce)
- *A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to the Forum*
- *Noises Off*
- *Fawlty Towers*

## Rubric
1. Is the situation highly improbable?
2. Does it escalate continuously?
3. Are the characters in over their heads?
4. Does the audience know more than the characters (dramatic irony)?
5. Is the pace breathless?
