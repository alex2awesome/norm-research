# Hannah Gadsby: Craft After Nanette

Synthesis of Gadsby's essays, talks, and interviews on the comedy-form changes she introduced via Nanette and Douglas.

## On the Joke as Trauma Cap
- The standard joke form ENDS the story at the laugh.
- This means the comic can never tell the rest of the story.
- For trauma material, the joke caps the wound — it doesn't heal it.
- Gadsby's argument: comedians sometimes need to refuse the punchline to honor the truth.

## On Tension Without Release
- The joke contract: build tension, release with laughter.
- A new contract: build tension, REFUSE to release, force the audience to sit with it.
- Used carefully, this is one of the most powerful tools in the medium.

## On Self-Deprecation as Self-Harm
- For marginalized comedians, self-deprecation about identity is humiliation as labor.
- The audience is being trained to laugh AT the identity.
- The comedian has to find another way to be funny that doesn't extract from self.

## On Story Architecture in Douglas
- Long, didactic frame establishes intellectual trust.
- Visual humor (art history slides) provides recognition + reframe.
- Personal material is interspersed, anchored by the intellectual frame.
- The structure permits both essay and joke to coexist.

## On Naming the Form
- Calling out the joke structure to the audience can BE the joke.
- "I am about to release the tension" — and then not — destabilizes meaningfully.
- This works once per show, perhaps twice. More than that becomes shtick.

## Gadsby-Inflected Working Rubric
1. What does the punchline ALLOW you to skip in the story?
2. Is that skip serving the audience, or protecting you?
3. Could withholding the laugh serve the material better here?
4. If you're self-deprecating, are you laughing AT your own identity?
5. Does the form of the show let intellect and laughter coexist?
6. Have you named the form deliberately, sparingly, or accidentally?
