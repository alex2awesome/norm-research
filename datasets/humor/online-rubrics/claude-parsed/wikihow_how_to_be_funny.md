# WikiHow: How to Be Funny

A consolidated rubric drawn from WikiHow's most-edited "How to Be Funny" article.

## Part 1 — Cultivating a Funny Mindset
1. Look at things from an absurd or unexpected angle. Comedy lives in the gap between expectation and reality.
2. Keep a humor journal: write down anything that makes you laugh and try to articulate WHY.
3. Be observant — funny people notice the small, weird, true details others ignore.
4. Read, watch, and study comedians whose style resonates; map what they do that lands.
5. Don't try to be funny — try to find what's already funny in the situation.

## Part 2 — Telling a Joke
1. Have a clear setup: establish the expected world in one sentence.
2. Create misdirection in the setup so the punchline can pivot.
3. Punchline goes at the END. Never bury it mid-sentence.
4. Use the rule of three: pattern, pattern, break.
5. Time your pause: brief silence before the punch lets the audience lean in.
6. Don't laugh at your own joke before the punch lands.
7. Commit to the bit — half-hearted delivery kills the laugh.

## Part 3 — Being Funny in Conversation
1. Riff on what's already happening; in-the-moment humor beats memorized jokes.
2. Self-deprecate sparingly — a little is charming, a lot is sad.
3. Read the room: tone, audience age, comfort level.
4. Avoid punching down — never the target of a marginalized group.
5. If a joke bombs, move on quickly; don't explain it.

## Part 4 — Avoiding Common Pitfalls
1. Don't repeat a joke that already landed once.
2. Don't preface ("This is so funny, you'll die"). Set expectations low, exceed them.
3. Don't be mean and call it a joke. Cruelty isn't comedy.
4. Don't force callbacks; let them feel discovered.

## WikiHow Rubric for "Is This Funny?"
1. Setup establishes a clear expectation.
2. Punchline subverts that expectation in a way the audience can connect.
3. Word economy: every word earns its place.
4. Delivery includes a pause, eye contact, and commitment.
5. Target is appropriate (self, situation, or shared experience — not vulnerable people).
6. The audience laughs (the only real metric).
