# Splitsider Archives: Comedy Craft Essay Synthesis

Splitsider (now archived) was a foundational online publication for comedy craft criticism, 2010–2018. Synthesized below: the recurring evaluative themes across hundreds of essays.

## On the Modern Stand-Up Hour
- The expectation has shifted from "string of bits" to "essayistic argument."
- Splitsider essays repeatedly argued that the best new specials had a THESIS, not just material.
- The thesis-driven hour creates emotional architecture; the bit-driven hour creates whiplash.

## On the "Alt" Comedy Continuum
- Alt comedy is not a style; it's a permission structure.
- Audiences in alt rooms forgive experimentation, demand originality.
- Audiences in mainstream rooms forgive familiarity, demand reliability.
- Comics develop in alt rooms, monetize in mainstream rooms.

## On Comic-Persona Authenticity
- The comedian's persona is a constructed instrument.
- The "authentic" comedian is one whose persona is closely fitted to their actual self.
- The "fake" comedian is one whose persona contradicts the off-stage person.
- Audiences detect the gap, even when they can't articulate it.

## On the "Comedians as Therapists" Trope
- Splitsider essays repeatedly criticized the framing of comedians as societal therapists.
- The comic's job is comedy; therapy is a side effect of GOOD comedy.
- Comics who lean into the therapist framing usually become less funny.

## On Recording Live Specials
- The medium changes the material.
- A bit that kills live can flatten on tape.
- Pros write WITH the recorded medium in mind for hours intended for streaming.

## Splitsider-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Does the hour have a thesis, or just bits?
2. Is your persona closely fitted to your actual self?
3. Have you developed in alt rooms before chasing mainstream?
4. Are you optimizing for funny first, message second?
5. Have you accounted for the recording medium?
6. Are you the comic, or are you the comic's marketing?
