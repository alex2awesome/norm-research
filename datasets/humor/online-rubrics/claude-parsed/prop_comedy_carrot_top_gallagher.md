# Prop Comedy

## Definition
Stand-up comedy in which performers incorporate physical objects — either inherently humorous or everyday items used in unexpected ways — to enhance routines.

## Structural Mechanics
- **Setup with prop**: arrange objects in everyday/familiar manner.
- **Foster expectation**: audience anticipates normal use.
- **Subvert via reveal**: sudden new function, malfunction, unexpected use.
- **Misdirection**: prop arrangement misleads, then surprises.

## Carrot Top's Approach
- Dozens of props in trunks on stage.
- Each joke: pull out prop, deliver one-liner describing it, toss away.
- Compact, pun-based contraptions.
- Cleaner setups, quicker resets.
- Influenced by: Steve Martin (absurdity), Gallagher (visual gags), Tonight Show (clean structure).

## Gallagher's Approach
- Sledge-O-Matic: smashing watermelons on stage.
- Audience showered in debris.
- Wild, messy, theatrical.
- Bigger, more spectacle-driven.
- One signature prop / set-piece dominates.

## Why Props Work
- Visual + verbal channel: doubled punch.
- Object = something concrete the punch can attach to.
- Breaks the "one comedian, one mic" monotony.
- Creates spectacle.

## Common Prop Comedy Techniques
- **Visual pun**: object embodies a wordplay.
- **Mechanical malfunction**: prop fails comically.
- **Repurposing**: everyday object used wrong.
- **Absurd specificity**: prop is too specific to be useful.
- **Repetition with variation**: each prop similar form, different punch.

## Rubric
1. Does the prop enable the joke (not just decorate)?
2. Is there visual/verbal interplay?
3. Is the prop quickly readable by the audience?
4. Does the routine maintain energy through transitions?
5. Could the joke work without the prop? If yes, why use it?
