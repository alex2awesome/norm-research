# Jerry Corley — Stand-Up Comedy Clinic

Jerry Corley wrote 80–120 jokes per day for The Tonight Show with Jay Leno.

## Core Principle
> "One of the key foundations of comedy is to set up an implied expectation for the audience, then shatter that expectation."

Surprise is the #1 element necessary to trigger human laughter.

## The 1-2-3 Jokes Method
Start with stuff close to you. Look at surroundings, situations, behaviors. Ask:
1. **What is new?**
2. **What has changed?**
3. **What have I acquired?**

These three prompts surface fresh, specific material.

## The 5 W's for Joke Building
Most jokes are a convergence of two or more clearly defined ideas. Use the journalistic 5 W's:
- **Who?**
- **What?**
- **Why?**
- **Where?**
- **When?**

For each W, generate associations. Then look for unexpected matches across categories.

## Joke Writing Volume
Professional comedy writers crank out 60–150 jokes per day using these techniques. Volume → quality through ruthless selection.

## Joke Anatomy (Corley)
- **Premise**: the situation/topic you're commenting on.
- **Setup**: builds an implied expectation.
- **Punch**: shatters the expectation.
- **Tag**: optional follow-up punch.

## Common Mistakes
- Punchline is too predictable.
- Setup gives away too much.
- No clear point of view.
- Generic topics (no "you" in it).

## Rubric (Corley System)
1. Does the setup establish a clear implied expectation?
2. Does the punch shatter that expectation surprisingly?
3. Is the topic specific to the comedian's life (new/changed/acquired)?
4. Could the 5 W's be answered concretely?
5. Is the joke selected from many candidates (not the first try)?
