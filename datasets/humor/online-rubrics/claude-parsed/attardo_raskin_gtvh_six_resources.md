# General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH)

By Victor Raskin & Salvatore Attardo (1991, Script Theory Revisited).

## The Six Knowledge Resources (Hierarchical)

Listed top-down (each level constrains levels below):

### 1. Script Opposition (SO) — most abstract
- Inherited from Raskin's SSTH.
- The joke must contain two opposing scripts.
- Themes: real/unreal, actual/non-actual, normal/abnormal, possible/impossible, sex/no-sex, money/no-money, life/death, etc.

### 2. Logical Mechanism (LM)
- The mechanism that resolves the incongruity between the opposed scripts.
- Examples: false analogy, garden-path, reasoning from a false premise, juxtaposition, chiasmus, role exchange, role reversal, vacuous reversal, etc.
- Attardo's catalog includes ~27 distinct LMs.

### 3. Situation (SI)
- The "props" of the joke: objects, participants, instruments, activities.
- Setting, scenery, what's happening.

### 4. Target (TA)
- The butt of the joke (person, group, stereotype).
- Optional in some jokes.
- Ethnic groups, professions, individuals, stereotyped roles.

### 5. Narrative Strategy (NS)
- How the text is organized: simple narrative, dialogue, riddle, question-answer, expository.

### 6. Language (LA) — most concrete
- The exact verbal expression.
- Phonemic, morphemic, syntactic choices.

## Hierarchy & Substitution
- Higher-level resources determine lower-level ones.
- Two jokes that differ only in Language are very similar.
- Two jokes that differ in Script Opposition are very different.

## Rubric for Verbal Joke
1. Are there two opposed scripts?
2. What is the Logical Mechanism resolving them?
3. Is the Situation specific and clear?
4. Is the Target appropriate?
5. Does the Narrative Strategy fit (riddle vs. story vs. dialogue)?
6. Is the Language tight and well-chosen?
