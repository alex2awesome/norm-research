# Misdirection in Comedy

## Definition
Misdirection is a comedic technique that involves leading the audience in one direction or setting up certain expectations, only to surprise or subvert those expectations with a twist or unexpected punchline.

More formally: misdirection is information presentation that implicitly encourages the audience to adopt one interpretation, despite a second interpretation being available and later becoming more obvious.

## How It Works
- The classic set-up/payoff device.
- Setup creates expectations; payoff subverts them.
- Both interpretations must be available in principle.
- The punch-line interpretation must be unlikely to be noticed during setup.
- The joke creator must express the setup so as to guide (misdirect) toward the wrong interpretation.

## Two Sub-Techniques
1. **Garden-Path Sentence**: syntactically misleading the parser.
   - "The horse raced past the barn fell." (the horse [that was] raced past the barn fell)
2. **Pull-Back-and-Reveal**: a long buildup with a twist that recasts everything.

## Effects
- Plays with audience assumptions.
- Creates a delightful contrast between what they anticipate and what unfolds.
- Adds surprise, wit, unpredictability.

## Rubric
1. Is there an obvious first interpretation primed by the setup?
2. Is the second (punchline) interpretation also valid in retrospect?
3. Is the second interpretation hidden enough to be surprising?
4. Does the setup actively guide toward the wrong interpretation?
5. Is the reveal clean — not just confusing?
