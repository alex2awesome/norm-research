# iO Chicago — The Harold

Developed by Del Close. Codified by Charna Halpern at iO Chicago (then ImprovOlympic) in the 1980s. *Truth in Comedy* (1994) became the long-form improv "Bible."

## Structure
A long-form improvised piece — collage of scenes inspired by a single suggestion, interwoven and connected.

### Three-Beat Structure
Three distinct storylines are:
1. **Established** (Beat 1)
2. **Heightened** (Beat 2)
3. **Merged** (Beat 3)

### Three Acts
Broadly correspond to:
- **Discovery** (find what's interesting)
- **Heightening** (escalate it)
- **Connecting** (tie storylines together)

## Key Principles

### Pattern-Making Over Storytelling
- Improvisors are pattern makers, not storytellers.
- Take what happened before; create scenes/group games/transitions that pattern with previous scenes.
- Heighten the patterns through the piece.

### Flexibility
- Del Close encouraged variation within the structure.
- Beats and games need not appear in fixed order or number.
- Malleable, organic form.

### Inclusivity of Content
- Storytelling, dance, music, poetry, performance art.
- "If you think you can't do something in a Harold, you're doing Harold wrong."

### "Art by Committee"
- Individual ego submerged into Group Mind.
- Comedy from TRUTH of human reaction, not invention of jokes.
- Commit to honesty over cleverness.

## Standard Harold Form
- Opening (group mind exercise)
- Scene 1A, 1B, 1C (three storylines)
- Group game
- Scene 2A, 2B, 2C (heightened)
- Group game
- Scene 3A, 3B, 3C (merging)

## Rubric
1. Are patterns being built (not just stories)?
2. Are storylines being established AND heightened?
3. Do the storylines eventually connect?
4. Is the group mind operating (no upstaging)?
5. Is comedy emerging from truth, not gags?
