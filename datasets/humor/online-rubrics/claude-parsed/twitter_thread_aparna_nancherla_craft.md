# Aparna Nancherla: Twitter Craft Threads

## On Quiet Stand-Up
- A quiet voice is a deliberate choice that forces the audience to lean in.
- The audience's attention BECOMES the joke's amplification.
- The downside: the room must be small or you're fighting acoustics.

## On Anxiety as Material
- Anxiety material works best when it's specific to a SPECIFIC SITUATION.
- "I have anxiety" is not material. "I rehearsed leaving the elevator" is material.
- The audience laughs at recognition of micro-rituals, not at diagnosis.

## On the Self-Deprecation Trap
- Self-deprecation can become a flinch.
- Audiences tire of it; comics get stuck in the persona.
- The cure: alongside self-deprecation, allow yourself ONE clear, sincere observation per set that you stand behind.

## On Twitter as Joke Workshop
- Twitter is a free open mic.
- Numbers (likes, retweets) approximate stage laughter — imperfectly.
- Use it to TEST premises, not just to perform.

## On Bombing Quietly
- The quiet comic's bomb is also quiet — the audience just looks confused.
- The work is reading whether the silence is engagement or disengagement.
- Practice: count how many people are LOOKING at you, not just listening.

## Nancherla-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Does the joke survive at low volume?
2. Is anxiety material specific to a micro-ritual?
3. Have you balanced self-deprecation with sincere observation?
4. Have you tested the premise in writing first?
5. Are you reading engagement, not just laughter?
6. Are you commanding silence, or being abandoned to it?
