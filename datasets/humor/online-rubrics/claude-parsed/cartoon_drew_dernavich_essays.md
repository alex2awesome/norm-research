# Drew Dernavich: New Yorker Cartoonist Essays

Drew Dernavich is a New Yorker cartoonist whose essays and interviews discuss the cartoon-craft equation.

## The Cartoon Idea Machine
- Step 1: collect odd PAIRS — two things that don't usually appear together.
- Step 2: ask, what relationship would these two things have?
- Step 3: who would say what to whom in that relationship?
- The caption is the LAST thing you write, not the first.

## On the Image-Caption Lock
- The image and caption must SHARE meaning, not duplicate it.
- A caption that just describes the image fails.
- A caption that has nothing to do with the image fails.
- The interplay (image says X, caption says Y, audience synthesizes Z) is the joke.

## On Why Most Submissions Fail
- The drawing is too generic to anchor a specific caption.
- The caption is a complete joke that doesn't need the drawing.
- The pair (image + caption) is one beat, not two — no synthesis required.

## On the One-Joke Rule
- A New Yorker cartoon has ONE joke.
- Multiple jokes confuse the eye.
- The drawing's other elements should be neutral, not competing.

## On Style as Brand
- A cartoonist's drawing style is a comedic instrument.
- Dernavich's woodcut-influenced style implies a tonal range that other styles can't.
- Develop a style that does comedic work for you.

## On Editor Bob Mankoff's Selection Logic
- Original concept (not seen before).
- Clean execution (image and caption lock).
- Tonal fit for the magazine.
- Recognizability of human truth.

## Dernavich-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Is the cartoon ONE joke, not two?
2. Do image and caption synthesize meaning, neither duplicating nor diverging?
3. Is the image specific enough to anchor a unique caption?
4. Is the caption incomplete without the image?
5. Does your style add comedic register?
6. Has the joke been seen before?
