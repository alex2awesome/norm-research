# Annoyance Theatre: Improv Without the Rules

Founded in Chicago by Mick Napier, the Annoyance is the contrarian school in the improv ecosystem. Its core argument: most improv "rules" are scaffolding for beginners that experienced players should DISCARD.

## The Core Heresy
- "Yes, And" is overrated. So is "Make Your Partner Look Good."
- The improviser's job is to take care of THEMSELVES first.
- A player who takes care of themselves takes care of the scene; a player obsessed with serving others becomes paralyzed.

## On Initiations
- Make a strong choice in the first beat. ANY choice.
- The strength of the choice matters more than its rightness.
- Rules-followers wait for permission; Annoyance players claim it.

## On Discomfort as Data
- If you feel uncomfortable in a scene, DO something with the discomfort, don't tamp it down.
- The discomfort is real and shared with the audience.
- Naming it can create the funniest moments.

## On the "Funny" Standard
- The Annoyance is unembarrassed to optimize directly for funny.
- Other schools dress this up as "honesty" or "game."
- Funny is the metric.

## On Saying No
- Sometimes "no" is the strongest move.
- A scene where one player legitimately resists creates real conflict.
- Conflict, played truthfully, is the engine.

## Annoyance-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Did you take care of YOURSELF in the scene first?
2. Did you make a strong initial choice without waiting for permission?
3. Did you USE discomfort rather than smoothing it?
4. Are you optimizing directly for funny?
5. Did you allow real resistance / conflict?
6. Are you free of rules-anxiety?
