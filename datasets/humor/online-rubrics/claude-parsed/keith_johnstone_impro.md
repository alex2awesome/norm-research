# Keith Johnstone — Impro

## Four Sections of the Book
1. **Status** — every interaction is a status transaction; comedy often depends on status play
2. **Spontaneity** — overcoming blocks to natural creativity
3. **Narrative Skills** — building stories that feel inevitable
4. **Masks and Trance** — character through mask work

## Status (Key for Comedy)
- Every encounter is a status transaction (high vs. low status).
- Comedy often comes from clear status differentials.
- Lowering status = becoming more relatable / sympathetic.
- Raising status = creating pomposity to puncture.
- Status reversals are inherently funny.

## Spontaneity Principles
- Normal education is designed to reduce spontaneity and make things orderly.
- Johnstone's techniques do exactly the opposite — restore the obvious, child-like response.
- "An artist who is inspired is being obvious. He's not making any decisions, he's not weighing one idea against another. He's accepting his first thoughts."
- The improviser is "like a man walking backwards" — sees where he has been, pays no attention to the future.

## Accepting Offers
- Block = "no" → kills the scene
- Accept = "yes" → continues
- Add = build on what was offered

## Narrative Skills
- Reincorporation: bring back earlier elements to make the story feel fated.
- The audience loves seeing patterns close.
- Be obvious in your choices — second-guessing kills magic.

## Rubric (for Status Comedy / Improv)
1. Is the status play clear (who is up, who is down)?
2. Do status reversals occur?
3. Are the performers accepting offers (yes-and)?
4. Are they being obvious / not over-thinking?
5. Are earlier elements reincorporated?
