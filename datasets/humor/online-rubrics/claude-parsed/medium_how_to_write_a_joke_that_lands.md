# Medium: How to Write a Joke That Actually Lands

## The Three Failure Modes
Every joke that bombs fails one of three ways:
1. **Recognition failure** — the audience doesn't share the assumption your setup needs.
2. **Surprise failure** — the audience saw the punch coming.
3. **Trust failure** — the audience doesn't believe YOU should be telling this joke.

Diagnose which one before you rewrite.

## Pre-Writing: Choose a True Premise
- Write down a complaint, opinion, or confusion you actually have.
- Why is it true for YOU specifically?
- What would a reasonable person object to about your view?
- The objection is your audience's assumption — your joke pivots off it.

## Writing: The Pivot Word
- Identify the single WORD in the setup that the punchline reinterprets.
- If there's no pivot word, your joke is doing two things at once. Cut.
- The punch should re-use the pivot word in its new sense.

## Editing: The Mumble Test
Read the joke aloud while mumbling. If the rhythm is right, you'll know where the laughs go even without the words. If the mumble has no shape, the joke has no shape.

## Performing: The Three-Beat Pause
- Beat 1: the setup ends, you stop talking.
- Beat 2: the audience processes (1 second).
- Beat 3: the punchline lands.
The amateur skips beat 2. The pro waits for it.

## Rubric for "Will This Land?"
1. Premise is something you genuinely believe.
2. Setup contains an assumption the AUDIENCE will share.
3. Punch pivots on a single re-interpreted word.
4. The mumble has shape.
5. There's a beat between setup and punch.
6. The performer has standing to make this joke (trust).
7. The total bit is shorter than what feels safe.
