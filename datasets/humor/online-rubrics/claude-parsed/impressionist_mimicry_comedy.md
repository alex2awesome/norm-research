# Impressionist Comedy / Mimicry

## Definition
A performer whose act consists of imitating sounds, voices, and mannerisms of celebrities and cartoon characters.

## Key Techniques

### Vocal Mimicry
- Voice match (pitch, cadence, accent).
- Distinctive phrases / catchphrases.
- Speech rhythm.

### Physical Mimicry
- Gestures and posture.
- Facial expressions.
- Walking gait.
- Idiosyncratic movements.

### Attitude / Persona
- "Get the swagger" — internal attitude.
- The mind behind the speech.
- What this person would say in a NEW context.

## Why It's Funny
- Recognition pleasure.
- The mimic captures something everyone has noticed but nobody can articulate.
- Putting the celebrity in absurd contexts (the "what if X said Y" engine).
- Surprise that ANYONE can sound like that.

## Comedic Use vs. Pure Imitation
- Pure imitation: tribute act.
- Comedic impression: places the impression in service of a joke.
- Best impressions REVEAL something true about the subject.

## Famous Impressionists
- Rich Little (60s onward, hundreds of impressions)
- Darrell Hammond (SNL — Clinton, Trump)
- Bill Hader (SNL)
- Jay Pharoah (Obama, Will Smith)
- Frank Caliendo (Madden, Bush)

## Levels of Difficulty
1. **Voice only**: easier; podcast-friendly.
2. **Voice + face**: harder; on-camera.
3. **Voice + face + body**: hardest; live.
4. **Improvised in character**: master level.

## Rubric
1. Is the voice recognizable on hearing only?
2. Are physical mannerisms captured?
3. Does the mimic reveal something true about the subject?
4. Is the impression in service of a joke (not just imitation)?
5. Could the mimic improvise in the character's voice?
