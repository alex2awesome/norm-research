# Crowd Work in Stand-Up Comedy

## Definition
A form of improv comedy used by stand-up comedians during live performances. Engaging the audience by asking personal questions and coming up with jokes in response.

## Why Comedians Do It
- **Intimacy**: connect with audience on personal level.
- **Unrepeatable**: no script; audience knows it's just for them.
- **Shareable**: comedians post crowd work clips online without "burning" written material.
- **Showcase improvisation skill**: prove you're sharp.
- **Set saver**: when written material doesn't land.

## Skills Required
- Comfort with small talk.
- Active listening (not waiting to speak).
- Letting go of ego.
- Working with whatever the volunteer offers.
- Catching workable details quickly.
- Creating conversational flow.

## How to Upgrade Crowd Work

### Listen for Specifics
- Don't ask generic questions.
- Find the unusual detail.
- Pull at the thread.

### Build Callbacks
- Reference earlier audience interactions.
- Stitch the show together.

### Don't Punch Down
- The audience member must feel safe.
- Tease, don't humiliate.
- Audience switches sides if it feels mean.

### Let Them Be Funny
- Audience members say funny things.
- Acknowledge it.
- Build on their setup.

## Famous Practitioners
- Anthony Jeselnik (sharp)
- Mark Normand
- Andrew Schulz (full crowd work specials)
- Bill Burr (combative)

## Pitfalls
- Becoming a substitute for written material.
- Repetitive setups ("what do you do?").
- Bullying.
- Losing the room when an interaction goes long.

## Rubric
1. Did the comedian listen and find a specific detail?
2. Was the audience member treated with care?
3. Did the joke build on what was offered?
4. Did the comedian return to written material gracefully?
5. Did the moment feel singular (not generic)?
