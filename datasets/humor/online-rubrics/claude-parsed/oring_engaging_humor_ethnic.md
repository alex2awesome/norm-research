# Oring on Concrete Humor Forms: Application of Appropriate Incongruity

In Engaging Humor, Oring applies his appropriate-incongruity framework to concrete humor genres — Jewish jokes, frontier humor, racist cartoons, blonde jokes, internet humor — and argues that the same theoretical apparatus illuminates each.

## The diagnostic principle

For any specific humor instance, identify:

1. **The categories that are incongruous** in the joke.
2. **The dimension along which they are nevertheless appropriate.**
3. **What social/cultural anxieties or commentaries the appropriate-incongruity expresses.**

## Genre-by-genre analysis

### Jewish humor
- **Incongruous categories:** sacred / profane, learned / impractical, suffering / cleverness, victim / survivor.
- **Appropriate fit:** Jewish wit is precisely the joining of these — the Talmudic thinker who cannot navigate everyday life, the survivor whose response to disaster is irony.
- **Cultural function:** affirms the humor as a coping mode shaped by historical conditions.

### Frontier humor (Mark Twain, tall tales)
- **Incongruous categories:** elevated literary register / crude reality, civilization / wilderness, pretense / actuality.
- **Appropriate fit:** frontier life routinely produces such collisions; the humor records the texture of that life.
- **Cultural function:** democratic deflation — the frontier laughs at refinement.

### Racist cartoons
- **Incongruous categories:** stereotype / individual, perceived "essence" / evidence to the contrary.
- **Appropriate fit:** the cartoon claims that the stereotype "fits" some real feature.
- **Critical question:** Is the appropriateness real, or is the joke fabricating it? Oring argues that to laugh requires perceiving appropriateness; if the audience rejects the supposed fit, the cartoon fails. This makes laughter at racist cartoons *diagnostic* of belief in the stereotype.
- **Cultural function:** much racist humor enforces or affirms stereotype. Some, however, mocks the stereotype itself — depending on framing.

### Blonde jokes
- **Incongruous categories:** beautiful/desirable woman / stupid/incapable.
- **Appropriate fit:** culturally encoded suspicion that beauty/sexuality and intellect are inversely related; laughter affirms a misogynist stereotype.
- **Cultural function:** restoration of (false) hierarchy in which conventionally attractive women are demoted on intellectual grounds.

### Internet / meme humor
- **Incongruous categories:** any cultural elements pulled from disparate domains.
- **Appropriate fit:** the meme format itself enables fast appropriate-incongruity production — any image can be paired with any caption to reveal unexpected fits.
- **Cultural function:** rapid testing of cultural fits across communities; memes that "stick" have particularly strong appropriate-incongruity.

## Oring's diagnostic principle for ethnic humor

To assess an ethnic joke:

1. What is the supposed appropriateness? (E.g., "the stereotype really fits this group.")
2. Does the audience actually perceive that appropriateness, or are they laughing for other reasons (in-group bonding, transgression-thrill, simple absurdity)?
3. If the audience perceives the appropriateness, what does that say about their beliefs?
4. If the audience doesn't perceive the appropriateness, the joke either fails (no fit perceived) or is being read as commentary on the stereotype rather than affirmation.

## The interpretive problem of racist humor

Oring's framework yields a sharp result:
- Laughing at a racist joke requires perceiving the stereotype as appropriate to the target.
- This perception is itself a substantive belief.
- Therefore, "I laughed but I don't believe the stereotype" is incoherent unless one is laughing at *something else* (the joke's structure, the absurdity, in-group transgression).

This makes laughter ethically diagnostic, not innocent.

## What this approach demands of any humor analysis

For any specific humor instance:
- Don't just identify the joke's structure.
- Identify what categories are incongruous.
- Identify what makes them appropriate to the audience.
- Identify what cultural beliefs or anxieties the appropriateness depends on.
- Read the joke as testimony about the audience's worldview, not just as standalone art.

## Diagnostic questions

- What categories does the joke present as appropriately incongruous?
- What background belief or cultural anxiety must the audience hold for the appropriateness to register?
- Does the audience perceive the appropriateness, or are they laughing for different reasons?
- What does laughter (or its absence) reveal about audience worldview?
- Is the humor reinforcing the connection or critically exhibiting it?
