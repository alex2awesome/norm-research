# Tony Veale — Computational Humor

Associate Professor of Computer Science at University College Dublin.

## Core Thesis
Joke generation is a **knowledge-based process** — sense of humor is a blend of wit and wisdom. Computers can have humor if they have:
- Wide knowledge bases.
- Mechanisms for blending concepts.
- Pattern templates from existing humor.

## Theoretical Foundations
- Structure-mapping theory (analogical reasoning).
- Conceptual metaphor theory (Lakoff).
- Conceptual blending theory (Fauconnier & Turner).

## Aspects of Humor Studied
- **Puns**: wordplay via phonological/lexical ambiguity.
- **Sarcasm**: detection of meaning inversion via context.
- **Metaphor and metonymy** in humor.
- **Offensiveness** detection and modulation.

## Pre-Scripted vs. Algorithmic
- Some jokes can be pre-scripted (canned templates).
- Others generated algorithmically (combinatorial blending).
- Hybrid systems most successful.

## Patterns of Computational Humor

### Conceptual Blending
- Take two concepts, blend their features.
- The blend should produce surprising, coherent output.

### Stereotype Inversion
- "X is to Y what Z is to W" — reverse expectations.

### Metaphor with Twist
- Standard metaphor (love is war), then literalize one term.

## Rubric (Veale-Style Computational Humor)
1. Is there a conceptual blend at the core?
2. Are two domains structure-mapped?
3. Does the blend produce coherent surprise?
4. Is the metaphor pushed into wit (not just description)?
5. Is wisdom (truth) present alongside wit?
