# SNL Sketch Writing Process

## Lorne Michaels' Founding Credo
> "Let's make each other laugh, and if we do, we'll put it on television and maybe other people will find it funny."

## Weekly Schedule

### Monday 6 PM
- Writers' meeting kicks off in Michaels's office.
- Day of redemption after Saturday's brood.
- Goal: make the host feel like one of the gang.
- Throw ideas around with cast and host.

### Tuesday Night (Infamous)
- Up literally all night writing sketches.
- Each writer drafts independently.
- High volume of sketches.

### Wednesday: Read-Through
- All sketches read aloud.
- Cast and host laugh (or don't).
- Lorne picks the keepers.

### Thursday-Friday: Rewrites
- Selected sketches rewritten in writers' rooms.
- Punch ups, cuts, replacements.

### Saturday: Dress + Live
- Dress rehearsal at 8pm.
- Final cuts before 10:45 producers' meeting.
- Live show 11:30pm.
- Last cuts based on dress rehearsal performance.

## Internal Competition Culture
- Tina Fey: "the rewrite tables were rough."
- Sketches torn apart sketch-by-sketch.
- High joke quota.
- Anonymous-feeling pitch process during rewrites.

## What Lorne Looks for in a Sketch
- Strong premise.
- Heightening structure.
- Memorable character.
- Quotable lines.
- Visual/topical hook.
- Star vehicle for the host.

## Sketch Categories at SNL
- Cold open (often political)
- Monologue (host's standup)
- Recurring character sketches
- One-off premise sketches
- Commercial parodies
- Game show parodies
- Music sketches
- Weekend Update (news satire)

## Rubric (SNL Sketch)
1. Is there a strong premise (one unusual thing)?
2. Does it heighten across beats?
3. Are characters distinct and quotable?
4. Could the host shine in this?
5. Does it land with broad audience?
