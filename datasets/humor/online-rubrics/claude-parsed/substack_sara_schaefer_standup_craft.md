# Sara Schaefer's Substack: Notes on Stand-Up Craft

## On Specificity
The single most important word in stand-up is "specifically." A joke about your mom is generic. A joke about your mom keeping the empty Cool Whip containers because "you never know when you'll need them" is alive. Specificity is the only way to be universal.

## On Honesty
The audience has a built-in lie detector. If you don't believe what you're saying, they won't either. This is why hack premises (airline food, men vs. women) die: nobody actually believes them anymore. The premises that hit hardest are the ones the comic actually thinks about in the shower.

## On the Difference Between Talking and Material
Talking is what you do at brunch. Material is what survives compression and stage-testing. The difference is editing: a piece of talking becomes material when you've removed every word that doesn't serve the laugh.

## On Bombing
- Bombing is data. It tells you exactly which assumption you got wrong.
- Sit with the bomb for 24 hours, then diagnose, don't despair.
- A bombed joke is recoverable; a comic who avoids stage time isn't.

## On Audience Trust
You earn the right to make a hard joke. The first 30 seconds you're on stage, you're auditioning to be trusted. Open with material that establishes you can be funny BEFORE you ask the audience to follow you somewhere uncomfortable.

## Sara Schaefer's Working Rubric
1. Is this premise something I genuinely believe?
2. Have I made it as specific as my actual life?
3. Does the audience trust me to make this joke yet?
4. Have I cut every word that's just talking?
5. Am I in service of the laugh, or my ego?
6. If this bombs, do I know why?
