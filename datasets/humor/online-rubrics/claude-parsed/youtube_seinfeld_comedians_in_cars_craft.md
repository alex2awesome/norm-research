# Jerry Seinfeld in Comedians in Cars: Craft Moments

Recurring observations from Seinfeld across episodes with Larry David, Norm Macdonald, Garry Shandling, and others.

## Seinfeld's Core Doctrine (across episodes)
- "The greatest skill in comedy is the SUBTRACTION of words."
- "The audience is the only opinion that matters."
- "If you don't believe what you're saying, they won't believe you."
- "A joke is a tiny machine."
- "Funny is the only currency I deal in."

## On Premise Selection
- Seinfeld talks about getting OBSESSED with small irritations.
- The longer he sits with the irritation, the more layers of joke he finds.
- "Boring is funny if you commit to how boring it is."

## On the Cliff Metaphor
- The setup creates the cliff edge.
- The audience must JUMP to the punchline.
- If the cliff is too narrow, they don't believe they need to jump.
- If too wide, they fall in the ravine and don't laugh.
- The art is calibrating the gap to be exactly LEAPABLE.

## On Larry David Conversations
- David: "Comedy comes from the place where you can't control what you're feeling."
- Seinfeld: "Yeah, but you can control how you describe it. That's the writing."
- The duality: feeling unfiltered, language fully filtered.

## On Norm Macdonald Conversations
- Norm: "The funniest thing is the thing the audience didn't know they were waiting for."
- Norm: "I don't try to be funny. I try to tell a story I find interesting and let the funny happen."
- Seinfeld appears to disagree gently — they represent two craft schools.

## Comedians in Cars Working Rubric
1. Have you sat with the premise long enough to find the LAYERS?
2. Have you subtracted enough words?
3. Is the cliff exactly leapable for the room?
4. Are you committed to the boring detail?
5. Are you in service of the audience's laugh, not your own taste?
6. Is the joke a small, precise machine?
