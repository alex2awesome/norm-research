# Daily Show / Last Week Tonight — Satire Formats

## The Daily Show Format

### Three Major Elements
1. **Opening Monologue** — summary of day's main stories.
2. **Mockumentary Feature** — correspondent does fake field report.
3. **Interview Segment** — celebrity or political figure.

### News Conventions Adapted for Comedy
- Video clips
- Soundbites
- Reporter packages
- News graphics
- Anchor-correspondent banter

### Iterative Writing Process
- Insane daily schedule.
- 4–5 drafts of each script.
- Segments put together in as little as 24 hours.
- Longer pieces: up to a week.

### Comedy Engine
- Deadpan delivery of absurd content.
- Juxtaposition of clips (showing politicians contradicting themselves).
- Anchor reaction shots.
- Correspondent in over their head.

## Last Week Tonight Format

### Structure
- Few short segments related to recent news.
- ONE main segment in depth (15–25 minutes).
- Main segment can cover topics not in current news cycle.

### Why In-Depth
- More time = more thorough satire.
- Can build cumulative absurdity.
- Educational + entertainment hybrid.

### Comedy Tools
- Animation breaks.
- Stunts (purchasing things, sponsorship parodies).
- Fake commercials.
- Final "...and now this" type segments.

### John Oliver's Voice
- High moral indignation.
- British rage at absurdity.
- Surreal tangents.
- Calls to action ("Go to thiswebsite.com").

## Writing Both Shows

### Common Elements
- Source video as material.
- Find the contradiction or absurdity.
- Frame with deadpan disbelief.
- Build to compound joke at the end.

## Rubric
1. Is there a real news premise?
2. Are clips juxtaposed to reveal contradiction?
3. Is the comedy deadpan / disbelief-based?
4. Does the segment build to an escalating reveal?
5. Does it have a clear point of view (not just neutral)?
