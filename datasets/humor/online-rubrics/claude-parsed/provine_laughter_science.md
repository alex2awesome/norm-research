# Provine — Laughter: A Scientific Investigation

## Key Findings

### Laughter is Social, Not Joke-Driven
- Most laughter occurs in social interactions, not in response to jokes.
- Only ~20% of laughter is preceded by anything joke-like.
- Most laughter is response to ordinary conversation: "I'll see you guys later" type lines.
- People laugh much more in groups than alone.

### Laughter Functions as Affiliation Signal
- Signals of agreement, empathy, belonging.
- Speakers laugh more than listeners (~46% more).
- Female speakers laugh more than male; female listeners laugh much more at male speakers.

### Laughter is Contagious
- Mirroring underpins facial mimicry and laughter contagion.
- Plays a crucial role in social bonding.

### "Grooming at a Distance"
- Laughter functions like primate grooming but at scale.
- Provides ~3× increase in grouping size compared to one-on-one grooming.
- May explain how hominins evolved larger social groups.

### Phonetics of Laughter
- "Ha-ha-ha" with regular spacing (~210ms).
- Variation between "ha" and "ho" tells us emotional valence.
- Hard to fake convincingly.

## Implications for "What Makes a Joke Funny"
- The social context matters more than content.
- Whether something is laughed at depends on whether the audience signals affiliation.
- Identity, group membership, mutual gaze, status all matter.

## Rubric
1. Is the joke designed to bond an in-group?
2. Does it land in a social context (vs. read alone)?
3. Does the comedian's laugh / smile invite audience to join?
4. Does the audience already share affiliation cues with the joke?
5. Is contagion possible (others laughing nearby)?
