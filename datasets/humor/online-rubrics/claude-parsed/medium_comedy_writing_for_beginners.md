# Medium: The Beginner's Guide to Comedy Writing

## Step 1: Define Your POV
Your comic POV is the lens through which you see ALL material. It's not a topic; it's an attitude. Examples:
- "Things designed to help us actually make us miserable."
- "Adults are children with money."
- "Modern intimacy is just well-dressed loneliness."

If you can't articulate your POV, every joke will feel like it could belong to someone else.

## Step 2: Generate Premises
- 5 minutes a day, write down opinions you have.
- For each opinion, ask: who would disagree, and why?
- The disagreement is a comedic engine.

## Step 3: Write the Joke
- State the premise plainly (this is your INTRO line).
- Add evidence/example (this becomes the SETUP).
- Subvert the example (this is the PUNCH).
- Hit the same target from a different angle (this is the TAG).

## Step 4: Cut, Cut, Cut
- Read the joke. Time it.
- Cut every word that doesn't earn a laugh or load the punch.
- Re-time. Aim to cut 30% on first edit.
- A 14-word joke is almost always better than a 21-word version of the same joke.

## Step 5: Test in Public
- Try it on three different audiences.
- A joke that works once is a coincidence.
- A joke that works three times is a joke.

## The Beginner's Rubric
1. Does this joke fit your POV (would only YOU write it)?
2. Is the premise an opinion, not an observation?
3. Does the punch reverse the example?
4. Have you cut at least 30% from the first draft?
5. Has it landed in three different rooms?
6. Did you record yourself and cringe productively?
