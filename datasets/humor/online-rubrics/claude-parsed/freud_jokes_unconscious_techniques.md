# Freud's Three Modes and the Joke-Work

Freud distinguishes three different humor phenomena that share an *economy* principle: each saves some kind of psychic expenditure, which is released as laughter.

## The three phenomena

1. **Der Witz (the joke / wit)** — economy in the expenditure of *inhibition*. The joke smuggles forbidden content past the inner censor.
2. **Das Komische (the comic)** — economy in the expenditure of *thought / ideation*. We laugh at mismatched movements, behavior, or appearance because we expended more mental energy than the object turned out to require.
3. **Der Humor (humor proper)** — economy in the expenditure of *affect*. A situation that should distress us is reframed by the superego in a benevolent voice that says "look at the dangers of this world; mere child's play."

## Joke techniques (the joke-work, parallel to dream-work)

These are Freud's enumerated *Techniken* for joke construction:

### Condensation techniques
- **Condensation with formation of composite word** (e.g., "famillionaire" — "he treated me familionairely").
- **Condensation with modification** (slight alteration of a word so two meanings press together).
- **Multiple use of the same material** — same word in different senses, in opposite senses, with full vs. modified meaning.

### Displacement techniques
- **Displacement of accent** — answering a different question than the one apparently asked.
- **Faulty reasoning that is recognized as faulty but enjoyed.**
- **Absurdity as sense.**
- **Indirect representation** — using allusion, omission, allegory, or imagery to point at the forbidden idea.
- **Representation by the opposite.**

### Conceptual techniques
- **Unification** — connecting two distant ideas via a third.
- **Allusion via small detail.**
- **Comparison with surprising tertium.**
- **Outdoing wit** (the answer that goes one further than the provocation).

## Tendentious vs. innocent jokes

- **Innocent jokes** save *intellectual expenditure* only — pleasure is small.
- **Tendentious jokes** carry one of two further purposes:
  - **Hostile jokes** (aggression against the target — replace impossible physical attack).
  - **Obscene jokes** (sexual exposure of a target who would otherwise be unavailable).
- Tendentious jokes give *bigger* laughs because they release inhibition energy on top of the joke-technique pleasure.

## The three-person structure of tendentious jokes

- **First person:** the joke-teller (who has the impulse).
- **Second person:** the target (who is being aggressed against or sexualized).
- **Third person:** the listener (whose laughter both grants permission and consummates the discharge).

The teller cannot laugh at his own tendentious joke — the listener laughs *for* him. The listener provides the social discharge of inhibition that the teller alone cannot give themselves.

## On humor proper (1928 essay)

Humor in the strict sense has a *liberating* and *sublime* quality:
- The superego, normally severe, here treats the ego as a parent treats a child.
- The ego is reassured: "Look at this thing that seemed so menacing; how trivial it is."
- This is the structure of gallows humor: the condemned man on the way to the gallows says "Well, this week's beginning nicely."

## Diagnostic questions

- Is the laughter coming from technique alone (condensation/displacement)?
- Is there a forbidden content (hostile or sexual) being smuggled past the censor?
- If so, who is the implied target, and is the listener's laughter a permission grant?
- Or is this humor proper — a benevolent superego reframing a distressing situation as play?
