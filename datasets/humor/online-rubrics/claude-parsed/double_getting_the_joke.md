# Oliver Double — Getting the Joke

Senior Lecturer in Drama at University of Kent. Started as a professional comedian.

## Definition of Stand-Up
Beyond "telling jokes," stand-up consists of **three core elements**:
1. **Personality** — the comedian's distinctive identity onstage.
2. **Direct communication** — speaking directly to the audience (no fourth wall).
3. **Present tense** — happening now, in this room, with this audience.

## Why These Three?
- Personality: distinguishes stand-up from sketch (where you play characters).
- Direct: distinguishes from acting (where you address other characters).
- Present tense: distinguishes from filmed comedy (which is past).

## Stand-Up Quality Per Double
- Distinctive voice — the comedian as themselves but heightened.
- Direct address that feels real.
- Present-tense responsiveness — bombing or killing visible.
- Specificity of material.
- Consistency of POV.

## Rubric (Double's Three-Element Test)
1. Is there a clear personality on stage?
2. Is there direct communication with audience?
3. Does it feel like present-tense (now, here)?
4. Could you describe the comedian's worldview after?
5. Does the comedian respond to the room?
