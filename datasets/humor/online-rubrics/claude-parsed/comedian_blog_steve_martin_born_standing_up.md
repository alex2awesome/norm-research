# Steve Martin: Born Standing Up (craft excerpts)

## The Withheld-Punchline Doctrine
Steve Martin's central craft innovation in 1970s stand-up: REMOVE THE PUNCHLINE. Build tension, never release. The audience laughs out of accumulated unresolved expectation.
- The audience eventually laughs at the WAITING itself.
- This destabilizes the contract: laughter becomes complicit, self-conscious.

## On Comedy as Architecture
- Each bit is a building block toward an evening's structure.
- The set is composed; the audience experiences it as inevitable.
- Sequence matters as much as material.

## On the Persona
- Martin's "wild and crazy" persona was a costume for sincerity.
- The persona allowed him to perform absurdity with full commitment.
- Without persona, absurdity reads as joke; with persona, it reads as character.

## On Originality
- "Be so good they can't ignore you."
- Don't borrow other comics' rhythms — they read as imitation.
- Find one structural choice that nobody else makes; own it.

## On Walking Away
- Martin walked away from stand-up at the peak.
- Knowing when a form has nothing more to give YOU is craft.
- Reinvent rather than repeat.

## On Editing for the Live Show
- Material is rewritten on stage, not at the desk.
- Audience reaction is the only data that matters.
- Cuts come fast; additions come slow.

## Martin-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Is the audience getting what they expect, or learning to want something new?
2. Is the bit a piece of architecture, not a free-standing joke?
3. Does your persona earn the absurdity?
4. Is there one structural choice that's distinctly yours?
5. Have you cut everything that didn't survive the audience?
6. Are you adding to the form, or repeating it?
