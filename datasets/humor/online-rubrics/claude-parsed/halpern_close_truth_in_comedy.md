# Truth in Comedy: The Manual of Improvisation

By Charna Halpern, Del Close, Kim Howard Johnson (1994).

## Central Philosophy
- Play scenes realistically with honesty, bravery, and integrity.
- Rather than relying on jokes, emphasize **pattern recognition** and **subversion of expectations**.
- These factors make people laugh without ever telling a joke.

## Core Techniques

### Pattern Recognition
- Notice what's emerging in a scene.
- Spot the unusual thing.
- Patterns are inherently satisfying — and subverting them is funny.

### Group Mind
- The primary focus of the work.
- Players think and react as one ensemble.
- No individual upstaging.

### "Yes, And"
- Accept everything offered.
- Add to it.

## The Harold (Long Form)
- Six to seven players.
- Many scenes: games, monologues, songs, skits.
- "Non-linear entertainment that remembers everything and wastes nothing."
- Three sets of three scenes interleaved with group games.

## Tools
- The Pattern Game
- Hot Spot
- The Harold

## Rubric for an Improv Scene (Truth in Comedy)
1. Are players being honest and brave (no jokey deflection)?
2. Is there pattern recognition happening?
3. Is the group mind operating (no upstaging)?
4. Are offers being accepted (yes-and)?
5. Does the scene "remember" earlier elements?
6. Is nothing wasted?
