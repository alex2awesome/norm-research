# Beyond a Joke: The Limits of Humour

Edited by Sharon Lockyer & Michael Pickering (Palgrave Macmillan, 2005).

## Central Question
Can something be **offensive yet funny** at the same time? What limits can or should be placed on humor?

## Key Themes

### Humor Is Not an Absolute Good
- Challenges the notion that humor is always positive.
- Humor can wound, exclude, reinforce hierarchies.
- "Beyond a joke" = past the line where humor becomes harm.

### Ethical Limits
- Some jokes target the powerless.
- Some normalize prejudice.
- The audience's identity matters in determining harm.

### Aesthetic vs. Ethical Quality
- A joke can be technically good (well-constructed) yet ethically bad.
- Critique requires both axes.

## Categories of Concern
- Gender humor (sexist jokes).
- Racial humor.
- Ethnic humor.
- Disability humor.
- Class-based humor.

## When Comedy Crosses the Line
Factors:
1. Power differential (punching up vs. down).
2. Stereotype reinforcement.
3. Target's consent / participation.
4. Real-world consequences.
5. Performer's identity vis-à-vis target.

## Why Relevant to "What Makes a Joke Funny"
- Funniness is not separable from social positioning.
- Some audiences find offense funny; others find it harmful.
- Quality assessment must consider audience.

## Rubric
1. Who is the target?
2. Is there a power differential (punch up/down/sideways)?
3. Does the audience share the comedian's identity vis-à-vis target?
4. Does the joke reinforce or challenge stereotypes?
5. Could a reasonable target find it funny?
