# Observational Comedy

## Definition
A form of humor based on commonplace aspects of everyday life — small details and shared experiences. The comedian highlights something familiar to the audience that is rarely discussed explicitly, framed to emphasize recognition or relatability.

## Key Techniques

### Watching the Gap
Watch the gap between what people say they want and what they actually do. The disconnect = comedy.

### Making the Invisible Visible
- Shows you what you see every day but never question.
- Pays attention to nuances.
- "Did you ever notice…?" / "What's the deal with…?"

### Tiny Problems Are Windows
- Tiny problems are windows into bigger truths.
- Small annoyances signal friction, inefficiency, unmet needs.

### Compression and Pacing
- "The closer you can get the jokes together, the bigger the laughs."
- Trigger "the roll" — laughter builds on itself.

### Joke Anatomy in Observational
- Setup builds expectation around the observation.
- Punchline relieves the tension.
- Anatomy applied to mundane subjects.

## What Makes It Work
- **Recognition**: "I've thought that too!"
- **Specificity**: vague observations don't land — specific ones do.
- **Truth**: rooted in actual everyday experience.
- **Surprise**: a fresh angle on the familiar.

## Rubric
1. Is the topic something the audience knows but rarely articulates?
2. Is the observation specific (not generic)?
3. Does the audience experience recognition?
4. Is there a surprising angle on the familiar?
5. Is the language tight enough to be quoted?
