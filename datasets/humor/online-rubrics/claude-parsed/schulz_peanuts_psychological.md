# Charles Schulz — Peanuts as Psychological Humor

## Core Philosophy
> "Happiness is not funny at all."

Misery became Schulz's strategy. Peanuts is about people working out the interior problems of daily lives WITHOUT ever solving them — the absence of solution is the center of the story.

## What Makes Peanuts Innovative
- Literary strip with philosophical, psychological, sociological overtones.
- Innovative in the 1950s.
- Humor psychologically complex, driven by characters' interactions and relationships.

## Reversal of Adult Assumptions
- A child's pain is MORE intensely felt than an adult's.
- A child's defeats more acutely experienced.
- Schulz gave kids lifelong dissatisfactions ("the stuff of which adulthood is made").

## Schulz's Own Depression
- Battled clinical depression throughout life.
- Channeled struggles into Charlie Brown's world.
- "Inner gloom was also the source of his outward light."

## Themes Tackled (Often Considered Taboo for Comics)
- Faith, intolerance
- Depression, loneliness
- Cruelty, despair
- Failure, persistence

## Style Elements
- Spare pen line.
- Jack Benny timing.
- Subtle sense of humor.
- Tiny gestures, big meaning.

## Recurring Comic Engines
- Charlie Brown's failed kite-flying.
- Lucy yanking the football.
- Snoopy's WWI Flying Ace.
- Linus's blanket and theology.
- Schroeder's piano and Beethoven.

## Why It's Funny (Subtly)
- Recognition: we've all been Charlie Brown.
- Patterns of failure repeat (running gag).
- Dignity in defeat.
- Hope in spite of evidence.
- The cosmic joke: trying anyway.

## Rubric (Peanuts-Style)
1. Are characters working through interior problems?
2. Are problems left unresolved (stays funny because uncured)?
3. Is the recurring failure dignified rather than cruel?
4. Does it tackle taboo emotional themes?
5. Does humor coexist with melancholy?
