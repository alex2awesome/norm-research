# Anna Drezen: SNL Sketch Writing Essays

Anna Drezen (former co-head writer SNL) on sketch construction.

## The SNL Sketch Anatomy
1. **Cold open premise**: stated in first 30 seconds.
2. **Premise lock**: by 60 seconds, the audience knows the rules of this world.
3. **Heightening Round 1**: a clear escalation of the premise.
4. **Heightening Round 2**: a complication / new angle.
5. **Heightening Round 3**: the absurdist version.
6. **Button**: a final beat that buttons the sketch.

The sketch is dead if any round just REPEATS rather than HEIGHTENS.

## On Writing for the Cast
- Each cast member has a tonal range.
- A sketch must give the lead something they can DO.
- Writing a sketch the cast can't perform = written for yourself, not for the show.

## On the Wednesday Read-Through
- 40+ sketches read for the show.
- ~12 chosen for blocking.
- ~8 make air.
- The Wednesday read teaches you which jokes survive a flat read.

## On the Premise Test
- Can the premise be stated in one sentence?
- Is the world of the sketch internally consistent?
- Does the premise generate at least three escalations?
- Does the premise have a button?

## On the Local-Joke Trap
- A "local joke" is a joke that works for the writers' room but not the country.
- Industry insider jokes; obscure references; political insider language.
- Cut these or rewrite for the audience that exists.

## Drezen-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Premise stated in 30 seconds?
2. World locked by 60 seconds?
3. Three rounds of heightening (not repeating)?
4. Button at the end?
5. Cast member has something to DO?
6. No local jokes the country won't get?
