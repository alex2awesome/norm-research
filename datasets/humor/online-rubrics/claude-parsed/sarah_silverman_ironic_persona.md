# Sarah Silverman — Ironic Persona Comedy

## Core Technique
Mocks bigots by **impersonating them**. The persona is a character named "Sarah Silverman" who inhabits her body — views expressed may not be shared by the real person.

## The "Demeaning Ditz" Character
- Self-absorbed, clueless racist.
- Beams while offending.
- Too dumb to understand the stupidity of her words.
- Described by Silverman: "ignorant and arrogant."
- Allows her "to say what I didn't mean, even preach the opposite of what I believed."

## Topics Tackled
- Racism, sexism, homophobia.
- Politics, religion.
- White privilege, post-feminism.
- Trivialization of serious events.

## Performance Mode
- Comic character endorses bigoted views satirically/deadpan.
- Subverts by exposing the absurdity.
- "Playing the role of the ignoramus."
- Aim: turn the public toward unnoticed bigotry.

## The Risk Profile
- Doesn't insulate herself with fictional characters.
- Persona looks/sounds exactly like Silverman.
- Delivers taboo punch lines with "almost pathological sincerity."
- Audience must hold the irony.

## When the Technique Works
- Audience sees through the persona.
- The targeted bigotry is being mocked.
- Catharsis at the recognition.

## When the Technique Fails
- Audience members agree with the bigoted line on its face.
- Without the irony marker, jokes can be repurposed.
- Out of context, devastating.

## Related Persona Comedians
- Stephen Colbert (*The Colbert Report*).
- Steve Allen.
- Andy Kaufman.
- Tony Clifton.

## Rubric
1. Is there a clear ironic persona?
2. Is the persona delivering bigoted views to mock them?
3. Is the irony detectable to the intended audience?
4. Could the joke be misread by the wrong audience?
5. Does the technique reveal something true about the targeted bigotry?
