# John Mulaney: On Writing Jokes (interview synthesis)

## The Mulaney Joke Construction
- Begin with a clear, fully formed image (a child sitting still in a restaurant).
- Walk the audience through that image with PRECISION.
- The joke is in the precise framing, not in any single line.
- The punchline often re-frames what kind of image we've been looking at.

## On Word Choice
- Use the funny noun, not the cool one.
- "Quiznos" is funnier than "sandwich shop."
- "Diane" is funnier than "my friend."
- Concrete, specific, slightly outdated proper nouns are gold.

## On Sentence Rhythm
- Mulaney's signature is the long, baroque sentence with a sudden plain ending.
- Setup luxuriates; punch contracts.
- The rhythmic deflation IS the joke.

## On Storytelling Persona
- The Mulaney persona is the precocious child explaining adulthood.
- The discovery-tone is essential: he's discovering the absurdity in real time, with the audience.
- This earns the audience's attention and forgives the cleverness.

## On Editing for the Special
- A bit is performed live for two years before recording.
- Final version cuts roughly 30% from the road version.
- What survives is what landed in 50+ different rooms.

## On the Callback as Architecture
- Mulaney sets up callbacks that pay off 10–15 minutes later.
- The audience doesn't see the seed; they discover the payoff.
- The architecture rewards re-watching.

## Mulaney-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Is the joke in PRECISION of image, not just in punchline?
2. Have you chosen the funny specific noun, not the generic one?
3. Does the sentence luxuriate before it contracts?
4. Is the persona discovering with the audience, not lecturing them?
5. Have you tested it in 50+ rooms?
6. Are callbacks architectural, not signposted?
