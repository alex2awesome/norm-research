# Deadpan / Dry Humor

## Definition
Deliberate display of emotional neutrality (or no emotion) as a form of comedic delivery, contrasting with the ridiculousness or absurdity of the subject matter. Delivery is blunt, ironic, laconic, or apparently unintentional.

## Key Characteristics
- Monotonous voice.
- Expressionless face.
- Minimal body language.
- Comedy comes from the contrast between content and delivery.

## How It Works
- Listener must look for humor in the contradiction between words, delivery, and context.
- Deadpan does NOT signal "this is a joke" — the audience must catch it.
- Heightens the effect of absurd content by playing it straight.

## Essential Elements
- **Timing**: critical for deadpan. Lines delivered at the perfect moment, balancing anticipation and audience comprehension.
- **Discrepancy**: between delivery and situation/setup.
- **Unexpected**: relies on context creating then subverting expectations.

## Famous Practitioners
- Buster Keaton (the original "Great Stone Face")
- Steven Wright
- Mitch Hedberg
- Aubrey Plaza
- Daria
- Bill Murray

## Why It Works
- Audience does the work of decoding — feels rewarded.
- The neutral surface lets absurdity shine without competing.
- Defies the cue-laughter convention.

## Rubric
1. Is the delivery emotionally flat?
2. Is the content absurd or charged?
3. Does the gap between delivery and content carry the joke?
4. Does the comedian avoid telegraphing the punchline?
5. Is the timing precise enough to land?
