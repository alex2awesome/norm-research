# Bergson — Laughter (Le Rire)

## Core Theory: Mechanical Encrusted on the Living

Laughter arises from the contrast between mechanical rigidity and living, adaptive humanity. The laughable element consists of a "certain mechanical inelasticity, just where one would expect to find the wide-awake adaptability and the living pliableness of a human being."

## The Source of the Comic
- Not ugliness, but **rigidity**.
- Behavior that is automatic, repetitive, mechanical when applied to a living thing.
- Examples: a man who slips and falls (rigid momentum vs. adaptive walking); the simpleton who is hoaxed (rigid expectation); absentmindedness; obsession.

## Three Conditions for Laughter
1. **The Comic is Human**: We laugh only at human (or human-like) things.
2. **Insensibility**: To laugh, the audience must momentarily anesthetize sympathy. "The greatest enemy of laughter is emotion."
3. **The Social**: Laughter is a social gesture — it requires a group.

## Social Function
Laughter is a corrective mechanism. By the fear of being laughed at, society restrains eccentricity and "softens down whatever the social body may retain of mechanical inelasticity." It promotes social cohesion by punishing rigidity.

## Forms of Comic
- **Comic of forms** (physical): the puppet, the jack-in-the-box, the snowball
- **Comic of movements** (gesture): mechanical gestures, repetitions
- **Comic of situations**: the runaway, repetition, inversion, reciprocal interference
- **Comic of words**: puns, automatisms of language, transposition
- **Comic of character**: vice as automatism, professional rigidity

## Rubric (Bergsonian)
1. Is there mechanical, rigid, or automatic behavior?
2. Is it applied to a living/human subject?
3. Is the audience emotionally detached enough to laugh?
4. Is the absurdity rooted in inflexibility (not malice)?
5. Does it correct a social rigidity?
