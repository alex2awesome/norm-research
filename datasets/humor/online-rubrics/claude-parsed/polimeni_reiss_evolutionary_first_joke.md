# Polimeni & Reiss: Why Humor Evolved

Polimeni and Reiss (2006) survey the comparative, neurological, developmental, and ethological evidence to argue that humor is an adaptive cognitive function with deep evolutionary roots, not a byproduct of intelligence.

## The central claim

Humor is one of the most cognitively complex behaviors known in the animal kingdom: even a simple joke recruits language, theory of mind, symbolism, abstract reasoning, and social perception. Such complexity strongly suggests adaptation rather than accident.

## The evolutionary precursors they identify

Four pre-human capacities likely contributed to humor's emergence:

### 1. Origins of language
Humor depends heavily on language; the evolutionary expansion of language produced the substrate for verbal humor. Early language likely included play with sound, repetition, and reference.

### 2. Cognition underlying spiritual feelings
The capacity to imagine non-actual scenarios — counterfactuals, hypotheticals, mythical agents — supports both spirituality and humor. Both require holding two realities (actual and imagined) simultaneously.

### 3. Hominid group size
Larger groups required more sophisticated tools for managing relationships. Humor functions as a tool for:
- Reducing tension.
- Probing alliance.
- Testing cognitive compatibility.
- Negotiating status nonviolently.

### 4. Primate teasing
Animal teasing in nonhuman primates (chimpanzees especially) shows precursors of humorous interaction:
- Mock aggression.
- Play faces.
- Repetition of an action that drew a reaction.
- Playful subversion of expectation.

## Adaptive functions of mature humor

Humor likely evolved because it provides:

### Mate selection signal
Producing humor displays cognitive fitness (creativity, language facility, social acuity). Receiving humor displays mental flexibility. Both serve in mate evaluation.

### Group cohesion
Shared laughter coordinates positive affect across the group, building trust and signaling alignment.

### Status negotiation without violence
Comic challenge (mockery, satire) can confer or remove status without physical conflict.

### Cognitive fitness display in within-sex competition
Same-sex peers compete for status partly through demonstrations of wit.

### Norm enforcement
Mockery of deviant behavior cheaply enforces group norms (cf. Bergson).

### Tension-reduction in dangerous situations
Mock-aggression and humor serve to defuse pre-conflict tension.

## What the evolutionary frame implies for "what makes a joke funny"

A successful joke must engage one or more of the evolved selection pressures that shaped humor:

- **Cognitive challenge / display** — the joke must demand and reward inferential work.
- **Social signaling potential** — the joke must work in a social context (laughter is much rarer when alone).
- **Tension-relieving structure** — the comic frame defuses something that might otherwise be threatening.
- **Norm reference** — many funny things implicitly invoke shared social norms.

## What is excluded by this framing

- Pure structural surprises with no social, signaling, or norm-related dimension are weak humor.
- Humor that doesn't engage any evolved function tends to produce only mild responses.

## Diagnostic questions

- What evolved function does this humor instance plausibly serve (mate signal, group bonding, tension reduction, norm enforcement)?
- Does the joke require sufficient cognitive complexity to function as a fitness signal?
- Is it occurring in a social context where its function can be realized?
- Does it engage shared norms whose violation/affirmation matters to the audience?
