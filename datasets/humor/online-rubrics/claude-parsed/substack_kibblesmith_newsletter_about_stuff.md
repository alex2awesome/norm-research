# Daniel Kibblesmith: A Newsletter About Stuff — Notes on Joke-Writing

Daniel Kibblesmith (Late Show with Stephen Colbert writer, comic book writer) on the craft of building jokes for a daily TV format.

## The Daily-Joke Workflow
- 9 a.m.: scan the news for absurd contradictions.
- 10 a.m.: pitch 10 jokes per topic, knowing 9 will die.
- 11 a.m.: the writer's room kills, refines, or selects.
- 1 p.m.: rewrite the survivors with the host's voice in mind.
- 4 p.m.: rehearsal cuts another 30%.
- 11:35 p.m.: live, and 80% of what you wrote is gone.

## What Survives the Rewrite Process
- Jokes that are specific to TODAY (perishable).
- Jokes that work with the host's persona (not yours).
- Jokes whose structure is clean enough to land on the first read.
- Jokes that don't require setup the host hasn't already given.

## The "Hard No" List
- Anything that requires explaining a Wikipedia article.
- Anything that punches at someone the audience hasn't been introduced to.
- Anything where the joke is "this happened" without a comedic angle.
- Anything that ages poorly within 24 hours.

## Comic-Book Crossover Lessons
- A panel is a punchline; the page is the setup.
- The funniest moment is usually the smallest panel, where the reader's eye is forced to slow.
- Lettering rhythm (line breaks within a balloon) is comic timing on the page.

## Kibblesmith Working Rubric
1. Is the joke specific to a real, current event?
2. Could the host say it without losing their persona?
3. Does it land on the first read aloud?
4. Is it cleaner than the joke before it in the script?
5. Will it still be funny tomorrow morning? (If yes — it might not be timely enough.)
6. Have you written nine others to compete with this one?
