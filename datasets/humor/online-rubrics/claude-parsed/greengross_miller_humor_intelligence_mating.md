# Greengross & Miller: Humor as Sexual Selection Fitness Indicator

Greengross & Miller (2011) provide the strongest empirical support for the *fitness indicator* theory of humor: humor production ability is a hard-to-fake signal of intelligence that has been shaped by mutual mate choice.

## The empirical model tested

In a study of 400 university students:
- General intelligence (Raven's Advanced Progressive Matrices).
- Verbal intelligence (vocabulary subtest).
- Humor production ability (rated funniness of cartoon captions written by participants).
- Mating success (lifetime number of sexual partners, etc.).

## The three confirmed predictions

1. **Intelligence predicts humor ability** — both general and verbal intelligence correlate with rated funniness of generated material.
2. **Humor ability predicts mating success** — controlling for other variables, humor production correlates with self-reported mating success.
3. **Males show higher mean humor production ability** — consistent with sexual selection where one sex (males) competes more intensely on signals of cognitive fitness.

## The fitness-indicator argument

Several human capacities (language facility, creativity, art, music, altruism, humor) appear to have evolved partly through *mutual mate choice*:

- These traits are *costly to fake* — you can't pretend to have them on cue.
- These traits *reliably correlate with general cognitive ability* — they tap into the same underlying machinery.
- They are *attractive across cultures* — though weighted differently.
- They are *prized in conversation when assessing mates* — both sexes report sense of humor as a top desirable trait.

Humor satisfies these criteria and is therefore best understood as a fitness indicator.

## Production vs. appreciation asymmetry

The theory predicts (and data confirms) a sex difference:

- **Men produce more humor** — they are the "displayers" trying to signal fitness.
- **Women appreciate more selectively** — they are the "choosers" evaluating signals.
- Both sexes laugh at funny things, but men *initiate* humor more often, especially in mating contexts.
- Greater female selectivity is a feature of choice, not lack of humor capacity.

## What constitutes humor "ability" measured this way

Operationally, humor ability is the capacity to:
- Generate captions / responses that *other people* find funny.
- Do so quickly and on demand.
- Across diverse prompts (general purpose, not topic-narrow).

This is distinct from humor *appreciation* (laughing at others' jokes) and humor *use* (frequency of joking).

## Multiple functions of humor in evolved psychology

Humor is not only a mating signal:

1. **Mate signal** — display of cognitive fitness for sexual selection.
2. **Same-sex status competition** — wit duels for hierarchical position among rivals.
3. **Tension reduction** — defusing potential conflict in close groups.
4. **Coalition formation** — shared laughter binds allies.
5. **Cognitive coordination** — testing whether others "get it" reveals shared mental architecture.

## Self-deprecating vs. other-deprecating in mating

Greengross & Miller's earlier work (2008) distinguished:

- **Self-deprecating humor** by high-status men is *attractive* (signals security).
- **Self-deprecating humor** by low-status men is *unattractive* (looks like weakness).
- **Other-deprecating humor** is generally less attractive than self-deprecating.

So the *function* of the humor (within this signal-based framework) depends on the displayer's social position and the audience's reading of cost.

## Implications for "what makes humor land"

For a humor instance:

1. Does it require cognitive effort that signals intelligence?
2. Is it produced spontaneously (more impressive) or rehearsed?
3. Does the producer face audience context where signaling matters?
4. Is the producer's status position appropriate for the chosen humor type (self-vs-other-deprecating)?

## Diagnostic questions

- Does the humor demonstrate cognitive complexity?
- Is it being produced in a signaling-relevant context (mate choice, status competition, in-group)?
- Is the producer displaying ability or merely repeating others' material?
- Does the type of humor (self-vs-other-deprecating) fit the producer's social position?
