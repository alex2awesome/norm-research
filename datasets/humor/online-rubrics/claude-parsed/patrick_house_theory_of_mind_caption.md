# Patrick House — How to Win the New Yorker Caption Contest

Patrick House is a Stanford-trained neuroscientist who won Contest No. 145 and wrote the seminal Slate essay on winning.

## The Counterintuitive Thesis
> "You are not trying to submit the funniest caption; you are trying to win The New Yorker's caption contest. Humor and victory are different matters entirely."

## Theory of Mind Captions
- 136 of 145 winners (94%) are "theory of mind" captions.
- These require the reader to project intents or beliefs into the minds of the cartoon's characters.
- The caption gives a character's interior reasoning that recasts the absurd scene.

## "Mild Chuckle" Rule
> "Your caption should elicit, at best, a mild chuckle."

Submitting anything genuinely laugh-out-loud funny would discomfit the neurotic, introverted New Yorker reader.

## Implications
- Avoid being too funny — be archly intelligent.
- The caption should make you smirk in recognition, not laugh out loud.
- Aim for "the New Yorker reader's nod."
- Subtle, dry, dignified.

## What Wins
- Captions that ascribe a believable inner state to the character.
- Captions that downplay the absurdity (treat it as mundane).
- Wry rather than slapstick.
- Self-aware, intellectualized.

## Rubric for a Caption Contest Submission
1. Does it ascribe a thought/belief/intent to a character (theory of mind)?
2. Is it more wry than laugh-out-loud?
3. Does it treat the absurd scene as mundane?
4. Does it appeal to the New Yorker reader's self-image (intelligent, urbane)?
5. Is it short, dignified, dry?
