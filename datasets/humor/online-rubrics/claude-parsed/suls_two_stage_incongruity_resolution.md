# Suls's Two-Stage Incongruity-Resolution Model

Suls formalized the cognitive processing of jokes and cartoons as an *information-processing* sequence with two distinct stages. The model became the standard framework for cognitive humor research.

## The two stages

### Stage 1 — Incongruity Detection
- The audience processes the setup linearly, generating a *prediction* about what comes next.
- The punchline arrives and **disconfirms** the prediction.
- The audience now perceives an incongruity: the punchline does not fit the predicted continuation.

### Stage 2 — Resolution (Cognitive Rule Discovery)
- The audience searches for a *cognitive rule* that makes the punchline make sense in light of the setup.
- This involves re-examining the setup, finding a previously unconsidered interpretation, and seeing how the punchline fits *that* interpretation.
- If the rule is found → **humor experience**.
- If no rule is found → **puzzlement / nonsense**.

## The diagnostic crucial point

> Humor results from **incongruity that is then resolved**, not from incongruity per se.

Mere weirdness is not funny. The audience must be able to "get it" — to discover the alternate cognitive frame that retroactively makes the punchline coherent.

## Why this matters: necessary conditions for joke success

1. **Setup creates a definite default prediction.** If the setup is too vague to license a prediction, the punchline cannot violate one.
2. **Punchline is incongruous with the prediction.** A predictable punchline has no surprise.
3. **A discoverable cognitive rule exists** that subsumes the punchline. This rule must be:
   - Accessible (audience can find it within the available processing time).
   - Plausible (audience accepts that it fits the setup retrospectively).
   - Neither trivial (too easy = not pleasing) nor obscure (impossible to find = puzzlement).
4. **The audience's processing speed must match the joke's pacing.** Too fast: incongruity not noticed. Too slow: tension dissipates.

## The "sweet spot" of difficulty

Empirically, jokes are funniest when resolution requires *some* cognitive effort but is achievable within seconds. Both extremes (no effort / too much effort) reduce funniness ratings.

## What this excludes

- **Pure incongruity without resolvability** → bewilderment or surrealism (not the comic).
- **Pure congruity** (predictable punch) → no humor.
- **Resolution that is morally or factually wrong** → confusion that the audience eventually rejects.

## Variants and refinements

- **Forward resolution:** the rule is found in the punchline itself.
- **Backward resolution:** the rule is found by re-examining the setup.
- Most jokes use *backward* resolution — the punchline triggers reinterpretation of the earlier text.

## Diagnostic questions

- What prediction does the setup license? Can you state it?
- Is the punchline genuinely incongruous with that prediction?
- What is the resolving cognitive rule? Can the audience reasonably find it?
- Is the resolution at the sweet spot of effort — discoverable but not trivial?
