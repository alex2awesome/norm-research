# iO Chicago: Long-Form Improv Pedagogy

## The Long-Form Lineage
iO Chicago (formerly ImprovOlympic), co-founded by Charna Halpern and Del Close, is the institutional home of long-form improv and the Harold form. Faculty essays emphasize PATIENCE, GROUP MIND, and the audience's reward for narrative structure.

## On the Harold (consensus among faculty)
The Harold is a 25–30 minute form with three rounds of three scenes, intercut with group games and a final tying-together. Its function is structural: TRAIN the team to find connections between disparate scenes that weren't planned.

## On Group Mind
- Group Mind is the felt sense that the team is improvising as ONE intelligence.
- Indicators: simultaneous edits, identical thoughts in tag-outs, callbacks no one set up.
- Build by playing together for years, not by trying.

## On Patience
- The first scene of a Harold sets the universe.
- Don't try to be funny in the first 60 seconds; ESTABLISH.
- The funny will arrive when the structure has earned it.
- Beginners panic and force jokes; pros wait.

## On Connection
- The third round of the Harold rewards CONNECTIONS made by the team.
- The audience laughs at recognizing connections the team also just discovered.
- This is the "magic" of long-form: pattern emergence in real time.

## On Del Close's Maxims (faculty-quoted)
- "Treat the audience as poets."
- "Trust your fellow players."
- "Don't try to be funny; the funny will come."
- "Find the GAME of the scene." (Originated here, refined at UCB.)

## iO Working Rubric
1. Did the first scene establish the universe before trying for laughs?
2. Did connections across scenes emerge organically?
3. Did the team display group mind?
4. Did the third round REWARD the audience's pattern recognition?
5. Did you trust your fellow players?
6. Did you treat the audience as poets?
