# Comedic Distance & Timing of Humor

McGraw, Williams, Warren (2014). "The Rise and Fall of Humor: Psychological Distance Modulates Humorous Responses to Tragedy."

## The Sweet Spot
Time creates a comedic sweet spot when psychological distance is:
- **Large enough** to buffer people from threat (creating benign violation).
- **Not so large** that the event becomes purely benign / nonthreatening.

## Empirical Pattern (Hurricane Sandy Study)
- Day 0: low humor (fresh tragedy).
- Day 15: low point (full damage realized).
- Day 36: peak humor.
- Day 99: humor drops off again (no longer relevant).

## Multiple Dimensions of Distance

### 1. Temporal
- Time since event.
- "Comedy is tragedy plus time."

### 2. Spatial
- Physical proximity to event.
- Far events more humorous than near.

### 3. Social
- Us vs. them distinction.
- Humor easier about strangers/outgroups (with caution re: punching down).

### 4. Imagination / Reality
- Fictional events more funny than real.
- Movie projectile vomit > real one.

## Severity × Distance Interaction
- Terrible events: need MORE distance.
- Mild mishaps: best to joke immediately (don't wait).
- Wrong distance for severity = silence or offense.

## Implications

### "Too Soon"
Insufficient distance → audience feels threat, not humor.

### "Too Late"
Too much distance → no felt violation; "why is this even a joke?"

### "Just Right"
Distance creates safety while violation remains live.

## Rubric
1. What's the severity of the underlying event?
2. What's the audience's distance (time, space, social, reality)?
3. Is there enough distance to feel safe?
4. Is the violation still live (not stale)?
5. Does the joke acknowledge / leverage the distance?
