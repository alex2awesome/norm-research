# Second City: Alumni Essays on Sketch and Long-Form

## Second City's Pedagogical Roots
Founded in Chicago, Second City pedagogy descends from Viola Spolin via Paul Sills, emphasizing GROUP MIND, exploration, and discovery over rule-following. The contrast with UCB's game-first approach is foundational.

## Core Tenets
1. **Yes, And** — agree and add (origin point of the phrase in modern improv).
2. **Bring a Brick, Not a Cathedral** — small offers, build together.
3. **Make Your Partner Look Good** — ensemble first, individual second.
4. **Play to the Top of Your Intelligence** — assume your character is competent.
5. **Discovery Over Invention** — find what's true in the scene rather than imposing it.

## On the Sketch Show Structure
- Open with a high-energy, broadly accessible sketch.
- Anchor mid-show with a sustained piece (a "set piece") that develops a single idea.
- Close with a sketch that callbacks earlier material.
- Blackouts (10–15 second jokes) bridge between longer sketches.

## On Writing for the Mainstage
- Sketches are workshopped on stage, not in rooms.
- Audience reaction across multiple performances drives revision.
- A sketch becomes "set" only after dozens of performances.

## On the "Truth in Comedy" Lineage
- Halpern, Close, Johnson formalized: the funny is in the truth, not the joke.
- Played seriously, absurdity becomes funnier.
- Played for the joke, truth becomes thinner.

## Second City Working Rubric
1. Did the scene EMERGE through ensemble play?
2. Did each player make the others look good?
3. Was the comedy played at the top of intelligence?
4. Was the truth honored before the joke?
5. Has the sketch survived multiple audiences?
6. Does the show structure breathe (long, short, long)?
