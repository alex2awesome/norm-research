# Transgressive / Shock Comedy

## Definition
Transgressive comedy invites an audience to find amusement in things typically considered off-limits — events that would normally bring about very different responses (anger, sympathy, outrage).

## Why It Works (Psychology)
Rooted in:
- **Surprise**: violation of expectation.
- **Relief**: tension released through laughter.
- **Norm violation**: social norms broken safely.

## Techniques
- Deliberate timing and delivery for maximum power.
- Body language, pauses, inflection to increase surprise.
- Deadpan to amplify violation.
- Persona commitment (no winking) to deny escape route.

## The Tightrope
Comedians must balance:
- **Funny** vs. **insensitive**
- **Cathartic** vs. **harmful**
- **Liberating** vs. **traumatizing**
- Subjects that are personal/traumatic for some, distant for others.

## Forms
- **Gross-out**: bodily disgust.
- **Taboo topic**: death, sex, atrocity.
- **In-group violation**: speaking unspeakable in the room.
- **Identity transgression**: speaking from a persona that's not yours.

## Critical Tensions
- Sheer shock value can overshadow inquiry into taste.
- Censorship vs. free expression debate.
- Whose distance gets to count?
- Punching down vs. punching up.

## When It Lands
- Audience consents (right venue).
- Audience has distance (BVT-style).
- Comedian has earned it (persona, history).
- The shock illuminates rather than just shocks.

## When It Fails
- Wrong audience.
- Real harm visible.
- Cheap shock without insight.
- Punching down with no awareness.

## Rubric
1. Does the joke transgress a norm?
2. Is the audience appropriately distant?
3. Has the comedian earned the right (persona / context)?
4. Does the shock illuminate or just shock?
5. Is there asymmetric power (punching up vs. down)?
