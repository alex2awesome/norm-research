# Tig Notaro: Craft Notes (essays and interviews)

## On Silence as Joke
- The pause is not the absence of joke; it is the joke.
- Notaro's signature is durations of silence longer than comedy permits.
- The audience laughs at their own discomfort with the silence.

## On the Famous Cancer Set (Largo, 2012)
- Opened with: "Hello, I have cancer."
- The joke was not the diagnosis; the joke was the AUDIENCE not knowing how to respond.
- Notaro turned audience confusion into comedy by acknowledging it.
- This bit is a masterclass in turning an emotional impossibility into comedy by being honest about the impossibility.

## On Deadpan as Trust
- Deadpan signals: I am not begging for your laugh.
- It transfers responsibility to the audience.
- Audiences laugh harder at jokes the comic doesn't seem to need.

## On Material as Endurance Test
- A bit gets stronger by being told MANY times.
- The fiftieth telling is rarely funnier than the tenth, but it's calmer.
- Calm makes hard material possible.

## On the Topless Set
- Notaro's choice to perform topless after her double mastectomy turned BODY into joke material directly.
- The bit was not "look at my body"; it was "look at how surreal it is for ME to have a body."
- Embodiment can be a craft tool.

## Notaro-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Are you afraid of silence in the joke? Stop being.
2. Is your delivery deadpan enough that you don't NEED the laugh?
3. Have you told it enough times to be calm?
4. Is the joke about the audience's discomfort, not just yours?
5. Could embodiment add to the bit?
6. Does the joke thrive on what would normally be unsayable?
