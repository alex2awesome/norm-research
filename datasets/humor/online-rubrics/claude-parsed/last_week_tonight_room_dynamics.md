# Last Week Tonight: Long-Form Comedy News Construction

## The LWT Format
- ~20 minutes on a single deep-dive topic.
- Structure: news hook → context → escalation of absurdity → call to action / button.
- A research team works for weeks on the deep dive; writers find jokes in the research.

## On Comedy as Argument Vehicle
- The piece is fundamentally an ARGUMENT.
- The jokes are the spoonful of sugar.
- If the argument is weak, the jokes can't save it.
- If the jokes are weak, the argument doesn't reach.

## The "Why Are We Telling You This" Test
- Every segment must answer: why does the audience care?
- Outrage alone is insufficient.
- The angle must be one the audience hasn't considered.

## On Joke Density in Long-Form
- Pace yourself: roughly one joke per 30 seconds early, accelerating.
- Punctuate hard information with comedic relief.
- The audience can absorb dense info ONLY if the comedy keeps them engaged.

## On the Closing Bit
- The closing bit is often a stunt (e.g., a viral product, a parody video).
- It functions as RELEASE after the heavy material.
- It also serves as virality vehicle (the bit gets shared standalone).

## On Tonal Discipline
- Cruelty to individuals is restrained even when targets deserve it.
- Anger directed at SYSTEMS, not people.
- Comedy is the wrapper; argument is the core.

## LWT-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Does the piece have an ARGUMENT, not just outrage?
2. Have you answered "why are we telling you this"?
3. Is the joke density adequate to sustain attention?
4. Are jokes targeting systems, not vulnerable individuals?
5. Does the closing bit provide release / virality?
6. Could the comedy be removed and the argument still stand?
