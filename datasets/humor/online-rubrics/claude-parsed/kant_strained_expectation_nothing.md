# Kant's Incongruity Theory of Laughter (CJ §54)

Kant's section 54 of the third Critique provides the canonical statement of the *incongruity-as-nothing* version of the incongruity theory.

## The defining formula

> "Laughter is an affection arising from the sudden transformation of a strained expectation into nothing."

## What each component means precisely

1. **Strained expectation (gespannte Erwartung)** — the joke or comic situation must first build up a particular cognitive anticipation. Without setup, no payoff.
2. **Sudden (plötzlich)** — the dissolution must happen in an instant. Slow disambiguation does not produce the bodily affect of laughter.
3. **Transformation into nothing (in nichts)** — and this is key — the expectation must NOT be transformed into the *opposite* of what was expected (that would be disappointment or tragedy). It must dissolve into something *insignificant*, into mere play of thought.
4. **Bodily resonance** — the play of mental representations triggers a play of the bodily organs (diaphragm, lungs); pleasure here is *physical*, not intellectual.

## Why "into nothing" is decisive

Kant explicitly distinguishes humor from disappointment:
- *Disappointment*: expectation → painful opposite.
- *Insight*: expectation → significant new truth.
- *Humor*: expectation → vacuum, an absurdity that cannot be cognized, where the understanding finds no satisfaction yet the bodily play produces pleasure.

## The Kantian conditions for a comic stimulus

- The setup creates a definite expectation in understanding.
- The punchline shows that expectation to be void (not wrong, not opposite — *void*).
- The mind cannot rest in any new cognition; the energy spills into the body.
- The pleasure derives entirely from this oscillation, not from the content of either side.

## What Kant excludes from genuine humor

- Things that please by their truth or beauty (these are aesthetic but not comic).
- Mere astonishment at marvels (this is admiration).
- Painful surprises (these are merely shocks).
- Slow-revealed truths (these belong to understanding, not to wit).

## Kant's three examples

- The merchant in Surat who laughs that the foam ran out of an Englishman's bottle of beer — expecting a sober drink, suddenly the bottle is empty.
- The heir who is moved by his rich relative's funeral, whose paid mourners "do less and less the more I pay them" — expectation of decorum reduced to absurd inverse causality.
- The Indian's astonishment at how the beer "got into" the bottle — strained explanation collapses into nonsense.

## Diagnostic questions

- Does the setup create a definite, testable expectation?
- Does the punchline dissolve that expectation into nothing — not into the opposite?
- Is there a brief mental vacuum in which the understanding finds no satisfaction?
- Is the pleasure felt as bodily release rather than as intellectual gain?
