# Slapstick / Physical Comedy

## Definition
A style of humor involving exaggerated physical activity that exceeds the boundaries of normal physical comedy. May involve intentional violence and violence by mishap, often resulting from physical abuse and/or inept use of props.

## Physical Comedy Range
- Slapstick proper
- Pratfalls
- Mime
- Clowning
- Exaggerated facial expressions
- Collisions
- Cartoonish actions

## Five Structural Elements
1. **Repetition** — same gag iterated.
2. **Inversion** — flip expected outcomes.
3. **Anticipation** — telegraph + delay = bigger laugh.
4. **Escalation** — each beat raises stakes.
5. **Timing** — micro-precision is everything.

## Performance Skills
The slapstick comic must be:
- An acrobat
- A stunt performer
- Something of a magician
- Master of uninhibited action and perfect timing

## Visual Primacy
Beauty and hilarity come from visual nature — ideally, audience just watches and laughs without dialogue.

## Famous Practitioners
- Charlie Chaplin
- Buster Keaton
- Harold Lloyd
- The Three Stooges
- Lucille Ball
- Mr. Bean (Rowan Atkinson)
- Jackie Chan

## Why It's Funny (Bergsonian Tie-In)
Slapstick exemplifies "the mechanical encrusted on the living" — a body that should be fluid and adaptive instead behaves like a rigid object.

## Rubric
1. Is the action visually self-explanatory?
2. Is timing precise (microseconds matter)?
3. Is there anticipation/escalation?
4. Is the performer fully committed (no flinching)?
5. Is there a body-as-machine element?
