# Comedy Subgenre Taxonomy

## Performance Subgenres

### Observational
- Draws attention to unnoticed humor in everyday life.
- Relationships, eating, travel, mundane.
- "Did you ever notice…?"

### Self-Deprecating
- Performers target themselves and their foibles.
- Examples: Rodney Dangerfield, Larry David.

### Anecdotal
- Comic personal stories (true or partly true, embellished).
- Storytelling focus.
- Long-form narrative beats > short quips.

### Blue (Off-Color / Risqué)
- Relies on impropriety / indecency.
- Sex, body, taboo content.

### Dark / Black
- Morbid subjects: war, death, crime.
- Incongruity of comedic + morbid.

### Wordplay
- Puns, double entendre, rhyme.
- Linguistic surface comedy.

### Insult / Roast
- Targets others or self.
- Friendly contract required.

### Surreal / Absurdist
- Defies logic deliberately.
- Reductio ad absurdum.

### Improvisational
- Spontaneous, unscripted.
- Yes-and engine.

### Slapstick / Physical
- Body-based humor.
- Visual gags.

### Topical / Political
- Current events.
- Timely references.

### Character-Based
- Comedian plays a persona.
- Andy Kaufman, Maria Bamford.

### Prop Comedy
- Objects as joke device.
- Carrot Top, Gallagher.

### Satire
- Critiques institutions / society.
- Onion, Daily Show.

## Most Comedians Mix Multiple Genres

## Rubric (Genre Tagging)
1. What is the dominant subgenre?
2. What secondary genres mix in?
3. Does the genre fit the audience expectations?
4. Are genre conventions used or subverted?
5. Is the comedian known for this genre?
