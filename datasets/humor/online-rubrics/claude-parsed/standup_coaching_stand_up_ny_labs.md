# Stand Up NY Labs: Working Comic's Blog

## On Stage Time as the Currency
- Three sets a week minimum to develop.
- Five sets a week to grow.
- Seven+ sets a week to professionalize.
- Below three: hobbyist trajectory regardless of talent.

## On the Different Rooms
- **Bringer rooms**: friends laugh — bad data.
- **Open mics with comics**: jaded crowd — useful for premise testing.
- **Bar shows**: civilians but distracted — tests ENERGY and projection.
- **Booked club shows**: civilians + ticket-buyers — closest to professional conditions.
- A working comic should hit all four.

## On the Comic's Notebook
- Date every entry.
- Premises in one section.
- Punchlines/tags in another.
- Bombs and diagnoses in a third.
- A comic without a notebook is a comic without an archive.

## On Avoiding Hack
Hack premises are dead because:
- Audiences have heard the punchline a hundred times.
- The premise no longer reflects current reality.
- The comic doesn't actually believe it.
List of currently-hack subjects (rotating): TSA agents, "smartphones make us antisocial," "kids these days don't go outside," airplane food, marriage as suffering. The premises CAN be salvaged with a fresh, specific angle, but the bar is high.

## On the Five Tiers of Comic
1. Open Micer (free, anywhere).
2. Bringer (paid in venue access).
3. Show Booker (paid in spots).
4. Working Pro (paid in money).
5. Headliner (paid + draws).
Move up by sustained excellence, not networking alone.

## Stand Up NY Labs Working Rubric
1. Are you doing 3+ sets per week?
2. Are you rotating across different room types?
3. Are you tracking material in a notebook?
4. Have you avoided / freshened hack premises?
5. Are you progressing through the five tiers?
6. Are you treating bombs as data, not crisis?
