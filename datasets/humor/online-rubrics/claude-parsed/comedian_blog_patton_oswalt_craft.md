# Patton Oswalt: Notes on Craft

Recurring craft observations from Patton Oswalt's blog posts, podcast appearances, and longer Twitter threads.

## On Specificity as Imagination's Disguise
- Specificity is what makes the audience trust you've actually thought about it.
- "A bag of Vienna sausages" is funnier than "snacks."
- Specificity is the cheapest, most reliable laugh upgrade.

## On Reading Outside Comedy
- Pros read history, philosophy, and weird genre fiction.
- The references seed associations stand-ups don't have.
- The originality of a joke is often the originality of the referent.

## On the Oswalt "Inside" Bit
- Long bits work when they're a constructed argument disguised as a story.
- The audience follows because the comic is BUILDING something, not just naming.
- The structure is: small detail → larger frame → unexpected synthesis.

## On Hack vs. Hack-Adjacent
- A hack premise is one whose joke shape is on rails.
- A hack-adjacent premise is one whose subject is overdone but whose angle is yours.
- The latter is salvageable; the former is dead.

## On Process Notes
- Always carry a notebook.
- Try the joke at a tiny room first.
- Record every set; listen at 1.5x speed for pacing.
- Find one comic whose taste you trust; trade material.

## Oswalt-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Is the joke as specific as your actual taste?
2. Is the reference one only YOU could have made?
3. Is the bit BUILDING toward a synthesis?
4. Is the premise hack, hack-adjacent, or genuinely your angle?
5. Have you recorded and reviewed the set?
6. Did you trade with a comic you trust?
