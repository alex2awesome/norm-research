# Comic Timing

## Definition
A performer's joke delivery as they interact with an audience using intonation, rhythm, cadence, tempo, and beat to guide the comedic narrative.

> "Often, it's not even the joke itself that's funny; it's the delivery."

## Key Principles

### Pacing Affects Meaning
- Pace can change the meaning of a joke.
- Faster ≠ better.
- Slower can be either devastating or boring.

### Building Expectations
- Great timing allows listeners to create expectations.
- Comedy = introducing expectation, then subverting.
- Timing manipulates the expectation curve.

### Allow Audience Reaction
- Pause after the punchline.
- Lets the audience process and laugh.
- Don't step on your own laughs.

## The Pause / Beat

### Definition
A beat is a pause for comic timing — to allow audience to recognize/react to the joke, or to heighten suspense before the punch.

### The Pregnant Pause
- Pausing just before the payoff word.
- "Pregnant" because it promises to deliver.
- Builds tension that releases at the punch.

### Why Pauses Work
- Tension/laughter relationship: laughter releases tension.
- Pause builds tension; the punch releases it.
- Anticipation amplifies the response.

### Famous Example: Jack Benny
- Asked his age, he held silence and stared.
- Audience anticipation amplified the humor.
- Sometimes the pause IS the joke.

## Other Timing Tools
- **Speed up** for absurdity / patter / list jokes.
- **Slow down** for drama / weight / final reveal.
- **Hold** for the audience to catch up.
- **Cut off** their laughter to keep them wanting more.

## Rubric
1. Does the pacing build expectation?
2. Is there a pregnant pause before payoff?
3. Does the comedian allow audience reaction time?
4. Is the rhythm consistent yet surprising?
5. Does delivery match the joke's emotional shape?
