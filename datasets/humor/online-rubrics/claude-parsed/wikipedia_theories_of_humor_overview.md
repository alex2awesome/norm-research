# Major Theories of Humor: A Comprehensive Summary

## 1. Relief Theory
**What Makes It Funny:** Release of psychological tension and pent-up emotions.
- "Laughter serves as a homeostatic mechanism by which psychological stress is reduced."
- Humor allows people to overcome social inhibitions and express suppressed desires safely.
- Tickling produces laughter due to tension buildup and release.
- Figures: Herbert Spencer, Sigmund Freud, Immanuel Kant

## 2. Superiority Theory
**What Makes It Funny:** Feeling superior to others, especially when witnessing their misfortunes or inferiorities.
- Hobbes: laughter as "sudden glory" from recognizing one's power over a target.
- Affective Disposition Theory variant: audiences find humor funnier when bad things happen to disliked people.
- Figures: Hobbes, Plato, Aristotle

## 3. Incongruity Theory
**What Makes It Funny:** Violation of expectations through unexpected twists, contradictions, or surprising juxtapositions.
- Kant: comedy as "the sudden transformation of a strained expectation into nothing."
- "What is black and white and red all over? A newspaper!" — works through unexpected word meaning shifts.
- Figures: Hutcheson, Schopenhauer, Beattie, Bergson

## 4. Benign Violation Theory (BVT)
**Three conditions must be met:**
1. Something threatens one's sense of how the world "ought to be"
2. The threatening situation seems benign
3. A person sees both interpretations at once
- Theorists: Peter McGraw, Caleb Warren

## 5. Script-Based Semantic Theory of Humor (SSTH)
**What Makes It Funny:** Words/situations activate two opposing semantic scripts simultaneously.
- A joke requires text compatible with two different scripts, and those scripts must be opposite.
- Example: "Is the doctor at home?" triggers both DOCTOR and LOVER scripts.
- Theorist: Victor Raskin (1985)

## 6. General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH)
Six knowledge resources working hierarchically:
1. Script Opposition (conflicting concepts)
2. Logical Mechanism (connection between scripts)
3. Situation (objects, props, context)
4. Target (who the joke targets)
5. Narrative Strategy (joke format)
6. Language (wording and placement)
- Theorists: Raskin, Attardo

## 7. Computer Model of Humor
- Rapid deletion of false interpretations from consciousness, releasing excess neural energy.
- Humor enhances "practical problem solving" by strengthening brain circuitry for detecting mistaken reasoning.
- Theorists: Suslov (1992); Hurley, Dennett, Adams (2011)

## 8. Misattribution Theory
- Audiences misidentify why they find something amusing, attributing laughter to innocent elements when actually responding to taboo or hostile content.
- Freud's tendentious vs. non-tendentious humor.
- Theorists: Freud, Zillmann & Bryant

## 9. Sexual Selection Theory
- Humor signals intelligence and other desirable traits, evolved through sexual selection.
- Theorist: Geoffrey Miller

## 10. Ontic-Epistemic Theory
- Recognition that social reality suddenly appears unreal.
- Theorist: P. Marteinson (2006)

## 11. Bergson's Mechanistic Theory
- Rigidity, inflexibility, mechanical behavior applied to living things.
- "The source of the comic is not ugliness but rigidity."
- Theorist: Henri Bergson

## 12. Defense Mechanism Theory
- Humor as a high-level defense mechanism.
- Theorist: George Eman Vaillant

## 13. O'Shannon Model
- Four interactive elements: context, information, aspects of awareness, enhancers/inhibitors.
- A joke need not rely on high incongruity if it triggers superiority, relief, aggression, or identification.
- Theorist: Dan O'Shannon (2012)

## 14. Complex Systems Theory
- Humor emerges from interacting parts in complex dynamic systems.
- Theorists: Tschacher & Haken, Demjén
