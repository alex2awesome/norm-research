# Anthony Jeselnik — Dark One-Liner Technique

## Style Identity
Old-fashioned setup-and-punch-line comedian. ~1–2 laughs per minute (high density). Not a storyteller — relies on engineering, not biography.

## Joke Construction
Each joke = a finely tuned machine. Sets up expectations only to subvert them with punchlines that hit harder than expected.

## Comedic Conflict
Mastery lies in "comedic conflict":
- The delicate balance between safety and violation.
- Deliberately leads audiences into uncomfortable territory.
- Knowing the resulting tension will amplify the eventual release.
- Operates near the edge of "too far."

## Influences
- Mitch Hedberg
- Dennis Miller
- Sarah Silverman
- Steven Wright

## "Lightbulb Moment"
Two years into stand-up, wrote a bizarre joke with a dark twist. Audience response encouraged him to focus on dark/offensive topics through a character.

## Character Commitment
- Anti-comedy approach centered on unwavering commitment to character.
- Maintains arrogant persona throughout.
- Refuses to soften with smiles or self-deprecation.
- Prevents audience from dismissing it as "just a joke."
- Forces them to confront the violation.

## Joke Structure (Jeselnik Pattern)
1. Innocuous setup ("My grandmother got hit by a car…")
2. Sharp pivot via word choice
3. Dark/disturbing reveal
4. No softening, no "I'm kidding"

## Rubric (Jeselnik Method)
1. Is the setup deceptively innocent?
2. Does the punch hit darker than the setup signaled?
3. Is the persona consistently committed (no break)?
4. Is the joke maximally compressed?
5. Does the comedian refuse to apologize?
