# Dark Comedy / Black Humor

## Definition
A style of comedy that makes light of subject matter generally considered taboo — particularly subjects normally considered serious or painful — to provoke discomfort, serious thought, and amusement.

## Topics Covered
- Death, disease
- Deformity, handicap
- Warfare, atrocities
- Mental illness
- Despair, suicide

## Academic Characterization
"Juxtaposes pain and joy, whimsical facts and unevenly calm reactions, cruelty and tenderness." Uses grotesque or morbid humor to express the absurdity, insensitivity, paradox, and cruelty of the modern world.

## Functions

### Coping
- Allows individuals to process difficult situations.
- Can aid long-term stress reduction.
- Helps acceptance of anxiety-inducing events.

### Social
- Strengthens morale of the oppressed.
- Undermines morale of the oppressors.
- Gallows humor: laughing at one's own dire situation.

## Cognitive Demands
Research shows black humor processing requires:
- High verbal intelligence.
- Mood stability.
- Low aggressiveness.

(People who appreciate dark humor most aren't more aggressive; they're often less so, with high intelligence.)

## Sub-Types
- **Gallows humor**: humor about one's own dire fate.
- **Sick joke**: extremely transgressive content.
- **Tragicomedy**: balanced pain and laughter.
- **Cringe comedy**: characters' pain.

## Rubric
1. Is the topic taboo or painful?
2. Is the treatment juxtaposed with calm/whimsy?
3. Does it offer cognitive insight, not just shock?
4. Does the audience have psychological distance from the subject?
5. Does it feel earned, not gratuitous?
