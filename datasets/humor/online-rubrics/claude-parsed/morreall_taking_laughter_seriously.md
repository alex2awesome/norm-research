# Morreall: Humor as Pleasure in a Cognitive Shift

Morreall argues that the three classical theories (superiority, relief, incongruity) each fail individually. He proposes a unifying account:

> **"Humor is the enjoyment of a cognitive shift."**

## The core formula

A humor experience occurs when:

1. **A psychological shift occurs** — from one mental state, expectation, or interpretation to another.
2. **The shift is sudden** (or at least quick enough to register as a switch).
3. **The shift is accompanied by pleasure** rather than by negative affect.
4. **The pleasure is taken in the shift itself**, not derived from the content of either state.

## What "cognitive shift" subsumes

The cognitive-shift account covers:

- **Incongruity perception** — the shift from expected interpretation to incongruous one.
- **Resolution** — the shift from puzzlement to understanding.
- **Reframing** — the shift from one frame to another (humor proper, gallows humor).
- **Recognition** — the shift from not seeing a pattern to seeing it.
- **Bisociation** — the shift from M1 to M2.
- **Script switching** — the shift from script A to script B.

## Conditions that turn a cognitive shift into humor (rather than something else)

A cognitive shift becomes a humor experience only when:

- **No serious practical concern is engaged** (otherwise the shift triggers planning, fear, etc., not amusement).
- **No strong negative emotion arises about the new state** (otherwise the shift triggers grief, anger, disgust).
- **The audience is in a play frame** that allows enjoying the shift itself.
- **The shift is non-trivial** — the new state must genuinely differ from the old.

## Critique of classical theories Morreall fixes

### Superiority (Hobbes) is too narrow
- Doesn't explain laughing at clever wordplay where no one is being put down.
- Doesn't explain laughing at oneself in present moment (no comparative superiority).
- Doesn't explain laughing at something one finds *better* than expected (Chaplin's grace).

### Relief (Spencer/Freud) has the wrong physiology
- Hydraulic nervous-energy model is empirically false.
- Doesn't explain humor in stimuli that arouse no prior emotional buildup.

### Incongruity (Kant/Schopenhauer) is too broad
- Pure incongruity also occurs in horror, surprise, mystery — not all of which are funny.
- Needs the *enjoyment* and *play frame* conditions to specify when incongruity becomes humor.

## Morreall's evolutionary embedding

Morreall connects the cognitive-shift theory to evolutionary cognitive function:
- The mind is built to detect mismatches between expectations and inputs.
- The pleasure of detecting and re-framing mismatches encourages mental flexibility.
- This mental flexibility is adaptive — humor reinforces the cognitive habit of considering alternative frames.

## Three "theoretical points" Morreall demands of any humor

1. **Cognitive content** — there is something the audience represents.
2. **Shift** — that representation suddenly changes.
3. **Hedonic positive valence** — the change is enjoyed.

Without any one of these three, the experience is not humor.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is there a definite mental representation in play before the comic moment?
- Does that representation undergo a sudden change?
- Is the change enjoyed rather than mourned/feared?
- Has the audience been kept out of serious practical engagement?
- Could the same shift, in another context, produce horror, sadness, or wonder instead?
