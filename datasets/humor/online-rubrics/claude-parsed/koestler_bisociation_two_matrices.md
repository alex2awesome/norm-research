# Koestler's Bisociation Theory of Humor

Koestler (1964) proposes that all creativity — humor, science, and art — operates by **bisociation**: the perceiving of a situation in two self-consistent but habitually incompatible frames of reference (matrices), labeled M1 and M2.

## The defining formula

A creative act is the simultaneous perception of one event/idea/situation in **two associative contexts that are normally incompatible**. The two matrices intersect at the *bisociative point*. In the moment of intersection, the same element belongs simultaneously to both M1 and M2.

## What a "matrix" is

A matrix is "any ability, habit, or skill, any pattern of ordered behaviour governed by a 'code' of fixed rules." Examples of matrices:
- Mathematical reasoning.
- Etiquette of a dinner party.
- Genres of narrative.
- Domain expertise (medicine, law, sports).
- Cultural scripts (wedding, funeral, job interview).

Each matrix has its own internal logic, vocabulary, expected behaviors, and modes of relevance.

## The structure of a joke (per Koestler)

1. **Setup** primes M1 — a single matrix is established (a story, a question, a context).
2. **Audience progresses inside M1** — interpreting events according to its rules.
3. **Punch line** is a stimulus that *also* fits M2 — but M2 was hidden, unexpected, or unusual in this context.
4. **Sudden bisociative perception** — the audience sees that the *same* element belongs to both matrices.
5. **M2 supplants M1** as the frame of interpretation, retroactively reinterpreting the setup.
6. **Discharge of laughter** — the redundant emotional energy of the M1 expectation has nowhere to go and discharges as laughter.

## Why humor uses bisociation specifically with self-assertive emotion

For Koestler, the *humor* variant of bisociation is distinguished by the **emotional climate**:
- Humor → the emotion that gets discharged is *self-assertive / aggressive* (mockery, hostility, schadenfreude).
- Science → the same bisociative structure, but emotion is intellectual *integration* — eureka.
- Art → the same structure, but emotion is *participatory* and self-transcendent — empathy, awe.

So a joke is a bisociation in which the discharged feeling is one of self-assertion or aggression.

## The criteria for a successful comic bisociation

1. **Two matrices must each be internally coherent.** A joke that just fails inside one matrix is not funny.
2. **The two matrices must be habitually incompatible.** If the bisociation is too easy or expected, no surprise.
3. **The bisociative point must be unmistakably double.** The audience must recognize that the same word, image, or action genuinely fits both frames.
4. **The shift from M1 to M2 must be sudden** — the punch must be a switch, not a fade.
5. **An aggressive or self-assertive emotion must be present** to discharge as laughter.

## Distinguishing strong from weak bisociation

- **Strong:** the M1 reading was robust and seemed inevitable; the M2 reveal feels surprising yet retroactively natural.
- **Weak:** M1 was thin and easy to suspend; M2 was telegraphed; the reveal feels mechanical.

## Diagnostic questions

- What is M1? What rules / expectations did the setup activate?
- What is M2? What alternative frame fits the punch?
- Does the bisociative element really fit both matrices?
- Is the shift sudden? Is the audience's emotional energy of M1 expectation now redundant and available for discharge?
- Is the affect of the discharge self-assertive (mockery, surprise-at-foolishness) rather than purely aesthetic?
