# Carolyn Wells — A Parody Anthology (1904)

## Defense of Parody
> "The main intent of the vast majority of parodies is simply to amuse; but to amuse intelligently and cleverly."

Wells argued parody had ancient sanction (going back to the Greeks).

## Parody Quality Criteria

### Intelligent Amusement
- Not mere mockery — clever recognition.
- The reader must know the original.
- Pleasure comes from spotting the substitution.

### Style Matching
- Successful parody captures the original's syntactic/lexical patterns.
- Replaces content while keeping structure.
- The form is paradoxically faithful.

### Affectionate vs. Hostile
- Most parodies are good-natured.
- Show respect through the labor of imitation.

## Examples
- Wells's own parody of Sinclair Lewis's *Main Street*: "Pto-maine Street" (1921) — Carol Kennicott becomes Warble Petticoat. Witty puns abound.

## Wells's Other Anthology
- *A Nonsense Anthology* — collection of literary nonsense (Edward Lear, Lewis Carroll style).
- Important early collection of nonsense humor.

## Why Parody Works (per Wells)
- Recognition pleasure (audience knows the original).
- Substitution surprise (new content in old form).
- Form-content tension (gap is comic).
- Affectionate critique.

## Rubric (Parody Quality)
1. Does the parody capture the original's distinctive style?
2. Is the substitution surprising?
3. Does it amuse intelligently (not crudely)?
4. Does it require knowledge of the original (rewarding the literate)?
5. Does it land with affection or contempt?
