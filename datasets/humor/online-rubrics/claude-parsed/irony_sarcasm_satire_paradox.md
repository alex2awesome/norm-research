# Irony, Sarcasm, Satire, Paradox

## Definitions

### Irony
Something happens that is the opposite of what was expected. Juxtaposition of what appears on the surface vs. what is actually the case. Neutral by definition.

### Sarcasm
An ironic remark meant to mock by saying something different than what the speaker really means. Caustic, often insulting. Sarcasm requires intent.

### Satire
The use of humor, irony, exaggeration, or ridicule to expose and criticize stupidity or vices. Always has a target/agenda.

### Paradox
An apparent contradiction that contains truth. Not strictly humorous but humor-adjacent.

## Types of Irony

### Verbal Irony
Saying something different from what you really mean.

### Situational Irony
The opposite of what's expected actually happens. (E.g., a fire station burns down.)

### Dramatic Irony
The audience knows something the characters don't. Creates tension and humor.

### Cosmic Irony
Fate or some higher power orchestrates an ironic outcome.

## Key Distinctions
- Situations are ironic; people are sarcastic.
- Irony can be unintentional; sarcasm requires intention.
- Irony is neutral; sarcasm aims to embarrass or insult.
- Sarcasm is a sub-type of verbal irony.

## Rubric (Irony / Sarcasm)
1. Is there a gap between surface and actual meaning?
2. Is it intentional?
3. Is the target clear?
4. Is the tone biting (sarcasm) or neutral (irony)?
5. Does the audience perceive the gap?
