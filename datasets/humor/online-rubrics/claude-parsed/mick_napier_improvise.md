# Mick Napier — Improvise: Scene from the Inside Out

Founder of the Annoyance Theatre in Chicago. Published 2004. Considered "revisionist" — questions accepted improv rules.

## Core Philosophy: "Do Something"

The deceptively straightforward approach to starting a scene:
1. Do something
2. Check out what you did
3. Hold onto it

"At the top of an improv scene, in the first crucial moments, it is far more important that you do something than what it is you actually do."

## Key Tenets
- The "Rules" of improv (yes-and, no questions, etc.) were corrective responses to bad habits, not laws.
- Take care of yourself first — then your scene partner.
- Trust your initial instinct.
- Specificity beats generality.
- Initiate boldly.
- Don't over-think; commit.

## Rubric (Napier-Style)
1. Did the performer do something at the top?
2. Did they take care of themselves (committed strongly)?
3. Was the choice specific?
4. Did they hold onto the original choice?
5. Did they trust their first instinct?
