# Bob Mankoff — How About Never—Is Never Good for You?

Former Cartoon Editor of The New Yorker (1997–2017), inventor of the New Yorker Caption Contest.

## What Mankoff Looks for in a Cartoon / Caption

### Surprise Is Essential
- Surprise is the essential ingredient in humor.
- Surprise violates expectations.
- When the unexpected occurs and we realize there's nothing to worry about, we laugh.

### What New Yorker Cartoons AVOID
- Obscenity.
- Aggression.
- Cruelty.
> "We remove some of the main levers by which we're funny — and we do it intentionally."

### Cartoon Quality Criteria
- Original premise (no warmed-over situations).
- Specific drawing (Roz Chast's wallpaper, Charles Addams's gloom).
- Caption fits the drawing exactly — not interchangeable.
- Subtle, not over-explained.
- "Twoness" — drawing says one thing, caption another, both resolve.

### Caption Contest Selection Insights
- Mankoff and team examine ~5,000 captions per week.
- Sort into three buckets: keepers, maybe, no.
- Keepers re-read; finalists chosen.
- Winner is most "New Yorker-y" — wry, urbane, character-mind.

### His Own Iconic Cartoon
"How about never—is never good for you?" — exemplifies:
- Conversational tone.
- Politeness mask.
- Devastating refusal.
- Reusable in many contexts.

## Rubric (Mankoff Criteria)
1. Is there genuine surprise?
2. Does the cartoon avoid obscenity, aggression, cruelty?
3. Does the caption fit ONLY this cartoon (not interchangeable)?
4. Is there a "twoness" of drawing and caption?
5. Is it wry/urbane (the New Yorker register)?
6. Could the caption become quotable / reusable?
