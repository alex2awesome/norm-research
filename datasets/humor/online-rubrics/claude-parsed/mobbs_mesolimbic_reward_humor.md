# Mobbs et al.: Humor as a Reward Signal

Mobbs et al. (2003) is the canonical demonstration that the experience of humor activates the same dopaminergic reward circuitry implicated in pleasure from food, money, sex, and addictive drugs.

## The finding

Funny cartoons (rated funny by the subject) activated:
- **Nucleus accumbens** — central node of the mesolimbic dopamine reward system.
- **Ventral tegmental area (VTA)** — origin of dopaminergic projections.
- **Amygdala** — salience and emotional valuation.
- **Anterior cingulate cortex** — conflict monitoring and reward integration.

Crucially, BOLD signal in these regions was **positively correlated with subjective humor intensity**: the funnier the cartoon was rated, the stronger the activation.

## Why this matters

Humor is not merely a cognitive event with a positive feeling tone — it is a *bona fide reward*. The brain treats successful humor comprehension as reinforcement, motivating us to seek more of it (and to seek out the people who provide it).

## Implications for what makes something "funny"

For a stimulus to count as funny in the strong neural sense:

1. **It must trigger reward circuitry**, not merely understanding.
2. **The strength of the funniness rating tracks reward signal strength** — there is a natural metric for funniness in the brain's evaluation of humor as reward.
3. **Personality and reward-sensitivity modulate the response** — people whose reward systems are more responsive find a wider range of stimuli funny (anhedonic individuals find less to be funny).

## Personality predictions confirmed

Follow-up work by Mobbs et al. (PNAS 2005) showed that *extraversion* predicts greater reward-region activity to humor. Introverts and those with reduced reward sensitivity show smaller reward-region responses to the same stimuli.

This is the neural basis for why "the same joke isn't equally funny to everyone" — the reward system's sensitivity is a personality variable.

## Distinguishing humor reward from other rewards

A 2018 follow-up dissociated humor reward from monetary reward:
- Both engage the ventral striatum (motivation).
- But humor uniquely engages additional medial prefrontal "hedonic" regions.

So humor is its own kind of reward — sharing motivational machinery with other rewards but having its own hedonic signature.

## What is excluded by this framework

- Stimuli that produce only cognitive understanding without reward (these are clever, not funny).
- Stimuli that produce reward only via other channels (relief, surprise) without engaging the humor-specific hedonic medial prefrontal pattern.

## Diagnostic questions

- Does the audience's reward circuitry actually engage (visible in laughter, smiles, sustained attention, voluntary repetition)?
- Is the audience reward-sensitive at this moment (extraversion, mood, no anhedonia)?
- Does intensity of expressed funniness match what the joke's structure would predict, or is reward-evaluation modulating it (e.g., the same joke is rated less funny by depressed audiences)?
