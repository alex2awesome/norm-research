# Joseph Boskin — Rebellious Laughter

People's Humor in American Culture (Syracuse University Press, 1997).

## Central Thesis
Humor in America is a primary cultural weapon. Jokes are a cultural barometer of concerns and anxieties — they appear in everyday language long before issues reach stand-up comics.

## Key Arguments

### Humor as Resistance
- Rebellious humor exerts effect during:
  - Political repression
  - Ethnic and gender conflicts
  - Collective worries over social/economic crises
- Easy transmission gives it tremendous impact.

### Joke Cycles as Social Texts
- Joke cycles emerge around traumatic events (Challenger, 9/11, etc.).
- They process collective shock.
- Then fade as the shock resolves.
- Examining cycles = social history.

### Humor from Many Sources
- Whites, Blacks, women, Hispanics
- Conservatives and liberals
- Public workers and university students
- The powerless and the power brokers

## Implications for Joke Quality

### Topical Currency
- Funny jokes plug into current anxieties.
- Stale topics don't land.
- The barometer reading must match the moment.

### Group Identification
- Audience must recognize their own anxieties in the joke.
- In-group humor lands harder.

### Catharsis
- Jokes about scary things let the audience release fear.
- Failed catharsis = uncomfortable silence.

## Rubric
1. Does the joke address a current cultural anxiety?
2. Does it process collective fear/shock?
3. Does it land with a particular in-group?
4. Does it punch up at power?
5. Does it provide catharsis?
