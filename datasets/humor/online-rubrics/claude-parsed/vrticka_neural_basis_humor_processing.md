# Vrticka, Black & Reiss: The Two-Network Neural Model of Humor

Vrticka et al. (2013) integrate over a decade of fMRI humor research into a two-network model that distinguishes the *detection/resolution* of humor from the *appreciation/reward* of humor.

## The two-network architecture

### Network 1: Detection & Resolution of Incongruity (cognitive)
Involves cortical areas:
- **Temporo-occipital-parietal junction** — detects mismatches between expected and presented stimuli.
- **Posterior temporal regions** — script/script comparison.
- **Dorsolateral prefrontal cortex** — working memory, conflict monitoring during reinterpretation.
- **Inferior frontal gyrus** — semantic/phonological resolution.

### Network 2: Appreciation & Reward (affective)
Involves subcortical areas:
- **Mesocorticolimbic dopaminergic system** — ventral tegmental area, nucleus accumbens, ventromedial prefrontal cortex.
- **Amygdala** — salience and emotional valuation.

## Sequence of processing (proposed)

1. Stimulus arrives → temporo-occipital-parietal regions detect mismatch.
2. Cortical comprehension network (DLPFC + temporal regions) attempts resolution.
3. If successful → mesolimbic reward circuit fires → subjective amusement.
4. Motor output → laughter via brainstem and cerebellar pathways.

## Why this matters

Humor is not a single brain process. It is the *coupling* of two systems. A complete account must specify:
- How the cognitive network processes the comic structure.
- How the affective network rewards the successful comprehension.

## Modulating factors documented

The review catalogues factors that modulate this coupling:

### Sex differences
- Women show greater reward activation per unit of "funniness" (more selective).
- Men show similar reward activation across a wider range of stimuli (less discriminating).
- Both sexes activate the same network architecture.

### Personality (extraversion)
- Extraverts show stronger ventral striatal response to humor.
- Introverts show weaker reward response to the same stimuli.
- Comprehension network unchanged.

### Age
- Children's humor comprehension network develops gradually; younger children appreciate physical/incongruity humor (matures earlier) before complex verbal humor.
- Older adults show preserved appreciation but slower comprehension.

### Psychiatric conditions
- Autism: altered theory-of-mind processing affects comprehension of social/sarcastic humor.
- Depression: reduced mesolimbic response → reduced humor enjoyment despite intact comprehension.
- Schizophrenia: disrupted incongruity-resolution coupling.

## Implications for "what makes something funny"

A stimulus's funniness depends on:

1. **Comprehensibility** to the audience's cognitive network (script knowledge, language facility, theory-of-mind capacity, age-appropriate complexity).
2. **Reward-engagement** matched to the audience's affective network (mood, extraversion, reward sensitivity, attention to social vs. structural features).
3. **The coupling** between them being intact (some clinical populations have one without the other).

## Diagnostic questions

- Does the audience have the cognitive prerequisites (vocabulary, scripts, theory-of-mind capacity) to comprehend?
- Is the audience's reward state (mood, extraversion, attention) primed for engagement?
- For populations with known coupling disruptions, has the joke been calibrated (e.g., simpler structure for younger audiences, less ToM-dependent for autism)?
