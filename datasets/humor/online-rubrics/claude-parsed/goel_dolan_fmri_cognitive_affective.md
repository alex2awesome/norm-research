# Goel & Dolan fMRI: Two Brain Systems for Humor

The 2001 fMRI study by Goel and Dolan provided the first clear neural evidence that humor processing has **two separable components**: a *cognitive* (joke-comprehension) component and an *affective* (amusement/reward) component.

## The methodological design

Subjects in fMRI listened to two types of jokes:
- **Semantic jokes** (jokes that rely on meaning, e.g., a play on the situation).
- **Phonological jokes** (puns — jokes that rely on sound similarity).

Subjects pressed a button to indicate whether or not each item was amusing.

## Two-system finding

### Cognitive component is type-specific
The brain regions activated by joke comprehension *differ* by joke type:

- **Semantic jokes** → left posterior middle temporal gyrus, left inferior temporal gyrus, right posterior middle temporal gyrus, cerebellum.
- **Phonological jokes (puns)** → left posterior inferior temporal gyrus, left inferior frontal gyrus (BA 44, 45 — Broca's region).

Therefore: getting a pun and getting a semantic joke are *different* cognitive operations using different language-processing pathways.

### Affective component is type-independent
But both joke types — once "gotten" — activated the same affective region:
- **Ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC)** — the brain's reward-evaluation hub.

Therefore: the *amusement itself* is a single, common reward signal regardless of how the joke was structurally constructed.

## What this implies for theory

A joke must succeed in **two stages**:

1. **Comprehension stage** — the appropriate cognitive system (semantic or phonological) must parse the joke and detect/resolve the incongruity. Failure here = "I don't get it."
2. **Reward stage** — the comprehended joke must trigger the vmPFC reward response. Failure here = "I get it but it's not funny."

A joke can fail in either or both stages independently.

## Diagnostic implications

For a joke to be funny, both must hold:

- **Right cognitive engagement** — the joke must engage the type of processing it is built for. A pun demands phonological attention; a situational joke demands semantic-pragmatic processing. A wrong frame defeats comprehension.
- **Reward valuation must trigger** — the brain must evaluate the comprehension event as reward-worthy. Audience temperament, current state, and prior expectations modulate this.

## The dissociation

Brain damage to language regions (e.g., left inferior frontal) impairs comprehension of *that type* of joke specifically.
Brain damage to vmPFC impairs the *amusement response* to all joke types — patients understand the structure but report no funniness.

## What this rules out as full theories

- Pure incongruity theories underestimate the affective stage.
- Pure relief theories underestimate the comprehension specificity.
- A complete theory must specify both how cognition processes the structural payload and how reward circuitry evaluates the result.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is the joke type matched to the cognitive system the audience is currently engaging (verbal pun → phonological mode; situational → semantic-pragmatic mode)?
- Once comprehended, does the joke trigger reward evaluation? (E.g., does the audience smile/laugh, or report flat understanding?)
- Are there comprehension failures (wrong "type" of attention) vs. valuation failures (got it but not funny)?
