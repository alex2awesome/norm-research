# GTVH: The Six Knowledge Resources

The General Theory of Verbal Humor (GTVH) extends Raskin's SSTH from a binary "is-this-a-joke" decision into a multi-dimensional framework that can describe joke *similarity* and *variation*. A joke is fully specified by six Knowledge Resources, hierarchically ordered.

## The hierarchy (top to bottom = more abstract to more surface)

1. **Script Opposition (SO)** — the deepest layer.
2. **Logical Mechanism (LM)** — how the script switch is justified.
3. **Situation (SI)** — the props, participants, activities.
4. **Target (TA)** — who or what is butt of the joke.
5. **Narrative Strategy (NS)** — the genre or rhetorical form.
6. **Language (LA)** — the surface verbal realization.

The hierarchy matters: jokes that share a higher-level KR are perceived as more similar than jokes that share only lower-level KRs.

## Each resource in detail

### 1. Script Opposition (SO)
The pair of opposed scripts identified by SSTH (real/unreal, sex/non-sex, etc.). The deepest invariant of the joke.

### 2. Logical Mechanism (LM)
The *resolution device* — the (often fallacious) reasoning that makes the script switch feel justifiable. Attardo lists examples:
- **Juxtaposition** (placing two scripts side by side).
- **Figure-ground reversal.**
- **Faulty reasoning / fallacy.**
- **Cratylism** (sound-meaning iconicity).
- **Garden path** (audience led to wrong inference, then corrected).
- **Self-undermining** (statement undercuts its own premise).
- **Almost-situation / parallelism / chiasmus / reasoning from false premises.**
- **Vacuous reversal / role exchange / coincidence / analogy.**

### 3. Situation (SI)
The "props" of the joke: the doctor's office, the bar, the desert island. Two jokes can share the same SI but differ in SO.

### 4. Target (TA)
The butt — ethnic group, profession, character type. Some jokes have no TA (resolution-only humor); these are sometimes called *non-targeted*.

### 5. Narrative Strategy (NS)
The genre / rhetorical form:
- Simple narrative.
- Dialogue / question-answer.
- Riddle.
- Pseudo-riddle.
- Aside / parenthetical.
- Garden-path narrative.

### 6. Language (LA)
All choices about exact word selection, phrasing, syntax, and the specific verbal realization of the punchline.

## Joke similarity prediction

Two jokes that share LA are nearly identical (the same joke). Two jokes that share only SO are merely "the same kind of joke." Sharing higher KRs = greater perceived similarity.

## Necessary conditions for a successful verbal joke

1. **A valid Script Opposition** (per SSTH).
2. **A discoverable Logical Mechanism** that makes the switch feel justified.
3. **An apt Situation** that supports both scripts.
4. **A target** (or none, if non-targeted) calibrated to audience.
5. **A Narrative Strategy** appropriate to delivery context.
6. **Language choices** that maximize surprise (punchline at end, key words placed for maximum cognitive effect).

## Diagnostic questions per KR

- SO: which two opposed scripts are at play?
- LM: what reasoning device justifies the switch?
- SI: what setting/props/activities anchor the joke?
- TA: who or what is being mocked, if anyone?
- NS: what genre is this (riddle, narrative, dialogue)?
- LA: are word choices and ordering optimized for the punch?
