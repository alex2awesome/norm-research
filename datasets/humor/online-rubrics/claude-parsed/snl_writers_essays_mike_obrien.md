# Mike O'Brien: SNL and 7 Minutes in Heaven Craft

## The "Found Joke" School
- Mike O'Brien's signature is finding the comedic moment in a real interaction (7 Minutes in Heaven).
- The interview structure CREATES the joke; the celebrity is the foil.
- This is the unscripted comedy of FRAMING.

## On Frame as Comedy
- A frame imposes RULES (you must answer in the closet, with no preparation).
- The audience laughs at the celebrity navigating the imposed rules.
- The frame is the joke; the dialogue is the execution.

## On SNL Sketch Writing
- O'Brien's SNL sketches often had a single bizarre premise played fully straight.
- The straightness is the joke; the world's commitment to the absurdity.
- Examples include "Diner Lobster" and "Chris Pine: Hilarious Disaster."

## On the One-Joke Sketch Risk
- A sketch that's ONE joke played four times is a bad sketch.
- A sketch that's ONE WORLD with multiple jokes within it is a good sketch.
- The distinction: does the premise GENERATE more material, or does it just repeat?

## On Pitching at SNL
- Monday is pitch night: each writer pitches 3–5 sketches to the host.
- The host picks favorites; the writer drafts by Wednesday.
- Pitch as performance: how you pitch determines whether it gets written.

## O'Brien-Inflected Working Rubric
1. Is the FRAME of the piece doing comedic work?
2. Is the world played straight despite its absurdity?
3. Does the premise generate, or just repeat?
4. Did you pitch with energy?
5. Have you found the comedy in real-world constraints?
6. Are you committing to the bit completely?
