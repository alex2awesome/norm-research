# Parody, Satire, Pastiche

## Satire
- Genre that criticizes and attacks vice, folly, abuse — particularly of those in power.
- Marked by anger and desire to change/destroy what it attacks.
- Uses humor to make a specific point.
- Doesn't simply make fun — seeks to inspire change.
- Targets a genre, cultural belief, or social movement.
- Critiques society broadly.

## Parody
- Practice of copying mannerisms, style, or appearance of a work to make a point about THAT work.
- Adopting the attacked work's guise to reveal its inherent ridiculousness.
- Generally gentler and less venomous than satire.
- Often good-natured or affectionate.
- Attacks style/content of a fictional work, NOT real-life events.

## Pastiche
- Copies/mimics elements of another work's style.
- Often humorous but can be admiring.
- Lacks satire or mockery.
- Pays homage; emulates distinctive qualities out of admiration.
- Not transformative the way parody is.

## Side-by-Side
| | Target | Tone | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Satire | Real world / society | Angry | Change |
| Parody | A specific work / genre | Playful | Critique via mimicry |
| Pastiche | A style | Affectionate | Homage |

## Examples
- *Don Quixote* — parody of chivalric romances.
- *Spaceballs* — parody of *Star Wars*.
- *A Modest Proposal* — satire on English treatment of Irish.
- *The Daily Show* — satire on news media.
- *The Hours* (novel) — pastiche of Virginia Woolf.

## Rubric
1. What is being targeted (work, genre, society)?
2. Tone (angry/playful/affectionate)?
3. Is it transformative or imitative?
4. Does it have an agenda for change?
5. Does the audience need to know the original?
