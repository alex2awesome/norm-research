# Greenbaum — Stand-Up Comedy as Rhetorical Argument

Andrea Greenbaum, HUMOR 12(1), 1999.

## Central Thesis
Stand-up comedy is inherently rhetorical discourse:
> "It strives not only to entertain, but to persuade, and stand-up comics can only be successful in their craft when they can convince an audience to look at the world through their comic vision."

## Implications

### Comedy = Argument
- Every joke makes a claim about the world.
- Audience laughs = audience consents to the claim.
- Comedians build "comic vision" through accumulated jokes.

### Persuasion Tools
- **Ethos**: comedian's persona/credibility.
- **Pathos**: emotional connection / shared experience.
- **Logos**: surprising logical inversions.
- **Kairos**: timing, topical relevance.

### Audience Negotiation
- Comic's vision must be accessible.
- Must overcome resistance to the worldview.
- Laughter signals agreement.
- Silence signals dissent.

## Why It Matters for "What Makes a Joke Funny"
- A joke isn't merely funny — it persuades.
- Funniness = persuasive power for that audience.
- Different audiences = different rhetorical contexts.
- "Funny to whom?" matters.

## Comic Culture
- Each comic establishes a "stance."
- Jokes consistent with the stance accumulate.
- Audience comes to anticipate the worldview.
- Identity-construction is ongoing.

## Rubric
1. Does the joke advance a coherent comic vision?
2. Is the rhetorical stance clear?
3. Is the audience implicitly persuaded?
4. Does it use ethos / pathos / logos / kairos?
5. Does laughter signal genuine agreement, not just relief?
