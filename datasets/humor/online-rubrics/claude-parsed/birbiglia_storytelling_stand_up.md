# Mike Birbiglia — Storytelling Stand-Up

## Distinguishing Approach
- Care and attention to **structure, pacing, cohesion**.
- Long-form storytelling, hyper-verbal.
- Less reliant on physicality / facial.
- "Essays with beats."
- Memoir-style structure.

## Material Selection Criterion
> "I always ask people, 'What's the story you don't want to tell anyone?' And, weirdly, that is the story you want to tell on stage."

> "It's the story that you're so reluctant to tell that the audience can feel it in the room."

## Why Reluctant Stories Land
- Vulnerability creates immediate empathy.
- Audience senses the cost of disclosure.
- Trust accelerates.
- Audience willing to go to "different places."

## Development Process
- Works in smaller chunks first.
- Painstakingly crafted over months in club and theater sets.
- Revises nightly.
- Tracks changes with color-coded setlists.
- "100 jokes that work" comes from "500 jokes that don't work."

## Long-Form Callback Loops
- Earlier story elements return.
- Recontextualized at the end.
- Audience feels structure click.

## Emotionally Honest Observational Humor
- Doesn't deflect with deflection humor.
- Real feelings present in the room.
- Comedy IS the emotional processing.

## Specials Structure
- Often a single thematic question (sleep, marriage, mortality, swimming).
- Many anecdotes, one through-line.
- Climactic emotional moment near the end.
- Final laugh that recontextualizes.

## Rubric (Birbiglia / Storytelling)
1. Is there a story the comedian was reluctant to tell?
2. Is there a clear through-line / theme?
3. Are there long-form callbacks?
4. Is the structure tight (no padding)?
5. Does emotional honesty land alongside laughs?
