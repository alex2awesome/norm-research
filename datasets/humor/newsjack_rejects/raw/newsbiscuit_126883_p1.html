    That's the lights on the tree sorted.

    JEREMY CORBYN Yes, see them twinkle, in an equally pretty way to those of Diwali, the Hindu festival of light.

    MRS CORBYN
    Doesn't the gender-neutral faith mascot look lovely at the top?

    JEREMY CORBYN
    It does indeed.

    MRS CORBYN
    I'll just get some more tinsel. Oh, have you put up the wreath?

    JEREMY CORBYN
    The, er, the what dear?

    MRS CORBYN
    The wreath. Did you put up the wreath on door?

    JEREMY CORBYN
    I was present at the porch at the time when the wreath was in the vicinity of the door.

    MRS CORBYN
    What does that mean? Did you put it up?

    JEREMY CORBYN
    There was a wreath-hanging event at the front of the house, which I attended to commemorate the traditional Yuletide period celebrated by Christians and non-Christians worldwide.

    MRS CORBYN
    Are you okay, Jeremy?

    JEREMY CORBYN
    I stood in the vicinity of the doorway, and the wreath, though I do not believe I was actively involved in the placement of that exact wreath on that specific door. There were many wreaths being laid on many doors in the street.

    DOOR KNOCKS AND OPENS

    NEIGHBOUR
    Oh hello Jeremy, I saw you putting the wreath on the door.

    JEREMY CORBYN
    Look, just because someone might pay their respects to the doorway with use of a wreath doesn't mean they support everything the doorway has ever done, or the methods used to achieve opening and closing and free access for everyone from any faith, or none, who lives in the property, or has been made homeless by the Tories.

    NEIGHBOUR
    So... Christmas at my mothers again this year?

    MRS CORBYN
    I'll get my coat.

    Nike have announced a surge in sales since launching an ad campaign featuring NFL star Colin Kapernick.     The Lib Dem Rebranding

    CHRISTINE: Ok everyone, as you know, according to the last opinion poll, despite the Tories having a slow motion car crash over Brexit and Labour imploding, the public still prefer both to us.
    JO: What's wrong with the electorate? Our ideas are well thought out. We offer sensible policies that appeal to geography teachers and Guardian readers alike, and they'd still prefer what else is on offer? A PM who dances like an uncle at a wedding and a leader of the opposition who's like a 70's sitcom labour voter?!
    STEPHEN: Our research says people think we're dull, uninspiring, forgettable. In fact, most could only name Tim Farron as a Lib Dem.
    TIM: Hurray! I'm memorable!
    CHRISTINE: That you are Tim.
    TIM: Gays are an abomination.
    JO: That's enough Tim, go back to your colouring.
    TIM: Yay!
    FX: SCRIBBLING
    STEPHEN: So what do we do? Kiss some babies? Have another leadership contest? That normally moves us up to at least item five on the news, after the big stuff but before the surfing dog. It’s a good place to be.
    CHRISTINE: We need to be more assertive, more partitioning, less middle of the road, less beige. Find a figurehead that's polarising, like Nike have done with Colin Kapernick. Someone that generates column inches, who's divisive. Here's my idea.
    FX: SLIDE CLICK
    JO: Piers Morgan?
    CHRISTINE: He's opinionated, forceful, a real Marmite character.
    STEPHEN: If you mean he looks like something scraped from a tramp's armpit, you're right.
    JO: I'd rather eat something scraped from a tramp's armpit than discuss anything with him.
    TIM: His face is like a boogieman. Slimy.
    CHRISTINE: Yes, thank you Tim. But you see, all of you have a real visceral reaction to the idea. Once everyone’s nausea fades we'll have people on Twitter burning their cards like they were doing with their sportswear. Think of the clicks!
    STEPHEN: Most of our membership think clickbait is something you fish with. And they wouldn't use it because it's not ethical, or is bad for dolphins.
    JO: I'm really not sure about this. I haven’t felt this uncomfortable since we made Nick do that “I’m sorry” video. I’d like to know that Vince thinks. Vince?
    FX: SNORING
    CHRISTINE: He won't wake up until he hears the Countdown theme and you know it. What are our other options? Most of the country oppose Brexit. We're the only party that publicly opposes Brexit and we still can't get people engaged. If you've got better ideas, now's the time people!
    FX: SNORING
    STEPHEN: I've got it! Everyone got interested in us when the BBC showed that Jeremy Thorpe drama, we should do that.
    JO: I don't think we can find someone to sleep with Piers Morgan. Rumour has it even his wife uses a blindfold.
    TIM: Jeremy is in hell for what he did. Dirty.
    CHRISTINE: Tim! Someone give him their phone so he can watch Baby Shark.
    TIM: Yay! Doo doo doo doo doo doo.
    JO: What I meant was we pay someone to try and bump him off, and the subsequent trial will get more coverage than anything we've done in years.
    CHRISTINE: Hmm. And if they successfully kill him?
    JO: Well, that's what we call a win-win.

    The BBC announced its autumn line up this week. While there was no room for my pitch, 'Maid Stone' where I’m proclaimed the queen of Kent and live like royalty for six episodes, there was news that Doctor Who would move to Sunday nights. With new Doctor Jodie Whittaker keeping her Yorkshire accent for the part, rumours were rife that the new series would have parallels with another Sunday night BBC perennial set in Yorkshire.

    Dr Who of the Summer Wine

    GRAMS: DR WHO THEME INTO LAST OF THE SUMMER WINE
    ADAM: It's great to get out the city isn't it?
    KERRY: Maybe, but I don't see why you had to drag me all the way to Yorkshire when we could have just gone to Hyde Park, or Chessington.
    ADAM: Oh come on, smell that air!
    KERRY: It smells funny.
    ADAM: No, it doesn't smell of anything.
    KERRY: That's the problem.
    ADAM: Sorry, I forgot you prefer your atmosphere with chunks in it.
    KERRY: Whatever, I'm knackered, when's the bus?
    ADAM: Not for another hour.
    KERRY: An hour? How do people here cope? Last week we had a ten minute wait in Balham and there was nearly a riot. People don’t want to spend that long in Balham, trust me.
    ADAM: Do you want to get a cup of tea at the café over there?
    KERRY: Nah, I'm just going to sit here and wait.
    FX: DOOR OPENS
    DOCTOR: What the bloody hell's thou doing?
    KERRY: Sitting...
    DOCTOR: Not on my step you're not, I've just swept that. Get away or you'll feel me sonic broomstick up your nether regions.
    KERRY: Sorry, I didn't expect anyone to be living in a little blue box.
    DOCTOR: Little. Blue. Box?! I'll tell you, it might be little on the outside, but inside is a palace.
    KERRY: Jeez, sorry!
    FX: EXPLOSION
    ADAM: What was that?
    DOCTOR: Oh bloody hell, it's the Daleks! Run for your life! They're soulless horrific creatures from an inhospitable hellhole.
    ADAM: What, another planet?
    DOCTOR: No, Lancashire. Quick, here they come.
    DALEK: (metallic, grating) Ecky Thump! Ecky Thump!
    DOCTOR: We've got to draw them away from here. Quick, in the escape craft.
    KERRY: That's a bath on wheels.
    DOCTOR: Aye. Fastest bath in the dales. Get in if you want to stay alive. They're getting closer!
    FX: SQUEAKY WHEELS
    DALEK: It's not a bap, it's a barm. Manchester is better than Leeds.
    DOCTOR: Oh that's too far! Take the wheel, I'm going to sort this out
    FX: TWANG
    DALEK: Cannot see! Cannot see!
    FX: CRASH
    DOCTOR: Nowt like a well-placed stocking to blind them. Let's get this thing back to the TARDIS.
    FX: SONIC SCREWDRIVER
    ADAM: How are we going uphill?
    DOCTOR: Sorry, let me introduce myself, I'm The Doctor. I can do about most things that are impossible. Ah, here we are, beloved TARDIS (pronounced “Tadis” with a hard-a)
    KERRY: Shouldn't that be pronounced TARDIS?
    DOCTOR: No, it's TARDIS, so don't thee be a Twaat about it.
    FX: DOOR OPENS
    DOCTOR: Now, I suppose you'll want to be coming along for the ride.
    ADAM: It's, it's.
    DOCTOR: Aye, bigger on the inside. I told you, a palace. Also a time machine. So, you coming?
    ADAM: Why not? By the way, I’m Adam, this is Kerry.
    DOCTOR: Fabulous. Come on Adam, Come on Kes. Alright, let me put some paper down. I'm not having your mucky boots make a state of my floor, not when I've just finished the cleaning. I hope thou's had your tea, I've got nowt in.
    KERRY: So we can go anywhere in time and space?
    DOCTOR: Aye, though mainly it'll be 21st century Earth, are you ready? Let's go.
