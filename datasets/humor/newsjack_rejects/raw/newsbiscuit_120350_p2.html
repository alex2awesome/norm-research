    There are two-and-a-half rules regarding apostrophes.

    RULE NUMBER ONE: When you contract what you're writing, use an apostrophe to show where the missing letters would have been.

    For example, to write “it is” in a screaming hurry, write “it's”.

    RULE NUMBER TWO: To make a noun possessive, add an apostrophe and an ’s’.

    For example, St James has a palace named St James’s.

    RULE NUMBER TWO (a): If the noun is plural already ending with the letter ’s’, just add an apostrophe.

    For example, “dogs’ dinners.”

    That’s it.

    ...this famous best-selling book of Hawking's was 'one of the most widely sold books which no-one read'...

    is equivalent to

    ...this famous best-selling book of his was 'one of the most widely sold books which no-one read'...

    not

    ...this famous best-selling book of he was 'one of the most widely sold books which no-one read'...

    nor

    ...this famous best-selling book of him was 'one of the most widely sold books which no-one read'...

    so, as I said before, possessive; an apostrophe is needed. It wasn't '... a book of stamps ...' nor a book about the sport of hunting with hawks.

    Nor was it a book about Hawking, the man. The book was by Hawking; it was his creation: it was his book. A book of his. Not a book of he, or a book him.
