# Open Philanthropy - Grantmaking Process and Criteria

## Four-Stage Grant Process
Open Philanthropy grants move through four standard stages:
1. **Grant Investigation** — investigator works with potential grantee to explore proposal
2. **Conditional Approval**
3. **Formal Approval**
4. **Evaluation and Close**

During investigation, an Open Philanthropy investigator works with a potential grantee to explore and develop a funding proposal by discussing:
- Proposed activities
- Budget
- Anticipated challenges
- Desired outcomes of the project

## Cause Selection Framework: I-T-N

When selecting which causes to fund, Open Philanthropy considers three dimensions:

### Importance
How many individuals are affected by the problem, and how deeply.

### Tractability
The likelihood that a philanthropic funder can contribute to significant progress.

### Neglectedness
Causes that receive less attention from others — including donors, businesses, and governments — where each dollar tends to go even further.

## Grant-Specific Evaluation

### Cost-Effectiveness Estimation
Grant investigators sometimes include a "back-of-the-envelope-calculation" (BOTEC) to roughly estimate the expected cost-effectiveness of the potential grant.

### Counterfactual Reasoning
When assessing whether a specific grantee is the right partner, Open Philanthropy considers:
- Whether it is the best organization to do this work
- Whether its work is the most valuable use of that organization's additional resources
- What would happen without the grant (counterfactual)

## Questions Asked Before Each Grant

Open Philanthropy publishes "Questions We Ask Ourselves Before Making a Grant" including:
- What's the case for the grant?
- What's our cost-effectiveness estimate?
- Who else is funding this work?
- What's our counterfactual impact?
- What are the risks?
- What's our plan for monitoring and evaluation?
- How will we know if this worked?

## Key Focus Areas

### Global Health and Wellbeing
- Global health
- Scientific research (esp. for the global south)
- Global development
- Farm animal welfare

### Longtermism / Existential Risk
- AI risks (alignment, governance)
- Biosecurity and pandemic preparedness
- Other potential catastrophic risks

## Distinctive Features

### Public Reasoning
- Detailed grant write-ups published
- Reasoning made transparent
- Cost-effectiveness models shared
- Mistakes acknowledged

### Effective Altruism Framework
- Grew out of effective altruism community
- Quantitative/explicit approach to philanthropy
- Cause-neutral comparisons across very different areas
- Willing to make unconventional bets

### Long-Term Bets
- Funds high-uncertainty, high-potential-payoff work
- Comfortable with low success rates if expected value high
- Willing to fund early, exploratory work

## RFP Approach
- Most grants made to invited applicants
- Some open Requests for Proposals
- Active in seeking out promising researchers
- Often funds new fields/topics that mainstream funders ignore

## Grant Sizes
- Small exploratory: $50K-$500K
- Standard: $500K-$5M
- Strategic: $5M-$50M
- Transformative: $50M+ (rare)

## Notable Differences from Traditional Funders
- Quantitative cost-effectiveness focus
- Cross-cause comparison
- Preference for neglected areas
- Public, detailed reasoning
- Comfort with uncertain/long-term outcomes
- Less reliance on traditional peer review
