# Female Science Professor (FSP) — Grant Writing Themes

Female Science Professor was a popular anonymous physical sciences PI blog focused on the academic career experience of women in STEM, with extensive coverage of NSF and DOE grant writing.

## Key Themes

### Writing as a Mid-Career Woman PI
- Reviewers may unconsciously discount confident claims from women PIs
- Strategy: over-document with citations and preliminary data
- Use first-person plural even for single-PI grants
- Avoid hedging language that may already be assumed

### NSF Specifics
- Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts get equal weight in language
- BI section has become longer and more substantive over time
- Reviewers want to see specific BI activities tied to specific outcomes
- "I will mentor underrepresented students" is too vague — name the program, the numbers, the outcomes

### The Project Description Structure
- 15 pages is short — every paragraph must justify itself
- Open with the big-picture question
- Connect to NSF's strategic priorities
- Show how your work fits into the field, not just your lab

### Reviewer Behavior
- NSF panels are heterogeneous in expertise
- The "ad hoc" outside reviewer can sink or save you
- Panel reviewers do most of the talking
- Mail reviewers' written critiques carry weight in close cases

### Career Award (NSF CAREER) Specifics
- The integrated research-and-education plan must actually integrate
- Education plan can't be an afterthought
- Use existing university programs, partnerships, outreach infrastructure
- Reviewers smell ad-hoc educational add-ons

### Collaborations and Letters of Support
- Letters must be specific and substantive
- Generic "I support this project" letters are worse than no letter
- Co-PI vs. collaborator distinction matters for budget allocation
- International collaborators need extra justification

### Resubmission and Persistence
- Most NSF grants are funded after multiple submissions
- Address all panel comments in the project description (not as response document)
- "We have substantially revised this proposal in response to prior feedback" framing
- Don't take rejection personally — funding rates are 15-25%

## Writing Process Themes
- Start the Project Summary last
- Write the Specific Aims / Project Description first
- Get internal feedback before external feedback
- Read your proposal aloud to catch awkward sentences
- Have a non-expert in your area read it for clarity

## What FSP Says Wins
- Clear, falsifiable hypotheses
- Strong preliminary data with figures
- Realistic timeline with milestones
- Concrete BI activities with measurable outcomes
- A research plan that builds on but extends prior work
- Demonstrated capacity to execute (publications, prior funding, students trained)

## What Loses
- Project Summary that's a paragraph of dense prose
- BI section as boilerplate
- Vague timeline
- Letters of support that are obviously templated
- No mention of how the work fits into the broader field
- Aim or task that requires another aim's success first

## Voice / Style Notes
- Be confident, not arrogant
- Cite competitors' work fairly
- Acknowledge limitations upfront in Discussion-style framing
- Use figures to convey complex concepts quickly
- White space is your friend
