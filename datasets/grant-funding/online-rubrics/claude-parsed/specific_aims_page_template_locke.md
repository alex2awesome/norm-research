# Specific Aims Page - Standard Template (Locke, after Russell, Morrison, Giddings, et al.)

This template was compiled by Rachel E. Locke, PhD, based on work from NIH and researchers including Drs. Morgan Giddings, Martie Thompson, Erica Whitney, Rita Balice-Gordon, SW Russell, and DC Morrison.

## Overall Structure
The specific aims page is a standalone, one-page document that succinctly conveys your research idea. The aims page should be well organized with good use of white space, bold, and underline.

## The Four-Paragraph Structure

### Paragraph 1: Hook / Introduction (3-5 sentences)
Set up the scientific problem in broad terms a non-expert can understand:
- Topic and importance (1-2 sentences)
- Current state of knowledge (1-2 sentences)
- The pivotal gap that prevents progress

End with a sentence that states the gap or problem. Often: "However, [the critical gap]..."

### Paragraph 2: Critical Need / Knowledge Gap (3-5 sentences)
- State the critical need: what is needed to fill the gap
- Long-term goal of your research program
- The objective of THIS application — what you propose to do
- The central hypothesis (if hypothesis-driven)
- Justification for your hypothesis (preliminary data + literature)

### Paragraph 3: Approach Overview / Rationale (3-4 sentences)
- Your approach to test the hypothesis
- Why you are well-positioned to do this work (preliminary data, expertise)
- Brief mention of methods/innovation

### Paragraph 4: The Aims (numbered list)
Each aim should:
- Start with "To xxx" where xxx is WHY you are doing the Aim
- Continue with Verb + WHAT you are going to do to test your hypothesis for that Aim
- State hypothesis (if hypothesis-driven area)
- Briefly describe methods (optional)
- State expected outcomes

Standard format for each aim:
**Aim 1: [Title - Active verb + key concept]**
*Hypothesis*: [if applicable]
*Approach*: [brief methods]
*Expected outcome*: [what you'll learn]

### Closing (1-2 sentences)
- Significance / impact of expected outcomes
- "Successful completion of these aims will [the impact]..."
- "This is a significant departure from the status quo because..."

## Key Principles

### Use Strong, Active Language
- **DO**: "We will determine," "We will establish," "We will characterize"
- **DON'T**: "We hope to," "We will attempt to," "We will try"

### Word Economy
- Every sentence must work
- One page = ~500-800 words depending on figures
- Cut hedging, redundancy, unnecessary qualifiers

### Visual Elements
- Consider adding a figure (conceptual model, hypothesis schematic)
- Simple figures save words and provide visual reinforcement
- Don't overcrowd

### Independence of Aims
- Aims should NOT be co-dependent
- One aim should not require success of another
- Each aim has a discrete falsifiable hypothesis

### Common Aim Structures
1. Test hypothesis A
2. Test hypothesis B (related but independent)
3. Apply findings (e.g., translation, model)

OR

1. Define mechanism
2. Identify regulators
3. Test perturbation effects

## What Reviewers Look For
- Clear statement of significance
- Compelling rationale
- Achievable in 4-5 years
- Independent aims
- Strong investigator/team (implied)
- Innovative but feasible
- Measurable endpoints

## Common Mistakes
- Aims that are descriptive rather than hypothesis-driven
- Hedging language signaling lack of confidence
- Co-dependent aims (if one fails, all fail)
- Too ambitious / unrealistic
- Insufficient context for non-expert reviewers
- Missing significance / "so what"
- No mention of preliminary data
