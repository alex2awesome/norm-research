# NIH Human Subjects Protections - Review Criterion

## Regulatory Framework
The HHS regulations for the protection of human subjects in 45 CFR Part 46 provide a framework based on established, internationally recognized ethical principles to safeguard the rights and welfare of individuals who participate in research supported by NIH.

## Five Areas Reviewers Evaluate

### 1. Risk to Subjects
- Risk Category (e.g., research not involving more than minimal risk)
- Identification of all risks (physical, psychological, social, economic, legal)
- Likelihood and severity of each risk
- Special protections for vulnerable populations

### 2. Adequacy of Protection Against Risk
- Recruitment and informed consent procedures
- Plan to protect privacy and confidentiality
- Data management and security plans
- Plan to address adverse events

### 3. Potential Benefits of the Proposed Research
- Importance of the knowledge to be gained
- Direct benefits to subjects (if any)
- Generalizable benefits to society
- Risk-benefit relationship is reasonable

### 4. Importance of Knowledge to be Gained
- Significance to the field
- Value to society
- Justification for research with these subjects

### 5. Data and Safety Monitoring (for Clinical Trials)
- Adequacy of monitoring plan
- Independent DSMB if needed
- Stopping rules
- Reporting requirements

## Application Requirements

### Required Form
All projects proposing human subjects research, either exempt or non-exempt, including those proposing clinical trials, must complete the **PHS Human Subjects and Clinical Trials Information form**.

### Protection of Human Subjects Section
Must describe:
- Proposed involvement of human subjects
- Assessment of risk
- Steps taken to protect subjects from risk
- Potential benefits from study to subjects and others
- Importance of the knowledge to be gained

### Inclusion Plan
A plan must describe:
- Inclusion of women, children, and minorities in the research
- Anticipated enrollment figures table
- Justification for any exclusions
- Outreach and recruitment strategies

## IRB Requirements
- Recipient institution must ensure all institutions engaged in research have a current, approved Federal Wide Assurance (FWA)
- Must obtain certification of approval by an IRB registered with OHRP for all nonexempt research before research may begin
- Single IRB (sIRB) policy applies to multi-site non-exempt human subjects research

## Inclusion Across the Lifespan
NIH expects inclusion of:
- Women
- Members of racial and ethnic minority groups
- Children (per Inclusion of Children in Research policy)
- Older adults (per Inclusion Across the Lifespan policy)

## NIH Definitions of Clinical Trials
NIH definition: A research study in which one or more human subjects are prospectively assigned to one or more interventions to evaluate the effects of those interventions on health-related biomedical or behavioral outcomes.
