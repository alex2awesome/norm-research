# NEA - Research Grants in the Arts Review Process

## Research Grant Overview

Research Grants in the Arts support research studies that investigate:
- Value and/or impact of the arts
- Either as individual components of the U.S. arts ecosystem
- Or as they interact with each other
- And/or with other domains of American life

### Award Tiers
- **Research Grants in the Arts**: $20,000 - $100,000
- **Research Labs**: $100,000 - $300,000

## Application Review Process

### Stage 1: Eligibility Check
Applications are checked for completeness and eligibility by NEA staff.

### Stage 2: Advisory Panel Review
Eligible applications are reviewed in closed session by **interdisciplinary research and evaluation advisory panelists**.

Each panel comprises:
- A diverse group of arts-research experts
- Other individuals
- At least one knowledgeable layperson

### Stage 3: Panel Recommendations
The panel recommends the projects to be supported.

### Stage 4: Staff Reconciliation
NEA staff reconciles panel recommendations with available funds.

### Stage 5: National Council on the Arts
- Recommendations forwarded to the National Council on the Arts
- Reviewed in an open public session
- Council makes recommendations to the NEA Chair

### Stage 6: Final Decision
The NEA Chair reviews the recommendations and makes the final decision on all grant awards.

## Transparency Standards
- The NEA seeks to make its review process transparent
- While protecting the confidentiality of applicants, panelists, staff, and Council members
- The names of applicants are kept confidential except for those who receive grants

## Review Criteria (typical)
- Significance of the research question
- Theoretical or methodological contribution
- Quality of research design
- Rigor of methods
- Capability of research team
- Potential to advance knowledge in the field
- Potential to inform arts policy/practice

## Eligible Research Areas
- Economic impact of the arts
- Cognitive/educational benefits
- Health and wellbeing benefits
- Community development
- Creative careers and labor markets
- Audience research
- Arts and technology
- Cross-domain effects of arts engagement

## Notable Features
- One of few federal funders for arts research
- Includes humanities, social science, and quantitative approaches
- Public-facing research valued
- Open data and reproducibility encouraged
- Strong methodological rigor required
- Competition is described as "extremely rigorous"

## Typical Grant Components
- Research design (clear hypothesis or research questions)
- Theoretical framework
- Methodology
- Sample/population
- Data collection plan
- Analysis plan
- Dissemination plan
- Budget
