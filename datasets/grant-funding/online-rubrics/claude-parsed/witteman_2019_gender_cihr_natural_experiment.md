# Witteman et al. 2019: Are Gender Gaps Due to Applicant or Science Evaluation?

Natural experiment exploiting CIHR's 2014 split into two new grant programs — one focused on the science, one focused on the principal investigator's calibre. Analyzed 23,918 applications from 7,093 PIs (2011-2016).

## Two Grant Programs Compared (the experimental rubric variation)

### "Project Grant" (science-focused)
Reviewers evaluate primarily:
1. Quality of the proposed research
2. Significance of the research question
3. Methodology
4. Feasibility

### "Foundation Grant" (PI-focused)
Reviewers evaluate primarily:
1. **Calibre / track record of the principal investigator**
2. Research direction / vision
3. Productivity and impact
4. Plus aspects of the proposed program

## Key Findings (the bias rubric)

- Overall application success rate: 15.8% across all programs.
- After adjusting for age and research domain:
  - **Traditional programs (mixed criteria)**: female success ~0.9 percentage points lower than male.
  - **New science-focused program**: gap remained ~0.9 percentage points.
  - **New PI-focused program**: gap WIDENED to **4.0 percentage points**.

## Conclusion

Gender gaps in grant funding are attributable to **less favourable assessments of women as principal investigators**, NOT to evaluations of the proposed science.

## Implications for Rubric Design

- Rubrics that emphasize "investigator quality" / "PI calibre" criteria amplify gender bias.
- Rubrics that focus reviewer attention on the proposed work itself (anonymizing or de-emphasizing PI identity) reduce gender disparities.
- Practical recommendation: shift weight from investigator criteria toward science criteria; consider redaction trials.

## Methodological Innovation

The natural experiment design isolates the effect of rubric *focus* from confounds (same applicants, same year, same general agency, only differing in how reviewers were instructed to weight applicant vs project).
