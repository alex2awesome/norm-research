# DFG (Germany): Review Criteria — Detailed (Wave 2 supplement)

Wave 1 has a general DFG file; this Wave 2 entry captures the explicit criteria the DFG publishes plus the exclusionary criteria.

## Three Primary Evaluation Criteria

1. **Scientific quality** of the proposed research
2. **Originality** of the project
3. **Relevance** to the field

## Three-Stage Review Architecture

### Stage 1: Written Reviewer Reports
Individual external reviewers (selected by DFG Head Office) provide written evaluations on:
- Research question quality
- Methodological approach
- Feasibility
- Applicant qualifications
- Innovation
- Embedding in current literature

### Stage 2: Review Board Evaluation
Elected review board members (chosen by the scientific community) place individual reviews in disciplinary context, judging:
- Quality of the reviews themselves
- Appropriateness of reviewer selection
- Comparative ranking within the discipline

### Stage 3: Joint Committee Decision
Final funding decision by Joint Committee (researcher-majority) considering:
- Strategic priorities
- Budget availability
- Programmatic fit

## Explicit Non-Scientific Criteria EXCLUDED (the anti-rubric)

DFG funding decisions must NOT be influenced by:

1. **Absolute age** of the applicant
2. **Gender**
3. **Ethnic origin**
4. **Sexual orientation**
5. **Illness or disability**
6. **Family status / parental leave history**
7. **Career interruptions** (these are positively accommodated, not penalized)

## Track-Record Assessment Caveat

When considering the applicant's scientific output, evaluators consider:
- **Effective research time** (excluding parental/medical leave, military service, etc.)
- Quality (not quantity) of selected publications
- A maximum of 10 publications per applicant in proposals

## Implications for Rubric Design

DFG's model treats "non-scientific criteria" as an explicit anti-rubric — articulating the things reviewers must NOT consider. This exclusion list is itself a rubric element rare in US/UK systems.
