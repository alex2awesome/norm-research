# NSF Broader Impacts - Comprehensive Guidance

## Definition
NSF expects researchers' work to have broader impacts: **the potential to benefit society and contribute to the achievement of specific, desired societal outcomes.**

## Five Review Criteria for Broader Impacts

Grant reviewers evaluate broader impacts statements on these five criteria:

1. **What is the potential for the proposed activity to benefit society or advance desired societal outcomes?**

2. **To what extent do the proposed activities suggest and explore creative, original or potentially transformative concepts?**

3. **Is the plan for carrying out the proposed activities well-reasoned, well-organized and based on sound rationale? Does the plan incorporate a mechanism to assess success?**

4. **How well qualified is the individual, team or institution to conduct the proposed activities?**

5. **Are there adequate resources available to the principal investigator to carry out the proposed activities?**

## How Broader Impacts Can Be Accomplished
- Through the research itself
- Through activities directly related to specific research projects
- Through activities supported by, but complementary to the project

## Examples of Desired Societal Outcomes

- Improving education and educator development in STEM
- Increasing public scientific literacy and public engagement with STEM
- Improving the well-being of individuals in society
- Developing a globally competitive STEM workforce
- Building partnerships between academia, industry and others
- Improving national security
- Increasing the economic competitiveness of the U.S.
- Enhancing infrastructure for research and education
- Increasing participation of underrepresented groups in STEM

## Key Writing Tips

### Be Specific
Don't just say "students will be trained in bioinformatics." Give details: how many students, what type of students, how will they be recruited, trained, and evaluated.

### Plan to Evaluate
Plan to evaluate. At the end of the project, you will need to report your impact. Having an evaluation plan in place shows reviewers that you care about whether your program is working.

### Identify Your Audience
Identify the intended community and match your strategy. If you propose an open-access tool that focuses on one part of society, ensure your tool is appropriate for that audience.

### Match Career Goals
Develop an "impact identity" matched with your career trajectory. Build sustained engagement rather than one-off activities.

### Avoid Common Pitfalls
- Avoid education components that are generic and expected of any PI in your field anyway (e.g., mentoring graduate students alone is not enough)
- Don't promise more than you can deliver
- Don't treat broader impacts as an afterthought
- Don't list activities without infrastructure to support them

## Common BI Activity Categories
1. Education (K-12 outreach, undergraduate research, graduate training)
2. Public engagement (museums, citizen science, media)
3. Workforce development (training, internships)
4. Diversity, equity, and inclusion (URM recruitment, accessibility)
5. Economic development (technology transfer, entrepreneurship)
6. Infrastructure (datasets, tools, instrumentation)
7. International collaboration
8. Societal/ethical implications
