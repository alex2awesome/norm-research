# Roy 1985: Real Defects of Peer Review

Influential critique of NSF/NIH-style competitive grant peer review by materials scientist Rustum Roy, with a proposed alternative funding mechanism.

## Defects Roy Identified (anti-rubric — what reviewers do badly)

1. **Excessive opportunity cost** — top scientists spend 25-50% of their time writing and reviewing grants rather than doing research.
2. **Conservatism / aversion to high-risk high-payoff work** — reviewers favor proposals where the answer is essentially known.
3. **Bias against young investigators** lacking established track records.
4. **Bias against established scientists changing fields** — track record in old field doesn't translate.
5. **Bias against interdisciplinary work** that doesn't fit cleanly into a single panel.
6. **"Mission" work crowds out curiosity-driven inquiry**.
7. **Reviewer conflicts of interest** are pervasive but under-managed.
8. **Reviewer competence is variable** and not audited.
9. **Anonymity prevents applicants from countering errors of fact** in reviews.
10. **The system rewards proposal-writing skill** rather than scientific ability.

## Roy's Alternative — Modified Block Grants

Every qualified scientist receives a guaranteed baseline of funding from the federal government. Additional discretionary funds are distributed by individual scientists themselves to colleagues whose work they value, with each scientist required to redistribute a fraction of what they receive. This shifts allocation from anonymous panel review to peer-recognition-by-action.

## Criteria for a Better Funding System (Roy's positive rubric)

1. Minimize time scientists spend on funding paperwork.
2. Tolerate failure — fund risky work.
3. Allocate based on demonstrated past output, not rhetorical proposal quality.
4. Distribute decision-making power widely rather than concentrating it in panels.
5. Build in self-correction through peer recognition.
