# AHRQ - Grant Application Peer Review Criteria

## Overview
Applications submitted to AHRQ are evaluated by the AHRQ peer review process to ensure a fair, competent and objective assessment of their scientific and technical merit in accordance with the Public Health Service Act and federal regulations. The peer review of grant applications involves an assessment conducted by panels of experts established according to scientific disciplines or medical specialty areas.

## Five Core Scored Criteria

The five scored criteria for research grant applications are (similar to traditional NIH):

### 1. Significance
- Importance of the health services research question
- Potential to advance understanding of healthcare delivery
- Relevance to AHRQ priorities (quality, safety, equity, accessibility)

### 2. Investigator(s)
- Qualifications of PI and team
- Track record in health services research
- Multi-disciplinary expertise

### 3. Innovation
- Novel approaches to healthcare research
- New methodologies
- Innovative use of data

### 4. Approach
- Soundness of methodology
- Appropriate study design
- Data sources and quality
- Analysis plan
- Implementation feasibility

### 5. Environment
- Institutional resources
- Access to data, populations
- Collaborations with healthcare delivery systems

## Scoring System
- 9-point scale: 1 (exceptional) to 9 (poor)
- Score of 5 considered average
- Final overall impact score = average × 10
- Final scores range from 10 (high impact) through 90 (low impact)

## AHRQ-Specific Considerations

### Priority Populations Inclusion
Peer reviewers assess the adequacy of plans to address the needs of AHRQ priority populations and must include their assessment of the proposed inclusion plan for priority populations in evaluating the overall scientific and technical merit of the application.

### AHRQ Priority Populations
- Low-income groups
- Minority groups
- Women
- Children
- Elderly
- Individuals with special healthcare needs
- Individuals living in inner-city and rural areas

### Implementation Science Focus
Many AHRQ grants emphasize:
- Translation of research to practice
- Stakeholder engagement
- Real-world implementation
- Evaluation of practice change

## Common AHRQ Grant Mechanisms
- R01 (Research Project Grant)
- R03 (Small Research Grant)
- R18 (Research Demonstration and Dissemination)
- R21 (Exploratory/Developmental)
- R36 (Dissertation Award)
- K-series (Career Development)
- T32 (Training)
- U18 (Cooperative Demonstration)

## Key Differences from NIH
- Stronger emphasis on healthcare delivery, not biomedical mechanisms
- Priority populations integration is mandatory
- Stakeholder engagement often expected
- Implementation/dissemination plans valued
- Cost-effectiveness sometimes evaluated
