# Luukkonen 2012: Conservatism and Risk-Taking in ERC Peer Review

Interview-based study of ERC panel members and chairs analyzing the practices that shape selection of "groundbreaking" frontier research. Examines whether the ERC's mission to fund high-risk/high-reward science is operationally achievable through peer review.

## ERC's Stated Single Criterion

**Scientific excellence** — applied to:
1. **Ground-breaking nature** of the research idea
2. **Ambition** of the proposed work
3. **Feasibility** within the timeframe
4. **Intellectual capacity** of the PI
5. **Creativity** of the PI
6. **Commitment** of the PI

## Operational Sub-Criteria Panelists Actually Apply (per Luukkonen's interviews)

1. **Track record of past output** — heavily weighted, even though formally not the central criterion.
2. **Clarity of hypotheses or research questions** — proposals with sharp, well-formed questions are favored.
3. **Detailed methodological plan** — even though "ground-breaking" might require open-ended methods.
4. **Preliminary data** showing the approach is viable.
5. **CV-based assessment of PI's prior excellence**.
6. **Proposal coherence and writing quality**.

## The Conservatism Problem (the anti-ground-breaking pattern)

Luukkonen documents that despite ERC's mission, the panel-based peer review process systematically constrains funding of truly innovative research because:

1. **High-risk proposals have less developed methodologies** by definition — and panels penalize this.
2. **Truly novel proposals lack preliminary data** — and panels reward preliminary data.
3. **Panel consensus** drives toward proposals all members can endorse — which means lowest-common-denominator novelty.
4. **Reviewers' own paradigm bias** disadvantages paradigm-crossing work.
5. **Two-stage process** (panel + external review) compounds conservative selection at each stage.
6. **Interview stage** (for top candidates) rewards confident communicators over original thinkers.

## Reform Implications

For a funding agency that wants to support genuinely high-risk science, the rubric must:
1. Explicitly allow / require some funding of proposals where panel disagreement is high.
2. Weight novelty / risk separately from feasibility / track record.
3. Consider lottery elements among "high-novelty" proposals where panel consensus is impossible.
4. Audit funded portfolio for paradigm-crossing and disciplinary distance to prior work.

## Implications for Rubric Design

The Luukkonen study is the most important empirical analysis of how "excellence" rubrics in practice penalize precisely the work they claim to seek (groundbreaking research). Any rubric using "excellence" as its sole criterion will tend toward conservatism unless deliberately counteracted.
