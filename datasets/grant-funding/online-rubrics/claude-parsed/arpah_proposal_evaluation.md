# ARPA-H - Proposal Evaluation Process and Criteria

## General Review Approach
ARPA-H conducts impartial, equitable, comprehensive proposal reviews based on the evaluation criteria listed in each solicitation, then selects the performers whose proposed solution meets ARPA-H's technical, policy, and programmatic goals.

## Three Core Evaluation Criteria
ARPA-H undertakes a merit-based assessment, evaluating proposals on:

### 1. Technical Merit
- Innovation and scientific soundness
- Likelihood of achieving stated technical goals
- Quality of methodology

### 2. Potential Health Impact
- Magnitude of potential public health benefit
- Path to deployment and adoption
- Equitable distribution of benefits

### 3. Cost-Effectiveness
- Realism of proposed budget
- Cost-benefit analysis
- Efficient use of resources

## Solicitation-Specific Criteria
Submissions are evaluated against the criteria outlined in the respective solicitations. Each solicitation will define specific evaluation criteria. Important to read carefully as criteria vary.

## Review Personnel
Submissions are evaluated by:
- ARPA-H Program Managers
- Qualified personnel from other government organizations and agencies
- Subject matter experts proficient in the pertinent research areas of the solicitation

## Heilmeier Catechism
For ARPA-H initiatives, the "Heilmeier Catechism" or "Heilmeier Questions" help agency officials evaluate proposed research projects. These questions are central to ARPA-H's approach and foundational to engaging with Program Managers:

1. What are you trying to do? Articulate your objectives using absolutely no jargon.
2. How is it done today, and what are the limits of current practice?
3. What is new in your approach and why do you think it will be successful?
4. Who cares? If you are successful, what difference will it make?
5. What are the risks?
6. How much will it cost?
7. How long will it take?
8. What are the mid-term and final "exams" to check for success?
