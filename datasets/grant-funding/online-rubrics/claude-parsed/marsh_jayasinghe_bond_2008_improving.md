# Marsh, Jayasinghe & Bond 2008: Improving Peer Review for Grants

Analysis of thousands of ARC grant proposal reviews using multilevel cross-classified models. Both diagnoses problems and proposes a "reader system" reform.

## Four Psychometric Criteria Applied to Grant Review

1. **Reliability** — agreement among reviewers (inter-rater reliability).
2. **Validity** — does the score predict subsequent research outcomes?
3. **Bias** — systematic favoring or disfavoring of identifiable applicant groups.
4. **Generalizability** — do scores transfer across panels, fields, years?

## Bias Variables Tested in the ARC Data

- Applicant **age**
- Applicant **gender**
- Applicant **academic rank** (lecturer/senior/professor/etc.)
- Applicant **university** prestige
- Applicant **nationality / English-as-first-language status**
- Applicant **research team composition** (size, gender mix, junior/senior)
- Reviewer **experience** as a reviewer
- Reviewer-applicant **field congruence**
- Whether the reviewer was **applicant-nominated** vs panel-selected

## Key Empirical Findings

- **Inter-rater reliability is low** in standard peer review (consistent with Cole et al. 1981).
- **The strongest systematic bias** identified: applicant-nominated reviewers give inflated, less reliable, less valid ratings. (Practical implication: stop allowing applicant-nominated reviewers.)
- Other bias variables (age, gender, rank, etc.) showed smaller and more inconsistent effects.

## The "Reader System" Reform (proposed alternative rubric structure)

Rather than 2-3 anonymous external reviewers per proposal, a small panel of "readers" each reads ALL proposals in a domain and ranks them comparatively. Empirically:
- Substantially higher inter-rater reliability than traditional reviews.
- Eliminates the inflation problem from applicant-nominated reviewers.
- Forces direct comparison rather than absolute scoring.

## Implications for Rubric Design

- Absolute-rating rubrics ("score 1-9") are inherently noisy.
- Comparative / forced-ranking rubrics produce more reliable signals.
- Applicant-nominated reviewers should be excluded or down-weighted.
- Multi-level statistical models should be applied to detect bias retroactively.
