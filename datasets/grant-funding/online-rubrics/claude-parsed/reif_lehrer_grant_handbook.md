# Reif-Lehrer - Grant Application Writer's Handbook

## About the Author
Dr. Liane Reif-Lehrer supported her research almost entirely with grant funds from 1964 until 1985, obtained primarily from the National Institutes of Health, which informed her expertise on the subject.

## Book Editions
- 1st Edition: 1995
- 4th Edition: addresses the key points in developing grant proposals in a hands-on, step-by-step method
- 472 pages, published by Jones and Bartlett Publishers

## Core Framework

### Pre-Writing Phase
1. **Research the funding agency**
   - Mission alignment
   - Past funded grants
   - Key program officers
2. **Idea development**
   - Brainstorming
   - Literature analysis
   - Conceptual framework
3. **Forming the team**
   - Co-investigators
   - Consultants
   - Mentors

### Writing Phase

#### The Specific Aims (Foundation)
- 2-4 hypothesis-driven aims
- Independent but related
- Achievable in funding period
- Clear endpoints

#### Background and Significance
- Why the problem matters
- What is known and unknown
- How your work fills gaps

#### Preliminary Studies
- Demonstrates feasibility
- Establishes credibility
- Justifies hypotheses

#### Research Design and Methods
- Detailed methodology for each aim
- Statistical approaches
- Alternative strategies
- Timeline

#### Project Personnel
- PI qualifications
- Key personnel roles
- Time commitments

#### Budget and Justification
- Realistic and detailed
- Tied to specific aims
- Justified line by line

### Post-Writing Phase
- Internal review
- Mock review
- Final formatting
- Submission strategy

## Reif-Lehrer's Key Principles

### Communication First
- Write for the reviewer, not yourself
- Use plain language where possible
- Define jargon
- Provide visual aids (figures, tables)

### Show, Don't Just Tell
- Use preliminary data prominently
- Reference your own and others' published work
- Use specific examples

### Address Reviewer Concerns
- Anticipate questions
- Address weaknesses head-on
- Offer alternative strategies

### Time Management
- Start months in advance
- Allow time for multiple drafts
- Get external feedback
- Don't submit at the last minute

## Common Pitfalls Identified
- Diffuse focus / too many aims
- Inadequate preliminary data
- Unrealistic budget
- Failure to follow agency guidelines
- Poor visual presentation
- Ignoring reviewer instructions
- Last-minute submission errors

## Notable Tone
- Pragmatic and direct
- Based on personal grant-writing experience
- Acknowledges politics of grant funding
- Encourages persistence and resilience
