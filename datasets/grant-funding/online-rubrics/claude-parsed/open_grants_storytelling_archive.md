# Open Grants — Public Application Archive Themes

Open Grants (ogrants.org) is a community archive of funded and unfunded grant applications across funders. Themes aggregated from successful applications and accompanying author commentaries.

## What Successful Applications Share

### Specific Aims / Project Summary Conventions
- Open with the question or knowledge gap
- 1-2 sentence "elevator pitch" before details
- Name the central hypothesis explicitly
- 2-4 numbered aims with clear logic
- Closing impact statement

### Investigator Framing
- Career-stage-appropriate scope
- Productivity proportionate to grant size
- Independence narrative for early-career
- Track record narrative for mid/senior
- Specific named collaborators with roles

### Methodology Conventions
- Cite published protocols
- Justify your approach over alternatives
- Power calculations with effect-size citations
- Statistical analysis plan with multiple-comparison correction
- Replicates: biological + technical specified
- Authentication/validation of key reagents

### Visual Conventions
- Conceptual figure in Significance / Background
- Quantitative preliminary data figures
- Timeline figure (Gantt-style)
- Schematic of overall research strategy

## Patterns from Author Commentaries

### Reuse Across Applications
- Personal Statement / biosketch sections recur
- Methodology blocks recur across related projects
- Investigator narratives persist with updates
- Significance framing adapts to funder priorities

### What Authors Wish They Knew
- Start 6+ months earlier
- Get program officer feedback at idea stage
- Find mock-review committee
- Read funded applications in your area before drafting
- Iterate Specific Aims 10+ times before moving on

### Common Author Reflections on Failures
- "Too ambitious for ESI / first R01"
- "Co-dependent aims sank me"
- "Methodology too vague to evaluate"
- "Didn't address rigor explicitly"
- "Reviewer outside subfield missed the point"

## Funder-Specific Themes from Archive

### NIH
- Specific Aims page is the most-iterated document
- Approach section is where most points are lost
- Resubmission is the norm, not exception
- Rigor and reproducibility language mandatory post-2016

### NSF
- Project Summary read by every panelist
- Intellectual Merit + Broader Impacts equal weight
- BI must be specific with named activities
- Project Description is short — every paragraph justifies itself

### NSF GRFP
- Personal Statement carries 60%+ weight
- Research Plan demonstrates ability, not commitment
- Reviewers are generalists; write accessibly
- Letters of recommendation matter

### Foundations
- Read foundation's annual report before applying
- Match recent funded portfolio
- Program officers are accessible
- Smaller foundations less competitive but smaller awards

## Implicit Rubric From Archive

### What Funded Applications Demonstrate
1. Clear question with significance
2. Hypothesis-driven approach
3. Independent aims (no failure cascade)
4. Quantitative preliminary data
5. Methodology specific enough to evaluate
6. Statistical analysis plan
7. Investigator-environment match
8. Realistic budget and timeline
9. Career-stage-appropriate scope
10. Funder-priority alignment

### What Unfunded Applications Often Show
1. Vague significance ("important")
2. Descriptive rather than hypothesis-driven aims
3. Co-dependent aims
4. Insufficient or qualitative preliminary data
5. Vague methodology
6. Missing power calculation
7. Investigator overreach for career stage
8. Padded budget
9. Unrealistic timeline
10. Mismatch with funder priorities

## On Sharing Applications

### Open Grants Norms
- Anonymized funded apps preferred
- Author commentary on what worked / didn't
- Reviewers' summary statements where shareable
- Timeline of submission to funding
- Resubmission history

### Why Sharing Matters
- Demystifies the genre for new applicants
- Reduces inequality in access to mentorship
- Provides examples for under-resourced institutions
- Supports replication of successful structures
