# NSERC Discovery Grants — Merit Indicators & Quality Bins (Wave 2 supplement)

Wave 1 has a brief NSERC entry; this Wave 2 file gives the explicit "merit indicators" and quality-bin matrix used by NSERC reviewers.

## Three Equally-Weighted Criteria (each rated independently)

1. **Excellence of the Researcher**
2. **Merit of the Proposal**
3. **Contributions to the Training of Highly Qualified Personnel (HQP)**

Each criterion is rated on a 6-level descriptor scale before combining into a quality bin.

## Six-Level Descriptor Scale (per criterion)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| **Exceptional** | E — top of category |
| **Outstanding** | O |
| **Very Strong** | VS |
| **Strong** | S |
| **Moderate** | M |
| **Insufficient** | I |

Critically: an "Insufficient" rating in ANY criterion means the application is NOT funded — regardless of the other criteria.

## Quality Bin Matrix (the funding allocation rubric)

The three criterion ratings combine into a quality bin (A through J), which determines funding amount:

- **Bin A** — All three criteria rated Exceptional → highest funding
- **Bin B** — Two Exceptional + one Outstanding
- **Bin C** — One Exceptional + two Outstanding (or 2 Exc + 1 VS)
- ...descending bins...
- **Bin J** — Three Strong ratings → minimum fundable level
- **Below Bin J** — Not funded

## Detailed Sub-Criteria

### Excellence of the Researcher (assessed over past 6 years)
1. **Quality and impact of contributions to research**
   - Publications (quality > quantity)
   - Patents, software, technical reports
   - Other forms of research output
2. **Stature in the field**
   - Invited talks
   - Editorial roles
   - Leadership positions
3. **Knowledge of the field**
   - Up-to-date with state of the art
   - Awareness of competing approaches
4. **Demonstrated expertise** in the proposed research area

Applicants list up to 5 most significant research contributions in the last 6 years and explain their influence.

### Merit of the Proposal
1. **Originality and innovation** of the proposed research
2. **Significance** of the expected contributions
3. **Clarity and scope** of the objectives
4. **Feasibility** of the approach
5. **Quality of the training plan** for HQP integrated into the research

### Contributions to HQP Training (three components)
1. **Training environment** — quality of the laboratory, mentoring, infrastructure
2. **HQP awards and research contributions** — students' awards, publications, fellowships received
3. **Outcomes and skills gained by HQP** — career trajectories, transferable skills, employability

## Notable Features of NSERC's Rubric

1. **Equal weighting** of all three criteria (not researcher-dominated like ERC, not proposal-dominated like NIH).
2. **Hard veto rule**: insufficient in any criterion = no funding.
3. **HQP training is a primary criterion**, not a side consideration — this is unusual internationally.
4. **6-year window** for past contributions standardizes career-stage evaluation.
5. **Bin-based funding allocation** rather than continuous score → funded grants get one of ~10 funding levels.

## Implications for Rubric Design

NSERC's rubric is unusually transparent about how individual criterion ratings combine into funding decisions (the bin matrix). The hard-veto rule on any single criterion creates strong asymmetry: a great proposal with poor HQP training plan cannot be funded, regardless of how good the science is.
