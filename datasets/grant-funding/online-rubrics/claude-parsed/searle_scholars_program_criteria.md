# Searle Scholars Program — Selection Criteria

Awards $300K over 3 years to ~15 exceptional young biomedical/chemistry assistant professors per year. Explicitly oriented toward "high risk, high reward" research.

## Single Primary Criterion

**Potential of applicants to make innovative and high-impact contributions to research over an extended period of time.**

This is operationalized through assessment of both proposal and applicant.

## Stated Programmatic Bias

The program EXPLICITLY supports **high risk, high reward research** — this differentiates it from agencies that favor incrementalism.

## Eligibility (threshold rubric)

- **Independent investigator at assistant professor level**
- **First tenure-track position** (or nearest equivalent)
- **Appointment must have begun on or after May 1** of two years before competition
- **U.S. institution** required
- One nomination per institution (limited submission)

## Eligible Research Areas

- Biochemistry
- Cell biology
- Genetics
- Immunology
- Neuroscience
- Pharmacology
- Related areas in chemistry, medicine, biological sciences

## Two-Stage Evaluation Process

### Stage 1: Application Pool Reduction
- ~150-200 applications received per year (one per institution among ~140 invited institutions)
- Reduced to **~40 finalists** by initial review

### Stage 2: Scientific Advisory Board Evaluation
- Two-day meeting of the Scientific Advisory Board
- Board composed of distinguished scientists, many former Searle Scholars
- Final selection of ~15 awardees per year
- ~10% overall acceptance rate

## Implicit Criteria (per program guidance)

1. **Innovation** — non-incremental research direction
2. **Risk tolerance** — proposals that might fail interestingly
3. **Independent vision** distinct from PhD/postdoc training
4. **Career trajectory promise**
5. **Quality of the science** at the proposal level
6. **Institutional environment** appropriate to support the work

## Award Structure

- **$300,000 total** over 3 years (~$100K/year)
- **Unrestricted use** within the lab
- No detailed accounting requirements

## Implications for Rubric Design

Searle's rubric is one of the few that EXPLICITLY names "high risk, high reward" as a positive criterion — most other rubrics implicitly punish risk through demands for preliminary data, detailed methods, and feasibility evidence. Searle's two-stage in-person SAB review allows holistic, qualitative judgment that escapes quantitative rubric noise.
