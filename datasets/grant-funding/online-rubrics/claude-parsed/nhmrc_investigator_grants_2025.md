# NHMRC (Australia) Investigator Grants — Peer Review Rubric

The NHMRC Investigator Grant scheme (Australia's largest medical research funding program) uses a structured rubric with explicit weights — unusual among international funders.

## Three Top-Level Assessment Elements (with weights)

| Element | Weight |
|---|---|
| **Knowledge Gain** (the proposed research) | 30% |
| **Track Record relative to opportunity** | 50% |
| **Research Impact** | 20% |

## Element 1: Knowledge Gain (30%)

Quality of proposed research and significance of knowledge to be gained.

Sub-components evaluated:
- **Theoretical concepts and hypotheses** — clarity, novelty, soundness
- **Research design** — appropriateness, rigor
- **Robustness** of methodology
- **Contribution to research area** and health outcomes
- **Innovation** in approach

## Element 2: Track Record Relative to Opportunity (50%)

Three sub-components:

### Publications (35% of total)
- Quality (not quantity) of selected publications
- Citations relative to field benchmarks
- Authorship position considerations
- Self-identified five most significant publications evaluated in depth

### Other Research Outputs (10% of total)
- Patents, software, datasets, clinical guidelines
- Tools and resources made available to community
- Code repositories, data shares

### Other Achievements (5% of total)
- Awards and prizes
- Editorial/leadership roles
- Mentoring and training contributions
- Translation of research into practice

## "Relative to Opportunity" Provision (the equity rubric)

A distinctive feature: applicants' track records are evaluated relative to the opportunities available to them, accounting for:
- **Career interruptions** (parental leave, caring duties, illness)
- **Career stage** at application
- **Geographic factors** (working in lower-resource environments)
- **Discipline norms** (clinical work norms differ from basic science)
- **Equity factors** (Aboriginal/Torres Strait Islander identity, etc.)

## Element 3: Research Impact (20%)

Two sub-criteria (revised from 3 in older versions):

### Reach and Significance (10%)
- Geographic reach (local, national, international)
- Sectoral reach (academia, policy, clinical, industry, community)
- Magnitude of influence demonstrated

### Applicant's Contribution to Impact (10%)
- Direct attribution of impact to the applicant's work
- Role in translation pathway
- Documented evidence of contribution

## Score Descriptors (the rubric scale)

7-point scale:
- **7** — Outstanding, very rare
- **6** — Excellent
- **5** — Very good
- **4** — Good
- **3** — Adequate
- **2** — Limited
- **1** — Poor

Each score descriptor is explicitly defined for each criterion.

## Performance Indicators (Appendix B)

The 2024+ guidelines include performance indicators "above" the score descriptors for three broad elements:
1. Quality of the proposed research
2. Potential for impact
3. Demonstrated capability of the applicant

## Implications for Rubric Design

NHMRC is the most explicit major funder about both:
1. **Numerical criterion weights** (30/50/20 split)
2. **"Relative to opportunity" adjustment** — formal accommodation for career interruptions
3. **Sub-criterion weights within each main criterion**

The detailed weighting and equity provision contrast with NIH's holistic Overall Impact approach.
