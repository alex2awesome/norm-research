# Eric Topol — Ground Truths Substack on Biomedical Funding

Eric Topol (Scripps, cardiologist, prolific writer) writes Ground Truths covering NIH funding, biomedical innovation, and research priorities. Implicit rubric for what makes biomedical research worth funding.

## Recurring Themes on Funding Priorities

### Translational Impact Over Mechanism
- Research that affects clinical practice within 5-10 years valued
- Basic science valued when path to clinical relevance is clear
- "Bench to bedside" remains the gold standard framing
- AI/ML in medicine specifically prioritized

### AI in Biomedical Research
- AI for diagnosis, drug discovery, multi-omic analysis prioritized
- Foundation models in medicine emerging area
- Reproducibility concerns particularly acute in AI/ML
- Preregistration and open data critical

### Genomics and Precision Medicine
- Population-scale genomics studies
- Multi-ancestry inclusion (addressing past bias)
- Functional follow-up of GWAS hits
- Clinical implementation of genomics

### Patient-Centered Research
- Patient-reported outcomes
- Real-world evidence
- Decentralized clinical trials
- Patient partnership in research design

## Topol's Critique Themes (Implicit Negative Rubric)

### What Topol Critiques in Funded Research
- Incremental work over disease-modifying targets
- Industry-funded research with COIs
- Lack of replication studies
- Single-ancestry genomics
- Black-box AI without interpretability
- Studies that don't pre-register

### Reform Themes
- Open science as default
- Preregistration mandatory
- Code and data sharing
- Slower, more careful peer review
- Smaller teams over mega-consortia

## Implicit Rubric Distilled

### What Topol-Style Reviewers Want
1. Clear translational path or major basic-science breakthrough potential
2. Multi-ancestry and diverse population inclusion
3. Pre-registered analysis plans
4. Open data and code sharing
5. AI/ML applications with interpretability
6. Patient-centered outcomes where relevant
7. Reproducibility infrastructure built in
8. Honest acknowledgment of limitations
9. Conservative effect size estimates
10. Replication-oriented design

### What Topol-Style Reviewers Penalize
1. Incremental mechanism work without translational vision
2. Single-ancestry genomics
3. Post-hoc analysis without pre-registration
4. AI/ML without interpretability or external validation
5. Industry COIs without disclosure
6. Mega-consortia with diffuse accountability
7. Hype language exceeding evidence
8. Studies designed not to replicate

## Notable Topol Themes Repeated

### On NIH Reform
- Reduce burden on applicants
- Address racial gap in funding
- Reduce concentration in top institutions
- Pay attention to clinical impact, not just citation counts

### On Preprints and Open Science
- Preprints expected for high-stakes findings
- Code on GitHub, data on appropriate repositories
- Preregistration on OSF, ClinicalTrials.gov
- Open peer review where possible

### On Industry-Academia Boundaries
- Disclose all COIs prominently
- Industry funding is legitimate but must be transparent
- Editorial firewalls in publication
- Independent replication

## What Applicants Can Take From Topol's Voice

### Frame Your Research For Translational Audience
- Lead with what your work means for patients/society
- Cite clinical implications even for basic work
- Acknowledge limitations honestly
- Pre-specify analysis plans
- Plan for open sharing

### Use Modern Methodological Standards
- Power calculations with conservative effect sizes
- Pre-registration on OSF or equivalent
- Code in GitHub from project start
- Diverse cohort recruitment plan
- AI/ML with proper validation framework

### Avoid Red Flags
- Hype language ("revolutionary", "paradigm-shifting" without justification)
- Single-population studies without justification
- Post-hoc subgroup analyses
- Black-box ML claims
- Mega-consortium structure without clear PI accountability
