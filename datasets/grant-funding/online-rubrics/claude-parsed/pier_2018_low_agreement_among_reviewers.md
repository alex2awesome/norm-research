# Pier et al. (2018) - Low Agreement Among Reviewers Evaluating NIH Grant Applications

## Citation
Pier, E. L., Brauer, M., Filut, A., Kaatz, A., Raclaw, J., Nathan, M. J., Ford, C. E., & Carnes, M. (2018). Low agreement among reviewers evaluating the same NIH grant applications. PNAS, 115(12), 2952-2957.

## Study Overview
The study examined 43 individual reviewers' ratings and written critiques of the same group of 25 NIH grant applications, published in Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences in 2018.

## Key Findings

### No Agreement on Quality
Results showed **no agreement** among reviewers regarding the quality of the applications in either:
- Their qualitative evaluations (written critiques)
- Their quantitative evaluations (numeric scores)

### No Agreement on Score Translation
Although all reviewers received the same instructions on how to rate applications and format their written critiques, the study also found **no agreement in how reviewers "translated" a given number of strengths and weaknesses into a numeric rating**.

### Outcome Depends on Reviewer
The outcome of the grant review appeared to depend more on the **reviewer to whom the grant was assigned** than on the research proposed in the grant.

## Implications for Grant Funding
- Highlights the subjectivity in reviewers' evaluations of grant applications
- Underscores the difficulty in comparing the evaluations of different applications from different reviewers
- Has become influential in discussions about the reliability and fairness of the NIH grant peer review system
- Has motivated some calls for partial lottery systems (modified at the funding line)

## Methodological Detail
- Reviewers each evaluated 4-6 R01 applications from a common set
- Same instructions, same evaluation form
- Inter-rater reliability was effectively zero (ICC near 0)
- Even number of strengths vs weaknesses noted by reviewers did not predict score consistently

## Related Critiques
- Score calibration talk during panel meetings can amplify (rather than reduce) variability
- Pre-meeting scores often diverge significantly across initial reviewers
- "Score creep" and "anchoring" effects observed in panel discussions

## Implications for Bias
The Pier et al. study supports concerns raised in:
- Ginther et al. (2011, 2018) on race disparities in NIH funding
- Hoppe et al. (2019) on topic choice and demographic disparities
- Witteman et al. (2019) on gender disparities in CIHR grant peer review
