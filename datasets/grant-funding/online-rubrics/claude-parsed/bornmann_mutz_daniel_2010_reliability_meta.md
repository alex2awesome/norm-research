# Bornmann, Mutz & Daniel 2010: Meta-Analysis of Inter-Rater Reliability

First systematic meta-analysis of inter-rater reliability (IRR) across peer review studies. Although the title says "journal peer reviews," many included studies cover grant peer review.

## Methodology

- 70 reliability coefficients from 48 independent studies
- Total of 19,443 manuscripts analyzed
- Average sample size: 311 manuscripts per study
- Mixed of Cohen's Kappa, ICC, and Pearson r

## Reliability Effect Sizes

Three reliability statistics computed:
- **Mean ICC / r²**: 0.34 (only 1/3 of variance explained by raters)
- **Mean Cohen's Kappa**: 0.17 (slight agreement)
- **Pearson r mean**: 0.48 (moderate)

These represent the **upper bounds** of inter-reviewer agreement empirically observed across 48 studies of peer review.

## Determinants of Reliability (the moderator rubric)

Multilevel meta-regression identified factors that increase or decrease IRR:

### Factors Increasing Reliability
1. **Larger numbers of reviewers per item** (averaging more reviewers reduces noise)
2. **More structured / specific criteria** vs. holistic global judgments
3. **Trained reviewers** vs. untrained ad hoc reviewers
4. **Same-discipline reviewers** vs. cross-disciplinary
5. **Lower-stakes decisions** (publication acceptance) vs. higher-stakes (grant funding)

### Factors Decreasing Reliability
1. **Highly competitive contexts** (low acceptance rates)
2. **High novelty / interdisciplinary applications**
3. **Subjective rather than operationalized criteria**
4. **Single reviewer per item**
5. **Holistic single-score evaluations** vs. multi-criterion structured evaluation

## Implications for the Reliability Ceiling

The meta-analysis establishes that:
1. **Even in best cases, peer review IRR is modest** (~0.4 ICC).
2. **No rubric design has achieved reliability comparable to** standardized testing (>0.8) or even structured personnel selection (>0.6).
3. **The ceiling is partly structural** — disagreement reflects genuine epistemic uncertainty, not fixable noise.

## Implications for Rubric Design

A well-designed grant rubric can push IRR from ~0.2 (typical) to perhaps ~0.4 (best case) by:
1. Using multiple reviewers and averaging
2. Operationalizing each criterion
3. Training reviewers on rubric application
4. Restricting reviewer pool to same-discipline experts
5. Using multi-criterion structured rather than holistic single-score

But fundamental ceiling around 0.4-0.5 means **fine-grained ranking is not statistically defensible** in most peer-review contexts.

## Connection to Other Studies

- Cole et al. 1981 ICC ~0.25 — within meta-analysis range
- Pier et al. 2018 IRR ~0 — at the floor of the range
- Reinhart 2009 ICC ~0.41 — at the upper end
- Fogelholm et al. 2012 κ ~0.23 — middle of range
