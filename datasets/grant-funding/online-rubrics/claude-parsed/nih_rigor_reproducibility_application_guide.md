# NIH Rigor and Reproducibility - Application Guidance

## Overview
Scientific rigor is the strict application of the scientific method to ensure unbiased and well-controlled experimental design, methodology, analysis, interpretation and reporting of results.

## How Rigor Is Scored
- Scientific rigor contributes to the **Approach** criterion for research project grant applications
- Contributes to the **Research Plan** criterion for mentored career development award applications
- Contributes to the **overall impact score**

In the Simplified Peer Review Framework (2025+), rigor is part of **Factor 2: Rigor and Feasibility**.

## Four Required Elements

### 1. Rigor of the Prior Research (Significance/Approach)
- Strengths and weaknesses of the published research and/or preliminary data
- Critical evaluation of prior work, not just description
- Support for proposed research
- Where science is contested or unsettled

Reviewers ask: "Is the scientific premise sound?"

### 2. Scientific Rigor (Approach)
The strict application of the scientific method:
- Robust experimental design
- Adequate statistical power
- Sample size justification
- Pre-specified analysis plan
- Methods to minimize bias (blinding, randomization)
- Inclusion of appropriate controls
- Plans for testing reproducibility
- Independent validation strategies

Reviewers ask: "Is the design rigorous?"

### 3. Consideration of Relevant Biological Variables (Approach)
- **Sex as a biological variable** (SABV) — required since 2016
- **Age** considerations
- **Race/ethnicity** when relevant to scientific question
- **Weight, body composition** as appropriate
- **Genetic background** in animal models
- Justification if studying single sex or limited demographics

Reviewers ask: "Are biological variables addressed?"

### 4. Authentication of Key Biological and/or Chemical Resources (Authentication Plan)
Submitted as a separate attachment:
- Cell lines (verify identity, mycoplasma testing)
- Antibodies (validation methods)
- Other key biological resources (e.g., transgenic animals)
- Custom-made or sourced from non-validated suppliers

Reviewers ask: "Are resources authenticated?"

## Specific Concerns by Field

### Animal Studies
- Number of animals justified by power calculation
- Both sexes used unless justification provided
- Randomization to experimental groups
- Blinded outcome assessment
- Pre-registered analysis where appropriate

### Cell Culture
- Cell line authentication (STR profiling)
- Mycoplasma testing
- Multiple independent biological replicates
- Use of multiple cell lines where relevant

### Human Studies
- Power calculation
- Recruitment plan to enroll diverse population
- Plan for handling missing data
- Blinding where feasible
- Pre-specified outcomes
- Statistical analysis plan

## Common Approach Pitfalls Related to Rigor
- Inadequate sample size justification
- Single-replicate experiments
- Cherry-picked data
- Underpowered studies
- Lack of negative/positive controls
- No randomization mentioned
- No blinding of analyses
- No mention of how variability will be handled
