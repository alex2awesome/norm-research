# NIH Study Section - Reviewer Process and Scoring

## Pre-Meeting Activities

### Application Assignment
- Each application assigned to 3-4 reviewers (1 primary, 2 secondary, 1 reader)
- Assignment based on expertise alignment
- Reviewers review for conflicts of interest

### Preliminary Scoring
Before the meeting:
- Assigned reviewers will read each application thoroughly
- Write a critique summarizing strengths and weaknesses
- Assign preliminary Overall Impact score (1-9)
- Submit critiques in advance

### Streamlining
In many review meetings, the preliminary scores are used to determine which applications will be discussed in full at the meeting:
- Approximately bottom 50% (in some sections, bottom 30-40%) are "streamlined" (Not Discussed)
- These applications still receive written critiques but no overall impact score
- Streamlining decisions made by reviewers, not by the Scientific Review Officer

## Discussion Order

### Randomized Order
- Review order for the discussed applications is **random**
- Reviewers discuss applications in randomized order
- NOT in the order of preliminary overall impact scores
- This better protects the integrity of the review process
- Prevents anchoring effects from one extreme score

## Discussion Process for Each Application

1. **Conflict announcements**: Reviewers with COI leave the room (or virtual breakout)
2. **Primary reviewer presents**: ~3-5 minute summary of application strengths/weaknesses
3. **Secondary reviewer(s) add**: Different perspective, agreements/disagreements
4. **Reader summarizes**: Reader was assigned to read but not score; provides additional perspective
5. **Open discussion**: All eligible panelists can comment
6. **Score range discussion**: Primary reviewer states score range; others may revise
7. **Final scoring**: All eligible panelists score privately

## Final Scoring Calculation

For each discussed application:
- A final Overall Impact score is given by each eligible panelist (no COI)
- Final Overall Impact score = mean of all eligible scores × 10
- Final Overall Impact scores range from 10 (high impact) through 90 (low impact)
- Percentile rank assigned (varies by study section)

## Components of Reviewer Critiques

Each critique includes:
- **Significance**: Strengths and weaknesses
- **Investigator(s)**: Strengths and weaknesses
- **Innovation**: Strengths and weaknesses
- **Approach**: Strengths and weaknesses
- **Environment**: Strengths and weaknesses
- **Overall Impact**: Bottom-line assessment

Plus:
- Protection of Human Subjects (where applicable)
- Vertebrate Animals (where applicable)
- Resource Sharing Plans
- Authentication of Key Biological Resources

## Reviewer Behaviors That Influence Outcomes

### Chalk Talks vs. Written Critiques
- Spoken discussions in panels can shift scores significantly
- Strong primary reviewer advocacy can rescue borderline grants
- Negative comments often resonate more than positive

### Score Calibration
- Pre-meeting scores serve as anchor
- Discussion can move scores significantly
- "Score creep" toward the middle observed
- Some panels more divergent than others

### Cognitive Biases
- Status quo bias (favors mainstream over innovative)
- Confirmation bias
- Reviewer fatigue (later applications may be reviewed less thoroughly)
- Order effects (despite randomization)

## NIH Center for Scientific Review (CSR)
- Manages 80%+ of NIH peer review
- Convenes ~250 standing study sections plus special emphasis panels
- Reviews ~80,000 applications annually
- Recruits and trains ~17,000 reviewers per year

## After Review
- Summary statements typically released 4-8 weeks post-meeting
- Council review (second-level) ~1-3 months after panel meeting
- Funding decisions typically 8-12 months from submission
