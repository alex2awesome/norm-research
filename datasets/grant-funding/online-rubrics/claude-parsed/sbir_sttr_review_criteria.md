# SBIR/STTR - Review Criteria

## Universal Review Criteria
Following submission of proposals, agencies make awards based on:
- Small business qualification
- Degree of innovation
- Technical merit
- Future market potential
- Commercial potential

## Phase I Review Focus
Phase I awards determine the scientific and technical merit and feasibility of ideas that appear to have commercial potential.
- Concept phase lasting 6 to 12 months
- Supports exploration of technical merit or feasibility of an idea or technology
- Typical award $50K-$300K depending on agency

### Phase I Evaluation Criteria
1. **The Innovation:** Quality of the proposed innovation
2. **Technical Approach:** Methods and feasibility
3. **Commercial Potential:** Market opportunity and competitive landscape
4. **Team:** Qualifications, expertise, infrastructure
5. **Customer Discovery:** Evidence of market need

## Phase II Review Focus
Phase II awards further develop work from Phase I that meets particular program needs and exhibits potential for commercial application.

The results achieved in Phase I, the scientific and technical merit, and the commercial potential determine whether a Phase II application is funded.

### Phase II Specific Requirements
- A succinct commercialization plan must be included with each Phase II proposal
- At NIH, the commercialization plan is weighted as heavily as the research plan
- At NSF, the commercialization plan is weighted even more prominently
- Typical award $750K-$2M depending on agency

## Phase III
- No SBIR funding (intended to use non-SBIR funds to commercialize)
- Government may issue contracts for products/services that derive from SBIR work
- Government can sole-source from SBIR Phase III companies

## Agency-Specific Variations
- **NIH**: Health-related innovations, similar to R01 review with commercialization addendum
- **NSF**: Broader STEM focus, heavy weight on customer discovery
- **DOE**: Energy/environmental; topics released annually
- **DOD**: Defense applications; topics released 3x per year
- **NASA**: Aerospace technology; topics aligned with mission directorates

## Differences Between SBIR and STTR
- **SBIR**: Small business performs primary work
- **STTR**: Requires partnership with research institution (≥30% of work performed by partner)
