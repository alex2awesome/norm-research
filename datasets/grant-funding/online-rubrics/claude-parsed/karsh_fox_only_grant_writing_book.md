# The Only Grant-Writing Book You'll Ever Need (Karsh & Fox)

## About the Book
Written by two expert authors who have secured millions of dollars in government and foundation grants. A comprehensive, step-by-step guide for government, nonprofit, and individual grant seekers.

## The 15-Lesson Framework

### Lesson 1: Self-Assessment
**"Who am I? (and what in the world do I want to do?)"**
- Identify your organization's mission, capacity, fit for funders
- Assess readiness for grant funding
- Match grants to organizational priorities

### Lesson 2: Grant Fundamentals
**"Wait a second... what is a grant, and where do I get one?"**
- Different types of grants (project, operating, capital, capacity)
- Funder ecosystem (government, foundation, corporate)
- Grant cycles and deadlines

### Lesson 3: Identifying Funders
- Funder research strategies
- Foundation Center / Candid resources
- Federal grant databases
- Building a prospect list

### Lesson 4: Reading and Decoding RFPs
- Following the funder's exact instructions
- Identifying scoring criteria
- Building a compliance checklist

### Lesson 5: Pre-Proposal Activities
- Letters of inquiry (LOI)
- Concept papers
- Building relationships with program officers

### Lesson 6: Project Design
- Logic models
- Theory of change
- Backward design from outcomes

### Lesson 7: Need Statement
**"Identifying and documenting the need: what problem will a grant fix?"**
- Local data and statistics
- National context
- Affected population characterization
- Why now / urgency
- Avoiding deficit framing

### Lesson 8: Goals and Objectives
**"What do you hope to achieve if you get the money?"**
- Goal: broad aspirational statement
- Objective: SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
- Process objectives vs. outcome objectives

### Lesson 9: Methods/Activities
- Detailed program description
- Staffing and roles
- Timeline (Gantt charts often helpful)
- Implementation steps

### Lesson 10: Theory of Change / Logic Model
- Inputs → Activities → Outputs → Outcomes → Impact
- Visual representation of your program
- Connecting evidence to design

### Lesson 11: Evaluation Plan
**"How can you be sure if your program worked?"**
- Process evaluation (implementation fidelity)
- Outcome evaluation (results)
- Internal vs. external evaluator
- Data collection plan
- Reporting timeline

### Lesson 12: Budget and Budget Narrative
**"How much will it cost, and is the cost reasonable?"**
- Direct vs. indirect costs
- Personnel, fringe benefits
- Travel, equipment, supplies
- Subcontracts/consultants
- Other direct costs
- Detailed justification for each line

### Lesson 13: Sustainability
**"How will you continue the program when the grant funds run out?"**
- Diversified funding strategy
- Built-in revenue (fees, subscriptions)
- Institutional commitment
- Reduced cost over time
- Spinoffs / replication

### Lesson 14: Cover Letter and Executive Summary
- The "elevator pitch" of your proposal
- Most important section to many funders
- Should be written last but appears first

### Lesson 15: Submission and Follow-Up
- Following submission instructions exactly
- Confirming receipt
- Responding to clarification requests
- Site visits
- Post-decision follow-up (whether funded or not)

## Key Components of Effective Proposals (Per Karsh & Fox)

### Foundational Elements
- Clear, compelling need
- Strong organizational fit
- Realistic and detailed plan
- Capable team
- Reasonable budget
- Believable sustainability

### Voice and Style
- Professional but human
- Specific not vague
- Confident not boastful
- Active not passive
- Fact-based not anecdotal alone
