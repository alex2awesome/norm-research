# CIHR Project Grant Competition — Detailed Peer Review Rubric (Wave 2 supplement)

Wave 1 has a brief CIHR entry; this Wave 2 file captures the explicit peer review structure used in the Project Grant competitions, including post-Witteman 2019 reforms.

## Two Adjudication Criteria

### Criterion 1: Concept (60% weight)
The quality of the proposed research:
- **Importance** of the research question
- **Originality** of the approach
- **Significance** of expected outcomes
- **Theoretical framework** soundness
- **Use of preliminary data** to support feasibility

### Criterion 2: Feasibility (40% weight)
The likelihood of success:
- **Methods** appropriate to the question
- **Approach** rigor
- **Team** expertise and capacity
- **Resources** sufficient
- **Timeline** realistic
- **Risk mitigation** plan

## Pre-Witteman 2019 Reform Structure

Originally CIHR had two grant programs (split as natural experiment):

### Project Grants (science-focused)
Reviewers asked to focus on:
- Quality of proposed research
- Methodology
- Significance

### Foundation Grants (PI-focused)
Reviewers asked to focus on:
- PI's calibre / track record
- Research vision
- PI productivity

Witteman 2019 showed Foundation Grants had 4× the gender gap of Project Grants. CIHR has subsequently:
- **Discontinued the Foundation Grant program** in current form
- **Consolidated into Project Grant program** with reformed criteria
- **Trained reviewers** on bias mitigation
- **Adopted DORA principles** (signatory)

## DORA-Aligned Reforms at CIHR

Following CIHR's adoption of DORA principles:
1. Reviewers instructed NOT to use Journal Impact Factor as proxy for quality
2. Applicants asked to explain context and significance of selected publications
3. Reviewers evaluate up to 5 most significant publications, not citation totals
4. Diverse research outputs (datasets, software, policy papers) counted as productivity

## Two-Tier Review Structure

### Tier 1: Internal Peer Review
- Each application assigned 4 internal reviewers (members of the Peer Review Committee)
- Reviewers score on Concept and Feasibility
- Pre-meeting individual scoring

### Tier 2: Committee Discussion
- Peer Review Committee meets virtually
- Discusses each application
- Final consensus scores assigned

## Score Scale

- **0-4.9** — does not meet criterion (low quality)
- **3.0-3.5** — competent but limited
- **3.5-4.0** — strong
- **4.0-4.5** — excellent
- **4.5-5.0** — exceptional

A combined score of >=4.0 typically required for funding.

## Implications for Rubric Design

CIHR's evolution illustrates:
1. **Empirical research can drive rubric reform** (Witteman 2019 → discontinuation of PI-focused program)
2. **DORA principles can be operationalized** in funder rubrics (away from JIF)
3. **Two-criterion rubric** (Concept + Feasibility) is more streamlined than NIH's five
4. **Explicit weighting** (60/40) is more transparent than NIH's holistic Overall Impact

CIHR represents one of the most direct cases of academic empirical research (Witteman) leading to operational rubric change at a major funder.
