# Eblen et al. 2016: How Criterion Scores Predict NIH Overall Impact

NIH-internal analysis of >123,700 R01 applications (FY2010-2013), examining how the five individual criterion scores combine to produce the Overall Impact score.

## NIH's Five Criterion Scores Studied

1. **Significance** (1-9)
2. **Investigator(s)** (1-9)
3. **Innovation** (1-9)
4. **Approach** (1-9)
5. **Environment** (1-9)
6. **Overall Impact Score** (1-9, assigned holistically)

## Statistical Analysis

Linear regression and variance decomposition of Overall Impact on the five criterion scores, plus prediction of funding outcomes.

## Key Findings (the criterion-weighting rubric)

### Predictive Strength of Each Criterion

- **Approach** is the strongest predictor of Overall Impact: **r = 0.84**
- **Significance** is the second strongest predictor
- **Innovation, Investigator, Environment** contribute modestly but distinctly

### Aggregate Predictability

- The five scored criteria together account for **78% of variance** in Overall Impact scores
- The remaining 22% reflects either noise or holistic judgment not captured by the five criteria
- **All five criteria matter** — but unequally

### Implications for Funding Likelihood

- Good scores on all five criteria are necessary; not just on Approach
- Order of importance for funding outcome: Approach > Significance > Innovation > Investigator > Environment
- Environment score has weakest contribution (least variance explained)

## Implicit Operational Rubric Revealed

Although the formal rubric weights each criterion equally, in practice reviewers (and the holistic Overall Impact judgment they produce) heavily favor Approach scores. This means:

1. **Methodology / approach is the dominant criterion** in practice.
2. **Innovation criterion has limited bite** — a "highly innovative" but "weak approach" proposal will not be funded.
3. **Investigator and Environment** function more as veto criteria (must be acceptable) than positive selection criteria.
4. **Significance** is necessary but not sufficient — important but methodologically weak proposals fail.

## Implications for Rubric Design

- The discrepancy between formal-rubric weights (equal) and operational weights (Approach-dominated) suggests the rubric is NOT functioning as advertised.
- This may explain the conservatism documented elsewhere — an Approach-dominant rubric inherently favors well-established, low-risk methodology over novel directions.
- The 2025 Simplified Review Framework consolidates Significance + Innovation into Factor 1 and Approach into Factor 2 — explicitly addressing the imbalance Eblen et al. document.

## Connection to Other Findings

- Erosheva 2020 used preliminary criterion scores from Eblen-style analysis methodology to show racial disparities at the criterion level.
- The Eblen et al. variance decomposition is the methodological basis for subsequent disparities research.
