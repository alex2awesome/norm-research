# Sally Rockey's Rock Talk — Archive Themes

Sally Rockey was NIH Deputy Director for Extramural Research before Mike Lauer. Rock Talk (2010-2015) established the genre of NIH leadership blogging on grant policy.

## Key Recurring Topics

### Application Trends
- Total applications per year, success rates by mechanism
- ESI vs. established investigator success rates
- Resubmission policy changes (A2 elimination, etc.)
- Application length (12-page R01 since 2010)

### Demographics and Workforce
- Age distribution of first R01 (rising past 42)
- Diversity of the workforce
- "Lost generation" of postdocs

### Peer Review Operational Data
- Number of reviewers per study section
- Time per application
- Reviewer recruitment challenges

### Specific Policy Posts on What Counts in Review

#### Significance
- "Important problem with broad implications"
- Connection to public health priorities
- Filling a known gap

#### Investigator
- Track record proportionate to career stage
- Independent productivity
- Collaborator strength for novel approaches

#### Innovation
- Novel concepts, approaches, methodologies, tools
- Shift in research/clinical paradigm
- Refinements vs. radical departures both count

#### Approach
- Strategy, methodology, analyses well-reasoned
- Address strengths and weaknesses
- Manage risk through alternatives
- Sex as biological variable (added during her tenure)

#### Environment
- Institutional support
- Resources match the project
- Collaborative arrangements add capability

## Cultural Themes Rockey Promoted
- Transparency about NIH data
- "Demystifying" the review process for applicants
- Making the case for biomedical research funding to the public
- Acknowledging system flaws while defending peer review

## Implicit Rubric Distilled From Rock Talk
1. Hit the five review criteria explicitly
2. Demonstrate awareness of NIH priorities and policy changes
3. Match your career stage to your aims (don't oversell, don't undersell)
4. Use NIH's own language (rigor, reproducibility, transparency, diversity)
5. Show how your project advances public health
6. Be a good citizen — review for NIH, share data, train the next generation

## Notable Rockey Quotes (Paraphrased from Archive)
- "Peer review is not perfect, but it remains the best system we have"
- "Scientific rigor begins with the application"
- "We are committed to maintaining a balanced portfolio across career stages"
- "Diversity in the scientific workforce strengthens science"

## What Made Rock Talk Influential
- First NIH official to write personally about review and funding
- Created template for engaging applicants directly
- Generated comments and dialogue rather than top-down communication
- Made review process feel less opaque
