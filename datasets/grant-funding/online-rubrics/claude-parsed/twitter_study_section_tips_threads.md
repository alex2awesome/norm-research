# Twitter/X — Study Section Tips Threads Aggregate

Aggregated wisdom from viral Twitter/X threads by recent NIH study section reviewers, on what they look for and what kills applications.

## "What Reviewers See" Threads

### Reviewer Workload Reality
- 8-15 grants per cycle
- 6-10 hours per grant (assigned reviewer)
- 2-4 hours per grant (third reviewer)
- Total: 60-100 hours over 2-3 weeks
- Often done at night/weekends after main job

### How Reviewers Actually Read
- First pass: Specific Aims page (5-10 minutes)
- Second pass: Significance, Innovation, Approach in order
- Third pass: Selected sections deep-dive
- Skim biosketch for productivity check
- Glance at budget for sanity check

### What Forms First Impression
- Specific Aims page in first 30 seconds
- Quality of writing in first paragraph
- Visual organization (white space, bold, figures)
- Clarity of central question

## "What Kills Applications" Threads

### Top Sins by Frequency
1. Co-dependent aims (failure cascade)
2. Vague Significance ("important")
3. No central hypothesis
4. Insufficient preliminary data
5. Missing rigor language
6. No statistical analysis plan
7. Padded budget
8. Generic letters of support
9. Investigator overreach for career stage
10. Hype language exceeding evidence

### Methodological Sins
- Sample size with no power calculation
- Cell line without authentication
- Antibody without validation
- No mention of sex as biological variable
- No statistical analysis plan
- Single biological replicate
- No alternative if primary fails

### Writing Sins
- Hedging language ("we hope", "potentially")
- Passive voice obscuring agency
- Acronyms undefined at first use
- Jargon without definition
- No topic sentences
- Walls of text without white space

## "What Saves Borderline Applications" Threads

### The Champion Reviewer
- One reviewer who advocates can save you
- Champion needs ammunition: clear preliminary data, strong rationale
- Make it easy for the champion to argue

### The Killer Critic
- One reviewer can sink you
- Anticipate predictable critiques
- Address them before they're raised
- Don't leave nits to pick

### The Indifferent Middle
- Most reviewers are indifferent
- Convert with structure, clarity, signposting
- Bullet points help
- Tables/figures convey information efficiently

## "What I Wish Applicants Knew" Threads

### Reviewer Attention Economy
- We're tired
- We're not your subfield experts
- Define everything
- Bold/underline help us
- White space helps us
- Don't bury the lede

### Pre-Read Behavior
- We read the Specific Aims page first
- We look at biosketch for "is this person credible?"
- We check budget for "is this scope reasonable?"
- Then we dive in

### What Wins Across Reviewer Threads
1. Strong Specific Aims with clear hypothesis
2. Independent aims (parallel logic)
3. Quantitative preliminary data
4. Rigor language explicit
5. Realistic timeline
6. Investigator credibility for scope
7. Letters of support that are specific
8. Budget aligned with scope
9. Innovation framed as feasible novelty
10. Significance landed in 30 seconds

## "How I Got My First R01" Threads

### Common Patterns
- Started 6+ months early
- Specific Aims iterated 20+ times
- Multiple internal mock reviews
- Program officer engaged at idea stage
- Resubmission needed (most common)

### Mentor Roles
- Senior PI gives feedback at draft 5, 10, 15
- Mock review committee scores 1-9 NIH style
- Outside-field reader for clarity check
- Editing services for non-native English speakers

### Common Resubmission Tactics
- Address every critique substantively
- Use reviewer language verbatim
- Quote critique, then say "we have addressed this by..."
- Bold the changes in body text
- Introduction page (1 page) for response summary
- Don't argue without conceding something

## On Implicit Bias and Inequities (Threads)

### What Reviewers Try to Counter
- Anchoring on first impressions
- Familiarity bias toward known PIs
- Methodological gatekeeping
- Topic preference bias
- Career stage assumptions

### How Applicants Mitigate
- Don't assume reviewer knows you
- Explain your independent contribution
- Anticipate methodological skepticism
- Frame topic with broad significance
- Show productivity proportionate to career stage
