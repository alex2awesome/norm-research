# Lauer et al. 2017: Next Generation Researchers Initiative at NIH

PNAS opinion piece + NIH policy implementing congressionally mandated (21st Century Cures Act §2021) prioritization of early- and early-established-stage investigators.

## Policy-Driven Rubric Reform

The Next Generation Researchers Initiative (NGRI) modifies NIH's funding decisions to favor early-career applicants, addressing the documented aging of the funded NIH workforce.

## Three Career-Stage Categories (the new rubric)

### Early Stage Investigator (ESI)
- PD/PI within **10 years of completing terminal research degree** (or end of post-graduate clinical training, whichever is later)
- Has NOT previously competed successfully as PD/PI for substantial NIH independent research award
- **Prioritized for funding** — meritorious applications get extra consideration

### Early Established Investigator (EEI)
- Within 10 years of receiving their first substantial NIH R01-equivalent award as an ESI
- **Prioritized for funding** if losing all R01 support or only 1 award remaining

### Established Investigator
- Beyond EEI window
- Standard review process applies

## Criteria for "Substantial" R01-Equivalent (the threshold rubric)

NIH's definition of competitively-substantial-equivalent is detailed in policy and includes:
- R01, R37, DP1, DP2, DP5, R23, R29, R35 mechanisms
- Total direct costs above specified thresholds
- Multi-year duration

## Reform Goals (the values rubric)

1. **Mitigate workforce aging** — reverse the documented trend of rising mean age of first R01 receipt
2. **Promote earlier research independence** for new investigators
3. **Stabilize careers of early-established investigators** at risk of attrition
4. **Provide statutory implementation** of 21st Century Cures Act mandate

## Implementation Mechanism

- Each Institute/Center sets ESI funding goals (initially: 200+ MORE ESI awards than FY2016)
- Program staff have explicit authority to fund ESI applications above the score-based payline
- Tracking of ESI funding rates as accountability metric

## Implications for Rubric Design

The NGRI is a "supplementary rubric" — it doesn't change the per-application scoring criteria but adds a programmatic priority that affects which scored applications get funded.

This reflects an important rubric-design pattern: even when the formal scoring rubric is unchanged, programmatic priorities (career stage, demographic representation, geographic distribution, topic balance) can be applied at the funding-decision stage.

The NGRI is unusual in being:
1. **Statutory** (mandated by Congress)
2. **Quantified** (specific numerical goals per IC)
3. **Audited** (public reporting of ESI funding rates)
4. **Transparent** (the priority is explicitly stated, not hidden)

## Connection to Other Reforms

NGRI complements other NIH reforms:
- Simplified Review Framework (2025) — restructure individual criteria
- Erosheva 2020 — racial disparities in scoring
- Hoppe 2019 — topic-choice disparities
- F32 revisions (2025) — separate candidate from sponsor evaluation

Together these constitute the largest evidence-driven reform of NIH peer review in two decades.
