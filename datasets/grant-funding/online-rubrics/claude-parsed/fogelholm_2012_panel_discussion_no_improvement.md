# Fogelholm et al. 2012: Panel Discussion Does NOT Improve Peer Review Reliability

Experimental study in which 30 reviewers were randomly allocated to two panels, each independently reviewing the same 65 grant proposals in clinical medicine and epidemiology. Tests whether panel discussion improves reliability over averaging individual scores.

## Reviewer Criteria Applied

Reviewers scored each proposal on standard medical-research grant dimensions:
1. Scientific quality
2. Originality
3. Methodology / feasibility
4. Importance of the research question
5. Applicant qualifications
6. Overall recommendation

## Experimental Design

- 65 proposals scored independently by reviewers in Panel A
- Same 65 proposals scored independently by reviewers in Panel B
- Each panel then held a discussion and produced **post-discussion consensus scores**
- Compared post-discussion scores from Panel A vs Panel B
- Compared **mean of individual reviewer scores** vs post-discussion scores for reliability

## Key Findings (the procedural failure rubric)

- **Post-discussion panel agreement κ = 0.23** — slight to fair agreement (95% CI: 0.08, 0.39).
- **Mean of individual reviewer scores κ = 0.23** — IDENTICAL reliability without the discussion.
- Proportion of large differences (≥2 points on the scale) between panels:
  - Reviewer A scores: 40%
  - Reviewer B scores: 36%
  - Panel discussion scores: 26%
  - Mean of two reviewer scores: **14%** (the BEST method)

## Conclusions

- Panel discussion is no more reliable than simply averaging the pre-discussion individual scores.
- The mean of independent reviewer scores OUTPERFORMS panel discussion in reducing extreme disagreements.
- Panels are **expensive** (in time, travel, money) but provide no reliability benefit over simpler aggregation.

## Implications for Rubric Design

1. The **aggregation procedure** (panel vs averaging) matters less than the rubric structure.
2. Funders could potentially eliminate panel meetings and use averaged independent scores at lower cost and equal reliability.
3. Discussion may serve OTHER functions (training reviewers, identifying issues for program staff, providing feedback to applicants) that justify its cost — but not reliability.
4. Hard kappa ceiling appears to be around 0.2-0.4 across multiple systems and methods.
