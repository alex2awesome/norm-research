# DORA: San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment

Major international declaration on research assessment principles that has been signed by 23,000+ individuals and 3,000+ organizations including major funders (Wellcome, NIH, MRC, ANR, DFG, FWF). Establishes a normative anti-rubric for use of journal-based metrics.

## Core Anti-Principle

**Do NOT use journal-based metrics (Journal Impact Factors) as a proxy for research quality** in:
- Hiring decisions
- Promotion decisions  
- Tenure decisions
- Funding decisions
- Individual researcher assessment

## Eighteen Specific Recommendations (the assessment rubric)

### General Recommendation
1. **Do not use journal-based metrics, such as Journal Impact Factors, as a surrogate measure of the quality of individual research articles, to assess an individual scientist's contributions, or in hiring, promotion, or funding decisions.**

### For Funding Agencies (relevant to grant rubrics)
2. Be **explicit about the criteria** used in evaluating the scientific productivity of grant applicants.
3. **Highlight, especially for early-stage investigators**, that the scientific content of a paper is much more important than publication metrics or the journal in which it was published.
4. **Consider value and impact of all research outputs** (including datasets and software) in addition to research publications.
5. Consider **broad range of impact measures** including qualitative indicators (influence on policy/practice).

### For Institutions
6. Be explicit about criteria used to reach hiring, tenure, promotion decisions.
7. Highlight scientific content over publication metrics.
8. Consider value and impact of all research outputs.

### For Publishers
9. Greatly reduce emphasis on the journal impact factor.
10. Make available a range of article-level metrics.
11. Encourage responsible authorship practices.
12. Remove all reuse limitations on reference lists for transparency.

### For Researchers
13. Make personnel-related decisions based on scientific content rather than publication metrics.
14. Cite primary literature rather than reviews when possible.
15. Use a range of article metrics and indicators.
16. Challenge research assessment practices that rely inappropriately on JIFs.

### For Metrics Suppliers
17. Be open and transparent about data and methods.
18. Provide data with no restrictions on reuse; provide computational access.

## Implications for Rubric Design

DORA defines what a defensible research-assessment rubric should AVOID:
- JIF-based proxy assessment
- Single-metric reductive evaluation
- Hidden weighting of journal prestige
- Implicit valuation of "high-impact" venues

A DORA-aligned grant rubric must:
- Evaluate research content directly
- Consider diverse research outputs (papers, data, software, policy briefs)
- Use qualitative AND quantitative indicators
- Be transparent about the criteria applied

## Adoption Status

Major signatories include:
- NIH (US)
- Wellcome Trust (UK)
- DFG (Germany)
- FWF (Austria)
- ANR (France)
- HHMI (US)
- ERC (de facto endorsement)
- Major publishers (Nature, Science, PLOS, eLife, AAAS)

Adoption is normative — signing organizations commit to revising their internal assessment practices. Implementation varies widely.
