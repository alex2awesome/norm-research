# REF 2021: UK Research Excellence Framework Panel Criteria

The UK Research Excellence Framework (REF) is the national assessment of university research quality, used to allocate ~£2 billion/year of block-grant research funding (QR funding) to UK universities. REF 2021 covered 76,132 academic staff across 157 institutions.

## Three Assessed Components

REF assesses each Unit of Assessment (UoA) on three components with weighted contributions to overall score:

1. **Research Outputs** — 60%
2. **Research Impact** — 25%
3. **Research Environment** — 15%

## Quality Star Ratings (the master scoring rubric)

All three components rated on 5-point scale:

- **4★ — World-leading** quality in terms of originality, significance, and rigour
- **3★ — Internationally excellent** in terms of originality, significance, and rigour, but falls short of the highest standards
- **2★ — Recognised internationally** in terms of originality, significance, and rigour
- **1★ — Recognised nationally** in terms of originality, significance, and rigour
- **Unclassified** — falls below the standard of nationally recognised work, or does not meet the published definition of research

## Three Universal Criteria (applied to outputs)

1. **Originality** — extent to which the output makes an important and innovative contribution to the field
2. **Significance** — extent to which the output has influenced, or has the capacity to influence, knowledge and scholarly thought
3. **Rigour** — extent to which the output demonstrates intellectual coherence and integrity, and adopts robust and appropriate concepts, analyses, sources, theories, and methodologies

## Impact Component Criteria

Research Impact case studies assessed on:

- **Reach** — extent and diversity of beneficiaries
- **Significance** — degree to which the impact has enabled, enriched, influenced, informed, or changed the performance, policies, practices, products, services, understanding, awareness, or well-being of the beneficiaries

## Environment Component Criteria

Research Environment assessed on:

- **Vitality** — quality and scale of research activity, including PhD student support, research income, infrastructure
- **Sustainability** — strategies for maintaining and developing the research environment over time
- Evidence base: research income, doctoral degrees awarded, research infrastructure, environment statement narrative

## Submission Structure

Each unit submits:
- **Outputs**: 2.5 outputs per FTE academic staff member on average (one per author min, five max for any individual)
- **Impact case studies**: minimum 2; rate of 1 per 15 FTE, plus 1
- **Environment statement**: narrative + data on income, infrastructure, doctoral degrees

## Panel Working Methods

- 4 Main Panels (A: Health and Life Sciences; B: Physical Sciences and Engineering; C: Social Sciences; D: Arts and Humanities)
- 34 Sub-Panels under the Main Panels
- Sub-panel members read and grade outputs; cross-referencing among panel members
- Output-level scores aggregated using a "quality profile" approach

## Implications for Rubric Design

REF's 4★/3★/2★/1★ rubric is the most consequential national research-quality rubric in the world by money-allocated. Its three universal criteria (originality, significance, rigour) have been widely adopted as a template by other systems, including informal use in grant peer review across the UK and Europe.
