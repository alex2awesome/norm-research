# Stuart Buck — Metascience and Funding Reform

Stuart Buck (formerly of Arnold Foundation, now Good Science Project) writes about metascience, research funding reform, and what makes good research investments. Implicit rubric for "what funders should value" from a reform perspective.

## Core Themes

### Replication and Reproducibility
- Funding should reward reproducible research
- Replication studies are systematically underfunded
- Pre-registration should be standard
- Open data and code as default
- Negative results have value

### High-Risk / High-Reward Funding
- Most NIH funding is incremental, not transformative
- HHMI, NSF EAGER, DARPA tackle higher risk
- Lottery-based funding (mentioned by Buck) addresses risk aversion
- Need mechanisms for "weird" ideas

### Funder Risk Aversion
- Reviewers are risk-averse by selection
- Career incentives reward established directions
- Bibliometric assessment perpetuates conservatism
- "Innovation" claims often mask incrementalism

### Funding Concentration Critique
- Top institutions and PIs get disproportionate funding
- Productivity per dollar declines with concentration
- New investigator pipelines need protection
- Geographic and demographic diversity matters

## Reform Proposals That Inform Implicit Rubric

### What "Good" Funded Research Looks Like (Per Buck)
1. Pre-registered analysis plan
2. Adequate sample sizes (powered for realistic effects)
3. Open data, code, materials
4. Replicable methodology
5. Honest acknowledgment of uncertainty
6. Clear contribution to knowledge (not just publication count)
7. Real risk-taking when the funder asks for it
8. Conservative effect-size estimates
9. Plans for replication where appropriate
10. Diverse cohorts and broad generalizability

### What Buck Critiques
1. Incrementalism dressed as innovation
2. P-hacking-friendly research designs
3. Selective reporting
4. Closed data and code
5. Mega-consortia with diffuse accountability
6. Citation-chasing rather than knowledge contribution
7. Hype language exceeding evidence
8. Single-population studies
9. Lack of replication infrastructure
10. Funder-grantee revolving door dynamics

## Specific Funder Critiques

### NIH
- Conservative review process selects for incremental work
- Resubmission churn wastes investigator time
- New investigator pipeline insufficient
- Reform efforts (simplified review) are step in right direction

### NSF
- Broader Impacts often boilerplate
- Innovation claims rarely substantiated
- Need more lottery-like mechanisms for risk

### Foundations
- Often more flexible than government
- Open Philanthropy, Arnold, Templeton experimenting
- Smaller grants enable more risk-taking

## Buck-Style Implicit Rubric

### What Should Win
- Clear hypothesis with well-defined predictions
- Pre-registered analysis plan
- Adequate power for plausible effects
- Open science commitments (data, code, preprints)
- Replication or generalizability built into design
- Cohort diversity addressed
- Independent replication encouraged

### What Should Lose
- Vague hypotheses with flexible predictions
- Post-hoc analysis flexibility
- Underpowered studies with hype claims
- Closed data/code
- Single-cohort studies framing as generalizable
- Citation-chasing over substantive contribution

## Notable Buck Quotes (Paraphrased)
- "Reviewers reward what looks safe, not what is true"
- "Pre-registration is the cheapest way to improve research quality"
- "Replication studies are the most undervalued use of grant dollars"
- "Funders should make risk-taking easier, not harder"

## What Applicants Can Take From Buck's Voice

### To Win With "Reform-Minded" Reviewers
- Pre-register your analysis plan in your application
- Include open science commitments explicitly
- Power calculations with conservative effect sizes
- Diverse cohort recruitment plans
- Acknowledge limitations and risks honestly
- Cite replication studies in your area
- Avoid hype language

### To Avoid Reform Critiques
- Don't hide behind "exploratory" framing
- Specify primary vs. secondary outcomes
- Justify subgroup analyses a priori
- Plan for sharing data and code
- Address generalizability honestly
