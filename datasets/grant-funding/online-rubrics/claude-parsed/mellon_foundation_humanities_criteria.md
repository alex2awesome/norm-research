# Mellon Foundation - Humanities Grant Evaluation Criteria

## General Grant-Making Approach
The Mellon Foundation staff review inquiry submissions and seek out organizations before inviting them to submit proposals, with most grants made through these invitations rather than through open competitive calls.

## Funding Priorities for Humanities Grants

### Higher Learning Programs
- Institutions of higher education and scholars addressing crucial societal issues and promoting learning
- Work broadening opportunities in the humanities through:
  - Academic research
  - Curricular development
  - Extracurricular programming
  - Internships
  - Community partnerships

### Humanities in Place
- Grantees that shine a spotlight on historically overlooked sites
- Communities and stories surrounding them
- Help broaden shared understanding
- Open new paths to discover what unites us and what we can learn from one another

### American History and Culture
- Scholars and academic leaders working to deepen understanding of American history and culture
- Advance new methods for scholarly interpretation
- Elevate underrepresented perspectives in academia

## Funding Restrictions
The Foundation does NOT fund:
- Tuition
- K-12 education and programming
- Fundraising events
- Direct unrestricted funding for individuals

Grants are designated for charitable and/or educational purposes.

## Selection Approach
Mellon uses a more developmental, relational approach to grant-making rather than purely competitive review:
- Identifies promising organizations and scholars
- Builds long-term relationships
- Strategic alignment with foundation's evolving priorities
- Equity-centered approach to scholarship and humanistic inquiry
