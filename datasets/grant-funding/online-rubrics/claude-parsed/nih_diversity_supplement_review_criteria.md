# NIH Diversity Supplement (R01 Add-on) - Review Criteria

## Mechanism Overview
NIH Research Supplements to Promote Diversity in Health-Related Research provide funding to enhance the diversity of the research workforce by recruiting and supporting eligible candidates from groups that have been shown to be underrepresented in health-related research.

## Eligible Candidate Groups
NIH is particularly interested in recruiting and retaining individuals from racial and ethnic groups shown to be underrepresented in health-related sciences on a national basis:
- Blacks or African Americans
- Hispanics or Latinos
- American Indians or Alaska Natives
- Native Hawaiians and other Pacific Islanders
- Individuals with disabilities
- Individuals from disadvantaged backgrounds (some institutes)

## Career Stage Eligibility
- High school students
- Undergraduate students
- Post-baccalaureate research education program participants
- Graduate students (predoctoral)
- Postdoctoral fellows
- Eligible faculty/investigators

## Review Criteria

NIH staff evaluate supplement requests using general criteria including:

### 1. Qualifications of the Candidate
- Career goals
- Prior research training
- Research potential
- Relevant experience
- Academic record (variable by career stage)

### 2. Impact on the Parent Award
- Ability of the proposed supplement activities to increase or preserve the parent award's overall impact
- Within the original scope of award (cannot expand scope)

### 3. Promotion of Diversity
- Strength of the description of how the appointment will promote diversity in biomedical, behavioral, or clinical research nationally
- Plan for the candidate's training and career development
- Mentorship arrangements

### 4. Research Plan Quality
- Soundness of the proposed research training experience
- Appropriateness of methods
- Relevance to the parent grant

### 5. Mentorship Plan
- Quality of mentorship by the PI
- Specific training activities
- Plan for the candidate's professional development
- Career development opportunities

## Administrative Process
- Diversity supplements do NOT require peer review
- Can be approved by NIH Project Officers
- Supported by funds specifically set aside for this purpose
- Supplement applications reviewed internally for scientific merit and training potential

## Parent Grant Eligibility
PIs are generally eligible to submit a request for a diversity supplement if they hold an active R01 grant (among many other qualifying mechanisms):
- R-series (R01, R34, R35, R37, etc.)
- P-series (P01, P30, P50, etc.)
- U-series (cooperative agreements)
- Some training grants
- Specific NCI mechanisms

## Strategic Considerations
- Stronger when candidate's research aligns clearly with parent grant aims
- Mentorship plan should be specific and substantive
- Career development plan should match career stage
- Letter of support from the candidate's department important
- Multiple supplements per parent grant allowed in some cases
