# SBIR/STTR - Agency-Specific Evaluation Criteria

## Universal SBIR Criteria
- **Innovation** is a criterion present in all SBIR/STTR solicitations
- **Experience, Qualifications and Facilities** is another common evaluation criterion across all SBIR/STTR programs

## DOD-Specific Evaluation Criteria

### Three Primary Criteria
1. **Soundness, Technical Merit and Innovation of the Proposed Approach**
   - Soundness of the proposed innovation
   - Originality and significance of approach
   - Likelihood of overcoming technical challenges

2. **Qualifications of the Proposed Principal/Key Investigators, Supporting Staff and Consultants**
   - Track record
   - Relevant expertise
   - Team composition

3. **Potential for Commercial Applications**
   - Government or private sector applications
   - Benefits expected from commercialization
   - Path to deployment

## NASA-Specific Evaluation Criteria

### Five Factors
1. **Scientific/Technical Merit and Feasibility**
   - Of what is proposed
   - Soundness of approach
   - Achievability of objectives

2. **Experience, Qualifications and Facilities**
   - Technical capabilities and experience of PI, project manager, key personnel, staff, consultants and subcontractors
   - Consistency with the research effort
   - Degree of commitment and availability

3. **Effectiveness of the Proposed Work Plan**
   - Plan structure and timeline
   - Milestones and deliverables
   - Risk management

4. **Commercial Potential and Feasibility**
   - Offeror's experience and record in technology commercialization
   - Co-funding commitments from private or non-SBIR/non-STTR funding sources
   - Existing and projected commitments for Phase III funding
   - Initial commercialization strategy for the innovation

5. **Price Reasonableness**
   - Realistic budget
   - Cost-benefit
   - Allocation across categories

## Phase Differences

### Phase I
- Concept feasibility
- Smaller awards (~$50K-$300K)
- 6-12 month period
- Concept proof, not full development

### Phase II
- Build on Phase I results
- Larger awards (~$750K-$2M)
- 24-month period typical
- Significant prototyping/development

### Phase III
- Commercialization
- Government or private sector
- Sole-source contracts allowed
- No additional SBIR funding

## NIH SBIR Specific Considerations
- Health-related innovations
- Similar review process to R-series with commercialization addendum
- Commercialization plan weighted equally to research plan
- NIH study sections plus commercial/regulatory experts

## NSF SBIR Specific Considerations
- Customer discovery (I-Corps style) heavily weighted
- Innovation considered broadly (including business model)
- Less prescriptive on technical methodology
- Often stronger commercialization emphasis

## Common Reasons for SBIR Rejection
- Weak commercialization plan
- Unclear technical innovation
- Missing or weak Customer Discovery evidence
- Wrong topic for solicitation
- Budget mismatch with scope
- Unqualified team
- Failure to address agency-specific priorities

## Application Components
- Cover sheet
- Project Summary
- Technical Proposal (~25 pages)
- Cost/Budget
- Commercialization Plan (Phase II especially)
- Letters of support (customers, partners)
- Bios/CVs
- Subcontractor information
