# Severin, Egger et al. 2020: Gender and Other Biases in 38,250 External Peer Reviews

Large-scale cross-sectional analysis of all external peer review reports submitted to the Swiss National Science Foundation (SNSF) over a multi-year period. Tests for gender, age, language, and field-specific biases in peer review reports themselves.

## Sample

- **38,250 external peer review reports**
- Submitted to SNSF for project grant applications
- All disciplines (life sciences, physical sciences, social sciences, humanities)
- Multi-year coverage

## Bias Variables Tested

### Applicant Variables
1. **Gender**
2. **Age** (career stage)
3. **Linguistic group** (German, French, Italian Switzerland; international)
4. **Discipline / field**

### Reviewer Variables
1. Reviewer gender
2. Reviewer geographic location
3. Reviewer-applicant gender interaction

### Outcome Variables
1. **Final score** given by reviewer
2. **Recommendation** to fund or not
3. **Length of review**
4. **Linguistic patterns** in review text

## Key Findings

### On Applicant Gender
- **Modest gender bias** detected — male applicants received slightly higher scores than female applicants on equivalent applications
- Effect size small but statistically significant given large sample
- Bias more pronounced in certain disciplines (e.g., engineering, physical sciences) than others

### On Reviewer-Applicant Gender Match
- **Same-gender review** showed slightly different patterns than cross-gender review
- Female reviewers somewhat more critical of female applicants in some disciplines

### On Linguistic Group
- **Native-language applicants** received slightly higher scores than international applicants
- Possibly reflects writing-quality effects rather than direct bias

### On Age / Career Stage
- **Senior applicants favored** in track-record-related criteria
- **Early-career applicants disadvantaged** in proposal-quality assessments where preliminary data matters

## Implicit Quality Criteria Embedded in Reviews (per linguistic analysis)

The 38,250 reviews showed reviewers consistently invoke:
1. **Methodological rigor**
2. **Originality / novelty**
3. **Track record / qualifications**
4. **Feasibility**
5. **Significance to the field**
6. **International competitiveness**
7. **Resources and team adequacy**

## Implications for Rubric Design

1. **Even at large scale**, gender bias in grant peer review is detectable but modest in magnitude (consistent with Bornmann meta-analysis 2007).
2. **Field-specific bias patterns** mean a one-size-fits-all rubric may obscure differential bias across disciplines.
3. **Reviewer-applicant matching** introduces interaction effects that simple bias-detection at the aggregate level misses.
4. **Linguistic / writing quality** confounds with applicant identity effects — accommodation for non-native speakers may be needed.

## Methodological Innovation

This is the largest single-funder dataset analyzed for grant-review bias. The fact that bias is detectable but small at this scale validates the meta-analytic findings (Bornmann et al. 2007, Marsh et al. 2009) and suggests that well-designed European review processes have reduced but not eliminated gender bias.
