# Hug & Aeschbach 2020: Systematic Review of Grant Assessment Criteria

Systematic review of all empirical studies that examined criteria used in grant peer review. Identifies the universe of criteria that funders ask reviewers to apply.

## Nine Domains of Grant Assessment Criteria (the master rubric)

The systematic review identifies nine recurring criterion domains across funding agencies:

1. **Relevance to the call for proposals** — fit with the funder's strategic priorities
2. **Usefulness** — practical value of the proposed research
3. **Originality** — novelty of the research question
4. **Innovativeness** — novelty of the methods / approach
5. **Methodology** — appropriateness, rigor of design
6. **Feasibility** — likelihood of completion in time/budget
7. **Funding** — adequacy and justification of requested resources
8. **Ethical aspects** — compliance with ethical norms; ethical implications
9. **Writing of the grant application** — clarity, organization, presentation

## Sub-Categories within the Nine Domains

### Originality (sub-criteria)
- Novel research question
- Novel theoretical framework
- Novel population studied
- Combination of known elements in new way

### Methodology (sub-criteria)
- Internal validity / design rigor
- External validity / generalizability
- Statistical / analytical plan
- Quality assurance procedures
- Sample size justification

### Feasibility (sub-criteria)
- Realistic timeline
- Track record on similar projects
- Resource availability (data, equipment, personnel)
- Risk mitigation plan
- Preliminary data showing approach works

### Funding (sub-criteria)
- Cost-effectiveness
- Budget justification
- Value-for-money
- Reasonableness of personnel costs

## Critical Findings on Application

- Criteria are **inconsistently applied** across reviewers.
- **Weights vary** dramatically: some reviewers give equal weight to all nine; others heavily weight one or two.
- **Originality and methodology** are most commonly weighted highest.
- **"Writing quality"** explicitly used as a criterion correlates with score, raising concerns about advantaging applicants with more polish/native English.
- Many criteria are vague — "originality" rarely has operational definition.

## Recommendations from the Review

1. Funders should **clearly define each criterion** to reduce subjectivity.
2. Funders should **specify weights** rather than leaving weighting to reviewer discretion.
3. Funders should **train reviewers** on operational definitions.
4. Funders should **monitor consistency** of criterion application across reviewers.

## Implications for Rubric Design

The nine-domain structure is the most-comprehensive consensus rubric in the literature. Most agency rubrics are subsets of these nine, with different combinations and weightings. A defensible rubric should explicitly enumerate, define, and weight each criterion it uses.
