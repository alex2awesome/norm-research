# Ginther et al. - Race, Ethnicity, and NIH Research Awards

## Citation
Ginther, D. K., Schaffer, W. T., Schnell, J., Masimore, B., Liu, F., Haak, L. L., & Kington, R. (2011). Race, Ethnicity, and NIH Research Awards. Science, 333(6045), 1015-1019.

## Study Overview
A 2011 Science paper by Ginther et al. reported a significant racial gap in NIH R01 equivalent grant funding, illuminating a lack of diversity among investigators conducting NIH-supported biomedical research.

## Key Findings (2011)

### Funding Gap
Black or African-American applicants are **13 percentage points less likely** to receive NIH investigator-initiated research funding compared with whites.

### After Controlling for Variables
After controlling for the applicant's:
- Educational background
- Country of origin
- Training
- Previous research awards
- Publication record
- Employer characteristics

Black applicants remain **10 percentage points less likely** to receive funding.

### What Was Explained
The 2011 paper could only explain one-third of the gap, leaving two potential explanations:
1. Omitted variables
2. Bias in the review process

## Follow-up Studies

### Ginther 2018 (PLOS ONE)
Examined publications as predictors of racial and ethnic differences. Even after controlling for productivity metrics, gaps persisted.

### 2019 Update (Hoppe et al., Science Advances)
- For independent research grants, applications from white PIs are **1.7 times more likely** to be funded than applications from African-American/Black PIs
- Applications with white PIs were still 1.7 times more likely to be funded than applications with African-American/Black PIs
- Topic choice partially explains gap (Black scientists more likely to work on community/applied/disparities topics that get systematically lower scores)

### Hoppe et al. (2019) "Topic Choice Contributes to NIH Funding Disparity"
- Topic choice accounts for ~21% of the funding gap
- Disease-focused, community-relevant, and translational topics receive lower priority scores

## Implications for Grant Funding
- Highlights persistent structural inequities in NIH funding
- Has driven NIH initiatives (UNITE, Diversity Supplements, etc.)
- Shapes ongoing reforms (Simplified Review Framework partly motivated by bias concerns)
- Notable that gap is largest in basic and translational research, narrower in some clinical areas

## Critical Context
- Reviewers can identify race indirectly from names, institutions, training history
- Topic effects suggest review reflects "what is valued" disparities
- NIH mandatory bias awareness training (2024) implements partial response
