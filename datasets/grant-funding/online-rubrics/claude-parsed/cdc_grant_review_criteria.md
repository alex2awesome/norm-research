# CDC - Grant Review Process and Criteria

## Grant Review Process Standards

The CDC ensures grant applications undergo an independent and objective merit review:
- Thorough and consistent examination of applications
- Based on unbiased evaluation of scientific or technical merit or other relevant aspects
- Conducted as soon as possible after the application submission deadline
- Results are advisory; approving program official considers the results when making final award recommendations

## Evaluation Criteria

Applications are reviewed in accordance with evaluation criteria published in the Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO):
- Reviewers score applications against published evaluation criteria
- Each NOFO has its own specific criteria
- Summary statement of strengths and weaknesses prepared for each scored application

## Standard CDC Evaluation Categories

### 1. Approach and Methods
- Soundness of public health intervention
- Evidence-based practices
- Implementation strategy
- Quality of data collection plan

### 2. Population Served
- Population characterization
- Need within target population
- Access strategy
- Cultural appropriateness

### 3. Outcomes/Evaluation Plan
- Process measures
- Outcome measures
- Long-term impact metrics
- Evaluation methodology

### 4. Capacity
- Organizational capability
- Personnel qualifications
- Infrastructure
- Past performance

### 5. Budget
- Cost reasonableness
- Cost allocation
- Justification

### 6. Sustainability
- Plans for sustaining the work
- Diversification of resources
- Built-in revenue/support

## Review Procedures

### Format Variations
Merit reviews for discretionary grant programs may be carried out through:
- Face-to-face meetings
- Technology-enabled remote discussions
- Independent assessment by individual reviewers (no panel discussion)

### Reviewer Requirements
- Only appointed reviewers score applications
- CDC merit reviewers must complete a statement attesting to the absence of conflict of interest before reviewing any application
- Reviewers chosen for relevant expertise
- Government and non-government reviewers used

## Outcomes and Communication
- Merit reviews ensure applications are evaluated through a process that is fair, equitable, objective, and transparent
- Applicants receive feedback on strengths and weaknesses
- Funding decisions consider review scores plus programmatic priorities

## CDC Strategic Priorities Influencing Funding

### Public Health Priorities
- Infectious disease prevention
- Chronic disease prevention
- Injury prevention
- Environmental health
- Workplace safety
- Maternal and child health
- Emergency preparedness
- Health equity

### Cross-Cutting Considerations
- Population health equity
- Stakeholder engagement
- Community partnerships
- Workforce development
- Data modernization

## CDC Grant Mechanisms
- R01 (Research Project Grant)
- R18 (Research Demonstration)
- U01, U18, U48, U54 (Cooperative Agreements)
- T01-T03 (Training)
- Cooperative Agreements for state/local health departments
- Cooperative Agreements for CBOs

## Distinct from NIH
- CDC programs often emphasize implementation over discovery
- Direct partnerships with state/local health departments
- Stakeholder engagement is paramount
- Evaluation and dissemination plans heavily weighted
