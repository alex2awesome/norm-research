# NIH F32 Postdoctoral Fellowship — Revised Review Criteria (Effective Jan 25, 2025)

Major restructuring of NIH's individual postdoctoral fellowship review parallel to the simplified R01 framework. Effective for receipt dates on or after January 25, 2025.

## Goals of the Revised Criteria

To help reviewers evaluate a candidate's potential and the quality of the research training plan **without undue influence of sponsor's or institution's reputation**.

This is a direct response to bias-research findings (Wenneras & Wold 1997, Hoppe 2019, Erosheva 2020, Witteman 2019) showing that sponsor/PI reputation amplifies disparate outcomes.

## Three Core Evaluation Elements (replacing previous five-criterion structure)

### 1. Fellowship Candidate's Preparedness and Potential
Reviewers assess:
- **Candidate's training and accomplishments to date** (not the sponsor's reputation)
- **Demonstrated scientific reasoning** in candidate's own writing
- **Self-assessment** — candidate's identification of skills they currently have AND skills they want to acquire during fellowship
- **Scientific perspective** — ability to think broadly about field, not just their specific research
- **Potential** — likelihood of becoming an independent researcher

### 2. Research Training Plan
Reviewers assess:
- **Research plan quality** — rigorous design, experimental methods, quantitative approaches, data analysis
- **Training plan tailored to candidate** — specific skills/concepts to be acquired
- **Documentation of need for and value of mentored training**
- **Fit between research project and training goals**

### 3. Sponsor's Commitment to the Candidate
Reviewers assess:
- **Mentoring approach tailored to candidate** — not just sponsor's general track record
- **Time commitment** to candidate's training
- **Specific mentoring plan** for this candidate's career goals
- **Resources and support** sponsor will provide

## Programmatic Constraint: New Site Requirement

In most cases, the F32 sponsoring organization should be a **site OTHER than where the candidate trained as a graduate student** — designed to maximize new skill acquisition.

## What the Reform Removes/De-emphasizes

The revised criteria explicitly de-emphasize:
- **Sponsor's overall research reputation** (separated from sponsor's commitment to this candidate)
- **Institutional prestige** (separated from environment-quality for this training)
- **Holistic "investigator" + "environment" judgment** (broken into more specific components)

## Implications for Rubric Design

The F32 reform parallels:
- **Witteman 2019 finding**: PI-focused criteria amplify disparities → de-emphasize sponsor reputation
- **Erosheva 2020 finding**: criterion scores carry the disparity → restructure criteria
- **Lauer 2017 NGRI policy**: focus on early-career independence → emphasize candidate's own potential
- **NIH simplified R01 framework**: parallel restructuring of investigator criterion

The F32 revision is a structural response to ~25 years of accumulated bias research — among the most evidence-driven rubric reforms by any major funder.
