# NIH BRAIN Initiative - Grant Evaluation Criteria

## Overview
The BRAIN Initiative supports development of innovative neurotechnologies, theories, models, and methods to understand brain function. Applications evaluated by Scientific Review Groups convened by NINDS, in accordance with NIH peer review policy.

## Standard Review Process
- Applications evaluated for scientific and technical merit by appropriate Scientific Review Groups
- Use stated review criteria
- All applications receive a written critique
- Applications may undergo selection process — only those deemed to have highest scientific and technical merit (generally top half) discussed and assigned an overall impact score

## R21 Grant-Specific Criteria for BRAIN
For R21 exploratory/developmental grants, reviewers emphasize:
- Conceptual framework
- Level of innovation
- Potential to significantly advance knowledge or understanding
- An R21 grant application need NOT have extensive background material or preliminary information

## BRAIN-Specific Requirements

### Project Planning Requirements
Projects must have:
- Clear timeline
- Quantitative benchmarks
- Deliverables
- Realistic and efficient timelines
- Proposed milestones for each year of requested support (evaluated as part of review)

### Plan for Enhancing Diverse Perspectives (PEDP)
Required for BRAIN Initiative NOFOs:
- Assessed as part of the scientific and technical peer review evaluation
- Describes how applicants will engage diverse perspectives in research design and team composition
- Considers participation of underrepresented groups, career stages, disciplines

## Distinct BRAIN Grant Mechanisms
- **U01**: Cooperative agreements (substantial NIH involvement)
- **R01**: Standard research project grants
- **R21**: Exploratory/developmental
- **U19**: Multi-PI cooperative research center
- **R34**: Planning grants
- **F32/K99**: Training mechanisms with BRAIN focus

## Cross-Cutting BRAIN Criteria
- Tool/technology development with broad applicability
- Open data sharing through BRAIN Initiative resources
- Integration across scales (molecular to behavioral)
- Cross-species and translational relevance
- Coordination with BRAIN Initiative consortia

## Public Access and Data Sharing (PADS)
- Plan for immediate public release of published research results and data
- Use of common BRAIN data formats
- Sharing through BRAIN-recognized repositories
