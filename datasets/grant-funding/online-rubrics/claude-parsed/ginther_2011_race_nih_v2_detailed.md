# Ginther et al. 2011: Race, Ethnicity, and NIH Research Awards (Detailed)

Wave 1 has a brief Ginther 2011 entry; this Wave 2 file gives the detailed criteria-and-controls structure. Landmark study using NIH IMPAC II + Web of Science data showing race-based funding disparities even after extensive controls.

## Outcome Studied

Probability of receiving a funded NIH R01 award.

## Applicant Characteristics Controlled For (the validity rubric)

The study controlled for ALL of the following objective applicant features:

### Educational Background
1. Degree type (PhD, MD, MD/PhD)
2. Year of highest degree
3. Country of training
4. Type and prestige of training institution

### Research Productivity
5. Number of prior publications
6. Citation count
7. Prior NIH funding
8. Prior R01 awards

### Career Stage
9. Years since training completed
10. Number of prior R01 applications
11. Resubmission status

### Demographics & Affiliation
12. Self-identified race/ethnicity
13. Country of birth / country of training
14. Employer type and characteristics
15. State / region

## Key Findings (the disparity rubric)

After controlling for all of the above:

- **Asian applicants**: 4 percentage points less likely to be funded than white applicants
- **Black/African-American applicants**: 13 percentage points less likely to be funded
  - Reduces to 10 percentage points after full controls (still significant)
- **Hispanic applicants**: small, mostly non-significant gap
- **Disparity at priority-score stage**: among proposals with strong priority scores, funding was equal across races. Disparity arises EARLIER in the review process (impact scores, decision to discuss).

## Implicit NIH Quality Criteria Embedded in the Findings

The five NIH criteria (significance, investigators, innovation, approach, environment) — when applied by reviewers, generated systematically lower scores for Black applicants of equivalent objective qualifications. The criteria themselves are biased in their application, not necessarily in their formal definition.

## Implications for Rubric Design

- The 13-pp gap (or 10-pp after controls) is among the largest documented racial disparities in any U.S. resource-allocation system.
- Suggests reviewer judgment of "significance," "approach," etc. is mediated by racial perception even controlling for objective credentials.
- Triggered NIH's subsequent reform efforts (NRMN, Diversity Program Consortium, anonymized review pilots).
- A defensible rubric would require regular auditing for race/ethnicity disparities at each criterion individually.

## Methodological Innovation

Ginther et al. matched NIH internal data with external bibliometrics, creating the first rigorously-controlled empirical study of racial disparities in NIH funding. Subsequent papers (Hoppe 2019, Erosheva 2020) build directly on this design.
