# Datahound — NIH Funding Analytics (Jeremy Berg)

Jeremy Berg, former NIGMS Director and Editor-in-Chief of Science, runs Datahound — a data-driven blog analyzing NIH funding patterns. Implicit rubric for what NIH leadership cares about.

## Key Analytical Findings That Inform Strategy

### Score-to-Funding Correlation
- Scores are noisy; ranking is correlated but not deterministic
- Same score in different study sections has different funding probability
- Institute paylines vary substantially (NIGMS vs. NIAID vs. NCI)
- Programmatic priority can rescue grants 5-10 percentile points outside payline

### Productivity vs. Funding Level
- Berg's analysis showed citation productivity per dollar declines with multiple R01s
- Grant Support Index (GSI) policy attempted to operationalize
- Implication: don't apply for what you can't productively use

### Geographic and Institutional Concentration
- Top 50 institutions receive disproportionate share
- Smaller institutions face headwinds in review
- Environment section matters more than people think

### Reviewer Demographics
- Reviewers skew senior, R1-affiliated, male (though improving)
- Implicit homophily in scoring
- Cross-discipline reviewers tend to score lower

### Application Behavior Patterns
- PIs who submit more applications have higher overall funding (volume effect)
- Resubmissions have higher success than de novo submissions of similar quality
- A1 success rate higher than A0 in most ICs

## Implicit Rubric From Berg's Analyses

### Strategic Choices Before Writing
1. Pick the right institute based on payline data
2. Pick the right mechanism based on your career stage
3. Pick the right study section based on member expertise (NIH RePORTER)
4. Plan to resubmit if not funded the first time
5. Don't over-apply — saturation hurts

### Writing for the Median Reviewer
- The median reviewer is mid-career, R1, male, 5+ years post-tenure
- Writes for someone reading your grant on a plane at 11pm
- Significance must land in 60 seconds
- Don't assume specialist knowledge

### What Berg's Data Suggests Wins
- Hypothesis-driven over descriptive
- Strong preliminary data
- Reasonable scope (not over-promising)
- Alignment with institute priorities
- ESI/diversity considerations where applicable

## Specific Berg Posts of Note (Topics)
- Distribution of scores across study sections
- The "luck factor" in funding decisions
- ESI policies and outcomes
- Foreign applicant disadvantage (varies by IC)
- The shrinking median R01 budget
- Indirect cost rate variation

## Author Voice
- Rigorous, data-first, not opinion-driven
- Acknowledges policy limits with data
- Skeptical of claims not backed by NIH RePORTER data
- Often closes with "more analysis needed"

## What Datahound Tells Applicants
- Know the data: paylines, success rates, study section composition
- The system has noise — don't take any single review personally
- Strategic choices (which IC, which mechanism, which SRG) matter
- Productivity per dollar is a real metric NIH tracks
- The game is partly numbers — submit, learn, resubmit
