# Packard Fellowships for Science and Engineering

## Overview
The Packard Fellowships for Science and Engineering were established in 1988 to support the nation's most promising early-career scientists and engineers. Each year, the Foundation selects 20 Fellows from 50 invited institutions to receive individual grants of $875,000, distributed over five years.

## Key Selection Criteria

### Career Stage
Candidates must be faculty members engaged in research in the natural and physical sciences or engineering and must be **within the first three years** of their faculty careers.

### Research Focus / Disciplines Considered
- Physics
- Chemistry
- Mathematics
- Biology
- Astronomy
- Computer science
- Earth science
- Ocean science
- All branches of engineering

### Excluded Areas
- Clinical research
- Research associated with large national facilities (accelerators, space stations)
- Applied research of direct relevance to national security
- Social sciences

### Research Approach
The Foundation emphasizes support for **innovative individual research** that involves the Fellows, their students, and junior colleagues, rather than extensions or components of large-scale, ongoing research programs.

## Evaluation Process
The Packard Fellowships Advisory Panel — a group of 12 internationally recognized scientists and engineers — evaluates the nominations and recommends Fellows for approval by the Packard Foundation Board of Trustees.

## Most Important Evaluation Criteria
1. **Recommendation letters** (recommenders who are themselves Packard Fellows or mentored Packard Fellows are highly regarded as letter writers)
2. **Importance/impact of research**

## Additional Considerations
- Publication track record is important to be recognized and labeled as an expert in a field for specific body of work
- Having previous funding and/or awards can be perceived as a positive
- Risk-taking and originality are valued over incremental work
