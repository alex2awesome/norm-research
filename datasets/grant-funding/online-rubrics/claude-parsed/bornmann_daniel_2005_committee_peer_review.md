# Bornmann & Daniel 2005: Committee Peer Review of Research Fellowships

First comprehensive empirical study of committee peer review for Ph.D. and post-doctoral fellowships. N = 2,697 applications (1,954 doctoral, 743 post-doctoral) at the Boehringer Ingelheim Fonds.

## Three Meta-Evaluation Dimensions

1. **Reliability** — agreement between reviewers
2. **Fairness** — absence of bias on irrelevant variables
3. **Predictive validity** — correlation with subsequent productivity

## Reliability Findings

- **76% reviewer agreement rate** on fellowship award decisions.
- Comparable to grant peer review reliability at major agencies.
- Higher than typical for grant decisions because:
  - Smaller pool of decisions (yes/no rather than continuous scoring)
  - Strong CV-based signal in fellowship contexts
  - Committee structure forced explicit deliberation

## Fairness Findings (variables tested for bias)

For **post-doctoral fellowships**:
- **Gender**: NOT significant
- **Nationality**: NOT significant
- **Major field of study**: NOT significant
- **Institutional affiliation**: NOT significant

For **doctoral fellowships**:
- **Institutional bias**: SIGNIFICANT — applicants from prestigious institutions favored
- **Field of study bias**: SIGNIFICANT — certain fields advantaged
- **Gender bias**: SIGNIFICANT — males slightly favored
- **Nationality**: NOT significant

The fairness profile is clearly stage-dependent: doctoral committees show more bias than post-doctoral committees.

## Predictive Validity Findings

- Funded vs. rejected applicants had measurably different subsequent bibliometric performance.
- The selection procedure DID achieve its stated aim of selecting the strongest junior scientists.
- Predictive validity was higher than typical for grant peer review.

## Selection Criteria Applied at BIF

Reviewers evaluated:
1. **Academic record** — grades, degree institution, prior achievements
2. **Research project quality** — scientific merit of proposed work
3. **Applicant qualifications** — experience, motivation
4. **Host laboratory quality** — supervisor reputation, resources
5. **Career trajectory potential** — likelihood of becoming research leader
6. **References / letters of recommendation**

## Implications for Rubric Design

- Committee structure with explicit deliberation can produce higher reliability than asynchronous external review.
- Fellowship contexts (with smaller candidate pools and stronger CV signals) may be inherently more reliable than grant contexts.
- Bias diminishes at later career stages — perhaps because the candidate pool is more homogeneous, or because evaluators apply different standards.
- Predictive validity for fellowships is higher than for grants — the selection criteria for "promising junior scientist" may be more learnable than "best research idea."
