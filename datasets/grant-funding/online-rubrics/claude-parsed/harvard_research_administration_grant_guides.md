# Harvard Office of Sponsored Programs — Grant Writing Themes

Harvard OSP provides centralized grant writing resources, mock review services, and policy guidance for one of the largest research portfolios in the US.

## Core OSP Resources

### Pre-Award Services
- Budget development assistance
- Compliance review (human subjects, animals, biosafety)
- Subaward and consortium agreements
- Cost-sharing analysis
- Indirect cost rate negotiation

### Faculty-Facing Resources
- Grant writing workshops (multi-day)
- Mock review committees
- Specific Aims page consultations
- Foundation grants strategy
- Foundation relations office (separate)

## Harvard-Specific Themes

### Multi-Funder Diversification Strategy
- Federal (NIH, NSF) primary
- Foundation (HHMI, Gates, Wellcome) secondary
- Industry partnerships growing
- Philanthropic gifts via Foundation Relations

### Working with Foundation Relations
- Major foundations want institutional alignment
- Faculty don't approach foundations directly without coordination
- Strategic priorities at university and school level
- Program officer meetings often arranged through OSP/Foundation Relations

### Budget Conventions
- Harvard indirect rate (~70% on-campus federal)
- Faculty effort on grants typically 10-30%
- Postdoc and student support standard
- Equipment >$5K requires special justification

## What OSP Emphasizes in Workshops

### Specific Aims Page
- Standard four-paragraph structure
- Hypothesis-driven aims
- Aim independence
- Conceptual figure where helpful
- Iterate 10+ times

### Significance and Innovation
- Frame for non-specialist reviewers
- "So what?" test
- Cite competitor work fairly
- Innovation as novel application

### Approach
- Each aim with subsections
- Statistical analysis plan
- Power calculation with citation
- Sample size justification
- Rigor and reproducibility language
- Authentication of resources
- Alternatives if primary fails

### Investigator and Environment
- Harvard institutional commitment cited
- Specific cores and resources named
- Collaborator letters specific
- Productivity proportionate to scope

## OSP's Implicit Rubric

### What Wins
1. Strong Specific Aims with clear hypothesis
2. Independent aims (no failure cascade)
3. Quantitative preliminary data
4. Rigor language explicit
5. Investigator-environment match leveraging Harvard resources
6. Realistic budget aligned with Harvard indirect costs
7. Compliance addressed (IRB, IACUC, biosafety)
8. Letters of support from senior collaborators
9. Innovation framed as feasible
10. Significance landed in 30 seconds

### What OSP Catches Pre-Submission
1. Budget errors (effort, indirect cost calculation)
2. Compliance gaps (IRB protocol numbers, IACUC)
3. Subaward complications
4. Format violations (page limits, font, margins)
5. Missing required sections (data sharing, vertebrate animals)

## Harvard Academic Voice
- Confident but not arrogant
- Cite competitor work generously
- Position your contribution within field
- Avoid hype language
- Acknowledge limitations in Pitfalls/Alternatives
