# Common Grant Proposal Errors

## Top Errors

### 1. Not Following Instructions
The biggest mistake, even though it's the easiest to avoid. Foundations, corporations, and government agencies receive thousands of grant proposals — one way they sort the amateurs from the professionals is to see who followed directions.

**Avoid by:**
- Read all the instructions in the application package, no matter how trivial they appear
- Use a checklist to double-check formatting, word count, and attachments
- Print out the RFA/NOFO and highlight requirements
- Have someone else verify compliance before submission

### 2. Poor Formatting and Organization
NIH and other agencies have set basic rules on page margin, font sizes, and figures. These instructions are simple and legible, yet most grant writers fail on the basics, accounting for most application rejections.

**Avoid by:**
- Verify font (typically Arial, Helvetica, Palatino, Georgia, 11pt minimum)
- Verify margins (0.5" minimum)
- Use clear section headers
- Number figures and tables consistently
- Verify page limits before final submission

### 3. Weak or Vague Needs Statement
A common error is failing to clearly articulate the problem your project addresses. The needs statement sets the stage for your proposal; if the funder doesn't understand the problem or why it matters, they're unlikely to fund your solution.

**Avoid by:**
- Use current data, case studies, and expert opinions
- Establish the problem clearly
- Show the issue's impact on targeted communities
- Use specific statistics, not generalizations

### 4. Misaligned Budget
Budgets reflect priorities for your project, not just figures. Far too many applicants send around inflated budgets that have little to do with the proposal's story, which catches the funders' eyes and diminishes your credibility.

**Avoid by:**
- Make sure the budget aligns with the goals and timeline specified in the proposal
- Don't use round numbers or unsubstantiated cost estimates
- Justify each line item
- Match personnel effort to scope
- Avoid unjustified equipment requests

### 5. Missing or Weak Evaluation Plan
Failing to show how you'll measure success is a critical oversight. Funders want to know their investment will make a measurable impact.

**Avoid by:**
- Include both process and outcome metrics
- Identify who will conduct the evaluation
- Specify data collection methods
- Include baseline assessments where appropriate
- Tie evaluation to specific objectives

### 6. Late Submission
Last-minute submission usually leads to typos, missing forms, and lost attachments. Reviewers can tell when a proposal is rushed.

**Avoid by:**
- Plan for submission 1 week before deadline
- Account for institutional sign-off time (often 5+ business days)
- Test all upload systems early
- Have backup plans for technical failures

### 7. Careless Editing
Careless editing is problematic when a program officer has to read many proposals on the same topic and has to reread your sentences because of typos and grammatical errors.

**Avoid by:**
- Multiple proofreading passes
- Read aloud
- Use Grammarly or similar tools
- Have non-author proofread
- Don't rely solely on spell-check

## Additional Common Errors

### 8. Not Having a Compelling Narrative
- Project lacks a story arc
- Reader can't easily follow why → what → how
- No emotional hook for the problem

### 9. Generic Proposal
- Could fit any organization or any funder
- No tailoring to specific RFA requirements
- No demonstration of unique fit

### 10. Failure to Address Sustainability
- No plan for what happens after grant ends
- Funder feels their investment will be wasted
- No diversification of funding strategy

### 11. Weak Letters of Support
- Generic letters that say nothing specific
- Letters from inappropriate people
- Missing key partner letters

### 12. Inadequate Preliminary Data
- Especially for research grants
- Reviewers can't gauge feasibility
- Hypothesis seems untested

### 13. Overpromising
- More than can be done in time/budget
- Reviewers see lack of realism
- Damage credibility

### 14. Underselling
- Too modest about innovations
- Failing to highlight competitive advantages
- Not making the case for funding

### 15. Ignoring the Funder's Voice
- Not using funder's preferred language
- Missing key strategic priorities
- Not showing alignment with mission

## Pre-Submission Checklist

- [ ] Read RFA/NOFO multiple times
- [ ] Verified all required documents
- [ ] Met page limits
- [ ] Formatted according to guidelines
- [ ] Budget matches narrative
- [ ] Letters of support obtained
- [ ] Biosketches updated
- [ ] References complete
- [ ] Proofread by multiple people
- [ ] Submitted with time to spare
- [ ] Follow-up plan in place
