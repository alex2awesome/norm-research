# Inger Mewburn — The Thesis Whisperer / I Love Mondays — Grant Themes

Inger Mewburn is an academic at ANU and runs the influential Thesis Whisperer blog and I Love Mondays newsletter. Covers grant writing primarily from an Australian/UK academic angle but globally influential.

## Recurring Grant-Writing Themes

### Grant Writing as a Genre
- Grants are a learned writing genre with conventions
- Reading funded examples teaches the genre
- Structure carries meaning; reviewers expect conventions
- Voice should be confident but not arrogant

### The Time Investment Reality
- Major grant: 200+ hours
- Smaller grant: 50-100 hours
- Build grant writing into normal weekly schedule
- Don't try to write a grant in 2 weeks before deadline

### "Write Now, Edit Later"
- First draft is exploration
- Don't edit while drafting
- Pomodoro-style writing sessions
- Get a complete draft 6+ weeks before deadline

### Feedback Hierarchy
1. Self-edit (first 3-5 drafts)
2. Trusted peer in your field (next 2-3 drafts)
3. Senior mentor (1-2 reviews)
4. Outside-field reader (1 review for clarity)
5. Research office formatting check (final)

## Australian-Specific Themes

### ARC Discovery Project
- Project Description: 6 pages (very short)
- Significance and Innovation: 1 page
- Research Plan: 4 pages
- Project Quality and Innovation, Investigator, Feasibility, Benefit criteria

### NHMRC Investigator Grant
- Grants the person, not the project (since 2019)
- Track record and leadership central
- Future research vision over specific aims

### Mid-Career Researcher Trap
- ARC/NHMRC scores plateau in mid-career
- Need to differentiate from senior PIs
- "Field of Research" code matters for benchmarking

## Mewburn's Universal Tips

### On Voice
- Be confident, not aggressive
- "We will determine X" not "We hope to elucidate X"
- Avoid academic hedging that signals weakness
- Don't oversell — reviewers smell hype

### On Structure
- Use signposting throughout
- Topic sentences carry the argument
- White space is your friend
- Headings and bold guide the eye

### On Significance
- Lead with the question, not the methods
- Connect to public/societal/disciplinary concerns
- Don't assume reviewers share your enthusiasm
- "So what?" test for every paragraph

### On Methodology
- Specific enough to evaluate
- Cite published methods
- Justify your approach over alternatives
- Acknowledge limitations and pitfalls

## On Resilience and Rejection

### Rejection Is the Norm
- Funding rates 10-25% across most schemes
- Most successful PIs have folder of rejected grants
- Don't take it personally
- Read feedback once, set aside, return with senior colleague

### Resubmission Strategy
- Address every critique substantively
- Use reviewer language verbatim where possible
- Acknowledge fair criticism
- Politely defend where critique is wrong
- Bold the changes in body text

## On Career Stage and Grant Strategy

### Early Career
- Smaller grants build CV
- Learn the genre with low stakes
- Reuse content across applications
- Find mentor for mock review

### Mid Career
- Compete for major grants
- Build collaborator network
- Service as reviewer
- Mentor early-career colleagues

### Senior Career
- Major / multi-year grants
- Consortia and centers
- Strategic funding diversification
- Mentorship and review service

## "I Love Mondays" Newsletter Specific Themes

### Productive Writing Habits
- Morning writing sessions
- Tiny goals (250 words/day)
- Track progress
- Celebrate small wins

### Work-Life in Academia
- Grant writing is work, but life continues
- Boundaries around evening/weekend work
- Rest is part of productivity
- Long-term sustainability over heroic effort

## Implicit Rubric From Thesis Whisperer
1. Clear question with broad significance
2. Conventional structure for the funder
3. Confident, declarative voice
4. Specific methodology with citations
5. Realistic timeline with milestones
6. Feasibility evidence (preliminary work, team, resources)
7. Investigator narrative appropriate for career stage
8. Engagement with the field's current debates
9. Acknowledgment of limitations
10. Polish — no typos, formatting errors, length violations
