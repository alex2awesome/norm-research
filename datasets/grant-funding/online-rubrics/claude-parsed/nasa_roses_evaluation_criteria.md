# NASA ROSES - Proposal Evaluation Criteria

## Three Standard Evaluation Criteria

NASA evaluates proposals using three standard criteria:

### 1. Intrinsic Scientific or Technical Merit of the Proposed Work
- The strength of the science questions, hypotheses, and goals
- Whether the methodology is sound and appropriate
- The qualifications of the proposing team
- Open Science and Data Management Plan (OSDMP) - if required, evaluated as part of intrinsic merit

### 2. Relevance of the Proposed Work to NASA
- Alignment with NASA's strategic goals
- Relevance to the specific program element being applied to
- Use of and contribution to NASA missions, datasets, models

### 3. Suitability of the Proposed Costs
- Cost reasonableness
- Cost realism
- Are the proposed costs commensurate with the scope of work?

## Evaluation Process

A peer review panel comprised of reviewers with expertise relevant to the content of the proposal evaluates the proposal based on:
- Scientific and technical merit
- Perceived programmatic relevance
- Cost reasonableness

A minimum of three unconflicted and unbiased panel reviewers shall contribute to the panel rating.

## Adjectival Ratings
Each criterion is given a rating: Excellent, Very Good, Good, Fair, or Poor, often summarized into:
- E/VG = high merit (likely selectable)
- G = competitive but with weaknesses
- F/P = generally not selectable

## Selection Recommendations

A NASA Program Officer develops a recommendation based on:
- The peer review
- Any program-unique criteria stated in the funding announcement
- Relevance to objectives stated in the funding announcement and to NASA's strategic goals
- Comparison with competing proposals of equal merit
- Available resources

## Dual-Anonymous Peer Review (DAPR)
NASA uses DAPR for many ROSES programs. Reviewers do not know the identity of proposers during the initial scientific evaluation. Identifying information about proposers is added only after merit assessment is complete.
