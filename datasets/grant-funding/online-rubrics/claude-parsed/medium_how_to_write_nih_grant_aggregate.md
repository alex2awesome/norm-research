# Medium — Aggregated NIH Grant Writing Advice

Aggregated themes from top-clapped Medium articles tagged "NIH grant writing", "specific aims page", and related, written by junior PIs and grant consultants.

## Universal Themes Across Top Articles

### Specific Aims Page Is the Application
- "I'd argue 90% of your grant is the Specific Aims page"
- 1 page = your entire chance for triaged applications
- Iterate 10-20 times before submitting
- Get feedback at this stage from non-experts

### The Four-Paragraph Specific Aims Structure
1. **The hook**: Importance + state of knowledge + critical gap
2. **The need**: Long-term goal + objective + central hypothesis + rationale
3. **The approach**: Strategy + your qualifications + innovation
4. **The aims**: 2-3 numbered, hypothesis-driven, independent aims + closing impact statement

This structure repeats across virtually every Medium article on the topic.

### "Show, Don't Tell" for Significance
- Don't write "this work is significant because..."
- Show significance through framing the gap
- Use a conceptual figure
- Cite the high-impact papers your work builds on
- Connect to public health priorities (NIH-specific)

### Innovation Without Recklessness
- "Novel application of established methods" framing
- High-risk/high-reward labels are a trap for junior PIs
- Innovation in concept + feasibility in approach
- Cite the methods you're adapting

## Common Medium Article Frameworks

### "The Triangle of Death"
- Significance + Innovation + Approach must be in balance
- Strong significance + weak approach = unscored
- Strong approach + low significance = funded only at high paylines
- Innovation alone cannot save a poorly-justified project

### "The Reviewer's Day in Your Grant"
- Reviewer 1 spends 6-10 hours
- Reviewer 2 spends 4-6 hours
- Reviewer 3 spends 3-5 hours
- 80% of impressions form in the first 2 pages

### "The 30-Second Test"
- Hand your Specific Aims to a non-specialist
- Ask: "What's the question? Why does it matter? What will you do?"
- If they can't answer in 30 seconds, rewrite

### "The Six Bullet Points You Need"
1. The problem
2. What's known
3. What's missing
4. Your hypothesis
5. Your approach
6. Why it matters

## Author-Voice Themes

### Junior PI Authors Repeatedly Say
- "I wish I'd started 6 months earlier"
- "Get program officer feedback at Specific Aims stage"
- "Find a senior PI to mock-review"
- "Read funded grants in your area before drafting"
- "Apply early and often"

### Grant Consultant Authors Emphasize
- Process: write daily, iterate, get feedback, polish
- Structure: follow conventions; reviewers expect them
- Voice: confident, declarative, specific
- Visuals: figures save words
- Editing: cut 20-30% in final pass

## Implicit Rubric From Medium Article Aggregate

### What Wins (in order of mention frequency)
1. Strong Specific Aims page with four-paragraph structure
2. Hypothesis-driven, independent Aims
3. Quantitative preliminary data with figures
4. Clear Significance landed in 30 seconds
5. Rigor and reproducibility language
6. Statistical analysis plan
7. Realistic timeline
8. Investigator-environment match
9. Innovation framed as feasible
10. Letters of support that are specific

### What Loses (in order of mention frequency)
1. Co-dependent aims
2. Vague Significance
3. Insufficient preliminary data
4. Methodological vagueness
5. Innovation overclaim
6. Missing rigor language
7. Padded budget
8. Generic letters
9. Unrealistic timeline
10. Investigator overreach
