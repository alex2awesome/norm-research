# NSF CAREER Award - Review Criteria

## Standard Merit Review Criteria
NSF CAREER proposals are evaluated using two National Science Board-approved merit review criteria:
- **Intellectual Merit**: encompasses the potential to advance knowledge
- **Broader Impacts**: encompasses the potential to benefit society

For a CAREER Award, the broader impacts criterion carries extra weight because the program explicitly values educational leadership.

## Five Elements of Evaluation
1. Potential to advance knowledge (Intellectual Merit) and benefit society (Broader Impacts)
2. Extent to which proposed activities suggest and explore creative, original, or potentially transformative concepts
3. Whether the plan for carrying out proposed activities is well-reasoned, well-organized, and based on sound rationale with mechanisms to assess success
4. How well qualified is the individual to conduct the proposed activities
5. Whether there are adequate resources available to carry out the proposed activities

## CAREER-Specific Criteria

### Integration of Research and Education
The defining feature of a CAREER proposal is the integration of research and education. Reviewers expect to see a cohesive plan where:
- Research activities inform educational activities
- Educational activities inform research activities
- The PI articulates a coherent vision for their academic career
- Plans extend beyond the typical 5-year award period

### Departmental/Institutional Endorsement
- Evidence of institutional support
- Department's commitment to the PI's career development
- Mentoring plan from senior faculty

## Career Development Plan
Reviewers also assess the CAREER plan as a coherent integrated career trajectory rather than two separate proposals (research + education).
