# Cheeky Scientist — Isaiah Hankel on Grant Strategy

Cheeky Scientist is an academic career site focused on PhD/postdoc transition. Hankel publishes occasional grant-focused content emphasizing career-stage strategy.

## Core Themes

### Grant Writing as Career Capital
- Grants are CV currency for academic and industry roles
- Even unfunded competitive proposals signal capability
- Industry roles (medical writing, grants administration, R&D management) value grant writing experience

### Postdoc Grant Strategy
- F32 (NRSA postdoc) before K99/R00 transition award
- Foundation postdoc fellowships (Burroughs Wellcome, HFSP, EMBO, Helen Hay Whitney) bridge to faculty
- Each application is practice for the next; reuse infrastructure
- A K99 application takes 6-12 months to do well

### The Three-Document Framework
1. **Research Plan** — what you'll do, why, how
2. **Career Development Plan** — what skills you'll gain, mentorship structure
3. **Mentor / Sponsor Statement** — your team's commitment and qualifications

All three must align. Reviewers look for inconsistencies.

### What Reviewers of Career Awards Want to See
- Clear trajectory from current postdoc to independent investigator
- Mentor track record of training successful independents
- Skills you'll gain that you don't currently have
- A research project that becomes your independent program
- Institutional commitment (lab space, startup, protected time)

### Hankel's Career Award Tips
- Don't propose your mentor's project — propose YOUR project
- Specific Aims should be doable in 5 years (2 K99 + 3 R00)
- Career Development Plan should list specific named courses, workshops, conferences
- Mentor team should cover your skills gaps explicitly
- Letters of support quantify protected time and resources

## Industry Crossover Themes

### SBIR/STTR Specific Advice
- Phase I: feasibility, $250-300K, 6-12 months
- Phase II: full development, $1.5-2M, 2 years
- Commercialization plan is half the application
- Letters of intent from customers/partners weighted heavily
- Technical sections must address commercial viability, not just science

### Foundation Grant Strategy
- Read the foundation's annual report before applying
- Match your project to recent funded portfolio
- Foundation officers are accessible — call them
- Smaller foundations have less competition but smaller awards
- Multi-year foundation grants signal independence to academic search committees

## Implicit Rubric Cheeky Scientist Promotes

### For Trainee Awards (F, K, foundation postdoc)
1. Clear training trajectory with measurable skills gain
2. Mentor team that visibly fills your gaps
3. Project that's yours, not your mentor's
4. Career Development Plan with specific activities
5. Institutional commitment documented in letters
6. Productivity proportionate to career stage
7. Strong letters from mentors and outside referees

### For Industry / SBIR
1. Clear commercial path with named partners
2. Technical feasibility demonstrated by preliminary data
3. Market analysis with realistic TAM/SAM
4. Team that includes business as well as technical expertise
5. Milestones tied to go/no-go decisions
6. IP strategy articulated

## Hankel's "Top Reasons Grants Fail" (Aggregated)
- Mismatch between applicant career stage and award mechanism
- Project too ambitious or too narrow
- Mentor team doesn't cover all proposed methods
- Career Development Plan is generic
- Specific Aims are not hypothesis-driven
- No clear path to independence in K applications
- Industry partnerships not concrete in SBIR
