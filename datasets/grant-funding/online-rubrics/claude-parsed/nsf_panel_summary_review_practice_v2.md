# NSF Merit Review Process — Detailed Procedural Rubric (Wave 2 supplement)

Wave 1 has a basic NSF Merit Review entry; this Wave 2 file captures the procedural changes and the five elements within each criterion.

## NSF's Two Merit Review Criteria (formal rubric)

### Intellectual Merit
The potential to advance knowledge.

### Broader Impacts
The potential to benefit society and contribute to the achievement of specific, desired societal outcomes.

## Five Elements Reviewed for Each Criterion

NSF's review guidelines specify five questions reviewers ask of BOTH Intellectual Merit AND Broader Impacts:

1. **What is the potential** for the proposed activity to:
   - Advance knowledge and understanding (IM)
   - Benefit society or advance desired societal outcomes (BI)
2. **To what extent do the proposed activities suggest and explore creative, original or potentially transformative concepts?**
3. **Is the plan well-reasoned, well-organized, and based on sound rationale?**
4. **Does the plan incorporate a mechanism to assess success?**
5. **How well qualified is the individual, team, or institution to conduct the proposed activities? Are there adequate resources?**

## Examples of Desired Broader Impacts Outcomes (NSF's list)

1. Improving STEM education and educator development
2. Increasing public scientific literacy and public engagement with STEM
3. Improving the well-being of individuals in society
4. Developing a globally competitive STEM workforce
5. Building partnerships between academia, industry, and others
6. Improving national security
7. Increasing the economic competitiveness of the U.S.
8. Enhancing infrastructure for research and education

## Recent Procedural Changes (2024-2025)

NSF has implemented multiple changes:

1. **Reduced minimum external reviewers** from 3 to 2 per proposal
2. **Panel review made optional** rather than mandatory
3. **Panel summaries limited to 3-5 sentences** (vs previous detailed summaries)
4. **Increased emphasis on programmatic priorities** in funding decisions

## Three-Stage Review Process (in current operation)

### Stage 1: External Review
- 2-5 external mail reviewers per proposal
- Each provides written review and 1-5 score
- Reviews shared with applicant after decision

### Stage 2: Panel Review (now optional)
- When held: 10-30 reviewers in 2-3 day panel
- Panel discusses each proposal
- Generates panel summary (3-5 sentences in current rules)
- Recommends funding category (Highly Competitive, Competitive, etc.)

### Stage 3: Program Officer Decision
- Program officer integrates external reviews and panel input
- Considers programmatic balance, strategic priorities
- Makes funding recommendation
- Senior NSF approval

## Rating Scale

External reviewers rate proposals on five-point scale:
- **E (Excellent)** — outstanding proposal
- **V (Very Good)** — high quality
- **G (Good)** — competent, no fatal flaws
- **F (Fair)** — has weaknesses
- **P (Poor)** — fundamental problems

Panel categories combine into:
- Highly Competitive
- Competitive
- Recommended for declination

## Implications for Rubric Design

NSF's 2024-2025 procedural changes reflect:
1. **Reviewer burden reduction** — fewer required reviewers, shorter summaries
2. **Increased program officer authority** — more discretion in funding decisions
3. **Less reliance on panel consensus** — external reviews can suffice
4. **Pragmatic acknowledgment** that elaborate panel summaries don't add proportional value

The shift away from intensive panel deliberation aligns with Fogelholm 2012 finding that panels don't improve reliability over averaged scores.
