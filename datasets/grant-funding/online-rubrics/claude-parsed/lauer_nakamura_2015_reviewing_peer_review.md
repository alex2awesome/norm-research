# Lauer & Nakamura 2015: Reviewing Peer Review at the NIH

NEJM perspective by NIH leaders summarizing what is known about NIH peer review and identifying targets for empirical study and reform.

## Issues Identified with Current NIH Review (rubric of concerns)

1. **Predictive validity is weak** — peer-review percentile scores in the top tier are at best imprecise predictors of bibliometric productivity (citation counts, RCR, etc.).
2. **Bibliometric measures themselves are contested** — citation counts, journal impact factors create distorting incentives.
3. **Possible bias on inappropriate factors**:
   - Investigator age / career stage
   - Sex
   - Race / ethnicity
   - Institutional wealth
4. **Score compression** at top of distribution — reviewers cannot reliably distinguish "great" from "good."
5. **Lack of accounting for portfolio effects** — individual scores don't reflect strategic balance, risk diversification.

## Reform Proposals

1. **Anonymized applications** — randomized trials of redacting investigator identifiers to test whether bias on age/gender/race/institution affects scores.
2. **Range reporting / score binning** — replace fine-grained percentile scores with bands that honestly reflect the precision of reviewer judgment.
3. **Explicit weighting for portfolio diversity** in program-officer decisions.
4. **Strategic-goals weighting** — formally separate "is this the best science" from "does this serve agency mission."
5. **Investigator track record** weighted explicitly and transparently.
6. **For clinical trials: focus on clinical end points** rather than mechanism alone.

## Quality Criteria Used at NIH (the five 2009 criteria they discuss)

1. Significance
2. Investigator(s)
3. Innovation
4. Approach
5. Environment

Plus the **Overall Impact** score that reviewers give holistically.
