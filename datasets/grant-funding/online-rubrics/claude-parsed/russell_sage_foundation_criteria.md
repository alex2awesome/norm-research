# Russell Sage Foundation - Core Research Grants Criteria

## Application Process
RSF has 3 funding cycles per year and only considers proposals that are invited following review of an initial Letter of Inquiry (LOI):
- ~15% of LOIs receive an invitation to submit a full proposal (after peer review)
- Final funding rate for core research grants is approximately 6-10% of submitted LOIs

## Common Reasons for Decline at Initial Screen

The most common reasons for applications being declined at the initial screen:

### 1. Mission Misalignment
Application not perceived to be well-aligned with the Foundation's funding priorities

### 2. Insufficient Detail
Lacks sufficient detail on the research design, data, and methods to allow reviewers to assess the viability of the project

### 3. Poor Framing or Writing
Application is poorly framed or written

## Evaluation Standard
Grants are awarded at Trustee-approved or Presidential-Authority levels based on:
- **Merit**
- **Feasibility**
- **Potential impact**

## Required LOI Elements

LOIs should reflect the key elements of a complete proposal, with most of the space dedicated to:
- Hypothesis
- Appropriateness of the data
- Sample size and power calculations for quantitative studies
- Preliminary or pilot findings (if available)
- Research design

## Project Readiness Standard
RSF rarely considers projects for which the investigators have NOT already fully-developed:
- The research design
- The sample framework
- Access to data
- Survey instruments (and pre-tested them)
- Preliminary data analyses (if data publicly available)
- Some preliminary interviews (for qualitative studies)

## Research Priorities
The foundation is particularly interested in:
- Analyses that make use of newly available data
- Demonstrate novel uses of existing data
- Answer emerging or long-standing questions of interest in foundation program areas

## Multi-Disciplinary Review Process

RSF employs rigorous multi-disciplinary review at every stage. All LOIs and proposals are reviewed by:
- Program staff
- External reviewers from multiple disciplines selected for relevant expertise
- Members of one of the standing Advisory Committees
- Or some combination of these

## RSF Program Areas

### Behavioral Economics
- Decisions in real-world settings
- Implications of behavioral findings for policy

### Decision Making and Human Behavior in Context
- Social/cultural context shaping decision making

### Future of Work
- Technology, automation, and labor market change
- Workforce inequality and policy

### Race, Ethnicity, and Immigration
- Inequality across racial/ethnic groups
- Immigration policy and outcomes

### Social, Political, and Economic Inequality
- Trends in inequality
- Causes and consequences
- Policy interventions

### Special Initiatives (rotating)
- E.g., Pandemic effects, AI and society

## Notable Features
- Strong preference for fully-developed research projects
- Multi-disciplinary social science focus
- Heavy use of administrative data and large-scale surveys
- Focus on inequality and social problems
- Encourages preliminary work before applying
