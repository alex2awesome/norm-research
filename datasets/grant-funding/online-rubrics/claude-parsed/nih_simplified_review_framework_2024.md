# NIH Simplified Peer Review Framework (effective Jan 2025)

Major restructuring of NIH R01-style review effective for applications with due dates from January 25, 2025. Reorganizes the five regulatory criteria into three Factors.

## Background — The Old Five Criteria

Before 2025, NIH reviewers scored each application on 5 criteria, each on a 1-9 scale:
1. Significance
2. Investigator(s)
3. Innovation
4. Approach
5. Environment

Plus an Overall Impact Score (1-9, holistic).

## The New Three Factors (effective Jan 2025)

### Factor 1: Importance of the Research (scored 1-9)
Combines: **Significance + Innovation**

Reviewers evaluate:
- Importance of the proposed research in context of current scientific challenges and opportunities
- Whether the research advances knowledge in the field broadly
- Novelty of concepts, approaches, methodologies, or instrumentation

### Factor 2: Rigor and Feasibility (scored 1-9)
Combines: **Approach** + previously-separate ARC items

Reviewers evaluate:
- Strategy, methodology, analyses
- Inclusion criteria and coding for clinical/translational research
- Study timelines for clinical trials
- Plans for valid design and analysis of Phase III clinical trials
- Scientific premise and rigor of prior research
- Consideration of biological variables (sex, age, etc.)
- Authentication of key biological/chemical resources

### Factor 3: Expertise and Resources (NOT scored numerically)
Combines: **Investigator + Environment**

Reviewers select from a dropdown:
- "Appropriate" — no comments required
- "Additional expertise/resources needed" — comments required identifying gaps

This factor is evaluated for SUFFICIENCY rather than scored on a 1-9 scale.

## Overall Impact Score

Reviewers still produce a holistic Overall Impact Score (1-9) integrating Factors 1, 2, and 3.

## Rationale for the Restructuring

NIH stated the reform is intended to:
1. **Reduce reviewer bias** by separating "is this researcher capable" (Factor 3, sufficiency check) from "is this science important" (Factors 1-2, scored).
2. **Address racial disparities** documented by Erosheva et al. 2020 — moving Investigator/Environment to a non-scored factor reduces the channel through which bias operates.
3. **Reduce reviewer cognitive load** — scoring 3 factors instead of 5.
4. **Increase focus on the science itself**.

## Items Removed from Reviewer Evaluation (administratively reviewed instead)

Following the reform, peer reviewers no longer evaluate:
- Applications from Foreign Organizations
- Select Agents work
- Resource Sharing Plans

These are administratively reviewed by NIH staff before funding.

## Implications for Rubric Design

Connection to academic literature:
- Witteman 2019 (CIHR natural experiment) showed PI-focused criteria amplified gender bias — NIH's reform mirrors this finding.
- Erosheva 2020 showed criterion scores carry the disparity — moving Investigator to sufficiency-only check is direct response.
- Pier 2018 showed reviewers cannot reliably distinguish granular scores — fewer scored factors may not solve agreement issues but reduces dimensionality.
