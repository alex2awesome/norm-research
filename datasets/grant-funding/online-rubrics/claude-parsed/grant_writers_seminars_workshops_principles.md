# Grant Writers' Seminars and Workshops — Core Principles

GWSW (Russell, Morrison, et al.) is the most widely-attended NIH grant writing workshop. The "Grant Application Workbook" they sell is widely cited. Distilled principles below.

## The Russell-Morrison Framework

### The Specific Aims Page Template
GWSW codified the four-paragraph template that became standard:
1. **Importance and Need** — Hook, state of knowledge, critical gap
2. **Long-Term Goal, Objective, Hypothesis** — what your research program does, what THIS app does, central hypothesis with rationale
3. **Approach Overview** — Strategy, qualifications, innovation
4. **Aims** — 2-3 numbered aims, each hypothesis-driven and independent + closing impact

### The Aims Themselves
Each Aim should:
- Start with "To [reason]" (the WHY)
- Continue with active verb + WHAT
- State hypothesis if hypothesis-driven
- Brief description of approach
- Expected outcomes

Example: "Aim 1: To determine the role of X in Y, we will measure Z under conditions A, B, C. We hypothesize that X drives Y through mechanism M. Expected outcome: identification of the molecular driver of Y."

### Aim Independence Doctrine
- Aims should not be co-dependent
- One aim must not require success of another
- Failure of Aim 1 should not prevent Aim 2/3
- This is the most-cited GWSW principle

## Writing Style Principles

### Active, Declarative Voice
- "We will determine X"
- "Our preliminary data demonstrate Y"
- Avoid: "It is hoped that", "We will attempt", "Could potentially"

### Word Economy
- Every sentence must work
- Cut hedging
- Cut redundancy
- Cut unnecessary qualifiers
- Active voice over passive

### Visual Organization
- Bold for aim titles
- Underline sparingly within paragraphs
- White space between sections
- Conceptual figure where possible

## The "Five Hot Buttons" GWSW Emphasizes

### 1. Significance
- Important problem with broad implications
- Connection to NIH mission
- Filling a critical gap

### 2. Innovation
- Novel concepts, approaches, methodologies, tools
- Shift in paradigm or refinement of existing
- Innovation should be feasible

### 3. Approach
- Strategy, methodology, analyses well-reasoned
- Address strengths and weaknesses
- Manage risk through alternatives
- Sex as biological variable
- Rigor and reproducibility

### 4. Investigator
- Track record proportionate to career stage
- Independent productivity
- Collaborator strength

### 5. Environment
- Institutional support
- Resources match the project
- Collaborative arrangements add capability

## GWSW on Reviewers

### Reviewer Behavior
- Reviewers are tired, busy, and skeptical
- Specific Aims page is the whole grant for triage
- First paragraph forms first impression
- Reviewers reward clarity, not cleverness

### What Reviewers Ding
- Co-dependent aims
- Vague significance
- Insufficient preliminary data
- Missing rigor language
- Methodological vagueness
- Investigator overreach

### What Reviewers Reward
- Hypothesis-driven aims
- Quantitative preliminary data
- Specific methodology with citations
- Statistical analysis plan
- Realistic timeline
- Independent aims
- Career-stage-appropriate scope

## On Resubmission

### Strategy
- Address every critique
- Use reviewer language verbatim
- Quote critique, respond with substance
- Bold changes in body text
- Introduction page (1 page) for response summary

### Tone
- Diplomatic, not defensive
- Acknowledge fair criticism
- Politely push back where critique is wrong
- Always offer substance, not just rebuttal

## On Working with Program Officers

### When to Contact
- Pre-submission with Specific Aims draft
- Post-summary statement for path forward
- During preparation for fit/advice

### What POs Can Do
- Tips on writing strong applications
- Clarity on critiques
- Resubmission guidance
- Information on RFAs, paylines

### What POs Cannot Do
- Influence reviewer scores
- Guarantee funding
- Share confidential review

## GWSW's Most-Repeated Maxims
- "The Specific Aims page is the entire grant"
- "Aims must be independent"
- "Reviewers reward the appearance of having thought carefully"
- "Address rigor explicitly"
- "Preliminary data must be quantitative"
- "Cut by 30% in the final pass"

## Why GWSW Endures
- Concrete templates and examples
- Workbook format with exercises
- Multi-day immersive workshop
- Updated for NIH policy changes (rigor, simplified review, data sharing)
- Endorsed by many NIH-funded PIs as transformative
