# Communicating with NIH Program Officers

## Role of the Program Officer
Program officers (POs) read the study section's recommendations and make the final grant funding decisions — incorporating together:
- The score
- The reviews (summary statement)
- The ability of the grant to address the mission of the institute

## When to Contact a Program Officer

Recommended contact points:
- **Before submission** (to discuss fit, get advice)
- **After receiving summary statement** (to discuss path forward)
- **During grant preparation** (with specific aims draft)
- **For resubmission strategy** (after rejection)

POs are most supportive when contacted **early** in the proposal planning process — NOT 3-4 weeks before an NIH deadline.

## What POs Can Do

POs can provide:
- General tips and strategies on writing strong applications
- Greater clarity on reviewer critiques
- Areas of focus for a potential resubmission
- Guidance on whether your project fits the institute's mission
- Information on RFA opportunities you may not have noticed
- Insight into funding outlook for your area
- Information on the peer review meeting (if they attended)

## What POs CANNOT Do
- Cannot influence individual reviewer scores
- Cannot guarantee funding
- Cannot share confidential review discussions
- Cannot lobby reviewers on your behalf

## How to Communicate Effectively

### Email Best Practices
Program officers receive many emails a day. Compose your inquiry concisely with:
- Your name
- Reason for contacting them
- Your years in the lab/career stage
- The grant you are applying for
- Specific question(s)

### What to Send
Research Development recommends:
- Develop ideas in form of a draft Specific Aims page
- Email to the program officer
- 1-page summary of the project
- Your CV/biosketch
- Specific questions you have

### Phone vs. Email
- Email first to set up phone discussion if needed
- Phone calls are appropriate for nuanced discussions
- Don't cold-call; schedule via email

## Finding Your Program Officer
- After review, the PO is listed on your eRA Commons account
- Before submission, find PO based on:
  - Your scientific area within an institute
  - Names of POs on similar funded grants (NIH RePORTER)
  - Institute's program staff page
- Multiple institutes may be relevant — match your project to the institute mission

## Discussion Topics

### Pre-submission
- Does this fit your institute's priorities?
- Is the right mechanism R01, R21, R03, K?
- What's the funding outlook?
- Any specific RFAs to consider?
- Should I contact a specific study section?

### Post-summary statement
- What did the panel discuss?
- How significant are the criticisms?
- Should I resubmit or move on?
- What's the realistic path to funding?
- Any administrative supplements available?

## Key Principles
- Be respectful of their time
- Be concise
- Be specific
- Don't ask them to write your grant
- Don't pressure or lobby
- Build a long-term relationship — POs remember
