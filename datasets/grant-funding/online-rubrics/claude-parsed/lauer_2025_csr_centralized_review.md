# NIH Centralized Peer Review (2025) — Procedural Rubric

In 2025 NIH centralized peer review of grants and contracts under the Center for Scientific Review (CSR), projected to save $65 million. This represents one of the largest peer-review process reforms in NIH history.

## What Changed in 2025

Pre-2025: Peer review distributed across NIH Institutes/Centers (ICs):
- Some ICs had their own internal peer review
- Inconsistent application of criteria across ICs
- Varying study section structures
- Different reviewer assignment processes

Post-2025: Centralized at CSR:
- Single peer-review office for the majority of competing applications
- Consistent application of criteria
- Standardized study section structure
- Unified reviewer assignment

## Stated Goals (the reform rubric)

1. **Efficiency** — reduce duplication, save administrative cost (~$65M projected)
2. **Strengthen integrity** — single accountable office for peer-review quality
3. **Increase competition** — proposals compete in larger common pools
4. **Improve transparency** — unified policies and metrics across NIH

## Operational Changes

### Reviewer Assignment
- CSR Scientific Review Officers manage all assignments
- Conflicts of interest verified centrally
- Field-expertise matching standardized

### Study Section Structure
- ~200+ study sections at CSR
- Each focused on a specific research area
- Membership rotating, vetted for diversity

### Score Standardization
- Same 1-9 score scale across all study sections
- Same five criterion structure (now Three Factors per Simplified Framework 2025)
- Same rules on scoring distributions

## Integration with Simplified Review Framework

The 2025 centralization went into effect simultaneously with the Simplified Review Framework. Together:
- Centralization provides administrative consistency
- Simplified Framework provides scoring consistency
- Combined effect: maximum standardization of NIH grant review

## Concerns Raised by Researchers

1. **Loss of IC-specific expertise** — some ICs had specialized review knowledge
2. **One-size-fits-all rubric** may not fit specialized domains (e.g., clinical trials, behavioral research)
3. **CSR capacity** — can a single office handle all NIH peer review?
4. **Reviewer pool depth** — recruiting enough qualified reviewers across all areas

## Implications for Rubric Design

NIH's 2025 centralization represents a bet that:
1. **Standardization improves fairness** more than specialization improves accuracy
2. **Administrative efficiency** is worth potential loss of IC-specific judgment
3. **Single-rubric application** is more defensible than multiple competing rubrics
4. **Bias mitigation** benefits from consistent criteria application

This contrasts with the historical argument (e.g., Lamont 2009) that **discipline-specific evaluation norms** are essential for valid assessment. The 2025 centralization assumes those discipline-specific norms can be embedded WITHIN study sections rather than across separate IC review processes.

## Connection to Other 2025 Reforms

NIH's 2024-2025 reform package includes:
1. **Simplified Review Framework** — 3 Factors instead of 5 criteria
2. **F32 revision** — separate candidate from sponsor evaluation
3. **CSR centralization** — unified administrative process
4. **Updated FORMS-I application** — streamlined submission
5. **Common Forms** for biographical sketch and current/pending support

This is the largest coordinated reform of NIH peer review since the introduction of the five-criterion structure in 2009.
