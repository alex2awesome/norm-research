# MIT VPR / RAS — Grant Writing and Administration Themes

MIT's Office of the Vice President for Research and Research Administration Services provide grant writing support, with strong DARPA, DOE, NSF orientation given MIT's portfolio.

## MIT-Specific Funder Mix

### Heavy DARPA / DoD / DOE Engagement
- DARPA BAA proposals
- ONR, ARO, AFOSR
- DOE Office of Science
- These have specific conventions different from NIH/NSF

### NSF Engineering and CS Heavy
- NSF CISE, ENG directorates
- Larger center grants (ERCs, STCs)
- Industry partnerships expected

### Foundation Grants
- Sloan Research Fellowships
- Packard Fellowships
- Simons Foundation
- HHMI

## DARPA Proposal Conventions (MIT-Specific Resources)

### Heilmeier Catechism (MIT teaches this)
- What are you trying to do? Articulate without jargon.
- How is it done today, and what are the limits?
- What is new in your approach and why will it succeed?
- Who cares? If you succeed, what difference will it make?
- What are the risks?
- How much will it cost? How long will it take?
- What are the mid-term and final exams?

### DARPA-Specific Sections
- Statement of Work (SOW) with detailed milestones
- Quad chart (1-page visual summary)
- Phase structure with go/no-go decisions
- Government purpose rights vs. unlimited rights
- Cost and schedule realism

### What Wins DARPA
- Bold technical vision
- Quantifiable performance metrics
- Risk acknowledged with mitigation
- Team includes industry partners (often)
- Phase milestones with measurable success criteria

## NSF Engineering / CS Themes

### Project Description Conventions
- 15 pages; very tight
- Intellectual Merit + Broader Impacts equal weight
- Industry collaboration adds weight in engineering
- Education/outreach plans must be specific

### Center Grants (ERC, STC)
- Multi-investigator, multi-institutional
- Industry membership required (ERC)
- Education at all levels (K-12 through grad)
- Diversity and inclusion plans substantive
- Strategic vision for 10-year horizon

## DOE Office of Science

### Specific Conventions
- Mission-oriented (energy, materials, basic science)
- National lab partnerships valued
- User facility usage (e.g., synchrotrons, supercomputers)
- Long-term research strategy

### Early Career Research Program
- Within 10 years of PhD
- 5-year award, ~$750K total
- Single PI, mission-relevant research
- Research plan + biographical sketch + budget

## MIT VPR / RAS Implicit Rubric

### What Wins Across Mechanisms
1. Bold technical vision with measurable success criteria
2. Industry/national lab partnerships where relevant
3. Quantitative preliminary data
4. Specific milestones with go/no-go decisions
5. Team composition that visibly covers all gaps
6. MIT-specific resources cited (cores, centers, MIT.nano)
7. Education/outreach plans specific
8. Diversity/inclusion plans substantive
9. Realistic budget aligned with MIT indirect costs
10. Risk acknowledged with mitigation

### What MIT VPR Catches Pre-Submission
1. Cost realism issues
2. Compliance gaps (export control, ITAR)
3. IP and data rights (especially for DARPA/DoD)
4. Budget errors (effort, indirect calculation)
5. Format violations
6. Missing sections (especially DoD-specific)

## Working With RAS

### Pre-Award Services
- Budget development
- Subcontract negotiation
- Compliance review
- Cost share analysis
- Submission via MIT Coeus system

### Timeline
- 4-6 weeks before deadline: budget + admin docs
- 2 weeks: full draft for compliance review
- 1 week: final formatting and submission

## Voice and Style for MIT-Style Proposals

### Engineering / DARPA Voice
- Quantitative claims with metrics
- Risk-aware but optimistic
- Industry partnerships highlighted
- Performance benchmarks specified
- Schedule with concrete milestones

### Academic NSF Voice
- More expansive on intellectual merit
- Broader impacts must be specific
- Education plans tied to MIT programs
- Diversity plans substantive
