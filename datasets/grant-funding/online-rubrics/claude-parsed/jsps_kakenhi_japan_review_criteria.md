# JSPS KAKENHI - Peer Review Process and Criteria

## Overview
KAKENHI is the largest competitive research funding scheme in Japan, supporting diverse academic research based on researchers' free ideas. The review for KAKENHI is carried out by the Scientific Research Grant Committee of the Japan Society for the Promotion of Science (JSPS), based on the Research Proposal Document.

## Reviewer Pool
- Approximately **8,000 reviewers** selected from each academic field
- Prominent researchers who work hard at each academic field
- Stand at the front of knowledge creation
- Review and assess applications for their peers

## Review Method
From FY2018 onward, call for proposals and proposal review are conducted in accordance with the new Review Section Table and by a revised Review Method. Diverse academic research based on researchers' free ideas is reviewed through peer review by researchers in related fields.

## Key Evaluation Items (Standard KAKENHI)

### 1. Research Subject
- Originality and creativity
- Importance and relevance
- Significance of contributions
- Position relative to international research

### 2. Research Method
- Validity of research plan
- Feasibility
- Achievability within funding period
- Logical structure of research approach

### 3. Research Capability
- Track record
- Research environment
- Necessary equipment, samples, expertise
- Track record of research execution

### 4. Research Funding
- Reasonableness of requested budget
- Necessity of expenses
- Cost-benefit reasonableness

## Two-Stage Review (Some Categories)
For larger grants:
- **Stage 1**: Document review by 5-6 reviewers
- **Stage 2**: Panel discussion and selection

## Grant Categories
- Grant-in-Aid for Specially Promoted Research (top tier, large)
- Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (S, A, B, C) — general categories by size
- Grant-in-Aid for Young Scientists
- Grant-in-Aid for Challenging Research (Pioneering, Exploratory)
- Grant-in-Aid for Early-Career Scientists
- Grant-in-Aid for Transformative Research Areas

## Evaluation Scale
- Generally uses a 4-point or 5-point scale
- Weighted across the four evaluation items
- Final ranking determines funding within available budget

## Notable Features
- Free-form research (researchers propose their own ideas)
- No predetermined topical priorities (unlike strategic programs)
- Continuous funding model with multi-year grants
