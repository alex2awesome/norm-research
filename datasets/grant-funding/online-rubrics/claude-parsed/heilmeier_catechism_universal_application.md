# Heilmeier Catechism - Universal Grant Proposal Framework

## Background
George Heilmeier developed a set of questions during his tenure at DARPA to help reviewers evaluate proposed research. The Catechism is integrated into review criteria for most other funding agencies today and is widely used in NSF program officer presentations to help researchers write competitive proposals.

## The 8 Universal Questions (Expanded Form)

### 1. What are you trying to do?
State the goal of your project using **absolutely no jargon**.
- Articulate objectives clearly
- A child or non-specialist should understand
- This becomes your "elevator pitch"

### 2. How is it done today, and what are the limits of current practice?
- Why are improvements needed?
- What are the consequences of doing nothing?
- What is the state of the art?
- Where is current practice failing?

### 3. What's new in your approach, and why do you think it will be successful?
- What preliminary work have you done?
- How have you tested your assumptions on a small scale?
- What new insight, tool, method, or perspective do you bring?
- Why is now the right time?

### 4. Who cares?
- Who will benefit from this work?
- What stakeholders will be affected?
- Specific audiences (researchers, clinicians, public, industry, policymakers)

### 5. If you're successful, what difference will it make?
- The "so what" question
- Concrete impacts and changes
- Both intellectual and practical contributions
- Long-term significance

### 6. What are the risks, and what is your contingency plan if the initial approach fails?
- Identify specific technical risks
- Identify managerial/operational risks
- Mitigation strategies for each
- Alternative approaches if Plan A doesn't work

### 7. How much will it cost?
- Total project cost
- Justification for the cost
- Cost-benefit comparison

### 8. How long will it take?
- Project duration
- Major phases and timing
- Critical milestones
- What are the mid-term and final "exams" to check for success?

## Mapping to Common Agency Criteria

| Heilmeier Question | NIH | NSF | ERC | Horizon EU |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. What | Specific Aims | Project Description | Synopsis | Excellence |
| 2. How is it done today | Significance background | Intellectual Merit context | State of the art | Excellence |
| 3. What's new | Innovation | Intellectual Merit creativity | Ground-breaking nature | Excellence |
| 4. Who cares | Significance impact | Broader Impacts | Excellence + ambition | Impact |
| 5. Difference | Significance + Approach | Broader Impacts | Ambition | Impact |
| 6. Risks | Approach (alternatives) | Plan + assess success | Feasibility | Implementation |
| 7. Cost | Budget | Budget | Resources | Implementation |
| 8. Time | Approach (timeline) | Timeline | Feasibility | Implementation |

## Why It Matters
- Forces clarity (no jargon)
- Demands honesty about risks
- Requires evidence of preliminary work
- Anchors review in measurable outcomes
- Provides a common language across agencies

## How to Use
1. Answer each question in 1-2 sentences before writing
2. Review each section of your proposal against the questions
3. Use as a "self-review" before submission
4. Show to non-expert colleagues — can they answer the questions from your text?

## When Heilmeier Doesn't Quite Fit
- Pure exploratory research where outcomes are unknown by design
- Humanities scholarship where "metrics" feel reductive
- Policy/social science research with diffuse impacts
- Long-term basic research where applications are decades away

Even in these cases, the spirit of the Catechism (clarity, honest risk assessment, measurable progress) applies.
