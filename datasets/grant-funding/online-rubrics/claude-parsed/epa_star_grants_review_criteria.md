# EPA Science to Achieve Results (STAR) - Grant Review Criteria

## Overview of STAR Program
EPA's Science to Achieve Results (STAR) program is the primary competitive, peer-reviewed extramural grant program. Has awarded over 4,100 grants nationwide since 1995.

## Review Approach
Applicants must propose EPA mission relevant research based on excellent science as determined through peer review by experts drawn from the national and international scientific community.

## Two-Tier Review Process

### Tier 1: Quality Peer Review
A panel of independent scientific experts evaluates each proposal for scientific quality, rating proposals as:
- **Excellent**
- **Very Good**
- **Good**
- **Fair**
- **Poor**

### Tier 2: Relevance Review
Following peer review, EPA staff conducts a "relevance review":
- Limited to proposals rated as "excellent" or "very good"
- Assesses relevance to EPA's mission and programmatic priorities
- The program funds NO proposals rated below "very good"

## Standard Evaluation Criteria

### 1. Research Quality
- Scientific merit of the proposed research
- Originality and significance of hypotheses
- Soundness of approach and methods

### 2. Innovation
- Use of innovative concepts and approaches
- Creative problem solving

### 3. Investigator Qualifications
- Track record of the PI and team
- Adequacy of facilities and resources

### 4. Project Plan/Timeline
- Realistic milestones and timelines
- Sample sizes and statistical power
- Risk mitigation

### 5. Quality Assurance/Quality Control
- Guidance on Satisfying EPA Quality Requirements for STAR Grants requires documenting quality assurance in the proposal

### 6. Relevance to EPA Mission
- Linkage to EPA strategic plan
- Application to environmental decision-making
- Stakeholder engagement
