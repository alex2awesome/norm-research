# Hello PhD Podcast — Grant Writing Themes

Hello PhD is a popular podcast for biomedical PhD students and postdocs. Recurring grant-writing episodes synthesize practical advice for trainees writing F31, F32, K99, and first R01 applications.

## Recurring Themes

### Start Early, Iterate Often
- F31/F32: 80-150 hours
- K99: 300-500 hours
- First R01: 200-400 hours
- Specific Aims: iterate 20+ times
- Get feedback at multiple stages

### Find a Mentor or Mock Reviewer
- Senior PIs read drafts at multiple stages
- Mock review committees score 1-9 NIH style
- Outside-field reader for clarity check
- Editing services for non-native English speakers

### Engage Program Officers Early
- POs accessible by email
- Send Specific Aims for fit feedback
- Ask about mechanism, payline
- Build relationship over time

### Read Funded Applications
- NIH RePORTER for titles, abstracts
- Ask senior PIs for full applications
- NIAID sample applications publicly available
- Pattern-match successful structures

## Trainee-Specific Themes

### F31 (Predoctoral Fellowship)
- Sponsor + Co-Sponsor letters detailed
- Training Plan specific (courses, conferences, skills)
- Career Goals articulate (academic PI, industry, gov)
- Research Project to become thesis
- Letters from outside committee members

### F32 (Postdoctoral Fellowship)
- Sponsor track record matters
- Training that prepares for independence
- Research project differs from PhD work but builds on skills
- 2-3 year timeline with milestones
- Career Goals articulate

### K99/R00 (Pathway to Independence)
- Independence narrative central
- Project distinct from sponsor's work
- Mentor team that visibly fills gaps
- Career Development Plan specific
- 5-year vision (2+3) realistic
- Institutional commitment letter

## Tactical Tips That Repeat

### Specific Aims Page
- Standard four-paragraph structure
- Hypothesis-driven aims
- Aim independence
- Conceptual figure encouraged
- Iterate 20+ times

### Significance and Innovation
- Lead with the question
- Connect to NIH institute mission
- Innovation as novel application
- Cite high-impact recent papers

### Approach
- Each aim with subsections
- Statistical analysis plan with power
- Sample size justification
- Rigor and reproducibility
- Authentication
- Sex as biological variable
- Alternatives

### Investigator and Environment
- Productivity proportionate to career stage
- Independence narrative for ESI
- Specific cores and resources cited
- Letters of support specific

## Resubmission Strategy

### Address Every Critique
- Use reviewer language verbatim
- Quote critique, respond with substance
- Bold changes in body text
- Introduction page (1 page) for response summary
- Don't argue without conceding something

### Tone
- Diplomatic, not defensive
- Acknowledge fair criticism
- Politely push back where wrong
- Always offer substance

## Career Stage Considerations

### Predoc / F31
- Demonstrate research aptitude
- Sponsor commitment central
- Training trajectory clear
- Letters from outside members add weight

### Postdoc / F32, K99
- Independence narrative
- Productivity proportionate to career stage
- Mentor team that fills gaps
- Project that's yours, not sponsor's

### Junior Faculty / First R01
- Productivity from postdoc strong
- Lab establishment evidence
- Specific Aims page critical
- Resubmission often needed

## What Hello PhD Hosts Repeatedly Say
- "Start earlier than you think you need to"
- "Get feedback on Specific Aims before drafting full application"
- "Engage your program officer"
- "Read funded applications in your area"
- "Mock review committees are invaluable"

- Hypothesis-driven over descriptive
- Aim independence
- Quantitative preliminary data

- Rigor and reproducibility
- Investigator-environment match
- Realistic budget and timeline

- Letters of support specific
