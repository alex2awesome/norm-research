# Pew Biomedical Scholars — Selection Criteria (Wave 2 supplement)

Wave 1 had a brief Pew entry; this Wave 2 file extracts the operational selection rubric. ~$300K over 4 years to ~22 outstanding early-career biomedical investigators.

## Selection Basis — Two-Component Evaluation

### Component 1: The Proposed Project
1. **Outstanding promise as contributor in science relevant to human health** — based on performance during education and training
2. **Creative and pioneering approaches** — to basic, translational, or applied biomedical research
3. **Particularly innovative** approaches encouraged
4. **Diverse field integration** — candidates whose work is biomedically grounded but draws concepts/theories from diverse fields are explicitly encouraged

### Component 2: The Researcher
1. **Successful independent investigator** — demonstrated capability to lead a research program
2. **Skill set needed to carry out high-impact proposal**
3. **Notable past accomplishments** including:
   - Honors and awards
   - Publications
   - Demonstrated research productivity

## Programmatic Constraints (anti-criteria)

- **Does NOT fund clinical trials research**
- Standard funding restrictions apply

## Application Selection Funnel

- **~200 applications** received per year (limited submission via institutional nomination)
- **22 grants awarded** = ~11% acceptance rate
- One nomination per eligible institution (~190 institutions)

## Selection Process

- **National Advisory Committee** evaluates all applications
- Committee considerable weight on BOTH project and researcher
- Selection emphasizes future trajectory rather than just past output

## Eligibility (threshold rubric)

- **Assistant professor** at start of award year
- **Appointment within previous 2-3 years**
- **PhD, MD, or equivalent**
- **U.S. institution**
- One nomination per eligible institution

## Application Materials

1. **Brief proposal** — typically 5 pages
2. **Specific aims**
3. **CV / biosketch**
4. **Two letters** — including from department head
5. **Institutional commitment**

## Award Structure

- **$300,000 over 4 years** (~$75K/year)
- Flexible use — research, equipment, salary
- No restrictive accounting

## Implications for Rubric Design

Pew's rubric is similar to Searle, HHMI Investigator, and Sloan in being heavily weighted toward person + trajectory rather than project specifics. This contrasts with NIH/NSF rubrics that center on project quality. The "outstanding promise" framing is doing significant work in the rubric — it's a placeholder for distinguished-committee judgment that escapes operationalization.
