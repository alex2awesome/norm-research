# 30 Reasons Your Grant Proposal May Not Have Been Funded

Common reasons grants are rejected, organized by category:

## Significance/Impact Issues
1. The proposed research is incremental, not significant
2. The hypothesis is not well-supported or compelling
3. The "so what?" question is not answered
4. The work is not state-of-the-art
5. Reviewers express low overall enthusiasm

## Scientific Approach Problems
6. Insufficient preliminary data
7. The proposed experiments will not prove the hypothesis
8. Lack of experimental detail in the research strategy
9. Methods are outdated
10. No clear plan for rigorous, reproducible research
11. Sample size justification is weak
12. Statistical analysis plan is inadequate
13. Unprovable hypothesis (a "fatal flaw")
14. Unrealistic timeline
15. Overly ambitious scope

## Investigator/Team Issues
16. PI lacks expertise in proposed methods
17. No clear plan for required collaborations
18. Mentor/sponsor inadequately committed
19. Track record does not support the proposed work

## Budget/Resources Issues
20. Budget does not align with proposed scope
21. Resources at institution insufficient
22. No protected time for the PI

## Writing/Presentation Issues
23. Poor organization, hard to follow
24. Excessive jargon
25. Specific Aims page is unclear or hedging
26. Figures are illegible or uninformative
27. Inconsistencies between sections

## Strategic/Process Issues
28. Failed to contact the program officer
29. Wrong study section/funding mechanism
30. Did not respond thoroughly to reviewer comments on resubmission

## Key Statistics
- Only approximately 15% of R01 and R01-equivalent grants are accepted for funding on the initial submission
- Once reviewers find a "fatal flaw," they may stop discussing the application to save time
