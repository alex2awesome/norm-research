# Grant Writing for Dummies (Beverly Browning) - Fundamentals

## About the Book and Author
In Grant Writing For Dummies, Dr. Beverly Browning draws on over four decades of experience writing grant applications and training grant writers. She has assisted clients throughout the United States in receiving awards of more than $100 million.

## Key Distinction: Grants vs. Contracts
The book differentiates between:
- **Grants**: Funds awarded to support specific projects or activities
- **Contracts**: Agreements for specific deliverables or services

## Funding Source Categories
1. **Federal grants** - Government agencies (NIH, NSF, etc.)
2. **State grants** - State agencies
3. **Foundation grants** - Private and community foundations
4. **Corporate grants** - Corporate giving programs

## Standard Grant Proposal Components

### 1. Executive Summary / Cover Letter
- Brief overview of the project
- Total amount requested
- Key impact

### 2. Statement of Need / Problem Statement
- What problem will the grant fix?
- Evidence-based documentation of the need
- Local data + national context

### 3. Goals and Objectives
- Goals: Broad statements of intent
- Objectives: Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound (SMART)

### 4. Methods / Project Design
- How will you accomplish the objectives?
- Who will do the work
- Timeline and activities
- Population to be served

### 5. Evaluation Plan
- How will you measure success?
- Process evaluation (was the work done?)
- Outcome evaluation (did it make a difference?)
- Independent vs. internal evaluation

### 6. Budget and Budget Narrative
- Personnel
- Travel
- Equipment
- Indirect costs
- Justification for each line

### 7. Sustainability Plan
- How will the project continue after grant funds end?
- Other funding sources
- Built-in revenue
- Institutional commitments

### 8. Organizational Capacity
- Why your organization is best positioned
- History of accomplishments
- Key staff
- Partnerships

## Browning's Writing Tips

### Storytelling Approach
Use a storytelling approach with supporting statistics in such a compelling way that the reader can't put down your application until making a positive funding decision.

### Sentence Structure
Write in short, hard-hitting sentences. Long-winded sentences almost always lose the reader.

### Tone
- Professional but engaging
- Confident but not arrogant
- Specific not vague
- Action-oriented

### Common Mistakes
- Generic language that could fit any organization
- Vague objectives without metrics
- Budget that doesn't match narrative
- Missing sustainability plan
- Not following funder guidelines
- Submitting late
