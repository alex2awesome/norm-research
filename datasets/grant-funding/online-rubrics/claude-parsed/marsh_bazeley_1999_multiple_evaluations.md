# Marsh & Bazeley 1999: Multiple Evaluations of Grant Proposals

Confirmatory factor analysis study of Australian Research Council Large Grants Scheme reviews. Examines the latent structure of peer review judgments across five discipline groups.

## Variables Captured

For each grant proposal:

### Track Record / Biographical Variables
1. Applicant age
2. Applicant gender
3. Type of position (lecturer, professor, etc.)
4. Status of position (continuing vs fixed-term)
5. Institutional base (university type)
6. Previous grants history
7. Publication record

### Review Variables
1. External assessor ratings on multiple criteria
2. Discipline review panel final outcomes
3. Number of assessors used per proposal
4. Geographic origin of assessors

## Two Latent Factors Identified by CFA

The factor analysis revealed peer review judgments cluster into two distinct latent factors:

### Factor 1: Quality of the Research Team
- Track record-based judgments
- Investigator productivity
- Prior funding success
- Reputation in field

### Factor 2: Quality of the Proposed Research
- Proposal-specific judgments
- Methodology
- Innovation
- Feasibility

## Key Findings

- **The two factors are correlated but distinct** — reviewers can rate a strong team's weak proposal differently from a weak team's strong proposal.
- **Reliability of researcher-quality judgments is HIGHER** than reliability of proposal-quality judgments.
- **Track-record variables (publication history, prior grants) most strongly predict** reviewer ratings on the team factor.
- **Proposal-quality factor shows more reviewer disagreement** — judgments about science quality are less convergent than judgments about CV quality.
- **Gender effects**: small but detectable in some discipline groups.
- **Institutional prestige effects**: present but moderate.

## Implicit Criteria Surfaced by the Study

The CFA structure suggests reviewers operationalize "merit" along two distinct dimensions:

### Researcher-Track-Record Operationalizations
- Number of publications
- Citation impact
- Prior funding success
- Position seniority
- Institutional ranking

### Proposal-Quality Operationalizations
- Originality of question
- Strength of theoretical framework
- Methodology rigor
- Feasibility of work plan
- Significance of expected outcomes

## Implications for Rubric Design

- A rubric should explicitly distinguish team-quality from proposal-quality criteria, since they have different reliability properties.
- Track-record-based ratings are more reliable but may simply reproduce existing inequities (Matthew effect).
- Proposal-quality ratings are less reliable but more meritocratic in principle.
- Funders must decide which dimension to weight, with awareness of the tradeoff between reliability and meritocracy.
- The Marsh & Bazeley structure foreshadows the later Witteman 2019 (CIHR natural experiment) finding that PI-focused vs project-focused criteria produce different bias patterns.
