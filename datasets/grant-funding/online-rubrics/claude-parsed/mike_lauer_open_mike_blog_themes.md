# Mike Lauer's Open Mike Blog — NIH Office of Extramural Research

Mike Lauer (NIH Deputy Director for Extramural Research) writes the Open Mike blog covering policy, peer review, and funding data. The recurring themes constitute an implicit rubric for what NIH leadership values.

## Recurring Themes

### Funding Concentration
- A small fraction of PIs receive a large fraction of NIH funds
- The Grant Support Index (GSI) and similar caps are policy responses
- Productivity per dollar declines after ~$700K direct costs
- Lauer regularly publishes data showing diminishing returns

### Reproducibility and Rigor
- Rigor is now scored explicitly in review
- Authentication of key biological/chemical resources required
- Sex as a biological variable must be addressed
- Statistical analysis plans must be pre-specified
- Failure to address rigor = score penalty

### Peer Review Validity
- Score-to-outcome correlations are weaker than people assume
- Inter-reviewer reliability is moderate at best
- NIH continues to study and refine review processes
- Implicit bias training mandated for reviewers

### Application Volume and Burden
- Per-PI application rates rising
- Resubmission churn is a known inefficiency
- Simplified review framework (2025) addresses some of this

### Diversity, Equity, Inclusion in Funding
- Persistent racial gap in R01 funding (Ginther 2011 onward)
- NIH UNITE initiative addressing structural barriers
- Diversity supplements, K awards, F awards as pipeline tools

### New Investigators / Early Stage Investigators
- ESI status (within 10 years of terminal degree, no prior R01)
- ESI applications get extra consideration at funding margins
- Paylines for ESI are typically more generous

### Data Sharing Mandates (2023 onward)
- Data Management and Sharing Plan required
- Reviewers consider feasibility and adequacy of plan
- Costs for data sharing are allowable

## Implicit Rubric From Lauer's Posts

A "good" application from NIH leadership perspective:
1. Addresses an important biomedical problem within institute mission
2. Comes from a PI not already saturated with funding
3. Has rigorous, pre-specified methods
4. Includes a credible data sharing plan
5. Authenticates key resources
6. Considers sex as biological variable
7. Has a feasible budget aligned with productivity expectations
8. Comes from a research environment that supports reproducibility
9. Has a clear training/mentorship plan if it's a K or F award
10. Demonstrates value for taxpayer dollars

## Author Voice Quotes (Paraphrased)

"We continue to examine whether our peer review system reliably identifies the strongest science."

"Productivity does not scale linearly with funding."

"Our goal is to fund the best science from the broadest possible pool of investigators."

"Rigor is not a checkbox — it is the foundation of reproducibility."

## What Lauer's Posts Tell Applicants
- The marginal R01 in your lab is harder to defend if you have multiple already
- Pre-registration and rigor language are no longer optional
- Pay attention to institute priorities, not just CSR study section fit
- Resubmission scores often improve — don't give up after A0
