# John Templeton Foundation - Grant Evaluation Criteria

## Five Core Evaluation Dimensions

Applications are assessed on:

### 1. Intellectual Rigor
- Scholarly excellence
- Methodological soundness
- Engagement with scholarly literature
- Logical coherence

### 2. Originality
- Novel ideas
- Fresh perspectives
- Unconventional questions
- Methodological innovation

### 3. Potential Impact
- Influence on field of study
- Societal impact
- Practical applications
- Long-term significance

### 4. Interdisciplinary Relevance
- Engagement across disciplines
- Bridging humanities, sciences, theology
- Multidimensional perspectives
- Cross-disciplinary collaborations

### 5. Alignment with the Foundation's Mission
- Relevance to "Big Questions"
- Engagement with character virtues, science of religion, free markets, etc.
- Genetics of human nature
- Mathematical, physical, and life sciences

## Specific Focus Areas

### Religion, Science, and Society
The Foundation is interested in projects that will "advance our collective understanding of the ways in which religious and spiritual beliefs and practices affect human flourishing." Especially interested in:
- Research that engages substantively and critically with the sciences
- Robust interdisciplinary collaborations in which philosophical or theological understanding informs the findings and methods of the sciences

### Key Priorities for Competitive Proposals
Demonstrate potential to:
- Intervene constructively in tensions characterizing modern life
- Clarify conditions that support meaning making
- Help individuals and communities cultivate better ways of being
- Show how interpretive frameworks, existential commitments, and communities form people into more resilient, fulfilled, and spiritually or socially attuned human beings

## Special Application Considerations

### Interdisciplinary Integration
The Foundation particularly encourages projects that:
- Integrate sciences with philosophy, theology, or religious studies
- Through robust interdisciplinary collaboration
- Produce findings relevant to pluralistic societies
- Address individuals who may or may not claim religious affiliations

## Templeton Big Questions

The Foundation funds research on Big Questions including:
- Free will and agency
- Character virtues
- Cognitive science of religion
- Cosmology and origins
- Forgiveness, gratitude, hope
- Human flourishing
- Generosity and altruism
- Limits of science and philosophy
- Consciousness
- Genetics and human nature
- Religion and health

## Application Process
- Two-stage process for many programs
- Online Funding Inquiry first
- Selected applicants invited to submit full proposal
- External peer review
- Strategic Programs Committee review
- Board of Trustees final approval

## Application Categories
- Discovery awards (small, exploratory)
- Project grants (mid-size, defined goals)
- Strategic awards (large, programmatic)
- Conference and travel grants

## Comparison: Templeton World Charity Foundation
- Distinct from John Templeton Foundation
- More applied focus on global development
- Often funds work in lower-income countries
- Different application processes

## Notable Stance
- Emphasis on "constructive engagement" between science and religion
- Pluralistic — funds work across religious traditions and secular perspectives
- Long-term commitment to building the field
- Welcomes nontraditional research
