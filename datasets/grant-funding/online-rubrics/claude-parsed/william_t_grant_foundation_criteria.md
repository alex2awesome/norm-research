# William T. Grant Foundation - Evaluation Criteria

## Mission
The William T. Grant Foundation invests in high-quality research focused on:
- Reducing inequality in youth outcomes
- Improving the use of research evidence in decisions that affect young people in the United States

## Key Evaluation Criteria

### 1. Research Quality and Design
External peer reviewers and internal staff evaluate:
- Research design
- Methods
- Team qualifications
- Potential impact

The Foundation focuses support on **empirical studies** for which applicants have already:
- Performed a literature review
- Identified specific research questions and/or hypotheses
- Possess sufficiently detailed research methods and data analysis plans
- So that reviewers can evaluate their rigor

### 2. Applicant Track Record
The Foundation evaluates an applicant's track record based on:
- Prior success managing a research project
- Peer-reviewed empirical publications
- For certain proposals, evidence of connections with audiences outside of the research community

### 3. Theoretical Rigor and Practical Relevance
Proposed evaluation studies should:
- Specify a theoretical basis for the program, policy, or practice
- Enhance understanding of its effects
- Consider mechanisms through which effects occur
- Examine variation in intervention effects
- Studies should shed light not solely on "what works," but on **what works for whom, under what conditions, and why**

### 4. Team Qualifications
- Prior training and publications should demonstrate that the research team has a track record of conducting strong research and communicating it successfully
- Teams should be well-positioned to address varied tasks
- Potentially combining expertise across disciplines or methods
- Specific value for each member's contributions

## Program Areas

### Reducing Inequality
Research that aims to:
- Build, test, or increase understanding of programs, policies, or practices that reduce inequality
- Reduce inequality in academic, social, behavioral, or economic outcomes
- Among young people aged 5-25
- Specifically focused on race, ethnicity, economic standing, language minority status, or immigrant origins

### Improving the Use of Research Evidence
Research that:
- Investigates strategies to improve the use of research evidence in policy and practice
- Examines challenges in research-practice partnerships
- Studies how to make research more usable
- Tests interventions to improve research uptake

### W.T. Grant Scholars Program
- Five-year career awards for early-career researchers
- $350,000 over 5 years
- Researchers who have received their doctorate within the last 7 years
- Support for ambitious learning agendas

## Application Components
- Letter of inquiry first
- Selected applicants invited to submit full proposal
- Detailed methods and analysis plan required
- Pre-tested instruments preferred
- Feasibility evidence required

## Notable Features
- Strong commitment to youth-focused research
- Methods-pluralistic (qualitative, quantitative, mixed)
- Two-pillar mission: research quality + research use
- Encourages reflection on policy/practice implications
