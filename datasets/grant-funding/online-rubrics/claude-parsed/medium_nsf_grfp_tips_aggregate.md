# Medium — Aggregated NSF GRFP Application Wisdom

Top-clapped Medium articles on NSF GRFP, written by recent winners, honorable mentions, and graduate student advisors.

## The Two-Document Structure

### Personal Statement (3 pages)
- Carries 60% of weight (per multiple winner accounts)
- Tells YOUR story: who you are, what shaped you, where you're going
- Intellectual Merit + Broader Impacts both addressed
- Concrete activities and outcomes, not platitudes

### Research Plan (2 pages)
- Demonstrates ability to formulate and propose research
- Doesn't have to be your thesis
- Should fit your training and current resources
- Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts subheadings explicit

## Personal Statement Frameworks

### "The Hero's Journey" Framework
1. Origin story (formative experience)
2. Call to research (undergraduate moment)
3. Trials (research experiences with lessons learned)
4. Mentors (specific people who shaped you)
5. Vision (concrete future career goals)
6. Broader contribution (specific outreach plans)

### "Three Research Experiences" Framework
Each experience covers:
- Lab/program name and PI
- Your specific contribution
- What you learned
- Output (paper, presentation, dataset)
- How it shaped your trajectory

### "Identity-Centered" Framework
- Open with your background and what you bring
- Connect identity to research interests
- Outreach as continuation of personal commitment
- Vision as service to your community

## Research Plan Best Practices

### Structure That Wins (per top articles)
- Title that signals the question
- 2-3 sentence hook
- 1 paragraph background (cite 3-5 key papers)
- 1 paragraph research question and hypothesis
- 1 paragraph methodology with timeline
- 1 paragraph expected outcomes and significance
- 1 paragraph Intellectual Merit (explicit subheading)
- 1 paragraph Broader Impacts (explicit subheading)

### Common Research Plan Mistakes
- Too literature-review-y
- No clear methodology
- No timeline
- Missing IM/BI subheadings
- Plan outside your current training
- Plan that requires resources you don't have

## Reviewer Insights from Top Articles

### Reviewers Are Generalists
- Your panel covers a broad area, not your subfield
- Define jargon
- Explain why your question matters to non-specialists
- Use accessible analogies

### Each Application Gets ~3 Reviewers
- Inter-reviewer agreement is moderate
- Outliers can pull scores in either direction
- Honorable Mention vs. Award decided by composite
- Bottom line: maximize all reviewers' impressions

### Time Per Application
- Reviewers spend ~30-60 minutes per application
- First impression is everything
- Personal Statement opening matters
- Research Plan opening matters

## Tactical Tips That Repeat

### Format
- 11pt Times New Roman or similar
- 1-inch margins
- Single-spaced
- Section headings bold
- Subheadings (IM, BI) bold and explicit

### Letters of Recommendation
- Three letters
- Best mix: 2 research mentors + 1 academic
- Provide writers with: CV, draft statements, talking points
- 6-8 weeks lead time
- Follow up politely

### Reuse Across Years
- First-year and second-year grad students can apply
- Save and revise; don't rewrite from scratch
- Track which letter writers, which experiences, which framing
- Each year improves with feedback

## What Top Articles Say About Broader Impacts

### Effective BI Activities
- Specific named outreach programs (with hours, students reached)
- Mentorship of underrepresented students (with names where possible)
- Public communication (blog, podcast, articles)
- Curriculum development (specific courses, workshops)
- Service in scientific societies
- Open science contributions (code, data, protocols)

### Ineffective BI Claims
- "I will mentor undergrads" (no specifics)
- "I will engage in outreach" (no specifics)
- "I will publish my work" (that's what scientists do)
- Generic diversity statements without personal grounding

## Multi-Fellowship Strategy
- Same content can be repurposed for Ford, NDSEG, Hertz, DOE SCGSR
- Different funders weight differently:
  - NSF GRFP: balanced IM/BI
  - Ford: diversity-centered narrative
  - NDSEG: defense-relevant research focus
  - Hertz: long-form interview matters
  - DOE SCGSR: specific lab placement at national lab
