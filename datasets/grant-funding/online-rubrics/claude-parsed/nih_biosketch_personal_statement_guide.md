# NIH Biosketch - Personal Statement and Contributions to Science

## Overview
The NIH Biosketch is required for all PIs and senior personnel on NIH grant applications. The updated NIH Biosketch Common Form is required for all applications with due dates on or after January 25, 2026. SciENcv must be used to complete the biosketch.

## Personal Statement (Section A)

### Length
- Limited to **3,500 characters** (including spaces)

### Purpose
The Personal Statement is where you make your case for why you are well-suited to your role on the project. You might include:
- Key aspects of training or past experience
- Technical expertise
- Significant collaborations
- Past performance in this or related fields
- An opportunity to explain anything else you'd like the reviewers to know about your career and research directions

### Style
- Use the first person (I, me, my, we) for the personal statement
- Tailor for each application — not generic
- Address career interruptions, transitions, special circumstances

### Content Guidelines
1. State your role on the project
2. Highlight expertise relevant to the proposed work
3. Cite key papers (NOT references — refer to publications/products in "Other Significant Products")
4. Mention awards, leadership roles
5. Explain career trajectory if non-traditional

## Contributions to Science (Section C)

### Length
- Briefly describe up to **five** of your most significant contributions to science
- Each Contribution to Science is limited to **2,000 characters** (including spaces)

### Strategy by Career Stage
- **Graduate students and postdocs**: Consider highlighting two or three contributions they consider most significant
- **Mid-career**: Three to four well-developed contributions
- **Senior**: All five, demonstrating breadth

### Format for Each Contribution
1. **Brief description** of:
   - Historical background of the contribution
   - Central finding(s)
   - Influence on the progression of the field
   - Your specific role in the contribution

2. **Citations**: 
   - You may NOT include references in Contributions to Science or Personal Statement
   - Instead, refer to publications/products selected for the "Other Significant Products" section
   - Each contribution can cite up to 4 supporting publications

### Reviewer Expectations
- Reviewers tend to base expectations on individual's seniority
- Focus on relevant accomplishments and expertise
- Choose **quality over quantity**
- Tell a story — what is your scientific identity?

## Common Mistakes
- Generic personal statements not tailored to the project
- Too many publications listed (low signal-to-noise)
- Listing every paper rather than synthesizing contributions
- Not explaining your specific role in collaborative work
- Missing connection between past work and proposed work
