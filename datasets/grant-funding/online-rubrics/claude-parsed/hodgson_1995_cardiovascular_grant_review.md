# Hodgson 1995: Cardiovascular Grant-in-Aid Peer Review

Retrospective analysis of 779 research proposals submitted to the Heart and Stroke Foundation of Ontario, identifying factors influencing scientific-merit scores and inter-reviewer agreement.

## Three-Tier Review Architecture Studied

The HSFO process used at the time had three reviewer types per application:

1. **Internal reviewer** — scientist on the foundation's review committee
2. **External reviewer(s)** — outside experts solicited for written reviews
3. **Committee** — panel discussion and final scoring

The study tested how each tier influenced final outcomes.

## Variables Tested as Score Predictors

For each application, the study captured:
1. **Application characteristics**: requested budget, type of research (basic, clinical, applied), discipline
2. **Applicant characteristics**: career stage, prior funding history, institution
3. **Reviewer characteristics**: internal vs external, geographic location, prior committee service
4. **Process characteristics**: number of reviewers, level of agreement, timing in committee meeting

## Key Findings

- **Internal and external reviewers showed substantial disagreement** on scientific merit scores
- The **committee discussion shifted scores** in ways not predictable from individual reviewer scores alone
- **Inter-reviewer agreement was modest**, consistent with the broader peer-review literature
- Final committee scores were better predicted by the **internal reviewers** than by external reviewers — suggesting the committee structure privileged in-group judgment

## Implicit Criteria Used in Cardiovascular Grant Review

Per the HSFO criteria of the time:
1. **Scientific merit** of the proposed research
2. **Applicant qualifications** and track record
3. **Methodology** and feasibility
4. **Significance** to cardiovascular science / clinical care
5. **Reasonableness** of budget request
6. **Institutional environment** and resources

## Implications for Rubric Design

- Multi-tier review architectures introduce additional sources of variance, not just additional information.
- The committee's structural advantage to internal reviewers means external review serves a more advisory than determinative function.
- The HSFO study is one of the few examining a focused medical subdomain, suggesting domain-specific reliability patterns may differ from cross-cutting agency studies.
