# Modified Lottery for Research Funding — Rubric & Empirical Studies

Proposal and emerging practice of replacing or supplementing peer review with formal randomization among meritorious applications. Implemented at major funders since 2013.

## The Two-Stage Modified Lottery Rubric

### Stage 1: Conventional Peer Review (filter for sufficiency)
- All applications scored on standard criteria
- Each application classified as **meritorious** or **non-meritorious**
- Non-meritorious applications can be revised and resubmitted
- No fine-grained ranking required at this stage

### Stage 2: Random Allocation Among Meritorious
- Computer randomly generates priority scores for all meritorious applications
- Funding awarded based on randomly generated scores up to budget limit

## Rationale (the design criteria)

1. **Eliminates fine-grained reviewer-judgment noise** — Pier 2018 and Fang 2016 show such judgments are essentially random anyway in the fundable range
2. **Reduces bias** — Wennerås, Ginther, Erosheva findings about disparities at the scoring stage are eliminated
3. **Reduces conservatism** — Luukkonen 2012 and Boudreau 2016 findings about novelty penalty don't operate
4. **Reduces application-preparation costs** — applicants need to demonstrate meritoriousness, not optimize for granular ranking
5. **Reduces reviewer burden** — binary classification is faster than nuanced ranking
6. **Improves diversity** — Lottery before peer review (Volkswagen) increased female PI representation

## Implementations

### Health Research Council of New Zealand (since 2013)
- **Explorer Grant scheme** — first modern major-funder lottery
- Targets "transformative research ideas with major potential impact"
- Applications screened for: feasibility, ethics, innovativeness
- Among meritorious, lottery selects funded grants
- Survey: 78% of funded researchers find lottery acceptable; 44% of unfunded researchers do

### Volkswagen Foundation (Germany, since 2017)
- **"Experiment!" funding line**
- Lottery used as a tie-breaker after initial screening
- 2025 Nature Communications paper: lottery before peer review increased female PI share AND reduced administrative cost

### Swiss National Science Foundation (since 2018)
- Used as tie-breaker for borderline applications
- Reduces reviewer burden and provides honest signaling about decision uncertainty

## Empirical Findings on Lottery Acceptance

- **Most scientists oppose pure lotteries** but support hybrid models
- Acceptance is higher among:
  - Researchers who have won lottery-allocated funding
  - Researchers from diverse / underrepresented groups
  - Researchers with shorter track records
- Acceptance is lower among:
  - Senior researchers with strong track records
  - Researchers in fields with high consensus norms

## Anti-Rubric Aspects (what lottery removes)

A modified-lottery system explicitly **removes** the following from grant decisions among meritorious applications:
- Fine-grained scoring of significance
- Comparative ranking on innovation
- Subjective judgment of investigator promise
- Panel deliberation effects
- Reviewer expertise matching
- Score-aggregation procedures

## Implications for Rubric Design

The modified lottery is the most radical existing critique of fine-grained rubric use. It accepts that some quality threshold can be determined (Stage 1) but argues the empirical evidence shows finer-grained distinctions (Stage 2) are not statistically defensible. The lottery rubric is the limit case of "humility about precision" in grant evaluation.
