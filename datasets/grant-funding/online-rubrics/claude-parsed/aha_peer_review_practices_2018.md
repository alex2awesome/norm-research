# American Heart Association — Peer Review Practices

Major medical-research foundation that publishes its peer-review practices as a scientific statement. Among the most transparent funders about review methodology.

## Score Scale

- **1-9 scale** (1 = exceptional, 9 = poor)
- Mirrors NIH approach
- Allows decimals for finer ranking

## Three-Stage Review Process

### Stage 1: Triage
- AHA reserves the right to triage at least half of submissions
- Removes applications unlikely to compete
- Reduces reviewer burden

### Stage 2: Scientific Review
- Three expert reviewers per non-triaged application
- Each reviewer conducts in-depth review of 6-9 proposals
- Reviewers prepare written critique with strengths and weaknesses
- Generate preliminary numeric scores

### Stage 3: Panel Discussion
- Review panel meets to discuss top-scoring applications
- Reviewers present orally; panel discusses
- Final scores assigned post-discussion

## Distinctive Element: Lay Reviewers

AHA panels include **lay reviewers** alongside scientific experts:
- Individuals **without formal scientific training**
- Strong personal interest in heart disease/stroke prevention or management
- Specifically evaluate the **"lay summary"** portion of applications
- Score the **potential impact on AHA mission**

This is unusual among major biomedical funders.

## AHA Review Criteria (per the 2018 Scientific Statement)

The AHA's published review criteria for research grants include:

1. **Significance** — importance of the research question to cardiovascular/stroke health
2. **Approach** — soundness of methodology
3. **Innovation** — novelty of concepts or methods
4. **Investigator** — applicant qualifications
5. **Environment** — institutional support
6. **AHA mission alignment** — relevance to heart disease and stroke

Plus the **lay-summary scoring** for public-impact assessment.

## AHA Funding Mechanism Categories (each with tailored review criteria)

1. **Predoctoral Fellowship** — early-career training
2. **Postdoctoral Fellowship**
3. **Career Development Award** — early-career independence
4. **Innovative Project Award** — high-risk innovative research
5. **Transformational Project Award** — paradigm-shifting work
6. **Established Investigator Award**
7. **Strategically Focused Research Networks** — collaborative team awards

## Implications for Rubric Design

The AHA's lay-reviewer integration is a distinctive rubric element rare in major funding agencies — it formalizes the "public benefit" criterion through actual public participation in review rather than indirect proxies. The scientific statement format publication of review criteria (Circulation Research 2018) is itself an unusual transparency commitment.
