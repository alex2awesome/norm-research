SEARLE SCHOLARS PROGRAM NAMES 15 SCIENTISTS AS SEARLE SCHOLARS FOR 2025

Members of the new class of Searle Scholars pursue ground-breaking research in chemistry and the biomedical sciences. Each receives an award of $300,000 in flexible funding to support their own work over the next three years. 

The Searle Scholars Program makes grants to selected universities and research centers to support the independent research of exceptional young faculty in the biomedical sciences and chemistry who have recently been appointed as assistant professors on a tenure-track appointment. Milan Mrksich, the program’s Scientific Director, appoints a Scientific Advisory Board of eminent scientists who choose the Scholars based on rigorous standards aimed at finding the most creative talent interested in pursuing an academic research career. This year, 194 applications were considered from nominations by 145 universities and research institutions. 

Fifteen exceptional early career scientists were selected by the Scientific Advisory Board, in recognition of their promise to change their fields by solving nature’s puzzles in a broad range of fields, and develop next-generation technologies that can reveal biological function.  The topics these scientists are pursuing include: 

  • How are new proteins identified that can shuttle drugs across the blood-brain barrier, where they can treat brain disease?
  • How do fungi manipulate the neural system of flies to impart zombie behaviors?
  • How do cancer cells control the sugars displayed on their surfaces to turn off the immune system?
  • Can complex signaling pathways be reconstituted outside of the cell and reveal mechanisms that regulate cell behavior?

Mrksich notes that the Scholars will advance fundamental understanding of a range of biological processes, and in some of these cases will reveal novel strategies for developing the next generation of therapeutics.

Since 1981, 707 scientists have been named Searle Scholars. Including this year, the Program has awarded more than $161 million. Eighty-five Searle Scholars have been inducted into the National Academy of Sciences. Twenty Searle Scholars have been recognized with a MacArthur Fellowship, known as the “genius grant,” and two Searle Scholars have been awarded the Nobel Prize for Chemistry. 

About the Searle Scholars Program 

The Searle Scholars Program supports high risk, high reward research across a broad range of scientific disciplines. Grants are $300,000 for a three-year term, with $100,000 payable each year of the grant. The Searle Scholars Program is funded through the Searle Funds at The Chicago Community Trust and administered by Kinship Foundation.
