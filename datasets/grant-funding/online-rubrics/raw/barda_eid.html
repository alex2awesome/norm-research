INFLUENZA & EMERGING INFECTIOUS DISEASES (EID) MEDICAL COUNTERMEASURES

We are investing in advanced research & development, manufacturing capacity, and procurement of medical products to protect the Nation from pandemic influenza and other emerging infectious disease threats to health security.

Influenza & Emerging Infectious Diseases
Medical Countermeasures

In alignment with the National Biodefense Strategy, BARDA is responsible for the advanced research and development (AR&D), manufacturing, and procurement of medical countermeasures (MCMs) to protect the health security of the US public. The Influenza and Emerging Infectious Diseases (IEID) Division plays a critical role in protecting Americans from pandemic influenza and other emerging infectious disease threats. These MCMs (diagnostics, therapeutics, and vaccines) are the foundation of the Nation’s capacity to respond to and recover from public health emergencies.

The IEID Division invests in a portfolio of MCMs aimed at ensuring their on-demand availability to mitigate the next influenza pandemic. To this end, our strategy prioritizes products that not only can be developed and manufactured quickly and provide greater efficacy but also can be repurposed and/or re-engineered to allow for continuous at-scale production of a range of MCMs to supply the U.S. healthcare market. To achieve this level of preparedness, we are investing in flexible platform technologies and sustainable domestic MCM manufacturing infrastructure.

Therapeutics Program
The Theraputics Program funds 1) novel antivirals to treat people with seasonal or pandemic influenza in outpatient settings; 2) immune modulators and other host-directed therapeutics to treat and improve clinical outcomes of acute lung injury (ALI)/acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) caused by pandemic and seasonal influenza as well as other pathogens of pandemic potential; and 3) long-acting antivirals to provide pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) options against pandemic and seasonal influenza when vaccines are not yet available and for people whose immune systems may not generate a sufficient response from available vaccines. Specific to immune modulators, the program has special interest in identifying reliable biomarkers to stratify ALI/ARDS severity and target broad-label therapeutic candidates in specific populations suffering from or at risk of developing ALI/ARDS due to influenza or other pathogens with pandemic potential. Check out Areas of Interest (AOIs) 9.1, 9.2, and 9.3 of the BARDA Broad Agency Announcement (BAA) for further details on the Influenza Therapeutics Program investments in influenza antivirals and other therapeutic MCMs for treating outpatients, hospitalized patients, and immunocompromised individuals.

Vaccine Development Program

The Vaccine Development Program funds the advancement of licensed, domestically manufactured vaccine capabilities that can rapidly pivot to 1) quickly develop vaccines from time of new virus threat identification to release of first vaccine doses for evaluation (goal: 100 days from sequence availability) and 2) swiftly ramp-up existing or establish new manufacturing facilities to produce enough doses to immunize the U.S. population (goal: 120 days from sequence availability). The program is prioritizing improved and more rapidly produced next-generation pandemic influenza vaccines that decrease pandemic response time, technologies that improve vaccine effectiveness and access (including alternative routes of administration that may expedite storage, shipping, and delivery), platforms that modernize vaccine production processes, and end-to-end solutions that expand vaccine manufacturing capabilities. The Vaccine Development Program collaborates with private partners under Areas of Interest (AOIs) 8.1 and 8.2 of the BARDA Broad Agency Announcement (BAA) to advance efforts that enable a rapid development and scale-up of safe and effective vaccines in the event of a public health emergency.

Pandemic Vaccines and Adjuvants Program

The Pandemic Vaccines and Adjuvants Program funds partnerships that shorten pandemic response timelines and increase vaccine accessibility to ensure the nation is prepared to respond to a potential future influenza pandemic. Through these partnerships, BARDA manages the National Pre-pandemic Influenza Vaccine Stockpile (NPIVS), which holds pre-pandemic bulk antigens from influenza viruses with pandemic potential and is an integral component of the overall Influenza & EID Program’s pandemic preparedness and response capability. The Pandemic Vaccines and Adjuvants Program also is expanding domestic manufacturing of pandemic influenza vaccines to deliver enough doses to vaccinate the U.S. population during a public health emergency, investing in a library of candidate pre-pandemic vaccines and licensed or authorized adjuvants to rapidly formulate enough vaccine doses to immunize critical care workers and at-risk populations early during a response, and clinically evaluating the safety and immunogenicity of stockpiled vaccines and adjuvants against influenza viruses with pandemic potential to enhance vaccine response readiness and effectiveness. Stay tuned for links to funding opportunities in these investment areas supported by the Pandemic Vaccines and Adjuvants Program.

ABOUT THE PROGRAM
Influenza Diagnostics and Devices Program

The Influenza Diagnostics and Devices Program funds advanced development of accurate, robust, and accessible influenza tests to inform early treatment decisions, patient management, and public health interventions. To enhance the effectiveness of influenza diagnostic tests, BARDA’s influenza diagnostic strategy focuses on developing tests that:

  1. Facilitate test-to-treat scenarios (e.g., provide accurate and rapid results to inform early consideration for antiviral drug use),
  2. Differentiate between Influenza A, Influenza B, and other respiratory viruses (like SARS-CoV-2 and RSV), and
  3. Enable the detection of novel influenza viruses.
To remove barriers to accessing tests, BARDA funds innovative solutions that emphasize rapid pharmacy-use platforms, enable at-home testing, lower costs, and provide automatic result reporting for public health authorities to monitor viral circulation in the community. This program has supported development of numerous influenza and SARS-CoV-2 tests that have achieved FDA 510(k) clearances, with many more tests in development. Learn more about the Influenza & EID Diagnostics and Devices Program investments in influenza and threat-agnostic diagnostics under Areas of Interest (AOIs) 7.3 and 7.4 of the BARDA Broad Agency Announcement (BAA).

Since BARDA’s inception, we have identified 6 key preparedness principles that enable a robust response to Pandemic Influenza and Emerging Infectious Diseases.

Pandemic Influenza Preparedness

Flexible Agreements

Our flexible partnerships with product developers allow us to rapidly pivot to address new threats.

Production Capacity

Ensuring sufficient production capacity by leveraging platforms used for commercial product manufacturing – because it does not matter how strong the safety and efficacy of a vaccine or therapeutic is if you cannot make enough of it.

Platforms for Faster Development

We leverage proven fast platform technologies with extensive clinical trial and manufacturing experience as the foundation of rapid pandemic MCM product development and at-scale production.

Improve Delivery

BARDA is actively seeking technologies that enable alternative ways to deliver vaccines – for example, moving away from traditional administration approaches such as needle and syringe that require specialized medical training, to an adhesive bandage-type patch that could be self-applied.

Expanded Access

We invest in end-to-end solutions that prioritize diversity and inclusion, including clinical development plans that ensure that all populations will have access to these products.

Sustainability

We strive to maintain an updated portfolio of sustainable pandemic MCMs and capabilities, providing support when commercial markets are insufficient. We do this by conducting product life-cycle analyses as part of portfolio reviews to ensure their continued availability for the next outbreak or pandemic response.
