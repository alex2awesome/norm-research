Introducing Discrete Analysis, an arXiv Overlay Journal

This month, British mathematician Sir Timothy Gowers announced the launch of one of the first arXiv overlay journals, Discrete Analysis (link to temporary website). We’re honored that the editorial board has opted to use Scholastica to manage the peer review process for this journal, which marks a new era in open access (OA) publishing.

Discrete Analysis will follow a “diamond open access” model, meaning that it will be completely free for both authors and readers, with no subscription or article processing charge (APC) costs. The journal will be able to maintain its no-cost promise by publishing solely via arXiv overlay, rather than publishing print issues or electronically hosting papers online. In doing so, the journal will avoid all overhead publishing costs including typesetting, which authors with a firm grasp of LaTeX can handle on their own. Discrete Analysis’ pathbreaking use of technology to replace traditional paid “publisher services,” such as typesetting articles, signifies new opportunities for journals to not only peer review and edit research independently, but also to publish.

How will the journal’s submission process work?

Discrete Analysis will operate in much the same way as traditional journals, following the peer review process, except that authors will submit to the journal by posting their papers to arXiv preprints using a CC-BY-4.0 license. Once articles have been posted on arXiv, in order to complete their submission to Discrete Analysis authors will visit the journal’s Scholastica page, click “submit,” and follow the series of simple submission steps. The journal’s editors will then assess manuscripts and assign them to reviewers, coordinating peer review on Scholastica. Accepted papers will be published on arXiv with all final edits and will be assigned an article number, DOI, and publication date in order to be officially added to Discrete Analysis. The journal plans to publish brief descriptions of all of its published papers on its website accompanied by links to the articles on arXiv, giving readers the ability to easily browse the journal’s content. Authors will also have the ability to update their articles and request that the journal’s editors include those updates in the article listing along with the link to the official peer-reviewed version.

The future of Discrete Analysis

According to Gowers, part of the motivation for the journal is to challenge existing publishing models and to contribute to lowering the cost barrier of making research OA. Gowers said that he hopes this model will become normalized in time. “I hope that in due course people will get used to this publication model, at which point the fact that Discrete Analysis is an arXiv overlay journal will no longer seem interesting or novel, and the main interest in the journal will be the mathematics it contains,” said Gowers.

Discrete Analysis will be broad in scope, accepting papers on subjects that are broadly related to additive combinatorics, including: harmonic analysis, ergodic theory, topological dynamics, growth in groups, analytic number theory, combinatorial number theory, extremal combinatorics, probabilistic combinatorics, combinatorial geometry, convexity, metric geometry, and mathematical focused theoretical computer science.

Gowers will serve as the journal’s managing editor along with a distinguished editorial board including: Ernie Croot, Gil Kalai, Bryna Kra, Tom Sanders, Jozsef Solymosi, Terence Tao, Julia Wolf, and Tamar Ziegler.

Discrete Analysis is already making waves in the mathematics community with the recent news that one of the journal’s editors, Terence Tao, successfully crowdsourced efforts to solve the famous Erdős discrepancy problem. A paper on this breakthrough was submitted to Discrete Analysis.

The Scholastica team is excited for the future of this journal and the innovative publishing model it is fostering. We encourage you to check it out!
