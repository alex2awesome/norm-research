Convergence

Welcome to Convergence! This MAA publication offers a wealth of resources to help you teach mathematics using its history.

  • See Featured Items (below) & 2021 Table of Contents for new articles.
  • Browse our annual Tables of Contents (What's in Convergence?).
  • Visit our Portrait Gallery & our Paul Halmos Photo Collection.
  • Explore our ever-expanding Mathematical Treasures collection.
  • Find groups of resources in our list of Article Series that have appeared in Convergence.
  • Access Translations of original sources written in languages other than English.
  • Looking for additional scholarship on the history of mathematics? Check out this index of articles and books that have earned awards from the MAA.

Make Convergence your source for math history and its use in teaching!

Mathematical Treasures

Featured Items

Wronskians and Linear Independence: A Theorem Misunderstood by Many – A Mini-Primary Source Project for Students of Differential Equations, Linear Algebra and Others

The author uses 19th-century efforts to prove the converse of the Function Independence Theorem to show students that struggles and missteps in mathematics are normal.
The Four Curves of Alexis Clairaut

Provides an English translation of a paper written in 1726 by a 12-year-old Alexis Clairaut.
The ‘Piling Up of Squares’ in Ancient China

Reprint of Frank Swetz's 1977 "The 'Piling up of Squares' in Ancient China," originally published in the NCTM's Mathematics Teacher.
Converting the Old Babylonian Tablet ‘Plimpton 322’ into the Decimal System as a Classroom Exercise

A student-ready activity, ideal for pre-service elementary mathematics teachers.
Babylonian Numeration: A Mini-Primary Source Project for Pre-service Teachers and Other Students

An open-ended exploration of a (lesser-known) Babylonian tablet.
The French Connection: Borda, Condorcet and the Mathematics of Voting Theory

A recounting of the 18th century discovery of voting theory, and a student project based on the tale.
Apportionment: What's Your Fair Share – An Activity for Liberal Arts and High School Students

A mathematical exploration of congressional representation, from the 1790 US Census to the Huntington-Hill Method.
Topology from Analysis: A Mini-Primary Source Project for Topology Students

A project based on a work by Cantor that helps students bridge the gap from more familiar mathematics to the abstract concepts of topology.
Pathways from the Past: Classroom-Ready Materials for Using History to Teach Mathematics

A collection of reproducible student activity sheets that use historical settings to teach or reinforce basic mathematical lessons.

 
HOM SIGMAA 2020 Student Paper Contest Winner

PDF of the 2020 winning entry by Jeffrey Powers of Grand Rapids Community College.
Braess’ Paradox in City Planning: A Mini-Primary Source Project for Multivariable Calculus Students

A student project in multivariable optimization based on a classic paper in transportation science.

 
Word Histories: Melding Mathematics and Meanings

The origins of some common mathematical words that can enrich student learning.
Teaching and Learning the Trigonometric Functions through Their Origins

A series of curricular units based on primary source texts for use in teaching and learning trigonometry.
Mabel Sykes: A Life Untold and an Architectural Geometry Book Rediscovered

An incredible compendium of geometry problems, illustrated with GeoGebra applets, by a little-known author.
