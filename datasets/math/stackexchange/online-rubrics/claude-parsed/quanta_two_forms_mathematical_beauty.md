# The Two Forms of Mathematical Beauty — Robbert Dijkgraaf, Quanta Magazine (2020)

An essay distinguishing two recognisable aesthetic registers in math praise: generic vs. exceptional beauty.

## Form 1 — Generic Beauty (the "Cold and Formal")
- Ethereal, formal, like "the beauty of an ice queen."
- Reflects abstract structures and patterns.
- Universal order, logical sequences.
- Examples: natural numbers, Euclidean spaces, formal logic.
- Values abstraction and general principles applicable to entire categories.

## Form 2 — Exceptional Beauty (the "Curiosity Cabinet")
- Focuses on outliers and one-of-a-kind objects.
- Relatable, exotic, intimate, subjective.
- Celebrates curiosities that don't fit larger patterns.
- Examples: dodecahedron, the Monster group.
- The dodecahedron is "complicated, but not too complicated."

## Comparative Verdict
- Both registers appeal to mathematicians.
- **Generic beauty has proven more powerful** for understanding the physical universe.
- Historical evidence:
  - Kepler's attraction to exceptional Platonic solids initially misled astronomical models.
  - Newton's abstract laws of gravity better explained planetary motion.
  - Modern science consistently discovers that "the universe's beauty lies in the abstract structures underlying physical phenomena."

## Implication for Rubrics
A "good" math piece may be praised for either:
- Capturing a *general* structural pattern (generic-beauty axis), or
- Illuminating a *singular* striking object (exceptional-beauty axis).
Conflating the two collapses real distinctions in mathematician taste.
