# Mathematical Proof — Wikipedia

The mainstream collated definition and standards for a mathematical proof.

## Foundational Standards

1. **Logical certainty.** Proofs establish certainty through exhaustive deductive reasoning, distinct from empirical or inductive arguments.
2. **Universal truth.** A proof "must demonstrate that the statement is true in *all* possible cases", not merely present supporting examples.
3. **Rigorous argumentation in natural language.** "A proof is expressed in natural language and is a rigorous argument intended to convince the audience of the truth of a statement."
4. **Community standards.** "To gain acceptance, a proof has to meet communal standards of rigor; an argument considered vague or incomplete may be rejected."

## Logical Foundation
5. **Axiom-based reasoning.** Construct proofs from axioms and accepted rules of inference.
6. **Deductive logic.** Deductive, not inductive, reasoning establishes certainty.

## Implication
The encyclopedia-level rubric: a proof is "good" iff it (i) is deductive, (ii) covers all cases, (iii) builds from axioms, (iv) is convincing to the relevant community. The community-relativity is itself notable — rigor is socially calibrated.
