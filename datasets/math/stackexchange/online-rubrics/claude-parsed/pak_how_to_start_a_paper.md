# How to Start a Paper — Igor Pak (2022)

## Core Principle
Rather than jumping directly to results, prepare readers by establishing context and motivation first.

## Recommendations

1. **Write a Foreword.** Include "a paragraph or two devoted to philosophy underlying your work or a high level explanation of the key idea" before stating main results.
2. **Make it literary and memorable.** "Consider putting in the Foreword some highly literary description of what you are doing. If it's beautiful or sufficiently memorable, it might be quoted in other papers."
3. **Discuss the big picture.** Cover motivational examples, general physical or philosophical principles, and connections to other fields — "there is no other place in the paper to do this."
4. **Use compelling openings.** Examples: nature of the field and its tools; classical context paired with modern developments; philosophical puzzles underlying the work; relationships between different mathematical areas.

## When the Direct Approach Works
"Get to the point" only succeeds for simple stories — e.g. resolving a famous conjecture where readers already understand the background.

## Caveat
Pak emphasises that some examples in his post are "for amusement purposes and definitely not [to] be emulated."

## Implication
A "good" paper signals at the outset which kind of paper it is — a famous-conjecture closure or a context-laden story — and writes the opening to match.
