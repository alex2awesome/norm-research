# Be Sceptical of Your Own Work — Terry Tao (Career Advice)

A blog post articulating warning signs that a "proof" may be wrong, used as quality criteria for one's own work before publication.

## Skepticism Triggers

1. **Suspect effortless solutions.** "If you unexpectedly find a problem solving itself almost effortlessly, and you can't quite see why, you should try to analyse your solution more sceptically."
2. **Test method strength.** Examine whether your approach can prove statements known to be false — this reveals methodological flaws.
3. **Seek counterexamples first.** Before proving ambitious claims, hunt for counterexamples; either you'll save time or gain insight into necessary proof conditions.
4. **Red flag: sudden simplification.** When difficulty drops suddenly mid-proof, scrutinise that transition; it likely contains the error.
5. **Examine proof-by-contradiction integrity.** Test whether your reductio works without your initial assumption — if so, the assumption wasn't essential.
6. **Watch for topic drift.** Be suspicious if computations move far from the problem's core mathematical context and connections.
7. **Question "novel twists."** When adapting established methods with your unique modification that experts "missed," expect hidden technical flaws others avoided deliberately.

## Contrast with Genuine Problem-Solving
Genuine problem-solving usually involves: incremental progress on toy models, method refinement, discovering limitations, integrating insights from other fields, and iterative development over years.

## Implication for Good Math
A "good" proof passes self-skepticism: the author has actively tried to break their own argument and failed.
