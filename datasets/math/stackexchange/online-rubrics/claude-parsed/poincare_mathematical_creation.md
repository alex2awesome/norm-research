# Poincaré: Mathematical Creation — Aesthetic Selection Criteria

Poincaré's classic essay locates the criterion for "useful" mathematical combinations in an aesthetic sensibility. His criteria for what makes mathematical work good:

## Aesthetic Criteria for Mathematical Quality

- **Harmony of disposition**: Elements are "harmoniously disposed so that the mind without effort can embrace their totality while realizing the details." Beauty consists in this unity-of-whole-and-parts.
- **Economy**: A good mathematical entity uses minimal apparatus. Avoid superfluous symbols, hypotheses, or steps.
- **Simplicity**: The simpler combination among equally valid ones is preferred.
- **Originality / unexpectedness**: Combinations that surprise — that link previously unrelated domains — are the most valuable.
- **Elegance**: Geometric elegance — the form of presentation matters as much as truth.
- **Profundity**: Depth that hints at a wider law beyond the immediate result.
- **Clarity**: The reader's mind grasps the structure "without effort."
- **Symmetry, regularity, infinity**: Inner mathematical features that signal beauty.

## The Selection Mechanism (the working mathematician's criterion)

Poincaré claims the **subliminal mind generates many combinations**, but only those that are "harmonious" rise to consciousness. Therefore:

- **Beautiful = useful**: "The useful combinations are precisely the most beautiful." Aesthetic merit is the working selector for mathematical truth, not a separate ornamental quality.
- **Aesthetic feeling is a cognitive faculty**: "The feeling of mathematical beauty, of the harmony of numbers and forms, of geometric elegance" is "a true aesthetic feeling that all real mathematicians know" — and it functions as a heuristic guide for what is worth pursuing.

## Stages of Mathematical Work (Poincaré's process model)

1. **Conscious preparation**: Hard, deliberate work on a problem.
2. **Unconscious incubation**: Setting the problem aside; the subliminal mind continues.
3. **Sudden illumination**: A solution presents itself, often in unrelated circumstances (the famous Fuchsian-functions stepping-on-the-bus moment).
4. **Conscious verification**: Checking the result rigorously and writing it up.

## Implied Rubric for Judging Mathematical Work

- Does the result **link distant domains** that previously seemed unrelated?
- Does its form make the underlying law **foreseeable** even before all details are checked?
- Can the **whole be grasped at once** without losing the details?
- Is each element **necessary** (no superfluous machinery)?
- Does it produce the characteristic **aesthetic emotion** in trained readers?
