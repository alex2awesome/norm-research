# Learn and Relearn Your Field — Terry Tao (Career Advice)

## Core Principles

1. **Deep understanding beyond surface knowledge.** Merely knowing a theorem's statement and proof is insufficient. "Dig deeper until you *really* understand what the lemma is all about."

2. **Multiple approaches.** Ask:
   - Can you find alternate proofs?
   - Do different proofs generalize in different ways?
   - What are their relative strengths and weaknesses?

3. **Test foundational assumptions.**
   - Why is each hypothesis necessary?
   - What generalizations exist (known, conjectured, or heuristic)?
   - Are simpler versions sufficient for some applications?

4. **Practical application and scope.**
   - What model examples demonstrate the lemma?
   - When is it useful versus unhelpful?
   - What problems can it solve or not solve?

5. **Broader context.**
   - Explore analogues in other mathematical areas.
   - Identify wider paradigms or programs the concept fits into.

## Methods

- **Lecturing and writing.** Teaching your field — through lectures, lecture notes, or expository material — internalises difficult results efficiently and frees mental space for advanced learning.
- **Citation searching.** Use tools like MathSciNet to find papers citing key works, discovering relevant research.
- **Reflective practice.** Return to solved problems rather than moving immediately onward; this reveals important connections and deeper insights.

## Implication for "Good Math"
Mastery is iterative. A "good" exposition is one written by an author who has *re-learned* their field, not just learned it once.
