# In Search of God's Perfect Proofs — Aigner & Ziegler (Quanta, 2018)

Interview-essay distilling the editorial criteria used by Aigner and Ziegler in their book *Proofs from THE BOOK* (Erdős's "the Book" of perfect proofs).

## Named Criteria

1. **Brevity.** "A proof that eats more than 10 pages cannot be a proof for our book." (Ziegler)
2. **Clarity.** The proof must be understandable to readers, not just technically correct.
3. **A Special Idea.** A proof should contain an innovative or surprising concept; mere correctness is not enough.
4. **Unexpected Connections.** The proof ideally "connect[s] things that usually one wouldn't think of as having any connection."
5. **Reader-Dependent Beauty.** "Whether the proof is understandable and beautiful depends not only on the proof but also on the reader: What do you know?"

## Companion Principles

- **Multiple proofs of the same theorem are valuable.** Different proofs reveal different truths and let mathematicians "understand the situation beyond the original basic theorem."
- **Surprise of tool.** Lovász's topological proof of the Kneser conjecture is exemplary — using topology in graph theory.
- **Inevitability vs. wonder.** Some proofs feel inevitable after discovery; others retain mystery. Both registers are admissible as beauty.
- **Confidence through ugliness.** Ugly proofs serve a purpose: establishing truth first, enabling later elegant simplifications. The first proof need not be beautiful.

## Implication for Rubrics
"Beauty" decomposes into: brevity + clarity + a single special idea + unexpected connection + reader-relative comprehension. All five are necessary; no one alone suffices.
