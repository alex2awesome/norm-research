# Tao: What is Good Mathematics? — The Complete Dimensional List

NOTE: An existing file `tao_what_is_good_mathematics.md` covers Tao's overall thesis. This file extracts the **complete enumerated list** of dimensions Tao explicitly names, with brief gloss on each, so the list can be used directly as a multi-axis rubric.

## Tao's Premise

There is no single criterion for "good mathematics." There are many distinct, sometimes orthogonal, dimensions on which a piece of mathematical work can be excellent. A good rubric uses several, not one.

## The Full Dimensional List (each is a separate axis)

A piece of mathematical work can be:

1. **Good problem-solving** — solves a major previously-open problem.
2. **Good technique** — develops or executes a virtuoso method that others can reuse.
3. **Good theory** — builds a framework that organizes a body of results.
4. **Good insight** — provides a conceptual shift that reorganizes understanding.
5. **Good discovery** — uncovers an unexpected phenomenon, connection, or object.
6. **Good application** — contributes to another science or engineering field.
7. **Good exposition** — presents existing material with unusual clarity, accessibility, or insight.
8. **Good pedagogy** — teaches others to do the work, develops the next generation.
9. **Good vision** — articulates a long-term direction or program (cf. Hilbert, Langlands).
10. **Good taste** — selects the right problems to work on, recognizes value early.
11. **Good public relations** — communicates mathematics to non-mathematicians effectively.
12. **Good meta-mathematics** — clarifies the foundations, methodology, or self-understanding of the field.
13. **Good rigor** — establishes results to the highest standard of certainty (especially relative to the field's norms).
14. **Good beauty** — produces results with strong aesthetic appeal in the Hardy/Poincaré sense.
15. **Good elegance** — minimal apparatus, surprising compression.
16. **Good creativity** — original ideas, unexpected approaches.
17. **Good usefulness** — generates further mathematical work as a tool.
18. **Good strength** — pushes a result to its sharpest form.
19. **Good depth** — non-routine, non-superficial.
20. **Good intuition** — shows the underlying picture, not just the formal argument.
21. **Good definitions** — introduces concepts that capture the right structure.
22. **Good notation** — uses or invents notation that clarifies (cf. Knuth on TeX).
23. **Good organization** — structures a body of material so the relations among parts are visible.

(Tao's exposition explicitly enumerates many of these; a few are derived from his Szemerédi case study and discussion.)

## Tao's Key Methodological Claims

1. **No single ranking exists.** A theorem strong on one axis may be weak on another, and rational mathematicians can disagree about overall ranking based on weighting.
2. **Different subfields weight axes differently.** Combinatorics rewards problem-solving; algebraic geometry rewards theory-building.
3. **Time changes the weighting.** What looks like good "discovery" in year 0 may look like good "theory" by year 50 once it has been organized.
4. **Multiple dimensions reinforce.** A great piece of mathematics typically scores high on several axes simultaneously, not just one.
5. **The Szemerédi theorem case study** shows a single result that is good problem-solving, good technique, good theory, good insight, good discovery, good application — to ergodic theory, harmonic analysis, combinatorics, model theory.

## Implied Rubric for Mathematical Work

For each piece of mathematics, score on the 23 dimensions above. Then:

- **Identify peaks**: which 2–3 dimensions does the work score high on?
- **Identify trade-offs**: which dimensions did it sacrifice to gain its peaks?
- **Compare across cultures**: would this work be ranked differently in a different subfield?
- **Project forward**: which dimensions will rise/fall as the work matures?

## Anti-Patterns Tao Implicitly Identifies

- **One-axis maximization**: chasing pure beauty at cost of utility, or pure rigor at cost of insight.
- **Conflating dimensions**: treating "good problem-solving" and "good theory" as the same thing.
- **Ignoring the meta-axes**: dismissing exposition, pedagogy, public relations as "second-tier" mathematics.

## Why Tao's List Is the Reference Rubric

Tao's enumeration is the most explicit, multi-dimensional rubric for mathematical quality produced by a working mathematician of the highest rank. It is widely cited because it operationalizes what most mathematicians already feel: that "quality" is not one thing.
