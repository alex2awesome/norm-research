# Mathematics in the Library of Babel — Daniel Litt (2026)

A reflective blog post articulating what mathematics is *for the human*, especially in the AI era.

## What Mathematics Is

> "Mathematics isn't only about saying true things. It's about asking the right questions, being confused, stumbling about, getting distracted, being wrong, recognizing when you're wrong, being stuck. Mostly being stuck. It's about clinging to a giant edifice and feeling it out until you understand some tiny piece of it."

Math involves embodied struggle — developing intuition by repeated engagement until comprehension emerges through accumulated contact.

## Criteria for Good Proofs and Writing

1. **Clarity and insight.** Expert proofs make "main ideas, obstructions to be overcome, etc." identifiable; weak proofs (e.g. AI-generated ones) often obscure the reasoning path entirely.
2. **Meaningful conceptualisation.** Humans "develop useful new objects, terminology, etc. to capture what they're doing" — creating lasting conceptual frameworks beyond mere argument execution.
3. **Truth-seeking orientation.** Mathematics depends on practitioners being "by and large truth-seeking" — not generating persuasive-sounding but false claims.

## Research Value
Proving existing lemmas is only a portion of math. The harder work is "discovering the statements of crucial lemmas" — theory-building, taste, asking which questions matter.

## Implication for "Good Math"
A "good" piece of mathematical writing exposes (a) what the author was confused about, (b) which conceptual scaffolding they built, (c) which obstructions they overcame. Polished mechanical correctness is necessary but not sufficient.
