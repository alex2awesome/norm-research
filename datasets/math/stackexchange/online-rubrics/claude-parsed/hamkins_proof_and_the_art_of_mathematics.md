# Proof and the Art of Mathematics — Joel David Hamkins (MIT Press, 2020 + blog)

A widely-adopted introduction to proof writing, with explicit aesthetic claims.

## Five Stated Criteria for a Good Proof

1. **Logical establishment.** Proofs must "logically establish their conclusions."
2. **Clarity.** "Clear and correct mathematical arguments."
3. **Communication of truth.** "Proofs tell us not only that a mathematical statement is true, but also why it is true, and they communicate this truth."
4. **Insight and elegance.** "Provide whatever insight and elegance we can muster."
5. **Deep understanding.** "The best proofs give us insight into the nature of mathematical reality" and create transformative moments when "a previously opaque, confounding issue becomes transparent."

## Pedagogical Notes
- The theorem-proof format helps distinguish the claim from its proof.
- Maintain "complete clarity about what precisely the theorem is."
- Choose topics that are intrinsically interesting to learn proof-writing on (Hamkins organises his book by topic, not method).

## Implication
Hamkins formalises the modern teaching consensus: a proof is "good" not just when correct but when it *teaches* — opacity → transparency is the operative criterion.
