# Learn the Power of Other Mathematicians' Tools — Terry Tao

## Core Principles

1. **Learn beyond your specialty.** Mathematicians should not become trapped by their own toolkit. When encountering an unfamiliar method, "try to see whether your own tools can in fact accomplish a similar task."
2. **Compare strengths and weaknesses.** Rather than dismissing other techniques, study "the simplest model case in which that tool does something non-trivial" to develop a meaningful comparison.
3. **Build a diverse repertoire.** Through consistent practice, "add that tool permanently to your repertoire."
4. **Cross-disciplinary study.** Use survey articles and proceedings (e.g. ICM materials) to understand how other fields approach their work.

## Quality Marker
A "good" piece of mathematics is one whose author has clearly absorbed tools from outside their immediate area; this shows in proof choice, references, and motivation.
