# How to Write a Clear Math Paper: Some 21st Century Tips — Igor Pak (2018)

Journal of Humanistic Mathematics 8 (1): 301–328. A modern, opinionated counterweight to Halmos: takes the specific writing problems of the post-arXiv era seriously.

## Pak's golden rule

**"Be clear!"** — the single rule that subsumes the others. Clear writing is the only sustainable competitive advantage when many groups race to the same theorem.

## Macro tips (organization of the paper)

1. **Title** — informative, searchable, no clever puns.
2. **Abstract** — self-contained; assumes no notation; states the main theorem in plain words; ≤ 200 words.
3. **Introduction** — six sub-sections:
   - Context and motivation.
   - Statement of main results.
   - Comparison to prior work.
   - Outline of methods.
   - Structure of the paper.
   - Acknowledgments and conventions.
4. **Table of contents** — for any paper > 30 pages.
5. **Foreword / final remarks** — connect to broader narrative; future directions.
6. **Acknowledgments** — every funding source; every helpful conversation.

## References (Pak devotes unusual attention)

7. **Cite all relevant prior work**, including work superseded by yours.
8. **Cite the original** source, not the textbook copy.
9. **Cite preprints** with arXiv IDs.
10. **Place citations precisely** — at the claim being supported.
11. **Choose a consistent format** (Pak prefers numeric, alphabetical-by-author).
12. **Cross-cite** — refer back to your earlier numbered claims.

## Micro tips (sentence-level)

13. **Master grammar, syntax, punctuation.** No excuses for a non-native speaker — copyedit professionally.
14. **Avoid pedantic language.** "We may write without loss of generality" → "Assume".
15. **Simplify writing style.** Short sentences, plain words, active voice.
16. **Use LaTeX carefully.** Don't trust auto-formatting; check spacing, hyphenation, line breaks by eye.
17. **Figures and illustrations matter.** A figure can replace a paragraph; design captions to be self-contained.

## Pak's distinctive 21st-century points

18. **The paper is online.** Readers find it via search; optimize titles and abstracts for search.
19. **The paper is read on screens.** Use displayed equations sparingly; long inline runs break on small screens.
20. **The paper has competitors.** Multiple groups race; clear writing wins credit-attribution disputes.
21. **The paper is accessed in pieces.** Sections must be independently readable.
22. **The paper has hyperlinks.** Use \cref and active citation links; arXiv IDs as URLs.
23. **The paper is read by graduate students worldwide** for whom English is a second language. Simplicity is mercy.

## On promoting your work (Pak's contrarian point)

- Writing well is necessary but not sufficient.
- Post to arXiv before journal acceptance.
- Present at conferences; be active on MathOverflow.
- Follow up with a blog post or expository note.
- Maintain a clean personal website.

## Recurring meta-principle

> "Writing is never finished, only abandoned."

Plan rewrites. Update preprints. The paper is a living object.
