# Learn the Limitations of Your Tools — Terry Tao

## Core Principles

1. **Distinguish knowledge from understanding.** "An education isn't how much you have committed to memory, or even how much you know. It's being able to differentiate between what you do know and what you don't." (Anatole France, cited by Tao.)
2. **Know when tools fail.** Understanding where your methods don't work prevents wasted effort on doomed strategies.
3. **Build a library of counterexamples.** Maintain model situations and obstructions that reveal which problems your tools can/cannot address.
4. **Avoid the "magic wand" trap.** If you view a tool as mysteriously solving problems without deeper comprehension, you need stronger foundations.
5. **Test understanding through reconstruction.** When proving significant results, try to rewrite arguments without your preferred tool. If impossible, you may be misapplying the method.
6. **Discuss limitations in publications.** Transparently addressing known constraints builds credibility and guides future research.

## Implication for "Good Math"
A "good" paper acknowledges its tools' limits — what its argument *cannot* show — rather than presenting the technique as omnipotent.
