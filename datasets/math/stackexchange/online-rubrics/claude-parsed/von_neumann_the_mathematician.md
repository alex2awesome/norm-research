# Von Neumann: The Mathematician — Aesthetic Selection in Mathematics

In this widely cited essay, von Neumann argues that mathematicians' criteria for selecting problems and judging success are "mainly aesthetical." He gives an unusually concrete description of what mathematical elegance consists of.

## Von Neumann's Criteria for Mathematical Elegance

A piece of mathematics is "elegant" — i.e., satisfies the working mathematician's aesthetic criteria — when:

1. **Ease of statement**: The problem can be stated simply and concisely (often with everyday words, not jargon).
2. **Difficulty of access**: It is genuinely hard. Standard approaches fail.
3. **Surprising twist**: The eventual solution turns on an unexpected idea — a reframing, a new connection, an unanticipated tool — that suddenly makes the problem tractable.
4. **Underlying explanation**: When deductions are long, there is **some simple general principle that "explains" the complications**. A long calculation without an organizing idea is inelegant even if correct.
5. **Architectural / structural makeup**: The proof has a discernible architecture — a global plan, not a sequence of local moves.

## Mathematics as Empirical-Aesthetic Hybrid

Von Neumann's deeper thesis is that pure mathematics, after a long autonomous run, risks **"baroque" degeneration** unless replenished by empirical contact:

- Mathematical fields severed from physical/scientific motivation tend to "art for art's sake": chasing technical refinement at the cost of substance.
- Periodic re-injection of empirical problems restores taste.
- A research program is suspect when it can be characterized only by "increasing technical sophistication" with no growth in scope or depth.

## Implied Rubric

- Can the result be **stated to a non-specialist** in a sentence or two?
- Was the problem genuinely hard, or merely a routine exercise in existing technique?
- Is there a **surprising idea** (one sentence one could quote) that drives the proof?
- Does any long calculation have a **principle that explains** it, or is it sheer manipulation?
- Does the work connect to **substantive motivation** — physical, computational, or another domain — or only to internal mathematical fashion?
- Architecture: can a reader **redraw the proof from a sketch** because it has structure?

## On Comparison with the Arts

Von Neumann concludes that selection criteria in mathematics "are clearly those of any creative art." This implies that mathematical work should be judged with the same evaluative apparatus as a piece of music or a painting: **economy, surprise, structure, depth, and connection to lived experience**.
