# NCTM Process Standards — National Council of Teachers of Mathematics

From *Principles and Standards for School Mathematics* (2000). The five process standards describe what mathematical *practice* looks like at every grade level, K–12. They form an implicit rubric for what makes mathematical work "good" beyond mere correctness.

## The five process standards

### 1. Problem Solving
A "good" mathematical performance involves:
- Building new mathematical knowledge through problem solving (not just exercising known knowledge).
- Solving problems that arise in mathematics and in other contexts.
- Applying and adapting a variety of strategies — generalize, specialize, work backward, draw a figure, look for symmetry.
- Monitoring and reflecting on the process — metacognition.

### 2. Reasoning and Proof
- Recognize reasoning and proof as fundamental aspects of mathematics.
- Make and investigate mathematical conjectures.
- Develop and evaluate mathematical arguments and proofs.
- Select and use various types of reasoning and methods of proof — direct, contrapositive, contradiction, induction, case analysis.

### 3. Communication
- Organize and consolidate thinking through communication.
- Communicate coherently and clearly to peers, teachers, others.
- Analyze and evaluate the thinking and strategies of others.
- Use the language of mathematics to express ideas precisely.

### 4. Connections
- Recognize and use connections among mathematical ideas.
- See mathematics as a coherent whole, not a list of disconnected procedures.
- Recognize and apply mathematics in contexts outside of mathematics.

### 5. Representation
- Create and use representations to organize, record, and communicate ideas.
- Select, apply, and translate among multiple representations (algebraic, geometric, graphical, tabular, verbal).
- Use representations to model and interpret physical, social, and mathematical phenomena.

## NCTM's implicit rubric for evaluating a math performance

A complete piece of mathematical work — a homework solution, a project, a Q&A answer — should exhibit all five processes:

| Process | Visible signal |
|---|---|
| Problem Solving | Multiple strategies considered; reflection at the end |
| Reasoning and Proof | Conjecture stated; argument follows; method named |
| Communication | Complete sentences; clear notation; precise vocabulary |
| Connections | Reference to related results; placement in larger picture |
| Representation | At least two of: algebra, picture, table, graph, words |

## Common Core's eight Standards for Mathematical Practice (descendants of NCTM's processes)

The Common Core's SMPs explicitly extend NCTM's framework:

1. Make sense of problems and persevere.
2. Reason abstractly and quantitatively.
3. Construct viable arguments and critique others.
4. Model with mathematics.
5. Use appropriate tools strategically.
6. **Attend to precision** — careful notation, units, definitions.
7. Look for and make use of structure.
8. Look for and express regularity in repeated reasoning.

## Influence

- Foundation of K–12 math curriculum in essentially every U.S. state.
- Standard for math-teacher credentialing (CCSSO, NCATE).
- Cited by Common Core; UK National Curriculum; Australian Curriculum; Singapore Maths Framework.
- Used by graders and assessment designers (Smarter Balanced, PARCC).
