# What Makes a Proof Beautiful — Olive Bradshaw, I,Science Magazine

A short popular-science feature distilling the working aesthetic vocabulary mathematicians use to praise proofs.

## The Five Criteria

1. **Simplicity.** "The proof uses few assumptions or previous results and comes together swiftly."
2. **Concision.** "The proof is surprisingly succinct; every part is effective and necessary."
3. **Unexpectedness.** "The proof derives a result in an unexpected or surprising way, often using an unrelated area of mathematics."
4. **Originality.** "The proof is based on an original or not-before-seen insight."
5. **Generalisability.** "The proof can be easily extended to a larger family of similar problems."

## Anti-Criteria
- Long, laborious calculations -> "ugly" or "clumsy."
- Heavy reliance on many previous results -> opaque.

## Reader/Process Caveats
- Beauty has a strong **experiential** dimension — felt while working through the proof.
- Beauty depends on the audience: "Beauty is in the eye of the beholder."
- **Presentation matters** — a beautiful proof should read as a narrative with clear structure.
