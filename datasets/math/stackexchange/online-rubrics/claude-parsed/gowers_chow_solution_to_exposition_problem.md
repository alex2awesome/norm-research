# A Solution to an Exposition Problem — Tim Gowers (2009), endorsing Tim Chow

A blog post in which Gowers endorses Timothy Chow's framework for solving "the exposition problem" — making seemingly miraculous proofs feel inevitable.

## Chow's Core Criteria

1. **Every step should be motivated and clear** — readers need logical justification for each move.
2. **Students should feel they could have arrived at the results themselves** — exposition should feel discoverable, not magical.
3. **Render ideas totally perspicuous** — completely transparent.

## Gowers's Additional Insights

1. **Weak vs. strong solutions.**
   - *Weak solution:* explains why each step is reasonable.
   - *Strong solution:* provides theoretical justification for why the calculation works, not just that it does.
2. **Motivation matters.** Readers should understand why problems matter before diving into technical details.
3. **Demystification.** The goal is to address the "exposition problem" of seemingly miraculous definitions or proofs — show how a reasonable mathematician could have discovered them.
4. **Natural development.** Rather than presenting finished arguments, exposition should trace plausible thought processes leading naturally to the key insight.

## Implication for "Good Math"
A "good" exposition is one that, in retrospect, makes the proof feel *inevitable* — the reader feels they could have done it themselves. The opposite is the "magic-trick" proof.
