# A Few Tips on Writing Papers with Mathematical Content — John N. Tsitsiklis (2017)

MIT LIDS technical note. Awarded the INFORMS Saul Gass Expository Writing Award. Strongly recommended for graduate students in operations research, EE, and applied math.

## Tsitsiklis's principles

### Sentence-level

1. **Break long sentences into multiple sentences.** A mathematical sentence with three subclauses is unparsable.
2. **One idea per sentence.** Definition, claim, and proof step do not share a sentence.
3. **Prefer short sentences over long.**
4. **Use active voice when possible.**
5. **Avoid nominalizations.** "We prove convergence" beats "A proof of convergence is provided".

### Section-level

6. **Keep sections to one theme.** When a section grows beyond 3 pages, split.
7. **Each section has a topic sentence** that previews its contents.
8. **End sections with a transition sentence** to the next.
9. **Use sub-sections aggressively.** Two-page chunks help the reader resume after interruption.
10. **Number sections so cross-references stay valid** under revision.

### Mathematical notation

11. **Minimal notation.** Don't introduce a symbol you use once.
12. **Mnemonic letters.** $G$ for graph, $A$ for matrix.
13. **Consistent typography.** All matrices uppercase bold; all vectors lowercase bold; all scalars italic.
14. **Avoid stacked subscripts and superscripts.** $A_i^{(j)}$ is fine; $A_{i_1}^{j_1^{k}}$ is not.
15. **Don't reuse a letter** for two different objects in the same paper.
16. **Define every symbol on first use.** Even "obvious" ones.

### Theorems and proofs

17. **State a theorem in full English** before stating it formally.
18. **Distinguish theorems, propositions, lemmas, corollaries** by significance and place in the argument.
19. **A proof comes immediately after the theorem.** Postponed proofs interrupt the reader's focus.
20. **Long proofs need a roadmap** — three-line preview of the global strategy.

### Figures and tables

21. **Every figure needs a self-contained caption.**
22. **Axes labeled with units.**
23. **Legends inside the plot, not in the caption** when possible.
24. **Tables: row totals at the bottom, column totals at the right** (consistency).

### The introduction

25. **First paragraph: the problem and why it matters.**
26. **Second paragraph: prior work in one paragraph.**
27. **Third paragraph: contribution.**
28. **Fourth paragraph: paper outline.**

### Revision discipline

29. **Sleep on the draft.**
30. **Read aloud.**
31. **Show to a colleague who is not in the field.**
32. **Cut 10 percent of every section** in the second pass.

## A characteristic Tsitsiklis observation

> "If a sentence requires a re-read to parse, it is the writer's failure, not the reader's."
