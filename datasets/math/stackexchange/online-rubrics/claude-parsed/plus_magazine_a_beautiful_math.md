# A Beautiful Math — Plus Magazine review of Siegfried's book on Nash & game theory

A book review/essay distilling principles for what makes a body of mathematics "beautiful" beyond a single proof.

## Six Principles

1. **Universal applicability.** Math that explains "the coexistence of selfishness and sympathy, competition and co-operation, war and peace" across diverse fields demonstrates elegance through breadth.
2. **Foundational power.** Nash "did for the biological and social universes what Newton and Einstein accomplished for the physical universe" — transformative explanatory capacity.
3. **Elegant simplicity underlying complexity.** Nash equilibrium provides "a special balancing point" that resolves "fabulously complex" systems.
4. **Predictive precision.** Solutions "could always be found" for any scenario, offering certainty amid real-world uncertainty.
5. **Cross-disciplinary resonance.** Beautiful math "came to revolutionise" military strategy, economics, evolutionary biology, psychology, sociology — truth transcends single domains.
6. **Accessibility without sacrifice.** Rigorous concepts in a "light and conversational style" while maintaining intellectual integrity.

## Implication
"Beautiful math" — at the body-of-work scale — reveals hidden order, connects disparate phenomena, and solves previously intractable problems through elegant rather than brute-force approaches.
