# Think Ahead — Terry Tao (Career Advice)

## Core Principle
> "Worse than being blind, is to see and have no vision." — Helen Keller

Pause regularly to recall *why* you're pursuing a goal, not just *what* you're doing.

## Five Key Questions for Any Lemma or Task

1. **Purpose.** "If the lemma were proven, how would it be used?"
2. **Importance.** "What features of the lemma are most important for you?"
3. **Optimization.** "Would a weaker lemma suffice?"
4. **Clarity.** "Is there a simpler formulation of the lemma?"
5. **Feasibility.** "Is it worth trying to omit a hypothesis of the lemma, if that hypothesis seems hard to obtain?"

## Scope of Application
- Small tasks (claims, computations).
- Medium work (proving lemmas).
- Large projects (theorems, research programs, career goals).

## Implication for Good Mathematical Writing
A "good" lemma in a paper is one where the author has already answered these five questions; the reader feels purpose and necessity rather than incidental machinery.
