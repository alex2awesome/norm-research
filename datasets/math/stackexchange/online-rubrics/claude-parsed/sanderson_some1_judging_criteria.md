# Summer of Math Exposition (SoME1) — Grant Sanderson, 3Blue1Brown (2021)

The launch announcement for the annual SoME competition, articulating four explicit criteria for what makes good online math exposition.

## Four Judging Criteria

1. **Clarity.** "Jargon should be explained, the goals of the lesson should be understandable with minimal background." Empathy for newcomers.
2. **Motivation.** "Viewers should understand why they care within the first 30 seconds."
3. **Novelty.** Offer an experience unavailable elsewhere — fresh angles on familiar topics or surfacing under-explored ideas.
4. **Memorable.** Content should remain vivid months later through presentation quality, presenter enthusiasm, or striking insights.

## Entry Requirements (signal what counts)
1. Created during the contest period.
2. Mathematics broadly defined (incl. physics/CS).
3. One submission per person/group.
4. English.
5. Publicly available and free.

## Selection Process
- Peer review phase (rate ~5 submissions each).
- Pre-filtering for manageable judging pool.
- Final judges evaluate using the four criteria.
- Judges allocate ~5–10 minutes per entry for longer works.

## Implication for "Good Math Exposition"
Modern online math exposition has converged on a 4-axis rubric: **clarity + motivation + novelty + memorability**. These map differently from print-paper criteria (e.g. "rigor" is absent; "memorability" is unique to oral/visual media).
