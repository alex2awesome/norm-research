# A Mathematician's Apology — G. H. Hardy (1940)

The classic essay-book on the aesthetics and value of pure mathematics. Hardy's framework is the earliest formal rubric for what makes mathematics "good".

## Hardy's two master criteria

> "A mathematician, like a painter or a poet, is a maker of patterns. … The mathematician's patterns, like the painter's or the poet's, must be **beautiful**; the ideas, like the colours or the words, must fit together in a harmonious way. **Beauty** is the first test: there is no permanent place in the world for ugly mathematics."

The two criteria for judging mathematical work:

1. **Beauty** — aesthetic harmony of the pattern of ideas.
2. **Seriousness** — significance, importance, depth.

## Components of mathematical beauty (Hardy's sub-criteria)

1. **Generality** — the idea is one of the constituents of many mathematical constructs.
2. **Depth** — the result depends on, and reveals, deep ideas; geological-strata metaphor: the harder to prove, the deeper.
3. **Unexpectedness** — surprise in the conclusion or the method.
4. **Inevitability** — once seen, the result feels uniquely right; no other path could lead there.
5. **Economy** — minimal hypotheses, minimal apparatus, no extraneous matter.

## Components of seriousness

1. **Significance of the underlying ideas** — connects to other parts of mathematics.
2. **Generality** — the result applies in many settings.
3. **Depth** — penetrates to fundamentals.

## Hardy's negative criteria (what makes mathematics bad)

- Ugliness; mere computation.
- Triviality; obvious from definitions.
- Specialness; isolated curiosities with no broader connections.
- Dullness; no surprise.

## Hardy's two paradigm examples of "good mathematics"

1. **Euclid's proof of the infinitude of primes** — short, surprising, deep, general.
2. **Pythagoras's proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational** — short, surprising, deep, general.

Both proofs Hardy holds up as touchstones: a few lines, an unexpected device, conclusions of unbounded scope.

## Useful vs useless mathematics

Hardy famously argues that "useful" applied mathematics is mostly trivial; the "useless" pure mathematics is what is beautiful and serious. (History of course rendered him spectacularly wrong about number theory.)

## Influence

Every later list of mathematical virtues (Rota, Tao, Atiyah) traces back to Hardy's beauty/seriousness framework.
