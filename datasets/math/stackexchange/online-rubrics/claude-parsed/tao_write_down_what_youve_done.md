# Write Down What You've Done — Terry Tao (Career Advice)

## Core Recommendation
Record sketches of interesting mathematical arguments encountered during research; writing serves several purposes beyond documentation.

## Four Key Principles

1. **Personal knowledge preservation.** "It makes the argument permanently available to you in the future, and may eventually be helpful in your later research papers, lecture notes, teaching, or research proposals."
2. **Technical skill development.** Writing provides practice with TeX, exposition, pedagogical clarity.
3. **Comprehension testing.** Writing forces genuine understanding. If you cannot articulate an argument coherently on paper, you haven't truly mastered it.
4. **Cognitive efficiency.** "It frees up mental space; you no longer have to remember the exact details of the argument, and so can devote your memory to learning newer topics."

## Additional Guidance
- Preserve incomplete or abandoned work through informal sketches stored digitally with backups; these may prove useful later.

## Implication
Writing is not merely *output* but a *tool of thought*. A "good" math piece reflects an author who has used writing to test their own understanding.
