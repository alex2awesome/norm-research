# Putnam Mathematical Competition Grading

The Putnam, the most famous undergraduate mathematics competition in North America, has the most distinctive partial-credit scheme in mathematics.

## The scoring scale

Each of 12 problems is worth 10 points. Possible scores per problem:

**0, 1, 2, 8, 9, 10**

The scores 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 are essentially never awarded.

## The "Gap of Death" (3–7)

> "Only virtually perfect solutions receive 8 or more points; flawed solutions fall below the 'Gap of Death' between 3 and 7 points."

The Putnam graders intentionally enforce a binary judgment: either the solution is correct (with at most cosmetic flaws), or it is not. There is no credit for "I had the right idea".

## Score meanings

| Score | What it means |
|---|---|
| 10 | Correct, complete, well-presented. |
| 9 | Correct argument, one minor slip (algebra typo, missed degenerate case). |
| 8 | Correct main argument; small but nontrivial gap easily filled. |
| 2 | Some genuine progress beyond restating the problem; key insight missing. |
| 1 | A correct but trivial observation; nothing substantive. |
| 0 | No progress; or argument contains a fatal flaw. |

## Why the Gap?

The Putnam philosophy: a math problem is either solved or not. Awarding 5 points for "I almost had it" rewards plausible-sounding but wrong arguments. The Gap forces graders to make a binary judgment.

## Implications for "good" Putnam solutions

A solution is good only if it would receive a 10. This forces:

1. **Every step proved** — no "clearly", no "easy to see".
2. **Every case handled** — no missed degenerate case.
3. **Every quantifier explicit** — no implicit "for all sufficiently large".
4. **Verification of any claimed witness** — if you say "consider $x = 7$", show $7$ works.
5. **Honest treatment of what's missing** — if a step is hard, prove it; don't paper over.
6. **Legibility** — graders won't decode handwriting.
7. **Conclusion stated** — the final answer in a box, with the actual question's wording.

## Statistics

The median total score (out of 120) is typically 1. The median score per problem is therefore 0. The vast majority of attempts fall in the Gap of Death and receive 0–2.

## Comparison with USAMO

- USAMO 0–7 with all values possible (but middle values rare on hard problems).
- Putnam 0/1/2/8/9/10 only.

Both reflect the same philosophy: a proof either works or it doesn't, and partial credit is heavily disincentivized.

## Influence

- Defines the standard for undergraduate math competition grading.
- Cited as the model for many internal university competition rubrics.
- Influences how undergraduate analysis and algebra problem sets are graded at competitive universities (the "Putnam culture" of strict grading).
