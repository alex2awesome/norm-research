# IB Mathematics Internal Assessment Rubric — International Baccalaureate

The most widely-used rubric for assessing student-generated mathematical writing internationally. Used in IB Diploma Programme schools in 150+ countries. Distinctive among rubrics for explicitly scoring *personal engagement* alongside technical correctness.

## The five criteria (total 20 marks)

### Criterion A: Presentation (4 marks)
Assesses organization and coherence.

| Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Does not reach the standards described below. |
| 1 | Some coherence or organization. |
| 2 | Some coherence and shows organization. |
| 3 | Coherent and well organized. |
| 4 | Coherent, well organized, concise. |

### Criterion B: Mathematical communication (4 marks)
Assesses use of appropriate notation, symbols, terminology, and figures.

| Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 0 | Does not reach the standard. |
| 1 | Some relevant communication. |
| 2 | Some appropriate notation; some clear definitions. |
| 3 | Appropriate notation throughout; defined variables; key terms defined. |
| 4 | Concise and precise notation; tables and graphs are well-labelled and self-explanatory. |

### Criterion C: Personal engagement (3 marks)
Assesses extent to which the student takes ownership of the topic.

| Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 0 | None evidenced. |
| 1 | Some personal engagement. |
| 2 | Significant personal engagement. |
| 3 | Outstanding personal engagement: independent thinking, creativity, presenting ideas in own way, exploring different perspectives, making and testing predictions. |

### Criterion D: Reflection (3 marks)
Assesses how the student reviews, analyses, and evaluates the exploration.

| Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 0 | None evidenced. |
| 1 | Limited reflection. |
| 2 | Meaningful reflection: evaluation of methods, discussion of limitations, comparison of approaches. |
| 3 | Critical reflection: deeply insightful; evaluates results and implications; addresses limitations and next steps. |

### Criterion E: Use of mathematics (6 marks)
Assesses the mathematics produced.

**SL version:**
| Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 0 | None or off-topic. |
| 1 | Some relevant mathematics, with errors. |
| 2 | Relevant mathematics; some understanding. |
| 3 | Relevant; mostly correct; partial demonstration of understanding. |
| 4 | Relevant, correct, demonstrates understanding. |
| 5 | Relevant, correct, demonstrates understanding, used precisely. |
| 6 | Relevant, correct, precise, demonstrates thorough knowledge and understanding. |

**HL version:** Same structure but additionally requires the mathematics to be at HL level (e.g., uses calculus / linear algebra / discrete-math beyond SL syllabus).

## Distinctive features

1. **Personal engagement** is rewarded. A correct but rote computation maxes at 4/6 on Criterion E and 0/3 on Criterion C. The IB explicitly does not want copies of textbook problems.
2. **Reflection** is rewarded. Doing the math is necessary; *thinking about* what the math means and what its limitations are is required for top marks.
3. **Communication** is graded separately from correctness. A correct but poorly-presented solution can score badly.
4. **Presentation** as a separate criterion forces students to think of the IA as a written document, not just a problem set.

## Influence

- 150+ countries' IB schools.
- Strongly resembles undergraduate research-paper rubrics.
- Often cited as a model for high-school capstone math projects in non-IB curricula (NCSSM, UK A-level coursework before its 2017 abolition).
