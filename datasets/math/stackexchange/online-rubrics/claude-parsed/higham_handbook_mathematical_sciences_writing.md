# Handbook of Writing for the Mathematical Sciences — Nicholas J. Higham (1993, 1998, 2020)

SIAM. The de-facto reference cited by AMS, SIAM, and the Chicago Manual of Style for mathematical-sciences writing. Currently in 3rd edition.

## Scope

Covers all writing tasks faced by a mathematician or applied mathematician: papers, theses, books, slides, posters, web pages, blog posts, referee reports, CVs, grant proposals, recommendation letters, email.

## Higham's main themes

1. **Choose your journal first.** Style is downstream of audience.
2. **English style.** Active voice, short sentences, concrete nouns, strong verbs.
3. **Mathematical style.**
   - Inline vs. displayed formulas — rules of thumb.
   - Punctuation of displayed formulas — punctuate them as English.
   - Notation — mnemonic, consistent, sparse.
   - Subscripts and superscripts — minimize stacking.
4. **Numerical and algorithmic writing.**
   - Pseudocode conventions.
   - Reporting numerical experiments — what tables must contain (problem size, machine, software version, timing units).
   - Reproducibility — code and data archived with the paper.
5. **Citation and references.**
   - Cite the original source, not the textbook.
   - Avoid "for a review see X" without naming X precisely.
   - Use BibTeX; check entries by hand.
6. **Tables and figures.**
   - Each figure needs a self-contained caption.
   - Axes labeled with units.
   - Logarithmic axes labeled "log".
   - Color-blind safe palettes; legible at print resolution.
7. **TeX and LaTeX usage.**
   - Use \cdot for products; \,d for differentials.
   - Don't \mathbf bold variables that should be italic.
   - Use \text inside math mode for English words.
   - macro hygiene; one source of truth per symbol.
8. **The whole-paper view.**
   - Title length and content.
   - Abstract = self-contained.
   - Introduction includes literature, motivation, contribution, structure.
   - Conclusion is *not* a summary; it is forward-looking.
9. **Talks and slides.**
   - One idea per slide.
   - Title bar = thesis sentence.
   - Avoid full sentences on slides.
   - 1 minute per slide rule of thumb.
   - Practice; time it.
10. **Posters.**
    - Visible from 2 m away.
    - Reader's path is a U or Z; mark it.
    - Title 1, abstract 0; figures dominant.
11. **Working with an editor / referee.**
    - Acknowledge every comment in the response letter.
    - Fight only for what matters.
12. **Career documents.**
    - CV: chronological, complete, no jargon.
    - Cover letter: target the institution.
    - Statement: research arc with one sentence per paper.

## Higham's "ten commandments"

(Not formally enumerated as such, but the recurring rules are:)

1. Have a thesis; state it.
2. Know your reader.
3. Cut adjectives, adverbs, "essentially", "basically", "very".
4. Symbols are nouns; use words for verbs.
5. Display unwieldy formulas; punctuate them as English.
6. Notation must be unique (one symbol = one meaning).
7. Tables and figures are first-class citizens; caption them fully.
8. Cite primary sources.
9. Reproducibility is non-negotiable in numerical work.
10. Revise multiple times; read aloud.

## Influence

Cited by AMS Author Handbook, SIAM Style Manual, Cambridge University Press style guide. Required reading for SIAM editors.
