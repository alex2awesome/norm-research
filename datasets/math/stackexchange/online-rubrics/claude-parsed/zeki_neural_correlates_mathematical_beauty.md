# Zeki et al.: Neural Correlates of Mathematical Beauty

This fMRI study with 15 mathematicians provides empirical evidence that mathematical beauty is a real perceptual phenomenon, processed by the same neural circuitry as beauty in art and music.

## Key Empirical Findings

1. **Mathematical beauty activates field A1 of the medial orbito-frontal cortex (mOFC)** — the same region activated by visual and musical beauty.
2. The activation **scales parametrically with rated beauty** (more beautiful → more activation), suggesting a continuous, not categorical, judgment.
3. **High inter-rater agreement** on which formulae are most beautiful, despite individual differences elsewhere — pointing to a shared aesthetic faculty within the mathematical community.

## The Most Beautiful Formulae (in the study)

Top of the list, reliably rated as most beautiful:

1. **Euler's identity**: $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$
2. **Pythagorean identity**: $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$
3. **Cauchy-Riemann equations**: $\partial u/\partial x = \partial v/\partial y$, $\partial u/\partial y = -\partial v/\partial x$
4. The **Gauss-Bonnet theorem**, the **Riemann zeta functional equation**, and others.

## The Most "Ugly" Formulae

Those that were most often rated ugly were typically:

- Long combinatorial expansions with no organizing structure.
- Formulae that could be derived from simpler ones but were stated in expanded form.
- Series identities with arbitrary-looking coefficients.

## Implicit Criteria Inferred from the Beautiful Examples

What unites the formulae mathematicians find beautiful?

- **Compactness**: short statement.
- **Connection of disparate domains**: Euler's identity links five fundamental constants from different areas.
- **Inevitability**: the result feels necessary, not contingent.
- **Surprising compression**: a deep relation expressed in minimal symbols.
- **Generative**: each is a starting point for a large theory.

## Implications for Rubric Design

- Mathematical beauty is **not subjective in the strong sense**: it is shared, parametric, and neurally real.
- A rubric for "mathematical beauty" should reward the features common to the high-rated formulae: **compression**, **cross-domain unification**, **inevitability**, **generativity**.
- A rubric should mark down **expanded forms** of expressions reducible to simpler ones — even if mathematically equivalent.

## Methodological Caveat

The study uses **formulae** (theorems), not full proofs. The neuroaesthetic basis of *proof* beauty (which involves reading and following) may engage additional circuits. Subsequent work by Inglis & Aberdein addresses proofs.
