# Hadamard: The Psychology of Invention in the Mathematical Field

Hadamard's empirical study (drawing on Poincaré, surveys of mathematicians, and his own introspection) lays out criteria for mathematical work and the conditions under which good mathematics is produced.

## Four-Stage Model of Mathematical Discovery

A good piece of mathematics — in Hadamard's reconstruction — typically passes through:

1. **Preparation** — sustained, conscious labor: defining the problem, surveying tools, attempting standard approaches.
2. **Incubation** — the problem is "put away"; unconscious combinatorial work proceeds.
3. **Illumination** — the answer (or the key idea) appears suddenly in consciousness.
4. **Verification** — conscious checking, formalization, and writing up.

A solution presented without evidence of stages 1 or 4 is suspect.

## The Aesthetic-Selection Filter

Hadamard endorses Poincaré's claim that the **unconscious does not deliver random combinations** to consciousness. The unconscious has an aesthetic filter: only combinations that are "elegant," "harmonious," or "useful" rise. Therefore:

- A worked solution that retains traces of this filter (clean structure, no false starts in final form) is evidence of mature mathematical thought.
- Mathematical taste is not ornamental — it is the **engine of selection**.

## Modes of Mathematical Thought (Hadamard's empirical findings)

From his survey of working mathematicians (including Einstein):

- **Imagery is rarely verbal**; it is typically visual, spatial, or kinesthetic. Heavy reliance on word-by-word verbal proof is suspect.
- **Vagueness is essential**: productive mathematicians work with imprecise images that they later sharpen. A demand for full formal precision at every step blocks invention.
- **Long preparation precedes any flash of insight**: shortcuts that skip preparation produce shallow work.

## Implied Rubric for Mathematical Quality

- Does the work show evidence of **deep preparation** (mastery of prior tools)?
- Is the final form **clean of false starts** (signs of unconscious aesthetic filtering)?
- Does it provide **verification** that is independent of the discovery process?
- Is there an **image** (geometric, structural) underlying the formal manipulation?
- Does the writer distinguish between the **logic of discovery** and the **logic of justification**?

## Conditions for Mathematical Productivity

- Sustained focus interleaved with rest (the incubation requirement is real).
- A mind populated with a wide stock of "combinations" — i.e., breadth of mathematical knowledge enables novel connections.
- Aesthetic training: cultivated taste increases the quality of the unconscious filter.
