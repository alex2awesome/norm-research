# Use the Wastebasket — Terry Tao (Career Advice)

## Core Principles for Revision and Writing

1. **Accept that not every idea succeeds.** "Not every idea leads to a success, and not every first draft forms a good template for the final draft."
2. **Know when to abandon projects.** Sometimes work must be "scaled down, refocused, or shelved altogether."
3. **Remove non-essential elements.** Lemmas or sections that don't strengthen the paper should be "reluctantly jettisoned or deferred to another paper."
4. **Rewrite completely when necessary.** Papers may require entire rewrites when "the structure of a half-written paper is clearly not optimal."
5. **Balance persistence with pragmatism.** Understand when to persevere versus when to abandon "dead ends" that waste time.
6. **Maintain quality standards.** Publishing work should meet your expected quality level; not every piece should be published.
7. **Back up everything.** Always backup work before major revisions; salvageable material may resurface.
8. **Learn from failed work.** Even unsuccessful projects stored privately teach you "the limitations of your own tools."

## Implication for "Good Math"
Editing is a virtue. A piece of mathematical writing is "good" partly because of what it *does not* contain — what was thrown away.
