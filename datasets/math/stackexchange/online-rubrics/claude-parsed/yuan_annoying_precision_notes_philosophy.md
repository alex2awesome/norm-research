# Annoying Precision — Qiaochu Yuan (blog philosophy + Notes post)

Qiaochu Yuan's blog name itself is a rubric: "annoying precision," echoing his teacher Glenn Stevens, a stance on what good math writing should look like — relentlessly rigorous about details others skip.

## Core Stances
- **Rigor as virtue.** The blog title and ethos: mathematics should be *annoyingly* precise. Skipping the careful definition is not a sign of sophistication; it's a sign of muddled thinking.
- **Halmos's epigraph.** "A good stock of examples, as large as possible, is indispensable for a thorough understanding of any concept, and when I want to learn something new, I make it my first job to build one."
- **Teaching to learn.** "The best way to learn is to teach"; the blog is partly a pedagogical exercise for the author.

## On Note-Taking and Writing
1. **Live-TeX lectures.** A "good exercise" for technical skills — especially commutative diagram typesetting.
2. **Visual integration.** For geometry-heavy material, hand-drawn illustrations enhance abstract concepts (Yuan used the Paper app).
3. **Disclaim limitations.** "I make no claim that these notes are complete or correct, but they may be useful to somebody." Humility about your documentation while recognising its potential value.
4. **Make the visualisable visible.** Effective math exposition should show curves on surfaces, not merely describe them — bridging abstract math with geometric intuition.

## Implication for "Good Math Writing"
The "annoying precision" rubric: better to be tedious about a definition than to wave hands. The "examples first" rubric: a well-built example library is more valuable than a polished theorem statement.
