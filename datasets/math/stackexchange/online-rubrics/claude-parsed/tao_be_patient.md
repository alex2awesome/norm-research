# Be Patient — Terry Tao (Career Advice)

## Core Principles

1. **Extended time investment.** "Any given problem generally requires months of effort" to achieve meaningful progress; breakthroughs require patient elimination of unsuccessful approaches.
2. **Acceptance of slow progress.** Long periods without visible advancement are normal and enable later faster completion.
3. **Strategic deferral.** Setting aside difficult problems temporarily — while continuing related work — allows skill development before returning.
4. **Rarity of eureka moments.** Most advances result from consistent, thoughtful effort, not sudden inspiration.
5. **Humility about breakthroughs.** Be skeptical of solutions that seem "too easy"; they may overlook real difficulties.
6. **Continuous learning.** Build capability through related problem work rather than obsessing over famous unsolved problems.
7. **Long-term perspective.** Mathematical progress rewards sustained concentration over time, not innate talent or miraculous insight.

## Implication for Good Math
A "good" piece of mathematics typically reflects months or years of patient elimination, not a single insight; readers should be able to feel that depth.
