# Arnold: On Teaching Mathematics — The Russian School's Criteria

V. I. Arnold's polemic against Bourbakist abstraction is also a positive statement of the Russian-school criteria for good mathematics.

## Core Thesis

> "Mathematics is a part of physics. Physics is an experimental science, a part of natural science. Mathematics is the part of physics where experiments are cheap."

A piece of mathematics that has no possible interpretation as a physical/computational/geometric phenomenon is suspect. Mathematics severed from intuition becomes "scholastic."

## Arnold's Criteria for Good Mathematics

1. **Concreteness of motivation**: A theorem should be tied to a real problem (physical, geometric, combinatorial, computational). "L'art de la définition c'est l'art de Procuste" — definition-first mathematics fits the world to its definitions.
2. **Geometric / visual content**: A theorem should have a picture. A proof that admits no diagram is suspect.
3. **Robustness to perturbation**: The result should not depend on hypotheses an experimentalist would never check (e.g., set-theoretic minutiae).
4. **Connection to old, hard problems**: A new theorem earns its keep by saying something about a previously-recognized hard problem, not by being formally novel within an artificial framework.
5. **Difficulty as a poor proxy for value**: Arnold attacks the "self-destructive democratic principle" that values mathematics by *difficulty alone*. Significance and applicability matter more than technical hardness.
6. **Intuition before formalism**: Concepts should be introduced through examples first, then formalized. The reverse order is pedagogically and aesthetically wrong.

## Anti-Patterns Arnold Identifies

- **Axiom-system worship**: All axiom systems being treated as having equal claim to study, regardless of whether they describe anything.
- **"Industrialised abstractness"**: Production of theorems whose only interpretation is within the formal system that generated them.
- **Loss of attribution**: "Shameless grabbing of discoveries from discoverers" by abstraction (giving the credit to the formal restatement, not the concrete original).
- **Dimensions and pictures suppressed**: Replacing 3D geometry with $n$-dimensional formal manipulations from the outset.
- **Definitions before examples**: A pedagogical/expository sin.

## Implied Rubric for Mathematical Work

- Can the theorem be **stated without jargon** to someone in another science?
- Does the proof come with a **picture** or a **physical analog**?
- Is the result **robust** to small changes in hypothesis?
- Does it speak to a **previously hard problem**, or is it internally novel only?
- Are **examples introduced before definitions**?
- Are **original discoverers credited**, not just the framework?

## Pedagogical Test

Arnold proposes a concrete test of whether a piece of "deep" abstract mathematics is genuine: try to teach it to a bright physicist or engineer. If no concrete content can be conveyed, the abstraction is empty.
