# Mathematical Beauty, Truth and Proof in the Age of AI — Quanta Magazine (2025)

A reflective essay revisiting the criteria for "good math" as AI tools enter the workflow.

## Core Criteria

1. **Elegance + Creativity + Beauty + Rigor.** "The best proofs are works of art. They're not just rigorous; they're elegant, creative and beautiful." Quality combines logical soundness with aesthetic and inventive elements.

2. **Sturdy Foundation.** A proof requires "a rigorous, logical argument" building from a "sturdy foundation of assumptions, or axioms" with statements "placed... one at a time" in valid chains.

3. **Architectural Structure.** The overarching structure — "walls and stairs and columns" — gives proofs their shape and meaning. Local validity is not enough; global form matters.

4. **Hands-Dirty Understanding.** Mathematicians traditionally valued knowing "every last detail" in proofs. Getting "your hands dirty" with practical work, rather than relying solely on abstractions, was seen as essential to true comprehension.

5. **Conceptual Insight Over Computation.** "Eureka moments" and the ability to make "an analogy or develop a new definition" are distinctly creative and valued above pure computation.

6. **Communicative Clarity.** As AI evolves, emphasis shifts to "providing explanations and conveying the most important ideas" rather than exhaustive detail — elegance includes effective communication of essential concepts.

## AI-Era Tension
- AI can produce certified proofs but typically cannot identify *which* proofs are beautiful or insightful.
- Mathematicians worry about losing the hands-dirty step.
- New criterion may emerge: "Did this proof teach me anything I didn't already know?"
