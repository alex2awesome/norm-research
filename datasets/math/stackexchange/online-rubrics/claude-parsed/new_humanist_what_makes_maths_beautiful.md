# What Makes Maths Beautiful — New Humanist Magazine

A general-interest essay that distinguishes mathematical beauty from artistic beauty and articulates eight axes.

## Eight Criteria

1. **Conceptual elegance.** "The thrilling conceptual elegance, which often involves elements of surprise, economy, depth, relevance and power."
2. **Simplicity & power.** Pythagoras's theorem exemplifies beauty through being "so simple, and so powerful."
3. **Unexpected connections.** Depth that "connects elements of maths that previously seemed unconnectable."
4. **Concision.** Elegant brevity in expression (Euler's identity).
5. **Surprise element.** Revealing something unexpected or counterintuitive.
6. **Emotional resonance.** Mathematical beauty triggers the same brain pleasure centres as art or music — correlating with "the medial orbito-frontal cortex" (per neuroscience studies).
7. **Purity & austerity.** Russell's "supreme beauty" — "cold and austere, like sculpture… sublimely pure."
8. **Acquired understanding.** Beauty "grows as we understand more"; requires patience.

## Implication
Unlike visual or musical beauty, mathematical beauty requires technical knowledge and sustained engagement to perceive fully.
