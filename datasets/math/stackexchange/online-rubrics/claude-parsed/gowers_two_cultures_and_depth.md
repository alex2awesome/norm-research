# Gowers: Two Cultures of Mathematics + Depth Criterion

NOTE: A separate file already exists for the broader "Two Cultures" essay. This file focuses on Gowers's *depth criterion* (from his 2008 blog post and related lectures), which is distinct and additionally captures his "What is the Importance of Mathematics" criteria.

## The Depth Criterion (2008 blog post)

Gowers proposes a working test for distinguishing **deep** from **shallow** mathematics:

- **Shallow**: A proof in which every step is "the obvious thing to do" — algorithmic translation of definitions, mechanical pattern-matching from hypothesis to conclusion. Example: continuous functions commute with limits.
- **Deep**: A proof that *requires an idea* — a non-obvious construction, a reframing, a choice from infinitely many possibilities. Example: showing $f(n\theta) \to 0$ for every $\theta > 0$ implies $f(x) \to 0$.

A key signal of depth: the proof must **construct an object satisfying infinitely many constraints simultaneously**, not finitely many. Non-uniqueness of the construction is itself a marker of depth.

## The Importance/Significance Criteria

From Gowers's "Importance of Mathematics" lecture, criteria for the value of a piece of mathematics:

1. **Internal mathematical significance** — does it answer a previously hard question or close a long-open conjecture?
2. **External applicability** — does it provide tools used in other sciences?
3. **Methodological novelty** — does it introduce a technique that will be reused elsewhere?
4. **Unification** — does it connect previously separate areas?
5. **Foundational impact** — does it reorganize the conceptual landscape of a subject?

A piece of work that scores zero on all five is unlikely to be remembered, even if technically demanding.

## Two-Cultures Criteria (problem-solver vs theory-builder)

Mathematicians fall on a spectrum, and judgments of quality often depend on which culture's standards are being applied:

### Theory-Builder Standards
- Long-term coherence of a research program
- Unification across subfields
- Conceptual generality
- Whether the theory subsumes earlier ad-hoc results
- Foundational reorganization

### Problem-Solver Standards
- Whether a specific previously-hard problem was solved
- Cleverness of technique
- Surprising application of existing tools
- A tight, self-contained argument
- Generation of new specific examples and counterexamples

A rubric for "good mathematics" must specify which culture's criteria apply, or it will misjudge work in the other tradition.

## Implied Rubric for Mathematical Quality (combining depth + importance)

- Does the proof require an **idea** (or only manipulation)?
- Is there a **non-uniqueness in construction** signaling genuine depth?
- Does the work satisfy any of: hard-problem-solved, applicable, methodologically novel, unifying, foundationally important?
- Is its quality being judged by the **right culture's** standards?
