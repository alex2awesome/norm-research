# Mathematics Is Beautiful (No, Really) — Vicky Neale, The Conversation

An essay explicitly aimed at non-mathematicians, arguing for beauty's accessibility.

## Six Criteria

1. **Elegance / efficiency.** Solutions that avoid unnecessary complexity and capture "the essence of the ideas."
2. **Explanatory power.** Mathematics that reveals *why* something works, not merely *that* it works. "This is critical to mathematicians."
3. **Generalisability.** Arguments that extend neatly from specific cases to universal principles "without drawing the dots" for each instance.
4. **Economy of expression.** "A single diagram captures everything we need to know."
5. **Memorability.** Solutions create lasting impressions through visual or conceptual clarity rather than rote formula memorisation.
6. **Accessibility with depth.** Beauty doesn't require university-level sophistication; elegant problems exist at school level.

## Important Caveat
Appreciating math beauty requires **understanding first** — like appreciating sophisticated music or visual art. Beauty emerges through engagement and exploration, not passive observation.

## Implication
A "good" piece of mathematical writing teaches the prerequisites needed to appreciate its own elegance. Without that scaffolding, the beauty is invisible.
