# Advice on Writing Mathematical Papers — Terence Tao (blog series, 2007–present)

A long-running blog series by Tao, with linked sub-pages on every aspect of paper writing. Among the most-read modern guides in the field.

## Section list (each is a linked sub-post)

### Macro structure
1. **Use the introduction to "sell" the key points.** State results in appropriate detail; consider stating a special case before the general theorem.
2. **Don't write a paper until your work is mature.** Premature publication wastes everyone's time.
3. **Cite generously.** Include not just direct sources but also adjacent work that contextualizes the result.

### Organization
4. **Create lemmas.** Break the paper into modular pieces; each piece does one thing.
5. **Choose good notation.** Mnemonic, consistent, sparse. Notation choice often dictates whether the proof reads cleanly.
6. **Write in your own voice.** Don't mimic; develop personal style. Write for clarity first.
7. **Take advantage of the English language.** Use prose where prose is clearer than symbols. English carries connotation that pure formula does not.

### Drafting
8. **Rapid prototype the paper.** A complete bad first draft is better than half a polished one.
9. **Don't over-optimize early sections.** Wait until the whole structure is stable before polishing prose.
10. **Anticipate the bored reader, the hostile reader, and the confused reader.** Pre-empt their objections.

### Polishing
11. **Be considerate of your audience.** What does your reader already know? What will they need?
12. **State main results in appropriate detail.** Restate after the proof if the result is hidden inside.
13. **Provide a roadmap section** in long papers explaining the global proof structure before the technical details.

### Submitting
14. **Proofread thoroughly.** Multiple passes; ideally several days apart.
15. **Choose the right journal.** Match scope, prestige, and audience.
16. **Respond to referee reports point-by-point.** Acknowledge every comment, even minor ones.

## On reading mathematical papers

Tao argues that good writing is shaped by skilled reading. Skills include:
- Identifying "compilation errors" — sentences that don't parse logically.
- Recognizing implicit notational conventions from context.
- Locating the "jumps in difficulty" — where the author has skipped real work.
- Distinguishing local from global errors.

## On career-stage writing

Tao distinguishes:
- **Pre-PhD**: copy structure of mentor's papers; focus on correctness.
- **Early postdoc**: polish prose; develop voice; work on introduction-craft.
- **Mid-career**: write expository/survey pieces; teach the field.
- **Senior**: aim for definitive treatments and books.

## Universal underlying principles

> "There are no rigid rules for writing a math paper… Writing a paper well is a skill that is learned through practice, feedback, and reflection."

But Tao does emphasize a recurring few:

1. **Clarity above brevity.**
2. **Motivation before formality.**
3. **English over symbols when both work.**
4. **State the theorem precisely before proving.**
5. **Write the introduction last.**
6. **Notation is design; spend real time on it.**
7. **Acknowledge intellectual debts.**
8. **A paper has many readers; write for the most distant.**
