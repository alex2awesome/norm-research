# What is Good Mathematics? — Terence Tao (2007)

Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 44 (4): 623–634, 2007. arXiv:math/0702396. The canonical modern catalogue of dimensions on which mathematical work can be judged "good". Tao's thesis: "good mathematics" is necessarily multi-dimensional, no single virtue is sufficient, and important results tend to satisfy several at once.

## Tao's 21 dimensions of good mathematics

(numbered as in the original)

1. **Good mathematical problem-solving** — a major breakthrough on an important problem (e.g., Wiles on Fermat).
2. **Good mathematical technique** — a masterful new way to compute, estimate, or manipulate (e.g., Stein's harmonic-analysis machinery).
3. **Good mathematical theory** — a conceptual framework that organizes many phenomena (e.g., Galois theory, scheme theory).
4. **Good mathematical insight** — a single idea that suddenly clarifies an obscure subject.
5. **Good mathematical discovery** — finding an unexpected truth, phenomenon, or counter-example.
6. **Good mathematical application** — solving an external problem (physics, engineering, biology, CS).
7. **Good mathematical exposition** — a paper, book, or talk that opens a field to outsiders.
8. **Good mathematical pedagogy** — training future researchers; raising the technical level of a community.
9. **Good mathematical vision** — a long-range program that takes generations to complete (Langlands, Grothendieck).
10. **Good mathematical taste** — choosing the right problems, definitions, and abstractions; rejecting the wrong ones.
11. **Good mathematical public relations** — communicating excitement and importance to non-specialists.
12. **Good meta-mathematics** — advances in foundations or in the philosophy of the subject.
13. **Rigorous mathematics** — airtight proofs with all details.
14. **Beautiful mathematics** — a result whose statement and proof are aesthetically pleasing.
15. **Elegant mathematics** — minimal, surprising, and economical arguments.
16. **Creative mathematics** — genuinely novel constructions, not recombinations of known ones.
17. **Useful mathematics** — feeds back into other parts of mathematics.
18. **Strong mathematics** — sharp constants, optimal exponents, best-possible bounds.
19. **Deep mathematics** — penetrating to underlying causes (analogy, structure).
20. **Intuitive mathematics** — fits and extends naturally one's prior mental picture.
21. **Definitive mathematics** — the final word on a subject; closes a chapter.

## Tao's discussion

- The dimensions are not independent — many results score on several.
- They are not totally ordered — at any moment a community must choose what to value, but over the long run *all* dimensions are needed for a healthy field.
- A field that optimizes only one dimension (only rigor, only applications, only beauty, only volume) will eventually decay. Tao lists five failure modes: cynicism, isolation, fragmentation, exhaustion, irrelevance.
- The case study of Szemerédi's theorem on arithmetic progressions illustrates how a single result can score highly on essentially every dimension simultaneously.

## Why this matters as a rubric

Tao's list is the most-cited modern enumeration of evaluative axes for mathematical work. It is widely used as a checklist in self-evaluation, grant writing, and exposition.
