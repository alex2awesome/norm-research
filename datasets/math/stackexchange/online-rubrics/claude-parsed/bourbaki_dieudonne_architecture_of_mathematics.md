# Bourbaki / Dieudonné: The Architecture of Mathematics

Bourbaki's manifesto (ghostwritten by Jean Dieudonné) lays out the structuralist criteria for what makes mathematical work coherent and unified. It is the canonical statement of the 20th-century axiomatic style.

## Core Thesis

Despite its specialization, mathematics has an **inherent unity**, achievable through the **axiomatic method**. The right way to organize mathematics is by **structures** — abstract patterns instantiated in many concrete settings.

> "To present a view of the entire field of mathematical science as it exists, this is an enterprise which presents, at first sight, almost insurmountable difficulties, on account of the extent and the varied character of the subject."

Bourbaki's answer: do not present the subject by topic; present it by **structural type**.

## The Three Mother Structures

Bourbaki ranks structures hierarchically, with three "mother structures" at the base:

1. **Algebraic structures** — defined by laws of composition (groups, rings, fields, modules).
2. **Order structures** — defined by an order relation (lattices, posets, ordered groups).
3. **Topological structures** — defined by neighborhood/limit/continuity (topological spaces, uniform spaces, metric spaces).

Above these, **multiple structures** combine two or more mother structures with axioms linking them (topological groups, ordered fields, normed spaces). At the top, **theories properly particular** (e.g., the theory of finite simple groups) combine multiple structures with additional specific axioms.

## Implicit Criteria for Good Mathematical Work (Bourbakist Standards)

A piece of mathematical work is good when it:

1. **Identifies the underlying structure** — recognizes that a phenomenon is an instance of a known structural pattern.
2. **States definitions axiomatically** — captures the essence in minimal axioms, not in dependent constructions.
3. **Maximally generalizes** — proves the result for the widest class of structures for which it holds, then specializes.
4. **Avoids ad-hoc construction** — replaces clever-but-narrow proofs with structural arguments that work in many settings.
5. **Connects to the larger architecture** — locates the result in the hierarchy of structures.
6. **Builds tools usable in adjacent areas** — a good lemma is one that other subjects will reuse.

## The Axiomatic Method as Quality Standard

> "The tool to achieve unity was Hilbert's axiomatic method: it provides clarity and rigor in the register of reasoning by using a systematization of mathematical theories."

A proof is well-written when it:

- Begins from **stated axioms** for the relevant structure.
- Uses only **structure-preserving operations** (homomorphisms, continuous maps, etc.).
- Avoids **importing structure** not needed for the result.
- Could be **checked by a reader** who knows only the axioms, not the intended interpretation.

## Anti-Patterns Bourbaki Targets

- **Computational virtuosity** without structural insight.
- **Geometric intuition** smuggled in without axiomatic justification.
- **Specific examples** treated as primary, with generalizations as afterthoughts.
- **National/local schools** that develop tools usable only within themselves.
- **Reverence for historical formulation** when a cleaner axiomatic version exists.

## Implied Rubric for Mathematical Work (Structuralist)

- Does the work **name the relevant structure** explicitly?
- Does it **prove at the right level of generality** (neither too narrow nor too abstract)?
- Does it **introduce minimal axioms** sufficient for the result?
- Does it **connect to other structures** in the architectural hierarchy?
- Are its **proofs structure-preserving** (not relying on accidental features)?
- Does it **enable transfer** of its result to other instantiations of the same structure?

## The Critique (Arnold and Others)

Bourbaki's standards are *not universal*. The Russian school (Arnold) and the problem-solver tradition (Erdős, Gowers's "two cultures") reject them as overly abstract. A complete rubric for mathematical quality should include the Bourbakist criteria as **one cluster** that applies to theory-building work, while recognizing other clusters apply to problem-solving work.

## Bourbaki's Lasting Influence

Even mathematicians who reject Bourbaki's program have absorbed its standards:

- The expectation that a paper **state its definitions before its theorems**.
- The expectation that proofs **work over a class of structures**, not a single example.
- The **organizational template** of "definitions → lemmas → main theorem → corollaries."

These conventions are themselves a rubric, often unstated, for what a research paper should look like.
