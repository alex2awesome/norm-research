# Don't Prematurely Obsess on a Single "Big Problem" or "Big Theory" — Terry Tao

## Core Principles

1. **Avoid premature obsession.** Don't fixate on a single big problem before establishing credentials and secure employment.
2. **Build a track record first.** Pursue major problems only after (1) "a proven track record of reliably producing significant papers in the area" and (2) "a secure career."
3. **Pursue low-hanging fruit.** "Spend most of your time on more feasible 'low-hanging fruit,' which will build up your experience, mathematical power, and credibility."
4. **Balance.** Don't bet everything on one speculative idea.
5. **Publish regularly.** Don't neglect "lesser" results while chasing big breakthroughs.

## Characteristics of Good Mathematical Research

- Clear, achievable milestones and key lemmas.
- Evidence of understanding *why* previous approaches failed.
- Awareness of known obstructions and limitations.
- Includes proof-of-concept results, not only attacks on major conjectures.
- Robust arguments with alternate proofs or supporting examples.
- Thorough self-critique and error-checking before publication.
- Appropriate balance between novel contribution and standard theory.
