# Proofs in Mathematics: How to Understand and Write Them — PhysicsForums Insights

A community-published primer that articulates the working definition of "proof" plus criteria for writing one well.

## Understanding Proofs

1. **Definition.** A proof is "a convincing explanation of why a mathematical statement is true" using only definitions, axioms, and previously proven theorems.
2. **Deductive reasoning.** Proofs employ logical deduction, not empirical evidence.
3. **Certainty over likelihood.** Math demands absolute truth/falsehood, not "usually true."

## Common Proof Methods
4. **Direct proof.** Logical steps from assumptions to conclusion.
5. **Proof by contradiction.** Assume the negation; derive contradiction.
6. **Proof by induction.** Base case + inductive step.

## Writing Effective Proofs
7. **Audience-appropriate detail.** "Adjust the level of detail to your audience" — high-school students need more explanation than research mathematicians.
8. **Balance symbols and language.** Avoid symbol-heavy presentations; sentences guide the reader through logical steps.
9. **Clarity over formality.** Prioritise readable, sufficiently rigorous proofs over dense symbolic logic.

## Implication
For non-specialist audiences, the rubric is heavier on *clarity + motivation + audience-fit* than on stylistic polish.
