# Writing Mathematical Papers in English: A Practical Guide / Mathematical English Usage Dictionary — Jerzy Trzeciak (1995, 2nd ed. 2012)

European Mathematical Society. The standard reference for non-native English speakers writing mathematics. Trzeciak is the senior copy editor at the Polish Academy of Sciences and reviewed thousands of submissions to *Studia Mathematica*, *Fundamenta Mathematicae*, and *Acta Arithmetica* over decades.

## Part A — Ready-made phrases by paper section

The book provides documented sentence patterns, drawn from published papers, organized by where they appear:

### Abstracts
- "We prove that …"
- "Let X be a …. We show that ..."
- "We extend a result of [Author] to ..."
- "It is shown that ..." (passive — discouraged but documented)

### Introductions
- "Throughout the paper, we assume ..."
- "Our main result is the following:"
- "The main idea of the proof is to ..."
- "We use the following notation ..."
- "The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we ..."

### Definitions
- "A is said to be B if ..."
- "We call A a B if ..."
- "By A we mean ..."
- "A is defined to be ..."

### Statements of theorems
- "Theorem 1. Let X be ... Then ..."
- "Proposition 2. Suppose that ... Under these assumptions, ..."
- "Lemma 3. For every ... there exists ..."

### Proofs
- "Proof. We argue by induction on n ..."
- "Suppose, for contradiction, that ..."
- "It suffices to show that ..."
- "By the previous lemma, ..."
- "This completes the proof."

### Referee reports
- "The paper is well-written and presents new results."
- "I recommend acceptance with minor revisions."
- "The proof of Theorem 2 contains a gap on page X."

## Part B — English grammar problems characteristic of mathematical writing

Trzeciak documents recurring non-native errors, with rules and examples:

### Articles (a / the / Ø)
- Use "the" when the object is unique or previously introduced.
- Use "a" when first introduced.
- Use no article (Ø) for plural / mass / abstract nouns: "homotopy theory", "compactness arguments".
- "the function f", but "a function from X to Y".

### Infinitives vs. -ing forms
- "We aim to prove" / "We aim at proving" — both possible; book documents which is more common in math English.
- "It is necessary to show" not "It is necessary showing".

### Passive voice
- Mathematical English uses passive more than literary English; not a fault.
- "It is well known that ..." is acceptable.
- But "we" is preferred when the agent is the author.

### Quantifiers
- "Any" is ambiguous in math; replace with "every" or "some".
- "Each" emphasizes individual; "every" emphasizes collective.
- "All" + plural; "every" + singular.

### Numbers and notation
- Numbers under ten: spell out.
- Numbers ≥ 10: digits.
- Equations are referred to as "(3)" not "Eq. (3)".

### Commas
- Comma before "where" in formula explanations.
- No comma before "that" in restrictive clauses.
- Comma in displayed-equation lists matches English punctuation.

### Hyphens
- "well-known result" (hyphen before noun); "the result is well known" (no hyphen).
- "real-valued function"; "non-trivial example".

## "Mathematical English Usage: A Dictionary" (companion book)

A glossary of mathematical English idioms — what each common phrase means and when to use it. Examples:
- "follows from" vs. "is implied by".
- "is true" vs. "holds".
- "we have" vs. "there is".
- "given" vs. "fixed".
