# How to Write Mathematics on Medium — Tyler Neylon

A practical Medium-platform guide that doubles as a rubric for what readable online math content should look like.

## Core Principles

### Inline expressions
1. **Variables in italics.** "Variables like *x* and *y* can simply be italicized."
2. **Use Unicode** to expand notation options.
3. **TeX-to-Unicode** browser extensions help LaTeX-trained writers.
4. **Edit > Emoji & Symbols** for Mac users.

### Displayed equations
5. **Render complex expressions as images** rather than relying on inline text.
6. **Keep image sizes small** so they display at full resolution.
7. **Use `@2x` filenames** to preserve retina display quality.
8. **Single-image width ~300px** for proper text scaling.
9. **Charter font, 10px x-height** for typography consistency with Medium.
10. **Use image grids** (505px for 2-column, 333px for 3-column) to optimise space.

### Software recommendations
11. **LaTeXiT** for generating math expression images at 200% scale.
12. **Photoshop** for image refinement.
13. **Standard LaTeX with screenshots** as alternative.
14. **SVG + JavaScript** for geometric diagrams requiring precise coordinates.
15. **QuickTime + GIFBrewery** for animations.
16. **ImageOptim** for shrinking GIFs.

### File management
17. **Tag images with short English phrases** for organisation.
18. **Maintain PSD source files** for future editing.

## Implication
Online platforms impose a distinct rubric: typography + image quality + retina-aware sizing become first-class concerns alongside mathematical content. A "good" Medium math piece looks polished even before it's read.
