# Don't Base Career Decisions on Glamour or Fame — Terry Tao

## Core Principle
Going into a field "simply because it is glamorous is not a good idea." There is little glamour to chase, and chasing it is the wrong primary objective.

## What Constitutes Good Mathematics in This Light

1. **Foundation before fame.** High-profile work attracts intense competition; success requires "solid backgrounds" with experience in less glamorous aspects.
2. **Incremental progress.** Famous unsolved problems rarely yield to fresh approaches; invest in "simpler model problems, acquiring techniques, intuition, partial results, context, and literature" first.
3. **Intrinsic value over recognition.** Aim to "produce good mathematics and contribute to your field, and the prizes and recognition will take care of themselves."

## What to Value Instead
- Specific institutional strengths over general prestige.
- Authentic interest in problems and areas.
- Understanding *why* problems or mathematicians are renowned (not just their fame).
- Long-term contribution.
- Solid, foundational work that enables future breakthroughs.

## Implication
A "good" piece of math is intrinsically motivated, built on careful groundwork, and measured by contribution — not celebrity.
