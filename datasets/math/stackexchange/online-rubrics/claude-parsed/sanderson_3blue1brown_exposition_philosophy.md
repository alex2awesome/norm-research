# Grant Sanderson's Philosophy on Mathematical Exposition (3Blue1Brown)

The "About" page of 3Blue1Brown plus interview/blog distillations articulating Sanderson's view of what makes good math exposition.

## Core Philosophy

Sanderson's overarching goal is to "make more people love math," grounded in the belief that "any such love begins with deep understanding."

## Key Principles

### Starting Points
1. **Never start with definitions.** "Definitions represent endpoints, not beginnings."
2. **The best pedagogical order of ideas is often very different from the correct logical order of ideas.**
3. **Concrete before abstract.** Provide examples before general frameworks; open with key exercises rather than placing them at the end.

### Content Strategy
4. **Topic choice matters way more than production quality.**
5. **Be niche.** Focus on ultra-specific areas rather than casting the widest net possible.
6. **Know your genre.** Different educational styles (student-documenting-learning, expert-conveying-knowledge, teacher-targeting-students, journalist-summarizing-breakthroughs) require different pacing and approaches.

### Visual Communication
7. **Be visual.** Continuously ask what pictures or visuals could elucidate a topic.
8. **Avoid pointless animations.** Every movement must be deliberate with identifiable purpose.
9. **Each visual and movement should communicate the same point that the narration is** — to reinforce rather than compete.

### Production
10. **Invest in audio quality** — many videos suffer from poor sound.
11. **Just start and iterate** based on feedback rather than overthinking.

## Quotable Standards
- Goal of an explanation: "I want you to come away from this series feeling like you could have invented [the subject] yourself."
- "When we put the visuals first, then start to articulate the meaning, you can get a sense of ownership over it."
- "Education" derives from "educe" — to *draw out* understanding, not impose it.
