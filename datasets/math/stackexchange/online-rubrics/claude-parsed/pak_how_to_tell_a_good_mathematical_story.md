# How to Tell a Good Mathematical Story — Igor Pak (2021, AMS Notices Early Career)

## Core Recommendation
Create a **non-technical abstract** of your math work that is concise enough for a tweet yet compelling enough to spark curiosity about the full details.

## Characteristics of a Good Story
1. **Short and to the point** — distilled to essentials.
2. **Straightforward** — clear enough for a general audience.
3. **Interesting** — engaging enough that someone would want to share it.
4. **Curiosity-inducing** — leaves listeners wanting more.

## The Inherent Tension
"Distilled products always lack flavor compared to the real thing." Acceptable, because the stripped-down story should contain enough intrigue to motivate deeper exploration.

## Models
- **Aesop's fable.** Reduces a story to its essential moral.
- **Terminator tagline.** Captures core conflict in a sentence.

## Implicit Advice
Mathematical storytelling differs fundamentally from fiction writing: it requires distilling complex ideas into their most essential and engaging form without sacrificing clarity.

## Implication
A "good" math paper is one whose abstract works as a tweet and whose introduction works as a story. Both are skills distinct from proof.
