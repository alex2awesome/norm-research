# Autzen (2014) — Press release as the new center of science communication

Autzen's commentary documents how university and research-institute communications offices have become the *primary* science-communication channel. Press releases from "trustworthy scientific institutions" function as free, easily-copied content for thinned newsrooms — a structural change that reshapes the rubric for pickup.

## Autzen's diagnostic claims
1. **Institutional press releases now substitute for science-journalism reporting.** Pickup-rate is partly a function of the issuing institution's perceived trustworthiness, not just text.
2. **Competition among research institutions has professionalized PR.** Releases now compete on production quality (visuals, video, embargo coordination).
3. **The release has become an "integrated part of science communication"** — not a journalist-facing artifact alone, but a public document the institution stands behind.

## Autzen-derived rubric criteria
1. **Institutional-trust signal.** Does the release prominently identify the issuing institution and use the institution's standard branding/letterhead/template? Editors lean on institutional reputation as a quality proxy.
2. **Author-authority alignment.** Is the named scientist a co-author on the cited paper *and* identified by full title at the issuing institution? Releases naming someone other than a paper author lose institutional credibility weight.
3. **Multimedia readiness.** Are images, video, and B-roll embedded with usage rights cleared, captioned, and high-resolution? Autzen documents this as the new competitive frontier among universities.
4. **Embargo discipline.** Does the release respect the journal's embargo and clearly state the embargo time / lift time?
5. **Direct-publication path.** Does the release contain a link to the open-access version of the paper or a media DOI?
6. **Issuing institution as publisher.** Does the release recognize that, in many cases, the public will read the release on the institution's news site directly, not through a journalist? Lead and structure should be readable as standalone public communication.
7. **Cross-channel coordination.** Is the release coordinated with the institution's social-media post, faculty page update, and open-day calendar, so each channel reinforces the others?
8. **Comparable-institution scan.** A pickup-worthy release does not duplicate a competing institution's release on the same week (Autzen highlights crowding effects).
9. **Public-document standard.** Does the release contain nothing the institution would not be comfortable defending as its own published statement?
10. **Translation-loss hedge.** Where a release will likely be translated into other languages by international wires, does the original release avoid idioms that translate poorly?

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Autzen's frame predicts that institutional press releases now operate in a quasi-publishing market: pickup goes to the institution that most reliably supplies high-quality, embargo-disciplined, multimedia-ready, direct-to-public copy. The release that makes the institution look good as a *publisher* gets picked up more than the release that merely announces a single study.
