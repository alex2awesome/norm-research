# PRSA Press Release Writing 101

## PRSA Writers Guidelines Overview

### Writing Style Recommendations
- Vary sentence structure: mix simple, compound, and complex sentences
- Avoid clichés and tired phrases
- Seek original images and descriptive adjectives
- Write in active voice (clearer, more concise, more colorful)
- Provide proper name of survey, who conducted it, and release date when citing data

## Press Release Structure

### Standard Layout
- **Logo**: Top and center
- **Contact Information**: Below logo or in upper right
- **"FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE"**: Top right
- **Headline**: Centered underneath contact information
- **Dateline**: Beginning of first paragraph
- **Three core paragraphs** typical structure
- **Boilerplate**: Near end
- **###**: At end as traditional signifier

### Three-Paragraph Structure
1. **First paragraph**: Date line + nut graph (who, what, when, where)
2. **Second paragraph**: Quote and additional context
3. **Third paragraph**: Background and details

## PRSA Writing Standards

### Accuracy Requirements
- Double-check names
- Confirm spellings
- Provide accurate job titles
- Include institutions responsible for and dates of any cited research
- Editors reserve right to edit

### Modern (2025-2026) Best Practices
- 35-word opening paragraph answering 5Ws
- 15-word "why now" sentence emphasis
- Avoid backstory or company history
- Focus on news itself
- Address current trends (AI, sustainability, social impact)

## Distribution Best Practices
- Best response: Thursdays 10:00 AM - 2:00 PM
- Major events: 4-6 weeks advance + 1-week reminder
- Product launches: embargoed 2-3 days ahead
- Press list curation matters more than blast size

## Implicit Quality Rubric (PRSA)
- Active voice throughout
- Original imagery (not clichés)
- Varied sentence structure
- Accurate names/titles/dates
- Cite all sources
- Proper format (logo, headline placement, etc.)
- Three-paragraph minimum structure
- Boilerplate present
- Contact info complete
- "###" at end
