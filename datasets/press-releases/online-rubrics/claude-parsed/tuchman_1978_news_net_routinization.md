# Tuchman (1978) — News Net & strategic ritual applied to releases

Tuchman's ethnography of newsrooms shows that news organizations *routinize the unexpected* by casting a "news net" of beats, geographic territories, and routinized formats. A press release is picked up to the extent that it falls into a strand of this net.

## Tuchman's three dimensions of the news net (release rubric)
1. **Geographic territory.** Has the issuer mapped the release to one or more papers' coverage zones (city-room metro desk; suburban edition; national bureau)? A release tied to a city the paper covers gets fast routing.
2. **Topical specialization (beat).** Does the release land at the *right* beat (health, courts, business, science, education) — and does the lead make the beat-fit obvious in the first sentence?
3. **Organizational specialization.** Is the release tied to an institution the paper has a designated reporter for (city hall, school district, major hospital, named-firm beat)?

## "Strategic ritual" of objectivity (Tuchman) as a release rubric
Tuchman shows journalists defend their work by reference to procedural rituals — quoting, sourcing, balancing. Releases that pre-supply the *materials of the ritual* are easier to publish:
1. **Quoting.** At least one direct, on-the-record quote from a named, titled source — preferably two distinct sources for "balance."
2. **Sourcing.** Documentary trail (study citation, court filing number, official report URL) so attribution is clean.
3. **Balancing.** A counter-perspective acknowledged (even if briefly) so the journalist doesn't have to manufacture balance under deadline pressure.
4. **Routinizing of the unexpected.** A release that *re-narrates* an unexpected event in routine form (frame: "X type of crisis"; precedent cases referenced) makes coverage decision easy.

## Tuchman's "hard news / soft news / spot news" typology — release rubric
- **Hard news** (urgent, decay-fast): release should be timestamped to the minute, embargoed if needed, and announce a present-tense fact.
- **Soft news** (no decay): release can be themed for feature placement; visuals and human-interest framing matter most.
- **Spot news** (unexpected, urgent): release should identify itself as breaking, supply confirmable facts, and route to wire desks.
- **Continuing news** (developing story): release should index its place in the developing arc and link to prior official statements.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Tuchman's frame predicts that releases falling cleanly into the news net *and* pre-equipped with the materials of the strategic ritual will be picked up almost automatically. Releases that demand the journalist build new beats, identify new sources, or invent balance are filtered out under deadline pressure.
