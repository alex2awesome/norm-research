# AP Style Press Release Conventions

## Overview of AP Style
AP style — short for Associated Press style — is a standardized set of rules for grammar, punctuation, spelling, capitalization, and language usage developed by The Associated Press and published annually in the AP Stylebook. It is the default writing standard for journalists, press release writers, and communications professionals.

## Key Conventions for Press Releases

### Structure and Format
A proper press release includes a headline, dateline, introductory paragraph, body paragraphs, quotes, boilerplate and media contact information. An AP-style press release should typically be between 300 and 500 words.

### Headlines
- Sentence case: capitalize only the first word and any proper nouns
- Avoid all-caps headlines
- Use active voice and strong verbs

### Datelines
- Format: CITY, State (Month Day, Year) — followed by content
- Cities in all caps
- States use AP abbreviations
- Major cities (e.g., NEW YORK, LOS ANGELES) stand alone without states

### Numbers
- Spell out one through nine
- Use numerals for 10 and above
- Numerals always for ages, percentages, money, dates

### Months
- Spell out alone (March)
- Abbreviate with specific dates (March 5)
- Abbreviations: Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec.
- May, June, July are never abbreviated

### Job Titles
- Capitalize before names: President John Smith
- Lowercase after: John Smith, president of Acme

### Quotes
- Always attribute with "said" rather than "stated" or "noted"
- Double quotation marks (single only inside)
- Introduce speakers with full name and title

### Punctuation
- No Oxford/serial comma
- One space after periods
- Avoid exclamation points

### Acronyms
- Spell out on first use with abbreviation in parentheses
- Use the abbreviation thereafter

### Font and Formatting
- Common readable fonts: Times New Roman, Arial, Calibri
- Body: 10-12pt; headlines 14-16pt

## Why AP Style Matters
Releases that follow AP style require less editing and signal professionalism. Many journalists will immediately notice style errors, which can detract from your message or even lead them to dismiss your release entirely.
