# EPA Communications Stylebook

## Foundation
Primary style: The Associated Press Stylebook and Briefing on Media Law (Basic Books, NY, 2007)
Spelling reference: Webster's Dictionary (first spelling preferred)

## Press Release Writing Guidelines

### Tone
- Press releases written in third person
- Honesty is the primary constituent and quality of all EPA communications
- Authoritative but not propagandistic
- Audience-appropriate

### Structure

#### Lead Paragraph
- Limited to 25 words or fewer
- Tell briefly: who, what, where, when, why
- About the event, issue, or project

#### First Paragraph Requirements
- Main message included
- Clear purpose statement
- Intended audience identified
- Called the "lead"

### Style Standards
- AP style throughout
- Active voice
- Plain language for general audience
- Technical accuracy
- Specific numbers/data
- Source attribution

## EPA-Specific Considerations

### Environmental Releases Should
- Provide scientific context
- Include data on affected populations
- Specify geographic scope
- Cite peer-reviewed sources where available
- Acknowledge uncertainty
- Avoid alarmism without basis
- Provide actionable information for affected populations
- Include resources for more information

### Required Elements for Federal Agency Releases
- Plain language (Plain Writing Act of 2010)
- Section 508 accessibility
- FOIA-compliant
- Coordinated with affected stakeholders
- Approved through proper channels

## Implicit Quality Rubric (EPA Standards)

A press release passes EPA standards if it:
1. Lead paragraph ≤25 words
2. Answers all 5Ws in lead
3. Third person throughout
4. Honest (no spin)
5. AP style adherent
6. Plain language (Grade 8 or below)
7. Cites scientific evidence
8. Identifies affected populations
9. Provides actionable information
10. Section 508 accessible
11. Webster's spelling consistent
12. Coordinated with stakeholders
13. Includes appropriate disclaimers
14. Specifies geographic scope
15. Includes EPA contact info
