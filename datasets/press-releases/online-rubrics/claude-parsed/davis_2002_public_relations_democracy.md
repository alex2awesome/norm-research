# Davis (2002) Public Relations Democracy — sector-by-sector pickup determinants

Davis provides the first systematic UK overview of professional PR's expansion into corporate, financial (City), pressure-group, trade-union and political domains. Each sector has distinct rubric implications because each operates in a different newsroom dependence-and-trust regime.

## Davis's three-part framework
- **Part 1.** Expansion of PR; impact on news production.
- **Part 2.** Corporate and City PR; how corporate sources shape national-media coverage.
- **Part 3.** Trade-union, outsider and resource-poor groups; their differential media access.

## Davis's sector-specific release rubric criteria

### Corporate / City releases
1. **Pre-briefed City editor relationship.** Embargoed pre-briefings to FT/WSJ City editors convert at higher rates than open wire blasts.
2. **Analyst-meeting alignment.** Releases timed to align with the analyst meeting cycle land into existing routine channels.
3. **CEO/CFO quotability discipline.** Quotes that satisfy disclosure rules (no forward-looking guidance ad lib) and supply a usable headline phrase score higher.
4. **Trade-press integration.** Industry-specific framing (sector context, peer comparison) earns trade-press pickup that subsequently feeds national pickup.

### Pressure-group releases (resource-poor)
5. **Counter-quote piggyback.** Resource-poor groups achieve pickup by attaching a counter-quote to a corporate or government release that day. Releases issued in isolation rarely break through.
6. **Exclusive investigation.** Resource-poor groups that supply pre-packaged investigation material (documents, FOI returns, original data) earn pickup proportional to the news value of the investigation, not their PR resources.
7. **Story-package readiness.** Pre-supplied case studies (named affected individuals, story arc, willing-to-be-quoted families) compensate for lack of corporate brand recognition.
8. **Coalition signal.** Pickup increases when a resource-poor group can demonstrate a coalition (multiple named groups, joint signatories).

### Trade-union releases
9. **Consumer-impact reframing.** Union releases reach lay press only when reframed as *consumer*-impact rather than labor-relations stories. The release author must do this reframing or the editor will not.
10. **Avoidance of insider-jargon.** Union jargon (negotiating positions, pay-band terminology) is stripped or rejected; releases must be written for a general newsroom.

### Political / spin-doctor releases
11. **Lobby-correspondent fit.** Political releases targeted at the parliamentary lobby work through prior trust relationships; outsider distribution mechanisms do not penetrate.
12. **Off-the-record + on-the-record pair.** Sophisticated political releases pair an on-the-record release with an off-the-record briefing that primes the angle.

## Cross-cutting Davis rubric criteria
- **Resource-asymmetry awareness.** A release should be calibrated to the issuer's *position* in the source-journalist network — a small NGO release should not pretend to corporate scale, and vice versa.
- **Long-run trust accumulation.** Pickup is partly a function of the issuer's track record on the receiving journalist's beat — a release benefits from years of accurate prior dealings.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Davis's central empirical finding — pickup is *strongly* sector-dependent and resource-asymmetric — implies that a single one-size release strategy under-performs. Effective release programs differentiate by sector and customize the package (briefing, exclusive, counter-quote, coalition signal) to the news access regime in each.
