# Cialdini's Seven Principles of Persuasion - Press Release Applications

## Background
Robert Cialdini's principles of persuasion are seven psychological mechanisms describing HOW people are influenced. Backed by 35 years of evidence-based, peer-reviewed scientific research.

## The Seven Principles

### 1. Reciprocity
Humans wired to return favors. Press release applications:
- Provide value to journalists (data, exclusives, access)
- Free expert availability
- Provide multimedia at no cost
- Offer briefings and tours
- Build long-term goodwill

### 2. Commitment and Consistency
People align actions with prior commitments. Press release applications:
- Reference past coverage
- Build on previous positions
- Cite prior agreements
- Show track record
- Match brand voice consistently

### 3. Social Proof
People look to similar others for correct actions. Press release applications:
- Cite customer numbers
- Showcase testimonials
- Reference industry adoption
- Quote multiple experts
- Show awards/recognition
- Mention notable users

### 4. Authority
Knowledge/expertise gains credibility. Press release applications:
- Quote credentialed experts
- Cite peer-reviewed research
- Reference established institutions
- Use authoritative spokespersons
- Cite government/regulatory data

### 5. Liking
Influenced more by people we like. Press release applications:
- Personal stories
- Founder narratives
- Authentic voice
- Shared values
- Approachable language
- Visual personality (not stiff)

### 6. Scarcity
Limited availability increases value. Press release applications:
- "First" or "only" claims (where true)
- Limited-time offers
- Exclusive access
- Limited spots/quantities
- Time-sensitive opportunities

### 7. Unity (Cialdini's 7th, added later)
Shared identity creates influence. Press release applications:
- Industry "we" language
- Community focus
- Cause alignment
- Shared mission
- Stakeholder collective identity

## Implicit Press Release Persuasion Rubric

A press release leverages persuasion principles ethically if:
1. Reciprocity: Provides value to journalists
2. Consistency: Aligns with brand history
3. Social proof: Cites adoption metrics
4. Authority: Quotes credentialed experts
5. Liking: Has authentic voice
6. Scarcity: Notes genuine exclusivity (no false scarcity)
7. Unity: Connects to shared identity

## Ethical Considerations
- Don't manufacture false scarcity
- Don't fake social proof
- Don't misrepresent authority
- Don't manipulate liking through false friendship
- Use principles to inform, not deceive

## Why This Matters for Pickup
- Releases using these principles are more memorable
- More likely to generate emotional response
- More likely to be shared
- Have higher pickup rates
- Drive more reader engagement
- Build long-term audience trust (when used ethically)

## Anti-Pattern: Manipulation
- Fake reviews
- Astroturfing (false grassroots)
- Manufactured urgency
- Ghostwritten "expert" quotes
- Disguised advertising
- Astro-influencer campaigns
