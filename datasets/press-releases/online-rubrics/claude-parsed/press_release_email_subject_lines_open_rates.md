# Press Release Email Subject Lines - Data-Driven Standards

## Length Standards
- **Under 10 words / 50 characters** for highest open rates
- Cut off on mobile after **60 characters**
- Most journalists first see emails on mobile

## Empirical Open Rate Findings

### Personalization
- **50.81% open rate** when mentioning publication
- **39.25% open rate** without publication mention
- **+11.56 percentage point lift** from personalization

### Visual Formatting
- Subject lines with **brackets**: 52.08% open rate, 2.24% reply rate
- Best performing format

### Subject Line Types

#### High-Performing Formats
- **Question format**: "How Did This Startup Increase Revenue 300%?"
- **Exclusive offer**: "Exclusive: [Story]" - significant lift
- **Specific numbers**: "[Number] + [Specific outcome]"
- **Local angle**: "[Local city] + [news]"
- **Celebrity/notable person reference**

#### Low-Performing Formats
- "Press Release: [topic]"
- "For Your Consideration"
- "Just Following Up"
- All caps
- Multiple exclamation points

## Click-Through Rate Insights
- Open rate ≠ click-through
- "PR" in subject line: slight open rate bump but no CTR lift
- CTR is the more important metric
- Headline + summary in body must deliver

## Spam Filter Triggers (Avoid)
- "Limited time"
- "Must-see"
- "Free"
- "$$$"
- ALL CAPS
- Excessive punctuation
- "Click here"
- "Act now"

## Implicit Subject Line Rubric

A press release email subject line is high-quality if:
1. Under 50 characters
2. Personalizes (mentions publication or journalist)
3. Specific (numbers, names)
4. Curiosity-inducing (question or specific claim)
5. Shows news angle clearly
6. Avoids spam triggers
7. Mobile-optimized
8. Doesn't lead with "Press Release:"
9. Offers something (exclusive, data, expert)
10. Free of jargon

## Sample High-Performing Subject Lines

- "Exclusive: [Company] launches [Product] in [Industry]"
- "How [Company] [Achievement] in [Time]"
- "[Number] [Specific outcome] from [Study]"
- "[Local city] [Event/news]"
- "[Notable person] joins [Company] as [Title]"

## Implicit Quality Test
Read aloud. Would a journalist with 200 unread emails open this one?
