# GSA Blog: Government Communications Best Practices

## Government Communication Principles
- Plain language (Plain Writing Act of 2010)
- Accessibility (Section 508 compliance)
- Transparency (FOIA-aware)
- Bipartisan tone
- Public benefit focus
- No partisan framing

## Press Release Format for Government

### Required Elements
- Agency seal/logo
- "FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE"
- Release number
- Date
- City of issuance
- Headline (news + agency action)
- Body with appropriate authorities cited
- Quote from senior official
- Boilerplate (agency mission)
- Media contact

### Plain Language Requirements
- 8th-grade reading level target
- Active voice
- Short sentences (under 25 words)
- Define acronyms on first use
- Concrete examples
- Avoid jargon and bureaucratic language

### Accessibility Requirements
- Plain text version available
- Multimedia with alt text
- Screen reader compatible
- Color contrast standards
- Translation for major languages

## Tone Guidelines
- Professional but accessible
- Bipartisan (no political framing)
- Service-oriented
- Public benefit emphasized
- Avoid promotional language

## Common Government Press Release Types
- Policy announcements
- Program launches
- Regulatory actions
- Service updates
- Award announcements
- Statistical reports
- Crisis/emergency notifications

## Distribution Channels
- Agency website news section
- Government News Network
- Direct outreach to congressional offices
- Wire syndication
- Social media (with content moderation)
- Sector-specific newsletters

## Quality Rubric (GSA)
- Plain language (8th grade)
- Active voice
- Section 508 accessibility
- Bipartisan tone
- Authorities cited
- Senior official quote
- Multilingual where appropriate
- Service-oriented framing
