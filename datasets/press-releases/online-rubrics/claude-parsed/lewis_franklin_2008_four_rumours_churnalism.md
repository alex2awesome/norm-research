# Lewis et al. — "Four Rumours" / churnalism — pickup determinants in a thinned newsroom

This companion piece to "A Compromised Fourth Estate" extends the Cardiff dataset and is the foundational empirical source for the *churnalism* concept (PR/wire copy republished with little or no editorial transformation).

## Headline finding
Of UK quality-press domestic news in 2006:
- **80%** was *not original* journalism (relied wholly or partly on PR or wire copy).
- **Only 12%** of stories were generated by reporters' own initiative.
- **~50%** of newspaper stories were "wholly or mainly" PR-derived; another ~10% had a clear PR component plus reporting.
- Average story output per journalist had ~tripled since 1985 — directly producing the demand for ready-to-publish copy.

## Rubric criteria operationalised
1. **Time-to-publish.** A pickup-worthy release must require minimal sub-editor effort. The political-economy point: a thinned newsroom literally cannot rewrite — it republishes.
2. **No-rewrite-needed prose.** Inverted-pyramid lead, AP-style attributions, no marketing adjectives a sub will have to strip. Releases riddled with promotional language force rewriting and so are *less* picked up at the busy desks they target.
3. **Wire-laundering pathway.** Releases that PA / AP will rewrite into wire copy reach far more outlets. The rubric: is the release pegged so a wire desk will treat it as news?
4. **Output-pressure exploit.** The release should arrive before the deadlines on the desk's actual production cycle (early morning for evening papers; mid-afternoon for morning papers; before the broadcast slot for radio).
5. **Single-source sufficiency.** Because thinned newsrooms can rarely make second-source calls, releases that supply *both* the announcement *and* an external-quote validator (a quoted independent expert, a trade-body comment) get full pickup.
6. **Pre-supplied B-roll/visuals.** Because broadcast desks cannot dispatch crews, picture-ready releases convert at radically higher rates.
7. **No "see attached" friction.** Statistical tables, methods, image rights — embedded directly, not attached or behind a portal — survive the editing pipeline.
8. **Structural-asymmetry awareness.** Lewis et al. argue churnalism rewards resource-rich PR over actually newsworthy lower-resource sources; an ethical release acknowledges this and tries to surface its own context rather than exploit the gap.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
The political-economic frame predicts that pickup tracks *production-cost reduction for an under-resourced newsroom* much more strongly than it tracks intrinsic news value. The most picked-up releases are those that make republication faster than ignoring them.
