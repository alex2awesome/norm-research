# The Five Ws + One H Formula

## Overview
The Five Ws is a checklist used in journalism to ensure that the lead contains all the essential points of a story. As far back as 1913, reporters were taught to answer these in the lead.

## The Six Questions
1. **Who** is the story about?
2. **What** is the story about?
3. **Where** did the story happen?
4. **When** did the story take place?
5. **Why** did it happen?
6. **How** did it happen? (sometimes added)

## Historical Background
Rudyard Kipling gave the framework its most memorable articulation in his 1902 Just So Stories, writing about six "honest serving-men" whose names were What, Why, When, How, Where, and Who.

## Application to Press Release Lead
The lead (first paragraph or first sentence) should answer all 5Ws + H. This is essential for:
- Inverted pyramid structure
- Reader skim-reading
- SEO/AI indexing
- Quick journalist evaluation

## Quality Standards
A good press release lead:
- Answers all 5Ws+H in 30 words or fewer
- Front-loads the most newsworthy "what"
- Identifies the actor (who)
- Specifies time (when - is it new?)
- Specifies location (where - relevance)
- Provides causal context (why - significance)
- May include method (how - especially for science)

## Common Failures
- Burying the actor (passive voice)
- Vague timing ("recently")
- No location anchor
- Missing significance
- Confusing who's responsible

## Implicit Lead Rubric
Score 1 point for each W answered clearly in lead:
- Who: clear actor named
- What: news event clearly stated
- When: specific date/time
- Where: specific location
- Why: significance/cause clear
- How: method or mechanism (where relevant)

A press release lead scoring 5/6 or 6/6 is well-formed.
