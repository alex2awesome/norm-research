# Johns Hopkins University Editorial Style Guide

## Foundation
Johns Hopkins editorial guidelines draw from:
- 56th edition of The Associated Press Stylebook
- 17th edition of The Chicago Manual of Style
- Written from a Johns Hopkins point of view

## Press Release Standards

### 10 Tenets of Good Writing (JHU Communication Courses)
Written work judged on:
1. **Organization** - Logical structure
2. **Persuasion** - Compelling argument
3. **Clarity** - Easy to understand
4. **Focus** - Single main message
5. **Flow** - Smooth transitions
6. **Tone** - Appropriate to audience and purpose
7. **Proper usage** - Grammar and word choice
8. **Timeliness** - Current and relevant
9. **Accuracy** - Verifiable facts
10. **Relevance** - Matters to readers

## Media Relations Approach
The media relations team works to ensure journalists worldwide know about newsworthy work at the university by:
- Connecting with journalists
- Reaching out regularly with news releases
- Sending advisories
- Providing story ideas

## What JHU Considers Newsworthy
- Significant research findings
- Major awards/honors
- Significant grants
- Notable hires
- Innovation announcements
- Public policy implications
- Community impact

## Style Specifics
- AP style throughout
- Hopkins-specific terminology consistent
- "Johns Hopkins" (never "John Hopkins")
- "Bloomberg School of Public Health" (specific)
- Faculty titles consistent (associate professor, etc.)
- Departments capitalized

## Implicit Rubric
A JHU-quality press release:
- Strong organization (inverted pyramid)
- Persuasive on relevance
- Clear (no jargon)
- Single focus
- Smooth flow
- Appropriate tone (academic but accessible)
- AP style throughout
- Timely
- Accurate (verifiable)
- Relevant to broader audience
