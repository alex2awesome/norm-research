# NIH Office of Communications: Press Release Blog

## NIH Communication Standards
- Scientific accuracy paramount
- Plain language for public understanding
- Methodology transparency
- Limitations explicitly stated
- Clinical relevance properly framed
- Translation to public health implications

## Press Release Components

### Headline
- Focus on finding, not the institution
- Active voice
- 80 characters or less
- Avoid "first" or "breakthrough" without verification
- News + relevance

### Lede
- Finding, methodology summary, significance in 50 words
- Specific researchers, institutions, dates
- Concrete numbers (sample size, effect size)

### Body
- Background on disease/topic
- Detailed methodology
- Specific findings with statistics
- Limitations explicitly stated
- Clinical relevance (or lack thereof)
- Future research directions

### Quotes
- From principal investigator
- From NIH official providing context
- From external expert when appropriate
- Substantive scientific reasoning
- Honest about limitations

## Scientific Communication Standards
- Distinguish preclinical vs. clinical
- Distinguish animal vs. human studies
- Provide both relative and absolute risks
- Methodology summarized clearly
- Funding sources disclosed
- Conflicts of interest noted

## What NIH Recommends Avoiding
- Causal claims for correlational findings
- "Cure" language for early-stage research
- Hype that exceeds the study
- Single-source claims on controversial topics
- Effect sizes without baseline rates
- Statistical significance without clinical significance

## Multimedia Standards
- Images of researchers (with permission)
- Visualizations of mechanisms
- Patient stories with consent
- Charts showing results properly
- Captions with credits

## Quality Rubric (NIH Communications)
- Scientific accuracy verified
- Methodology summarized
- Limitations stated
- Effect sizes given fully
- Clinical relevance properly framed
- Conflicts of interest disclosed
- Multiple expert voices
- Plain language for accessibility
