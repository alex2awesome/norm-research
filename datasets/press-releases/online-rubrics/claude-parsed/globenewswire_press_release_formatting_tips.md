# GlobeNewswire Press Release Formatting Tips

## Key Topics Covered
- How to write a headline that grabs attention
- The ideal length and style for readability
- How to use subheadings, bullet points and quotes effectively

## Core Principle
"Formatting your press release correctly is just as important as the content." A well-structured document enhances engagement and attracts media interest.

## Style Standards
The Associated Press (AP) Style is the widely used standard for journalism and press releases. According to AP Style, capitalize only the first word and proper nouns of the headline and keep it clear and concise. Start the first paragraph with the city in all caps, followed by the state abbreviation and the date.

## Headline and Structure Tips
Headlines should be specific, intriguing and informative, and keep the headline to less than 100 characters if possible. Highlight the key point in your headline and support it with a compelling subhead that shares another critical point.

Keep your press release organized using the inverted pyramid writing style.

## Content Best Practices
- Avoid using overly promotional language - press releases are factual and written with the media and public stakeholders in mind
- Include quotes from subject matter experts to create trust with readers
- The body should be three to six paragraphs in length - longer is not better

## Formatting and Links
- A standard for the number of links in a press release is one link per 100 words; search engines regard a press release with an excessive number of links as spam
- Offer visuals with your press release - GlobeNewswire's Media Snippets can help bring news to life

## Editing and Contact Information
- Check and double-check spelling and grammar
- Include contact information to make it easy for media reviewing the press release
- Include at least the name and title of the individual that the media can reach out to

## Expected Benefits
Proper formatting makes news "more engaging, grabs media attention and helps your story shine."
