# AAAS Communication Toolkit - Working with Journalists

## Why Engage with Media
Scientists can leverage journalism to:
- Reach a wider audience beyond those actively seeking information
- Build public consciousness through consistent and accurate news coverage
- Foster enthusiasm supporting research funding through discovery stories

## Understanding Your Audiences

### The Journalist
Reporters operate under specific constraints:
- Consult multiple sources
- Prioritize accuracy
- Face tight deadlines (sometimes producing more than 5 stories a day)
- Write in distinctive styles without pre-approval requirements
- Focus on informing rather than comprehensive education

### The Public
Tailor messages to audience values and interests. Prepare answers to:
- What's new?
- Why should readers care?

## Interview Preparation Essentials

Before engaging media:
- Identify journalist audience demographics and concerns
- Develop a "three-point message" with clear relevance
- Anticipate challenging questions
- Arrive punctually with core messages pre-planned

## Building Professional Networks
- Connect with institutional public information officers
- Learn from experienced scientist-communicators
- Cultivate relationships with beat reporters in your field
- Consider the AAAS Mass Media Science and Engineering Fellows Program

## Implicit Press Release Rubric (AAAS perspective)

A press release that supports good science journalism:
1. **Three-point message** structure
2. **What's new** clearly stated upfront
3. **Why should readers care** addressed
4. **Multiple verifiable sources** offered
5. **Accuracy paramount** - facts checkable
6. **Distinctive but accessible** language
7. **Public information officer accessible** for follow-up
8. **Researcher available** for interview
9. **Deadline-friendly** - delivers what journalists need quickly
10. **Audience-appropriate** - matches outlet's coverage focus
