# Brechman, Lee & Cappella (2009) — translation fidelity in cancer-genetics releases

Quantitative + qualitative content analysis: 20 cancer-genetics journal papers (prostate, breast, colon, smoking, obesity), N=23 associated press releases, N=71 lay-press news articles. A pool of N=40 genetics graduate students rated representativeness.

## Headline empirical findings
1. **Press releases were judged significantly more representative of the source paper than news articles were.** PR offices add less distortion than newsrooms — but newsrooms cannot correct distortion already in the release.
2. **67.5% of stories presented genetic discoveries in a "biologically deterministic" simplified frame** (e.g., "the gene for X"), with the deterministic framing introduced *equally* by press releases and by news.
3. **>40% of news stories on the same scientific finding were *inconsistent* with each other**, indicating that release framing has direct downstream consequences for the public's coherent understanding.

## Brechman-Lee-Cappella rubric criteria for genetics / mechanistic releases
1. **Anti-determinism markers.** Does the release explicitly use "associated with" / "increased risk of" rather than "causes" or "the gene for"?
2. **Effect-size context.** For a reported genetic association, does the release give *both* relative risk and absolute risk increase (e.g., "from 4% baseline to 6% — a 50% relative increase")?
3. **Penetrance disclosure.** For mendelian-style claims, does the release state the penetrance (proportion of carriers actually developing the trait)?
4. **Population specificity.** Was the population studied a single ethnicity / age cohort / sex? Does the release flag where generalisation is unjustified?
5. **Explanatory chain length.** Does the release distinguish "associated with" from "mechanistically linked to" from "intervening in this pathway changes outcome X"? Each step is a different epistemic level.
6. **Translational status.** Is the finding bench science, animal model, or human clinical? Does the release state which?
7. **Replication status.** Has the finding been replicated in independent samples? If not, does the release say so?
8. **Self-deterministic-headline test.** Would the release's headline pass the genetics-graduate-student panel as "non-deterministic"?

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Brechman et al. show that *deterministic, simplified* releases are picked up more often by lay press but at high downstream cost: cross-paper inconsistency, reader confusion, and accumulated distortion that quality outlets (genetics-trade press, medical journals' news sections) explicitly screen against. A pickup-worthy *and* fidelity-preserving genetics release passes the eight criteria above.
