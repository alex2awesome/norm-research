# News Quality Dimensions Journalists and PR Practitioners Agree On

## Background
Research has consistently found "remarkable" agreement between journalists and PR practitioners on which elements of news are most important. This forms the basis for a shared rubric for evaluating press releases.

## The Eight Shared News Quality Elements

### 1. Accuracy
- Verifiable facts
- Correct names, titles, numbers
- No errors of fact
- No misleading framing

### 2. Interest to Reader
- Captures attention
- Story angle that engages
- Beyond mere announcement
- Emotional/practical relevance

### 3. Usefulness to Reader
- Provides actionable information
- Answers reader questions
- Affects reader's life
- Has practical application

### 4. Completeness
- All necessary information present
- 5Ws + H answered
- Context provided
- Background available
- Sources identified

### 5. Prompt Publication
- Timeliness of release
- Aligned with news cycle
- Distributed in time for journalists' deadlines
- Embargo timed appropriately

### 6. Favorable Depiction of Subject
- Subject portrayed in best legitimate light
- Spokespersons quoted appropriately
- Context that supports subject
- (PR concern primarily)

### 7. Mechanical / Grammatical Correctness
- Proper grammar
- AP style adherence
- Punctuation
- Spelling
- Format conventions

### 8. News Story Style
- Inverted pyramid
- Lead with news
- Supporting paragraphs
- Quotes
- Standard news article structure

## Implications for Press Release Quality

Press releases that score highly on these eight dimensions are more likely to:
- Be picked up by journalists
- Be used with minimal editing
- Lead to accurate coverage
- Build credibility for the source

## Implicit Rubric
Score press release on each dimension 1-5:
- Accuracy: All facts verifiable? (5 = all, 1 = errors found)
- Interest: Compelling angle? (5 = grabbing, 1 = boring)
- Usefulness: Reader benefit? (5 = clear value, 1 = none)
- Completeness: All info present? (5 = complete, 1 = gaps)
- Timeliness: Right moment? (5 = perfect, 1 = stale or premature)
- Subject depiction: Appropriate? (5 = balanced, 1 = puffery)
- Mechanical: Error-free? (5 = perfect, 1 = errors)
- News style: Proper structure? (5 = textbook, 1 = improper)

Total: 40 = exemplary; 30+ = good; 20-29 = needs work; <20 = poor
