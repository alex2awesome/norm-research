# de Semir, Ribas & Revuelta (1998 JAMA) — does a press release predict newspaper coverage?

Retrospective content analysis Dec 1996–Feb 1997 of press releases and tables of contents from BMJ, Nature, Science, The Lancet against newspaper coverage in NY Times, Le Figaro, Le Monde, El País, La Vanguardia, La Repubblica, IHT.

## Headline empirical finding
A journal article was much more likely to be covered by a newspaper if it had a press release. Coverage probability was concentrated on press-released articles to a striking degree.

## de Semir / Ribas / Revuelta rubric criteria for journal-paper releases
1. **Journal-press-release existence.** Has the journal itself, or the publishing institution, issued a press release for the article? (Without this, newspaper pickup is rare.)
2. **Journal prestige (BMJ / Lancet / Science / Nature).** Releases from these high-impact venues amplify pickup; sub-rubric for issuers: identify the highest-impact venue the result legitimately fits and place there.
3. **Cross-language translation.** Does the release exist in the target country's language, or is the headline phrased so it survives a wire translator?
4. **Time-zone embargo coordination.** Does the embargo lift at a time-of-day workable in the target outlet's edition cycle? European journal embargo lifts at a US-friendly hour.
5. **Topic concentration.** de Semir et al. found certain topics (cardiovascular, oncology, infectious disease) dominate press-release-driven coverage. Releases pegged to a topic the receiving newspapers are *currently* covering compound their pickup advantage.
6. **Front-page-photo opportunity.** Print papers prefer releases with at least one print-quality image of either the lead author or a representative subject.
7. **Independent-comment pre-supply.** Releases that include a comment from an independent expert at another institution reduce the journalist's verification cost and increase pickup.
8. **Concurrent-day distribution.** Coordinated same-day distribution to multiple international wires increases pickup correlation across newspapers.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
The de Semir et al. data make the press-release-existence question the *primary* predictor of newspaper coverage of a journal article. Within the press-released set, the secondary predictors are journal prestige, topic match to current news, and quality of the supplied multimedia / expert package.
