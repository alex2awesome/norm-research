# Murcott & Williams (2013) — Science journalism's "churnalism" challenge

Cardiff team's analysis of the structural state of UK science journalism. Argues that resource squeeze has shifted science news production from journalists to PR offices, with implications for what releases get picked up and how.

## Headline empirical findings
1. **Most science news in the UK is processed PR rather than independently reported.** Journalists' role has compressed to "processing supplied information."
2. **Homogeneity is a symptom of churnalism** — when many outlets carry near-identical stories, it traces to a single press release.
3. **Specialist science correspondents have been thinned out** — fewer journalists with the training to evaluate scientific claims.
4. **PR offices have professionalized** as journalism has thinned, shifting power.

## Murcott-Williams rubric criteria
1. **Multi-outlet detection-resistance.** Releases that yield differentiated stories at different outlets are higher-quality (genuine news) than releases that yield identical stories everywhere (pure churnalism). Issuers can encourage this by supplying outlet-specific quotes and angles.
2. **Specialist-correspondent targeting.** Releases pitched to remaining specialist science correspondents (with appropriate scientific depth, methodological detail, and prior-literature context) earn deeper coverage than wire-blast releases.
3. **Anti-homogenization measures.** Issuers concerned with churnalism can offer different angles to different outlets (exclusives, region-specific framings, embargoed first looks), trading uniform reach for diversified coverage.
4. **Press-release-as-final-product disclosure.** Releases should indicate that no journalist has independently verified the work, so editors can decide whether to invest the verification cost or run as-is.
5. **Independent-verification path.** Releases should make independent verification *cheap* (links to data, named external experts, study protocol) — addressing churnalism's root cause (verification cost).
6. **Counter-press-release supply.** Releases on contested topics should anticipate counter-releases from competing groups and supply enough context for the journalist to triangulate.
7. **Embargo with prep window.** Embargoes that give specialists time to call independent experts before lift produce higher-quality coverage than zero-prep-window blasts.
8. **Public-database posting.** Posting the press release to a public database (EurekAlert, AlphaGalileo) plus the issuing institution's news site supports both pickup and after-the-fact accountability.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Murcott & Williams's analysis predicts that a release optimized for *churnalism* will be picked up most widely (homogeneously) but will have low long-run trust capital. A release optimized for *quality coverage* will be picked up by fewer outlets but earn deeper, more accurate, more durable stories. The optimal modern strategy is bifurcated: serve both audiences with a wire-friendly base release plus deeper specialist material.
