# Y Combinator (Sharon Pope): Startup PR Tactics

## YC's Core PR Philosophy
- PR is not about you — it's about your customers and your market
- Best startup stories come from talking about a problem, not a product
- Founders are the best spokespeople, not PR firms
- Build relationships before you need them

## When to Do PR (YC Framework)
- Funding announcements at Series A+
- Product launches with genuine new capability
- Industry data report from your unique vantage
- Major customer wins (with permission)
- Pivot stories with honest learnings
- Acquisition or major hiring news

## When NOT to Do PR
- Pre-product
- Pre-revenue without strong differentiation
- Minor product updates
- Generic "thought leadership" pieces
- Trying to ride someone else's news cycle inappropriately

## The Founder-First Pitch
- Founder writes the first draft
- PR firm/team polishes, doesn't ghostwrite
- Quote sounds like founder actually talks
- Founder available for interviews directly
- Founder's LinkedIn matches PR claims

## YC's Pitch Email Structure
- Subject line: 8 words, news + entity
- Para 1: The news (1 sentence)
- Para 2: The "why now" (1-2 sentences)
- Para 3: What you can offer (interview, demo, exclusive)
- Sign off: phone number, calendar link

## Story Angles That Work for YC Companies
- "Why we built X" (origin story)
- "We were wrong about X" (humility)
- "The hidden problem in [industry]"
- "How we got from 0 to 100,000 users"
- "Our biggest customer wasn't who we expected"

## Pitching Hierarchy (YC Recommended)
1. Reporter who has covered your space recently
2. Reporter at the right outlet for your audience
3. Trade publication covering your industry
4. Newsletter writers in your space
5. Wire service (only after personal pitches)

## Quality Rubric From YC
- Founder is the spokesperson
- Real story, not promotional release
- Specific to journalist's beat
- Personal email, not blast
- Phone number / calendar link provided
- Honest framing, no hype
