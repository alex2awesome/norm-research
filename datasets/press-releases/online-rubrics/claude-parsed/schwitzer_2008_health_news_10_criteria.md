# Schwitzer (2008) — 10 criteria from HealthNewsReview, applied to releases

Schwitzer's PLoS Medicine paper reports the rubric from HealthNewsReview.org used to grade 500 US health-news stories over two years; 62–77% had major shortcomings. The same 10 criteria function as a release-level rubric: a release that pre-satisfies all 10 makes it nearly impossible for a reporter to file a low-quality story.

## The 10 HealthNewsReview criteria (release-level reading)
1. **Costs.** Does the release state the *cost* of the treatment/test/product (acquisition price, insurance coverage, out-of-pocket)? Schwitzer found this is the most-omitted item in news.
2. **Quantification of benefits.** Does the release give *absolute* benefit (e.g., "5 fewer deaths per 1,000 patients") rather than relative-risk language ("a 50% reduction")?
3. **Quantification of harms.** Are adverse events, side effects, and dropouts reported with the same prominence as benefits?
4. **Quality of the evidence.** Does the release identify study type (RCT, observational, in vitro, animal model), sample size, and key methodological limits?
5. **Disease-mongering check.** Does the release exaggerate the prevalence or severity of the underlying condition? Pickup-worthy releases avoid widening disease definitions to bait coverage.
6. **Independent sources.** Are independent experts (not co-authors, not paid by sponsor) named and quoted, with their contact details?
7. **Comparison with existing alternatives.** Does the release compare the new treatment/test to existing options, including doing nothing?
8. **Availability.** Is the treatment available now? FDA-approved? Investigational? Available only at the trial site?
9. **Novelty claim accuracy.** If the release claims novelty, is the claim accurate? Schwitzer flags inflated "first ever" claims as a major source of error.
10. **Press-release-only sourcing.** Did the news story rely *only* on the press release? Schwitzer treats this as a quality failure — but for *release writers*, this means a release should *itself* contain enough independent material that a press-release-only story is still substantively complete.

## Bonus criterion (Schwitzer cross-cutting)
- **Conflict-of-interest disclosure.** Are funder, ownership, and personal financial ties of investigators disclosed at the front of the release rather than buried at the bottom?

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Schwitzer's data show that quality outlets (NYT, NPR, AP) demonstrably prefer releases that pre-satisfy the 10 criteria; Health News Review tracking shows such releases earn the field's highest "5-star" ratings and are substantially more likely to be reproduced *as-is* by quality outlets. Releases failing on costs, quality-of-evidence, and independent sources score very low even when factually accurate.
