# Wired: Pitch Guide

## What Wired Wants
- Stories about technology's impact on culture
- Investigations into power dynamics in tech
- Long-form profiles of consequential figures
- Science stories with technical depth
- Climate-tech with verifiable progress
- Future-oriented pieces with present-day grounding

## What Wired Doesn't Want
- Routine product launches
- Funding announcements (without bigger story)
- Vanity company milestones
- Generic "AI is the future" pieces
- Hype without verification

## Pitch Structure
- Subject line: specific story angle
- Para 1: The story angle (1-2 sentences)
- Para 2: Why now (timeliness)
- Para 3: Sources and access available
- Para 4: Why you / why this source

## What Strengthens a Pitch
- Exclusive access to people or documents
- Verifiable data with methodology
- Multiple expert sources arrayed
- Visual storytelling potential
- Connection to broader trend

## What Weakens a Pitch
- Story has been covered elsewhere
- Single source on a controversial claim
- Anonymous sources without justification
- Visual story without visual assets
- Tech-bro framing without consumer impact

## Wired's Editorial Standards
- Multiple sources for major claims
- Methodology disclosed for data
- Conflicts of interest declared
- Quotes verified with sources
- Right of reply offered to subjects

## Submission Process
- Pitch via specific section editor
- Wait 2 weeks for response before following up
- One follow-up max
- Prepared to discuss in 30-min call
- Available for back-and-forth on framing

## Quality Rubric (Wired)
- Substantive cultural/technological story
- Verifiable sources and data
- Visual storytelling potential
- Connection to broader trends
- Multiple sources arrayed
- Exclusive access advantage
- Right of reply observed
