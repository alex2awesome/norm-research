# WFSJ Guiding Principles — international science-journalism standards as a release rubric

The World Federation of Science Journalists' principles are written for journalists, but each one inverts cleanly into a release-evaluation rubric: a release that pre-satisfies WFSJ principles passes the journalist's intake check.

## WFSJ principles as press-release criteria
1. **Accuracy and verification.** Does the release supply facts the journalist can verify against the source paper / dataset / study within minutes?
2. **Source credibility.** Are sources clearly identified with their institutional role and qualifications, so the journalist can assess expertise and bias?
3. **Conflict-of-interest disclosure.** Does the release reveal funding, financial ties, and competing interests at the top of the release rather than burying them?
4. **Press-release scrutiny passed pre-emptively.** Does the release acknowledge its own promotional posture and avoid sensationalizing? Releases that anticipate the journalist's scrutiny ("this is a preliminary finding requiring replication") earn trust.
5. **Independent expert availability.** Does the release supply contact info for at least one *unaffiliated* expert who can comment to the journalist?
6. **Uncertainty reporting.** Does the release communicate confidence intervals, the preliminary nature of findings, and remaining unknowns?
7. **Embargo discipline.** Does the release respect the journal embargo and clearly state lift time/date so the journalist can plan coverage?
8. **Balanced context.** Does the release include relevant counter-perspectives or alternative interpretations rather than presenting a single-study conclusion?
9. **Evidence-quality differentiation.** Does the release explicitly identify the level of evidence (peer-reviewed, preprint, conference abstract, anecdotal) and avoid conflating types?
10. **Avoidance of exaggeration.** Does the release resist overstating significance, novelty, or certainty? WFSJ explicitly flags "first ever," "breakthrough," "miracle," "cure" claims for scrutiny.

## Cross-cutting WFSJ rubric criteria
- **False-balance avoidance.** When scientific consensus is well-established (vaccines, climate, evolution), the release should not give equal time to fringe positions just to appear balanced.
- **Source diversity.** Quote sources from multiple institutions, geographic regions, and demographic backgrounds where possible.
- **Editorial independence preservation.** The release should not impose conditions (e.g., right to review the journalist's article pre-publication) that would compromise editorial independence.
- **Public-interest framing.** Releases should articulate why the finding matters to the journalist's public, not only to the issuing institution.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
A release that pre-satisfies the WFSJ principles passes intake at the world's reputable science journalism outlets (national papers' science desks, BBC News, Nature News, Reuters Health, AFP science). Releases that violate principles 4 (sensationalism), 5 (no independent expert), 9 (evidence-quality conflation), and 10 (exaggeration) get filtered out — even if the underlying science is sound — because they fail the journalist's defensibility check.
