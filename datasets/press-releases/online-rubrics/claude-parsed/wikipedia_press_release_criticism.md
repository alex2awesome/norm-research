# Wikipedia: Press Release (Criticism + Best Practices)

## Definition
"A press release is an official statement delivered to members of the news media for the purpose of providing information, creating an official statement, or making an announcement directed for public release."

## Standard Format Components
- Letterhead with company logo
- "FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE" or embargo notice
- Dateline (city, state, date)
- Headline
- Subheadline (optional)
- Lede paragraph (5 W's)
- Body (3-5 paragraphs)
- Boilerplate (about company)
- Media contact information
- "###" or "-30-" end marker

## Criticism (Wikipedia's Critical Section)

### Churnalism Concern
- Press releases often republished verbatim by understaffed newsrooms
- Erosion of editorial independence
- Public information mistaken for journalism
- Cardiff University study: 60% of UK press articles came partly from PR

### Manipulation Concerns
- "Astroturfing" via fake third-party press releases
- Strategic leaks disguised as official releases
- Selective fact disclosure
- Deceptive framing of corporate news

### Information Subsidy Critique
- Wealthy organizations dominate news cycle
- Smaller voices marginalized
- Corporate framing of public issues
- Loss of investigative depth

## Best Practices Section

### Writing
- News-first headline
- Inverted pyramid structure
- 5 W's in opening
- Quote from spokesperson
- Concise and factual

### Distribution
- Wire services (PR Newswire, Business Wire, etc.)
- Direct journalist outreach
- Social media amplification
- Company newsroom posting

### Ethics
- Truthful claims
- Verifiable sources
- Disclosure of relationships
- Honest framing
- Responsiveness to inquiries

## Modern Evolution
- Multimedia integration
- Social media kits
- Mobile optimization
- AI-summarization-friendly formatting
- Real-time analytics

## Quality Rubric (Wikipedia)
- Standard format components
- Truthful and verifiable claims
- Inverted pyramid structure
- Substantive quote
- Multiple distribution channels
- Disclosure of conflicts
- Responsiveness to follow-ups
