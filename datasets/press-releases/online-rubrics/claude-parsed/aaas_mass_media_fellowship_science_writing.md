# AAAS Mass Media Fellowship - Science Writing Standards

## Program Overview
The AAAS Mass Media Science & Engineering Fellowship strengthens the community of scientists who can communicate complex ideas to general audiences. Places scientists at media organizations for 10 weeks during summer.

## Training Curriculum (Science Writing Crash Course)

### Core Topics Covered
- Tracking down news stories
- Pitching them to editors
- Interviewing sources
- Writing news stories like a journalist
- Newsroom ethics
- Using social media as journalists
- Convincing readers news is important without overselling/overhyping

## Writing Standards Taught

### Tailored for Public Audience
- Not technical
- Journalistic style
- Plain language
- Story-driven

### Source Material
- Peer-reviewed primary research literature
- Research papers in research journals
- Not other science news articles
- Not review articles

## Implicit Standards for Science Press Releases

### A press release that satisfies AAAS Mass Media Fellow training:

#### Source Quality
- Based on peer-reviewed primary research
- Cites specific journal article (DOI)
- Not derived from other news coverage
- Original research focus

#### Public Accessibility
- Written for general audience
- Not technical jargon
- Story-driven framing
- Why this matters now

#### Honest Framing
- Doesn't oversell
- Doesn't overhype
- Acknowledges limitations
- Distinguishes proven from speculative

#### Journalistic Quality
- Inverted pyramid
- 5Ws in lead
- Active voice
- Quotes from researchers
- Independent expert validation possible

#### Ethical Standards
- Avoid misleading framing
- Disclose conflicts of interest
- Acknowledge funding sources
- Use neutral language
- Distinguish observed from hypothetical

## Implicit Press Release Rubric (AAAS Fellow Training)
1. Based on peer-reviewed paper (not preprint hype)
2. Plain language for public
3. Story angle clear
4. No overselling
5. Limitations acknowledged
6. Researcher quote authentic
7. Conflicts of interest disclosed
8. Independent expert sources possible
9. Inverted pyramid structure
10. Active voice
11. Specific data with provenance
12. Distinguishes correlation from causation
13. Distinguishes animal/cell from human findings
14. Distinguishes proof of concept from product
15. Provides context (where field stands)
