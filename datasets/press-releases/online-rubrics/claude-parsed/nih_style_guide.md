# NIH Style Guide Summary

## Core Purpose
The guide applies to "web content, fact sheets, brochures, newsletters, and other promotional materials."

## Primary Style Authority
NIH primarily follows the **Associated Press (AP) Stylebook**, with additional guidance from the **HHS Style Guide** for topics not covered by AP.

## Key Content Areas
The guide addresses:
- Government terminology
- Medical language and person-first language
- Destigmatizing language for conditions (autism, obesity, disabilities, substance use, etc.)
- Age, race, and national origin terminology
- Grammar, punctuation, and numerals
- Structure and formatting
- Biomedical definitions

## Press Release Coordination Requirements
- Public information officers should contact the funding NIH institute or center (IC) prior to issuing a press release for coordination
- Specific acknowledgment statements must be included (in footer or early in press release): name of the Institute(s)/Center, grant number
- Required disclaimer: "The content is solely the responsibility of the authors and does not necessarily represent the official views of the National Institutes of Health."

## Important Distinction
This style guide is **not** for scientific publications, which follow different standards (American Medical Association, National Library of Medicine).
