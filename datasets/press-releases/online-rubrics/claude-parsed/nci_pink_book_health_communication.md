# NCI "Pink Book" - Making Health Communication Programs Work

## Background
First published 1989, revised by the Office of Communications of the National Cancer Institute. A classic guide to health communication program planning.

## Four-Stage Communication Program Framework

### Stage 1: Planning and Strategy Development
- Define problem
- Identify audiences
- Set objectives
- Assess organizational capabilities

### Stage 2: Developing and Pretesting Concepts, Messages, and Materials
- Develop concept
- Pretest with audience
- Refine based on feedback
- Develop draft materials
- Pretest materials

### Stage 3: Implementing the Program
- Distribute materials
- Engage partners
- Monitor implementation
- Address barriers

### Stage 4: Assessing Effectiveness and Making Refinements
- Measure outcomes
- Evaluate effectiveness
- Refine for future programs
- Document lessons

## Press Release Standards (from Pink Book + NCI Plain Writing)

### Plain Language Requirements
- Plain Writing Act of 2010 mandates federal agencies write clearly
- NCI uses Federal Plain Language Guidelines
- Health literacy considerations

### Health Press Release Specific Elements
- Patient-friendly language (Grade 6-8)
- Clear risk/benefit communication
- Absolute numbers (not just relative)
- Source credibility (peer-reviewed citations)
- Action steps for patients
- Resources for more information
- Disclaimer about treatment decisions

## Cancer-Specific Considerations
- Avoid disease-mongering
- Avoid "cure" language without basis
- Differentiate trial findings from approved treatments
- Specify cancer type/stage
- Include incidence/prevalence context
- Distinguish from general claims

## Implicit Quality Rubric for Health Press Releases

A health press release passes NCI standards if it:
1. Plain language (8th grade or lower)
2. Absolute risk/benefit numbers
3. Cites peer-reviewed source
4. Distinguishes trial vs approved
5. Includes appropriate caveats
6. Has clear next steps for affected populations
7. Includes mental health/support resources
8. Translates technical jargon
9. Notes study limitations
10. Identifies funder
11. Includes appropriate disclaimers
12. Provides accessible formats (alt text, etc.)
13. Coordinates with NIH/HHS comms protocol
14. Distinguishes individual benefit from population effect
