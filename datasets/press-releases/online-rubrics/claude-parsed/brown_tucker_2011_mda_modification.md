# Brown & Tucker (2011) — Year-over-year modification as a narrative-disclosure rubric

Brown & Tucker introduce a cosine-similarity measure of how much a firm's MD&A differs from the prior year. Findings establish a release-rubric concept — *meaningful modification*: when the underlying business has changed, the disclosure should change correspondingly.

## Headline empirical findings
1. **Firms with larger economic changes modify the MD&A more than those with smaller economic changes** — modification scores correlate with substantive change.
2. **Stock prices respond positively to MD&A-modification scores** — investors use modification as a signal of new information.
3. **Analyst forecast revisions do not respond to MD&A-modification scores** — analysts get the same information through other channels (calls, briefings).
4. **MD&A modification has declined over time** even as length has grown — a "less informative more verbose" trend.

## Brown-Tucker rubric criteria for periodic financial press releases
1. **Modification-as-information.** Repeating last year's text verbatim signals "no change worth reporting." A press release that re-uses last quarter's language wholesale is correctly read by investors as low-information.
2. **Differential-update discipline.** When the underlying business has materially changed, the press release should *visibly* update its narrative segments — not just the numbers.
3. **Length discipline.** Longer is not better. Brown-Tucker show that growing length without growing modification is *negatively* informative.
4. **Cosine-distance pre-publication audit.** Before release, run a cosine-similarity check against the prior period's release; flag any segment with similarity above ~0.95 for review (is the underlying truly unchanged?).
5. **New-language flag.** When new product, market, regulatory, or risk-factor language enters the release, foreground it — investors weight new language more.
6. **Boilerplate flagging.** Mark genuinely-recurring boilerplate (safe harbor, "About" section) clearly so investors can filter it out and focus on substance.
7. **Risk-factor evolution disclosure.** When risk factors change, the release should highlight the change rather than burying it in tracked-changes.
8. **Year-over-year comparison block.** Provide an explicit Y/Y comparison block that quantifies the change rather than requiring investors to compute it.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
For periodic releases (10-K/10-Q, annual reports, sustainability reports), Brown-Tucker's evidence shows that pickup at sophisticated outlets (and price impact at the announcement window) tracks meaningful narrative modification. Releases that recycle prior text are picked up only mechanically; releases with genuine modification on substantive change are picked up substantively. Modern analyst-side text-similarity tooling makes this rubric concretely auditable.
