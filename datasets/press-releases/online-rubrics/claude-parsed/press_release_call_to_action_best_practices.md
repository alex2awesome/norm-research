# Press Release Call to Action Best Practices

## Why CTAs Matter
CTAs convert reader interest into measurable actions. Without a clear CTA, readers may not know how to engage further, reducing the press release's effectiveness.

## Best Practices

### Clarity and Directness
- Be direct about the action wanted
- Ambiguity causes friction
- One primary CTA per release

### CTA Placement
- Typically at the end of the main body
- Some recommend 2-3 CTAs spaced throughout (beginning hook, mid-content reinforcement, end as final prompt)
- Don't put primary CTA before key facts established

### Action-Oriented Language
Use action verbs:
- Subscribe
- Buy Now
- Register
- Download
- Learn More
- Read the Report
- Schedule a Demo
- Join the Webinar

### Examples by Purpose
- **Awareness**: "Learn more" or "Read more"
- **Lead generation**: "Request demo" or "Sign up"
- **Sales**: "Buy now" or "Order today"
- **Event**: "Register" or "RSVP"
- **Research**: "Download the report"

### Creating Urgency
For product launches, promotions, services - create scarcity:
- "Limited time"
- "First 100 customers"
- "Available through [date]"

## CTA Examples for News Releases
- "Learn more about how [KEY FINDINGS] impact you, visit [LINK]"
- "Get the full report at [URL]"
- "Schedule a media interview by contacting [PR CONTACT]"

## Implicit Rubric
A good press release CTA:
- Uses an action verb
- Is specific (not vague)
- Provides a destination (URL)
- Aligns with the release's purpose
- Is placed at logical decision moment
- Is measurable (can track clicks/conversions)
