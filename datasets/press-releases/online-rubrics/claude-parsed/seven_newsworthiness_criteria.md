# Seven Core Newsworthiness Criteria

## Definition
Newsworthiness is the quality of a story interesting enough to warrant a public report. News values are professional guidelines journalists use to decide what is worth reporting.

## The Seven Core Criteria

### 1. Timeliness
- How recently did the event unfold?
- Critical in 24-hour news cycle
- Press release must be tied to recent event
- Stale news rarely covered

### 2. Proximity
- Local stories gain more attention
- Affects audience directly
- "Hyper-local" angles increase pickup
- Geographic relevance matters

### 3. Impact
- Number of people affected
- Magnitude of effect
- Reach of consequences
- Severity for those affected

### 4. Prominence
- Notable people, organizations, brands
- Politicians, celebrities, athletes more newsworthy
- Major institutions
- Recognized brands

### 5. The Unusual / Novelty
- Strange, surprising, or unprecedented
- Counter to expectations
- "Man bites dog" phenomenon
- First / largest / most distant

### 6. Conflict
- Disputes
- Opposing parties
- Resolution arc
- Legal, political, competitive
- Tension creates story

### 7. Human Interest
- Emotion-driven stories
- Empathy, grief, admiration, joy
- Personal narratives
- Beyond facts and figures

## Historical Foundation
The formal study began in 1965 with Johan Galtung and Mari Holmboe Ruge's analysis of Norwegian newspapers, identifying "news factors" that predicted coverage.

## Application in Press Releases

### A press release is more newsworthy if it:
- Is tied to a recent event (timeliness)
- Has local angle (proximity)
- Affects many people significantly (impact)
- Involves notable people/orgs (prominence)
- Has surprising element (unusual)
- Has tension/conflict
- Has personal/emotional story

### Practical Strategies
- **Timeliness**: Tie to current news cycle, anniversaries, awareness months
- **Proximity**: Customize for regional outlets with local data
- **Impact**: Quantify reach (X people affected)
- **Prominence**: Include named experts, celebrities
- **Unusual**: Lead with the surprise
- **Conflict**: Frame as challenge being addressed
- **Human Interest**: Include personal story

## Additional Modern News Values
- **Magnitude** (Harcup & O'Neill)
- **Relevance**
- **Follow-up potential**
- **Surprise**
- **Power elite**
- **Celebrity**
- **Entertainment value**
- **Visual potential**
- **Shareability** (digital era)

## Implicit Newsworthiness Rubric
Score press release 0-2 on each:
- Timeliness
- Proximity (for target outlet)
- Impact (size and severity)
- Prominence
- Unusual angle
- Conflict element
- Human interest

Total: out of 14
- 12-14: highly newsworthy
- 8-11: solidly newsworthy
- 4-7: marginal
- 0-3: probably won't get covered
