# Sissons (2012) — Two-discourse analysis: PR text in, news text out

Sissons applies Erjavec's comparative discourse method and Fairclough's "texts as a set of options" frame to two press-release-to-news transformations. The analysis surfaces *which textual features survive* the journalist's transformation and which do not — directly useful as a release-design rubric.

## Headline empirical findings
1. **Journalists in the analyzed cases behaved as "churnalists"** — replicators rather than reporters or interpreters.
2. **The discourse of PR is increasingly setting agendas** that journalists' value-frames once set.
3. **Choice patterns of journalists are not always grounded in classical news-value rules** — they map to the textual options the release made available.

## Sissons rubric criteria
1. **Survival-of-quoted-material.** Direct quotes from the release are the most likely segment to survive into news verbatim. *A pickup-worthy release uses its quote slot to deliver the message-payload.*
2. **Headline-as-template.** Release headlines that are publishable as wire headlines are reproduced more often. The release headline should be a complete subject-verb-object structure ready to print.
3. **Lead-survival.** The first 35 words of the release survive into news at the highest rate. Maximum information density should be in the lead.
4. **Pre-canned context paragraphs.** Background paragraphs ("Company X is the largest...", "Y disease affects 1.3 million Americans...") are replicated when supplied. Without them, journalists either omit context (degrading coverage) or invent context (introducing error).
5. **Quote attribution clarity.** Quotes survive most reliably when attributed to a single named, titled source rather than to "a spokesperson."
6. **Embedded statistics.** Numerical claims survive intact; rhetorical claims get rewritten or dropped.
7. **Avoidance of insider register.** Releases written in industry jargon trigger journalist rewriting (with attendant slippage); plain-register releases survive intact.
8. **Single message frame.** Sissons documents that journalists adopt the release's frame when the release commits to one. Releases that hedge across multiple frames cede the framing decision back to the journalist.

## Critical / ethical caveat
Sissons's normative point is that churnalism erodes the journalistic agenda-setting function. A pickup-worthy *and* democratically-responsible release flags its sponsoring interest and supplies enough context for the journalist to interrogate, even when copy-paste use is enabled.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Sissons's discourse data show that the textual elements designed-in by the PR writer (headline, lead, quote, statistics, context paragraph) are precisely those that survive the transformation pipeline. A release that places its high-value claims in those load-bearing slots wins both pickup *and* fidelity of representation.
