# Lewis, Williams & Franklin - "The Quality and Independence of British Journalism"

## Overview
Authored by Justin Lewis, Andy Williams, Robert Franklin, James Thomas, and Nicholas Mosdell (2008). Published as "A Compromised Fourth Estate?" in Journalism Studies.

## Methodology
- Examined two weeks of domestic news coverage (March 2006 and April 2006)
- Four UK quality dailies: Guardian, Independent, Times, Telegraph
- Plus Daily Mail, BBC, ITV evening news, Radio 4
- 2,207 news items from quality newspapers
- 402 broadcast news reports

## Key Findings
- 60% of press articles came wholly or mainly from PR or wire services
- 34% of broadcast stories came wholly or mainly from PR/wire sources
- Only 12% of stories were generated entirely from journalist initiative
- PR materials and wire copy used without verification in many cases

## What This Means for Press Releases

### Why press releases get picked up
- Newsroom resources have shrunk
- Reporters required to produce more stories
- Less time for original reporting
- Press releases offer "ready-to-print" content
- Wire services amplify reach

### Implicit Pickup Determinants (from Lewis et al.)
A press release is more likely to be used (sometimes verbatim) if:
1. **Pre-formatted as news**: AP-style, inverted pyramid
2. **Contains all necessary attribution**: Names, titles, contact
3. **Has quotes ready to use**: No need to do interviews
4. **Has visuals attached**: Reduces production work
5. **Comes from trusted source**: Major institution
6. **Distributed via wire**: Reuters, AP, PA pickup
7. **Embargoed properly**: Aligns with publication schedule
8. **Localized**: Has regional angle for regional outlets

## Critical Implications
- "Churnalism" - the practice of recycling press releases as news
- Reduces democratic accountability
- Concentrates power among well-resourced PR producers
- Press releases function as "pre-packaged news"

## Implicit Rubric for "Churnalism-Ready" Press Release
- Length matches news article expectations (300-500 words for a brief, longer for feature)
- Headlines work as news headlines
- Lead works as news lead
- Quotes are usable as-is
- Background paragraph works as standalone context
- Author/source information ready for byline
