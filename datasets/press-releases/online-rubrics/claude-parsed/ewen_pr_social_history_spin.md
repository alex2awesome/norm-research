# Stuart Ewen - PR! A Social History of Spin

## Author and Status
Stuart Ewen, Distinguished Professor at Hunter College and CUNY Graduate Center. The book traces the evolution of public relations throughout the 20th century.

## Critical Thesis
Ewen argues there is no fundamental difference between PR and propaganda. The PR industry developed largely from corporate fear that genuine democracy would obstruct big business.

## Historical Context for Press Releases

### Origins
- U.S. Committee on Public Information (WWI)
- "Engineering of consent" (Bernays' phrase)
- Corporate response to populist/labor movements
- Pioneers: Edward Bernays, Ivy Lee

### The Bernays Method
- Manipulating public opinion
- Inspired Joseph Goebbels (per Ewen)
- Mapping out modern PR practices
- Shaping mass psychology

## Critical Implications for Press Release Practice

### What Ewen Identifies as Manipulative Patterns
- Pseudo-events (Boorstin)
- Manufacturing consent (Chomsky/Herman)
- Engineering of consent (Bernays)
- Strategic framing
- Information subsidies (Gandy)
- "Spin" rather than information

### Critical Counter-Standards (How NOT to Do PR Per Ewen)
- Don't manufacture artificial events
- Don't manipulate public opinion
- Don't disguise advocacy as news
- Don't exploit power asymmetries
- Don't suppress unfavorable information
- Don't astroturf
- Don't silence dissent

## Implications for Press Release Ethics

### Honest Press Release Standards
1. Distinguish information from advocacy
2. Disclose sponsors and conflicts of interest
3. Avoid manufactured pseudo-events
4. Acknowledge alternative viewpoints
5. Don't suppress unfavorable evidence
6. Be transparent about source motivations
7. Provide independent verifiability
8. Don't exploit power imbalances
9. Respect audience autonomy
10. Provide complete context

## Implicit Critical Rubric

A press release passes Ewen's critical standards if:
- Source organization clearly identified
- Funding/sponsorship disclosed
- Distinguishes facts from advocacy
- Includes alternative perspectives where relevant
- Doesn't astroturf (fake grassroots)
- Doesn't manufacture artificial controversy
- Doesn't exploit information asymmetries
- Provides verifiable claims
- Acknowledges limitations
- Respects audience to evaluate
- Doesn't silence dissent
- Doesn't bury inconvenient truths

## Why This Matters for Pickup
Releases that pass the Ewen ethical test are also more likely to:
- Build long-term media credibility
- Avoid "burning" sources
- Generate sustainable coverage
- Survive scrutiny
- Build audience trust
