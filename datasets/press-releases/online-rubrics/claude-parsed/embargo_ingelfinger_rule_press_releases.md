# Press Release Embargoes and the Ingelfinger Rule

## What is a News Embargo?
A news embargo is a request or requirement that information not be published until a certain date or until certain conditions have been met. Embargoes are common in:
- Business product announcements
- Medical/scientific journals
- Government policy initiatives

The media is given advance knowledge so reports can be prepared to coincide with the announcement and meet press time.

## Embargo Format Requirements
- Press releases must be clearly identified as embargoed
- Must include the embargo date and time
- Press officers are responsible for ensuring third parties honor the embargo
- Conventional formats: "EMBARGOED UNTIL [DATE/TIME]" at top of release

## The Ingelfinger Rule
Coined by Franz J. Ingelfinger, then-editor of NEJM in 1969. The original rule: "articles are accepted for consideration with the understanding that they are contributed for publication solely in this journal."

Many journals use variants of the Ingelfinger rule to restrain scientists from disclosing research to journalists prior to publication. To still allow stories to develop in advance of publication, journals distribute advance copies under embargo.

## Implications for Press Release Writing
- Coordinate timing with the journal/publisher embargo
- Embargo time = official "publication" time
- Releases sent to EurekAlert/Newswise typically go out under embargo
- Violating embargo can result in being cut off from advance copies

## Embargo Best Practices
- Send releases 2-7 days before embargo lift
- Include precise embargo time (e.g., "Embargoed until 11:00 a.m. ET, Monday, March 15, 2026")
- Specify time zone
- Provide journalists enough lead time to verify, get expert quotes, and write
- Identify all media materials (figures, supplementary materials) clearly as embargoed
