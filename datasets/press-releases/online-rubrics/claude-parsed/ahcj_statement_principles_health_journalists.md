# AHCJ Statement of Principles - Implications for Press Releases

## About AHCJ
The Association of Health Care Journalists (AHCJ) is an independent, nonprofit organization with 1,500+ members dedicated to advancing public understanding of health care issues. Mission: improve quality, accuracy and visibility of health care reporting.

## The Statement of Principles
Written by Gary Schwitzer (also founder of HealthNewsReview). Published in The American Journal of Bioethics. Addresses unique challenges of covering complex health care topics.

## Core Areas Covered

### Professionalism, Content, and Accuracy
- Accuracy paramount
- Distinguish facts from opinion
- Distinguish correlation from causation
- Avoid sensational language
- Acknowledge uncertainty
- Quantify benefits and harms
- Disclose conflicts of interest

### Independence
- Maintain editorial independence from sources
- Resist pressure from advertisers
- Don't accept gifts/junkets that create conflicts
- Disclose sponsorships

### Integrity
- Cite sources
- Identify experts honestly
- Note conflicts of interest of sources
- Verify claims independently

### Responsibility
- Consider impact on patients
- Avoid creating false hope or fear
- Place findings in context
- Provide actionable information

## What This Means for Health Press Releases

A press release that supports good health journalism (per AHCJ principles):

### Accuracy Requirements
- Verifiable facts only
- Distinguish associations from causation
- Specific (not vague) claims
- Cite peer-reviewed source
- Note study design limitations

### No Sensational Language
- Avoid "breakthrough" without basis
- Avoid "cure" language
- Avoid "first" claims unless verified
- Use precise statistics (absolute numbers)

### Acknowledge Uncertainty
- Confidence intervals
- Sample size
- Generalizability limits
- Replication status

### Quantify Benefits and Harms
- Absolute risk reduction (not just relative)
- Number needed to treat
- Side effects with frequencies
- Costs

### Disclose COIs
- Funding sources
- Industry ties of researchers
- Patent/financial interests
- Sponsoring organizations

### Provide Context
- Comparison with existing treatments
- Standard of care
- Cost-benefit considerations
- Available alternatives

## Implicit Press Release Rubric (AHCJ Principles)

A press release that meets AHCJ standards:
1. All claims verifiable
2. Causal vs correlational distinguished
3. Sample size and design noted
4. Effect sizes in absolute terms
5. Confidence intervals included
6. Harms quantified
7. Funding disclosed
8. COIs disclosed
9. Context vs alternatives provided
10. Limitations acknowledged
11. No sensational language
12. Provides reader-actionable info
13. Cites peer-reviewed source
14. Distinguishes hypothetical from observed
