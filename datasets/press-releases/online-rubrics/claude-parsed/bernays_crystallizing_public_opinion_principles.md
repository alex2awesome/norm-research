# Bernays (1923) Crystallizing Public Opinion — principles for press-release-grade publicity

Bernays argues that publicity is not the dissemination of an existing fact but the *creation* of conditions under which an event becomes news. The press release is one tactic in a larger campaign of crystallization. Rubric criteria distilled from the book:

1. **Overt-act test.** Does the release report an *act* (an event that has actually occurred or is staged to occur) rather than an abstract claim or opinion? Per Lippmann (whom Bernays quotes), only an "overt act" is hard enough to be reported as news.
2. **News creation, not mere announcement.** Has the source manufactured a concrete, demonstrable occurrence (an award, a survey, a coalition, an institutional ceremony) that gives editors something to point to?
3. **Third-party authority.** Does the release foreground respected outside figures — physicians, academics, civic leaders, opinion leaders — rather than the client's own self-praise?
4. **Audience segmentation.** Is the message tailored to the psychology of distinct publics (lay readers, legislators, specialists), with separate framings/data packages for each?
5. **Repackaging of existing material.** Does the release take pre-existing studies, reports, or expert opinion and reformulate it so its importance is *felt* and *appreciated*, not merely available?
6. **Dramatization of abstract ideas.** Is the underlying idea translated into a vivid concrete demonstration (an institution, a ceremony, a record, a "first") rather than left as a proposition?
7. **Alignment with existing public values.** Does the release attach the client's interest to a value the public *already* holds, rather than asking readers to adopt a new one?
8. **Multi-channel coordination.** Is the printed release part of a coordinated push across the spoken, the staged, the visualized, and the mailed — i.e. is the release one prong of an integrated campaign?
9. **Visible group action / consensus signal.** Does the release make a coordinated stance by many actors visible at once, so the very fact of unanimity becomes the story?
10. **Public-interest framing over private gain.** Does the release describe the action in terms of community welfare rather than commercial advantage?
11. **Continuous adaptation to opinion shifts.** Is the message calibrated to the *current* state of public opinion (recent events, prevailing mood) rather than a stale or generic posture?
12. **Truthful basis.** Underneath the staging, do the claims rest on verifiable fact? Bernays insists sustainable influence requires a real factual foundation.

## Heuristics for graders
- A "pickup-worthy" Bernaysian release names a concrete overt act, attaches it to third-party authority, and frames it as serving a public the editor's audience already cares about.
- Releases that argue a position without supplying an act, or that lead with the client's self-promotion, score low on the overt-act and public-interest tests.
- Multi-public segmentation is implicit: a release that simultaneously satisfies a trade press editor, a lay reader, and a policymaker scores highest.
