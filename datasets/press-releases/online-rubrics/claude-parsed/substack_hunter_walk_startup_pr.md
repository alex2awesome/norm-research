# Hunter Walk: Early-Stage Startup PR

## My Two Filters Before Pitching Press
1. Will this story exist in Google search 2 years from now in a way that helps us?
2. If a competitor copies the announcement next week, will we still look distinct?

## Why Most Startup PR Fails
- Founders mistake "press" for "validation"
- Funding announcements that say nothing useful
- Customer logos used without quotes or context
- "First in our category" claims that aren't verifiable
- Anonymous beta testers as the only proof

## The Honesty Test
- Would your competitors say this about you?
- Would your customers say this about you?
- Would your engineers cringe at this claim?
- If yes to any: rewrite

## Practical Tactics for Seed Stage
- Skip wire releases entirely
- Build 1:1 with 5 reporters who cover your space
- Become a useful source on industry stories you're NOT in
- Earn coverage by being quoted in others' stories first
- Long-tail blog post on your own site for SEO

## What Investors Actually Look at in PR
- Does the founder come across as substantive?
- Are the metrics specific or hand-wavy?
- Is there real differentiation in the framing?
- Does the company seem to know its market?

## On Funding Announcement Language
- Lead with what you're building, not the dollar amount
- Name specific use of funds (engineers in X, expansion to Y)
- Quote a lead investor with substantive reasoning
- Avoid "to fuel growth" and "to accelerate" language
- If you must mention dollar amount, say what runway it gives

## Quality Rubric
- Story that's still useful in 2 years
- Differentiated framing that competitors can't claim
- Honesty test passed
- 1:1 outreach to relevant beat reporters
- Substantive metrics, not vanity numbers
- Founder presents as smart and humble
