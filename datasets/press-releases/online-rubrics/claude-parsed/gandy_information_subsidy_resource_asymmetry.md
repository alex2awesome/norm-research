# Davis (2000, 2002) — resource asymmetry and pickup

Davis extended Gandy's information-subsidy thesis empirically using British corporate, financial, trade-union and pressure-group case material. He shows that pickup is dramatically a function of (a) the *target* media targeted, (b) the resource asymmetry between issuer and recipient, and (c) the deliberate cultivation of journalist beat relationships.

## Davis's empirical observations applied to a release rubric
1. **Target-media-fit.** The release should be optimized for a specific paper or wire (not blasted). Davis documents corporate PR successes by tailoring distinct releases for the FT, the Sun, and trade titles.
2. **Beat-relationship leverage.** Long-cultivated journalist contacts amplify pickup of even routine releases. Pickup-rate is a function of the issuer's *historical reliability* on the beat, not just the current text.
3. **City-page / financial-press bypass.** For corporate releases, a brief embargoed pre-release to City editors converts at far higher rates than an open wire blast. Mode-of-distribution as much as content drives pickup.
4. **Pressure-group asymmetry.** Davis shows resource-poor groups achieve pickup only when (a) they piggyback on corporate releases (counter-quote), (b) they break exclusives, or (c) they deliver pre-packaged investigation material. Pure announcement-style releases from resource-poor sources have ~zero pickup.
5. **Trade-union case.** Releases from unions reach lay press only when framed as *consumer*-impact stories rather than as labor-relations stories. Re-framing for the receiving audience's editorial priors is required.
6. **Spokesperson availability premium.** Pickup increases sharply when a named spokesperson is on standby for an immediate phone call from any inquiring journalist within 30 minutes.
7. **Counter-source mobilization.** Davis documents elite-source coordination: when multiple *separate* trusted sources echo a release talking-point, downstream pickup multiplies. Releases that name allied parties willing to be contacted have higher conversion.
8. **Pre-emption of routine criticisms.** A release that includes the obvious critic's likely objection plus a substantive response converts more reliably than one that leaves the critic uncalled — because the journalist would otherwise have to stop and chase the quote.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Davis's evidence implies pickup is determined more by the *issuer's structural position in the source–journalist network* than by release text quality. For a release-text rubric, the operational implication is: instrument the release with everything required to substitute for that network position when it is absent (named expert quotes, immediate spokesperson contact, pre-emptive critic response).
