# How to Write a Press Release: Complete Guide

## Structure and Components

A well-organized press release includes these essential elements:

- **Headline**: Under 100 characters using strong action verbs, answering "Why should I care?"
- **Dateline**: City and date of issuance
- **Lead paragraph**: Summary using the five Ws (who, what, when, where, why)
- **Body content**: Supporting details with short paragraphs containing one idea each
- **Quote**: From a relevant stakeholder or expert for credibility
- **Data**: Statistics, survey results, or industry trends backing claims
- **Call to action**: Clear next steps (website link, registration, contact info)
- **Boilerplate**: Brief organizational description highlighting mission
- **Media contact**: Names, emails, phone numbers
- **Multimedia**: Images, video, or other engaging visual elements

## Best Practices for Effective Press Releases

**Know Your Audience**
Understanding readers—whether industry professionals or potential customers—allows you to tailor messaging to what resonates most with them.

**Be Concise**
Aim for "300 to 500 words" to maintain clarity and focus while covering essential information without overwhelming readers.

**Stay Relevant**
Connect your story to current trends or broader social issues that matter to your target audience, increasing likelihood of media pickup.

**Tell a Story**
Humanize content by weaving a compelling narrative with beginning, middle, and end that includes perspectives from affected individuals.

**Incorporate SEO**
Use relevant keywords, optimize headlines, and utilize backlinks to improve search engine visibility and reach broader audiences.

**Provide Credibility**
Back claims with "statistics, research or industry reports" from reputable sources to build trust and increase pickup chances.

**Include Strong Call to Action**
Direct readers toward the next step aligned with your press release's primary goal.

**Review and Refine**
Thoroughly proofread for grammar, style, and flow, ensuring each word adds value before distribution.

## Headline Guidelines

Effective headlines should:

- Stay under 70 characters when possible
- Use keywords relevant to your audience
- Be clear and direct, avoiding ambiguous or vague language
- Employ strong action verbs (note: "unveil" and "reveal" generate more engagement than "launch")
- Write in active voice implying immediate relevance
- Include numbers or data supporting the claim

## Press Release Length Considerations

**Shorter releases (300 words)** work for minor updates, time-sensitive news, or event teasers.

**Longer releases** suit complex topics requiring technical explanation, research presentations, or significant announcements like mergers or leadership changes.

## AP Style Standards

Key formatting rules include:

- Capitalize only first word and proper nouns in headlines
- Spell out numbers one through nine; use numerals for 10+
- Write full month names standalone; abbreviate when used with specific dates
- Use double quotation marks; introduce speakers with full name and title
- Capitalize job titles only before names
- Spell out acronyms on first use with abbreviation in parentheses
- Use one space after periods
- Avoid Oxford commas

## Key Takeaway

Success requires understanding your audience, crafting attention-grabbing headlines, supporting claims with data, telling human-centered stories, and thoroughly editing before distribution through trusted networks reaching media outlets across numerous countries.
