# Newsom & Haynes - Public Relations Writing: Form & Style

## Overview
A long-standing PR textbook providing introduction to skills and theories underlying public relations writing. Doug Newsom is professor emerita at TCU's Schieffer School of Journalism.

## Press Release Writing Framework

### Pre-Writing Considerations
- Define purpose: What action do you want?
- Identify audiences (multiple, often)
- Choose appropriate channel/format
- Consider timing and competition for attention
- Determine "news peg"

### Press Release Form Standards
- Standard letterhead with logo
- "For Immediate Release" or specific embargo
- Contact info: name, title, phone, email at top
- Headline above
- Dateline at start of first paragraph
- Body in inverted pyramid
- "###" or "-30-" at end

### Style Standards
- AP Style throughout
- Active voice
- Short sentences (15-20 words avg)
- Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences)
- Concrete words over abstract
- Specific over general
- Avoid jargon and acronyms
- Use second references (avoid repetition)

### The Five W's + H
- Who, What, When, Where, Why, How
- Answer all in lead paragraph
- Most important first

### News Triangle (Newsom)
1. Top: Lead with most newsworthy
2. Middle: Supporting details
3. Bottom: Background, less critical

### Persuasion Principles
- Source credibility
- Message clarity
- Audience-appropriate appeal
- Evidence and examples
- Ethical persuasion (no manipulation)

## Special Press Release Types Covered
- Standard news release
- Media kit elements
- Public service announcement
- Brochure copy
- Newsletter article
- Annual report content

## Implicit Quality Rubric
A good PR release per Newsom:
- Serves specific identified purpose
- Targets identified audience
- Follows AP style and standard format
- Front-loads news in 5Ws
- Uses active voice
- Uses concrete language
- Has credible source/quote
- Has clear next steps
- Edited rigorously
