# Media Pitch and Press Release - Journalist Perspective

## Pitch vs. Press Release - The Distinction
- **Press release**: Formal, full announcement intended for distribution
- **Pitch**: Personalized email/message to specific journalist suggesting story angle
- Best practice: Pitch first, then send press release

## What Journalists Want (Cision State of Journalism 2021)

### Top Criteria
- **Relevance**: 33% of survey responses about "perfect pitch" emphasized relevance
- Pitches relevant to journalist's beat and audience

### Length
- 91% of journalists prefer pitches under 200 words
- Brief, scannable
- 4-5 paragraphs max

### Structure
- Subject line tells the story
- Lead with the news
- 5Ws clear immediately
- Jargon-free
- Readable in a few minutes

### Personalization
- Reference journalist's past work
- Tailor to their beat
- Avoid mass emails
- Address by name

## What Journalists Hate
- Mass-blast generic emails
- "Just checking in" follow-ups
- Multiple follow-ups
- Calling instead of email
- Broken links
- Promotional language
- Press releases for non-news
- No exclusivity or angle
- Irrelevant to their coverage

## Implicit Pitch + Release Rubric

### A successful pitch + release combination:
1. Subject line that summarizes news in <80 chars
2. Personalized opening referencing journalist's past work
3. The news in 1-2 sentences
4. Why their audience cares
5. What's available (interviews, data, exclusives)
6. Press release attached or linked
7. Direct contact info
8. Reasonable response window
9. One polite follow-up after 3-5 days
10. Then leave alone

## Specific Tips
- Send Tuesday-Thursday, mid-morning local time
- Avoid Mondays (inbox overflow) and Fridays (weekend mode)
- Personalize subject line and first sentence at minimum
- Offer exclusives to top-tier outlets
- Provide multimedia upfront (don't ask "if interested")
