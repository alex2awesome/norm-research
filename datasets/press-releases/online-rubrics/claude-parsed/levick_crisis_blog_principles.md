# Levick Crisis Blog: Press Release Principles in Crisis

## The Levick Principles
1. Speed matters — first 24 hours determine narrative
2. Specificity matters — vague statements erode trust
3. Spokesperson authority matters — CEO must be visible
4. Stakeholder hierarchy matters — communicate in right order
5. Substance matters — actions, not just statements

## The Crisis Press Release Anatomy

### Headline
- State the issue directly
- No PR-speak hedging
- No "alleged" if facts are clear
- Focus on action being taken

### Lede
- What happened (concrete facts)
- When it happened (timeline)
- What we're doing (specific actions)
- Who's accountable (named individual)

### Body
- Detailed timeline of events
- Root cause analysis (when known)
- Specific corrective actions
- Independent oversight
- Stakeholder commitments

### Quote
- From CEO or appropriate executive
- Empathy first, action second
- No legalese
- Specific, not abstract
- Sounds human

### Boilerplate
- Brief and factual
- Mention crisis-relevant context
- No defensive language

### Contact
- Crisis communications line
- 24-hour availability
- Spokesperson available
- Update schedule specified

## Crisis Pitfalls
- "We take this seriously" without specifics
- Apologizing for "any inconvenience"
- Blaming victims or third parties
- Lawyered language without empathy
- Going silent
- Inconsistent messaging across channels
- Spokesperson hiding behind PR

## What Effective Crisis Releases Do
- Acknowledge harm specifically
- Take responsibility clearly
- Outline concrete actions with timelines
- Provide regular updates
- Make CEO accessible
- Honor commitments made

## Quality Rubric (Levick)
- Speed: under 24 hours
- Specificity: concrete facts and actions
- Spokesperson: CEO visible
- Stakeholder hierarchy: right order
- Substance: actions over statements
- Empathy: human language
- Update commitments: honored
