# Investigative Reporter Press Release / Pitch Best Practices

## How Investigative Pitching Differs from Standard PR

Investigative journalists need:
- Substantive, reliable information
- Source credibility
- Document verifiability
- Story leads, not announcements
- Trust-based relationships

## Key Principles

### Build Relationships Long-Term
- Meet investigative reporters in person
- Coffee meetings before pitching
- Don't pitch only when you need coverage
- Become known as a reliable source

### Substance Over Hype
- Data, documents, sources
- "Never before shared" exclusives
- Hidden angles
- Patterns vs. events

### Pitch Format for Investigators
- Less promotional
- More document-rich
- Background materials provided
- Source contacts offered
- Timeline of events
- Anonymized examples (where appropriate)

## What Investigative Reporters Want

### From Press Releases
- Hard data
- Internal documents
- Whistleblower-quality info (if applicable)
- Industry patterns
- Wrongdoing or misconduct revealed
- Public interest angle
- Verifiability

### From PR Contacts
- Honest answers to tough questions
- Access to sources who'll go on record
- Documents (not just summaries)
- Time to investigate properly
- Exclusivity for major stories

## Anti-Patterns
- Boilerplate releases
- Marketing copy as news
- Hiding key facts
- Off-the-record after pitching
- Stonewalling on follow-ups
- Threatening legal action
- Astroturf campaigns

## Implicit Rubric for Investigative-Worthy Press Releases

A press release is more useful to investigative journalists if it:
1. Contains verifiable facts
2. Provides primary documents
3. Identifies named sources willing to talk
4. Has public interest angle
5. Reveals patterns or systemic issues
6. Provides context (history, comparisons)
7. Is honest about source organization's stake
8. Distinguishes facts from interpretation
9. Includes data files (CSV, spreadsheets)
10. Acknowledges complications/competing views
11. Provides timeline
12. Has clear public benefit framing
13. Goes beyond announcement to story
14. Is offered exclusively (or labeled as such)
15. Made available to FOI/document searches
