# Gandy - Information Subsidy Theory (Press Releases as News Subsidy)

## Overview
An information subsidy is the provision of ready-to-use newsworthy information to the news media by sources interested in gaining access to media time and space. Press releases are the primary form.

## Gandy's Framework (Beyond Agenda Setting, 1982)
Oscar H. Gandy Jr. argued that subsidies operate as a form of market rationing, where access to public discourse is allocated based on production capacity rather than merit or diversity. This disadvantages under-resourced actors such as grassroots organizations or individuals lacking PR infrastructure.

## Source Dependence and Power Imbalances
- Resource-disparate sources, particularly elite institutions with PR apparatuses, dominate subsidy flows
- Agenda bias toward elite interests over grassroots voices
- Media reliance on subsidies prioritizes efficiency over independent sourcing

## Empirical Magnitude
Studies estimate that subsidized content (press releases, etc.) comprises 15% to 80% of news stories due to practical constraints in newsrooms.

## Implications for Press Release Effectiveness

### A press release is more effective as a subsidy if it:
- **Reduces journalist workload**: Pre-written, copy-paste ready
- **Provides everything needed**: Quotes, data, multimedia, context
- **Comes from credible source**: Established institution, known PIO
- **Hits relevance criteria**: Matches outlet's beat and audience
- **Saves verification time**: Pre-fact-checked, with source links
- **Provides exclusivity options**: Differentiated for top outlets

### Implicit Quality Subsidy Rubric
- Drop-in ready (minimal editing required)
- Includes broadcast-ready elements (b-roll, sound bites)
- Pre-arranged interviews offered
- Background materials provided
- Translates jargon to lay terms
- Provides quotable quotes
- Offers data in multiple formats
- Comes with embargo for planning

## Critical View
Heavy reliance on PR subsidies has been linked to:
- Decline in original journalism
- Echo chamber effects
- Source bias (elite voices over diverse)
- "Churnalism" - rewriting press releases as news
