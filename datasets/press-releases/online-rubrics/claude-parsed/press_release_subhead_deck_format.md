# Press Release Subheadline / Deck Standards

## Definitions
A press release deck (also called subheadline or "dek") is a one-sentence summary that appears right below your headline. It provides additional context and supports the headline.

## Purpose and Function
- Provides additional context
- Reinforces main message
- Encourages people to keep reading
- Bridges headline and lead paragraph
- Gives reader reason to continue
- Summarizes who/what/why

## Format Guidelines

### Length
- No more than 14 words ideal
- Some sources say up to 25 words
- One sentence maximum
- Concise but informative

### Style
- Sentence case
- Summary of press release
- Every word counts
- Active voice
- No clichés

### Content Requirements
- ADD new information (not rephrasing headline)
- Include who/what/why details not in headline
- Bridge to first paragraph

## Common Mistakes
- Repeating the headline
- Too long (loses impact)
- Vague or generic
- Marketing-speak
- All caps
- Multiple sentences

## Examples

### Headline + Deck Combination
- HEADLINE: "Acme Corp Unveils AI Platform That Cuts Document Processing Time by 75%"
- DECK: "New Acme Intelligence reduces enterprise document review from hours to minutes, deployed at 50 Fortune 500 companies"

## Implicit Subhead Rubric
- Within 14-25 word limit
- Adds NEW information
- Includes specifics (numbers, names)
- Sentence case
- Active voice
- No buzzwords
- Bridges to first paragraph
- Standalone meaningful (works without headline)
