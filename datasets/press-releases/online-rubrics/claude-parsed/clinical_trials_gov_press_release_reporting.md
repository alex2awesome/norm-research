# ClinicalTrials.gov / FDA Reporting Standards for Trial Press Releases

## Federal Requirements (FDAAA 2007 / Final Rule)
The Food and Drug Administration Amendments Act of 2007 (FDAAA) mandates that certain clinical trials register and submit results information to ClinicalTrials.gov.

### Mandatory Reporting Includes
- Applicable trials registered before initiation
- Results submission within 1 year of primary completion
- Both positive AND negative results
- Adverse events
- Protocol details

## Compliance Issues
- 29.6% of mandatory studies have no results submitted
- Negative trial results often unreported
- Creates publication bias
- Obscures true drug development outcomes

## Implications for Press Releases About Trials

### Required Disclosures in Press Releases
- ClinicalTrials.gov identifier (NCT number)
- Trial registration date
- Results posting status
- Trial sponsor (named)
- Trial phase (Phase I, II, III, etc.)
- Study design (RCT, single-arm, etc.)
- Sample size
- Primary endpoint(s)
- Whether endpoint(s) met

### Honest Reporting Standards
- Distinguish primary from secondary endpoints
- Report whether primary endpoint achieved
- Report adverse events
- Note discontinuations
- Provide effect size with confidence interval
- Note study limitations
- Distinguish hypothesis-generating from confirmatory

## FDA Regulatory Considerations

### For Drug/Device Releases
- FDA approval status clear
- Approved indication specified
- Off-label use cautions
- Boxed warnings noted
- Safety profile summarized
- Required prescribing information referenced

### For Investigational Drugs
- "Investigational" status clear
- Not approved for marketing
- Not for prescription
- Trial enrollment status

## Implicit Quality Rubric for Trial Press Releases

A press release for clinical trial results meets standards if:
1. NCT number prominently included
2. Trial registration linked
3. Trial phase specified
4. Study design clear (RCT, single-arm, observational)
5. Sample size disclosed
6. Primary endpoint pre-specified
7. Endpoint achievement honestly reported
8. Effect sizes with confidence intervals
9. Adverse events reported
10. Discontinuations noted
11. Subgroup analyses noted
12. Limitations acknowledged
13. Funding source disclosed
14. COIs disclosed
15. Distinguishes from approved use
16. Comparable with prior literature
17. Cites peer-reviewed publication if available
18. Doesn't oversell preliminary findings
