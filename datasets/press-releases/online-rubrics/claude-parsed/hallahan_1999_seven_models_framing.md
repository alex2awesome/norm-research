# Hallahan (1999) — Seven framing models as press-release rubric

Hallahan systematizes seven distinct framing operations available to PR practitioners. Each is a release-level rubric: which frame the release commits to predicts how journalists will reproduce, evaluate, and amplify the message.

## The seven framing models (release rubric)
1. **Framing of situations.** Does the release define the *situation context* (a market shift, a public-health emergency, a regulatory inflection)? Pickup is much higher when the situation is named upfront so the journalist knows the story-type.
2. **Framing of attributes.** Does the release foreground *which* attributes of the subject (a product, person, organization) it wants emphasized, and *which it wants suppressed*? Specific attribute selection drives the journalist's headline.
3. **Framing of choices.** Does the release present a *choice structure* (this option vs. that option, action vs. inaction)? Choice frames carry decision-relevance and increase pickup at policy / business beats.
4. **Framing of actions.** Does the release frame the announcement as an *action* by an actor (rather than as a passive event)? Action frames produce shorter, sharper journalist transformations.
5. **Framing of issues.** Does the release situate the story within an existing issue narrative (climate, AI safety, healthcare costs)? Issue-framed releases attach to ongoing news waves.
6. **Framing of responsibility.** Does the release attribute responsibility (credit/blame) explicitly to a named actor? Responsibility frames increase pickup at accountability beats.
7. **Framing of news.** Does the release pre-frame the news *as* news — supplying angle, "so what," and freshness signals — so the journalist accepts the news-frame rather than building one?

## Cross-cutting Hallahan rubric criteria
1. **Frame consistency.** Does the release commit to one of the seven frames primarily and use the others in support, or does it scatter across all seven? Multi-framed releases lose pickup.
2. **Frame-fit to outcome.** Hallahan emphasizes choosing a frame that aligns with desired *journalistic* outcome (coverage type, downstream public response).
3. **Frame defensibility.** A frame the journalist can defend to their editor survives; one that requires editorial cover for the issuer's bias is stripped.
4. **Frame durability.** Does the release supply a frame that will continue to apply across follow-on coverage, or one that decays after the first article?
5. **Counter-frame anticipation.** Does the release pre-empt the most likely opposing frame? Hallahan: framing is competitive; releases that ignore counter-frames cede the public conversation.
6. **Frame-language symmetry.** Headline, lead, and quotes should all reinforce the same frame. Multi-frame internal contradiction breaks pickup.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Hallahan's seven-model frame predicts that a release with one clear, consistent, well-chosen frame outperforms multi-framed copy. The frame choice should match the receiving beat: action / responsibility for accountability beats; choice / issue for policy beats; situation / attribute for feature beats; news for breaking-news desks.
