# University Research Press Release Tip Sheet (Compiled)

## Length and Format
- 300-500 words (one page)
- Maximum 800 words
- Shorter releases more likely read and used

## Research Press Release Content

### Lead with What Matters
- Research impact or innovation
- Relevant data and statistics
- Connect to broader trends
- National conversation alignment
- "Firsts," "largest," or unique
- Broader implications

### Quote Selection
- Key administrators
- Lead researchers
- Co-authors (if collaborative)
- Independent experts (where possible)

### Tone
- Objective rather than promotional
- Plain language
- Avoid jargon
- Translate technical terms

## What Makes Research Newsworthy

### Field Importance
- Major contribution to field
- New discovery
- First time something done
- Methodological advancement
- Replication of important finding
- Refutation of prior thinking

### Public Impact
- Technology/breakthrough affecting lives
- Changes understanding of world
- Personal relatability
- Practical applications
- Health implications
- Environmental relevance

### Explainability
- Can be explained without specialist knowledge
- Visual or memorable
- Story angle exists

## Review and Approval Process

### Before Distribution
- Supervisor approval
- Communications office review
- Legal review (sensitive topics)
- Quoted individuals approve
- Partner organizations sign off
- 3-5 business days for review

### Required Approvals
- Research integrity office (for sensitive findings)
- IP/tech transfer (if patentable)
- Funding agency (if required)
- Co-investigators
- Industry partners

## Distribution Timing

### Future Events
- 1-2 weeks before

### Past Events / Achievements
- Within 24-48 hours
- While still news

### Embargoed (Journal Articles)
- Coordinated with journal embargo
- Distribute under embargo to selected media
- Lift embargo when journal publishes

## Implicit Quality Rubric for University Research Releases
1. Newsworthy in field
2. Public impact clear
3. Plain language
4. Researcher/admin quote
5. Independent expert (where possible)
6. Reviewed by all stakeholders
7. Properly timed
8. Embargo coordinated
9. Funding source acknowledged
10. Cites peer-reviewed publication
11. Multimedia provided
12. Researcher available for interview
13. Appropriate caveats
14. Distinguishes correlation/causation
15. Distinguishes preclinical/clinical
16. Notes limitations
17. Lab affiliation correct
18. Co-authors acknowledged
19. Compliance with funding agency requirements
20. AP style throughout

## Common Failures
- Overhype "breakthrough" without basis
- Animal-to-human extrapolation without caveat
- Missing limitations
- Burying the actor
- Marketing-speak
- No multimedia
- Wrong embargo time
- Missing funder acknowledgment
- Internal jargon used
- No researcher contact
