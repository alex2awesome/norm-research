# Arthur W. Page Society - The Page Principles

## Background
Arthur W. Page is "the father of corporate public relations." The Arthur W. Page Society is an organization for senior PR executives. The principles emphasize ethics for press releases and corporate communications.

## The Seven Page Principles

### 1. Tell the Truth
The public should know what's happening. Provide an ethically accurate picture of the enterprise's character, values, ideals and actions.

### 2. Prove It with Action
Public perception of an enterprise is determined 90% by what it does and 10% by what it says.

### 3. Listen to Stakeholders
Understand what the public wants and needs. Keep top decision-makers and other employees informed about public reaction.

### 4. Manage for Tomorrow
Anticipate public reaction and eliminate practices that create difficulties. Generate goodwill.

### 5. Conduct Public Relations as if the Whole Enterprise Depends on It
Corporate relations is a management function. No corporate strategy should be implemented without considering its impact on the public.

### 6. Realize an Enterprise's True Character is Expressed by Its People
The strongest opinions—good or bad—about a company are shaped by the words and deeds of its employees.

### 7. Remain Calm, Patient and Good-Humored
Lay the groundwork for PR miracles with consistent and reasoned attention to information and contacts. When a crisis arises, remember cool heads communicate best.

## Application to Press Release Writing

### A press release that follows Page Principles:
1. **Truthful**: Verifiable facts, no spin, no exaggeration
2. **Backed by action**: Announces real change, not just claims
3. **Stakeholder-aware**: Anticipates how multiple publics will react
4. **Long-term oriented**: Doesn't sacrifice future trust for short-term gain
5. **Strategic**: Aligned with overall enterprise strategy
6. **Authentic**: Reflects what the company actually is
7. **Composed**: Professional tone, even in crisis

## Implicit Ethical Press Release Rubric
- All claims verifiable
- All quotes accurate (people actually said this)
- All data sources cited
- Limitations disclosed
- Conflicts of interest revealed
- Stakeholder impact considered
- Long-term consequences accepted
- Tone professional and measured
