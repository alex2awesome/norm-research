# Sumner, Vivian-Griffiths et al. (BMJ 2014) - Hype in Science Press Releases

## Key Findings

Most exaggeration in health related science news is already present in academic press releases, according to research led by Petroc Sumner and Christopher D. Chambers at Cardiff University, with Solveiga Vivian-Griffiths as a co-author.

## Categories of Exaggeration Measured

The study coded press releases and matched news stories on three exaggeration dimensions:

1. **Exaggerated advice** - claims that go beyond what the underlying study supports
2. **Exaggerated causal claims** - turning correlation into causation
3. **Exaggerated inference to humans** - extrapolating animal/cell research to humans

## Extent of Exaggeration in Press Releases

- 40% of the press releases contained exaggerated advice
- 33% contained exaggerated causal claims
- 36% contained exaggerated inference to humans from animal research

## Connection Between Press Releases and News Coverage

When press releases contained exaggeration, news stories also exaggerated at high rates:
- 58% of news stories contained similar exaggeration in advice categories
- 81% of news stories contained similar exaggeration in causal claims
- 86% of news stories contained similar exaggeration in human inference

When press releases were not exaggerated, downstream exaggeration in news was much lower.

## Implications

The researchers suggest that improving the accuracy of academic press releases "could represent a key opportunity for reducing misleading health related news." Notably, there was little evidence that exaggeration in press releases increased the uptake of news (i.e., hype did not significantly increase pickup rates).

## Implicit Rubric for Evaluating Press Releases

Based on this work, a press release should:
- Avoid advice that goes beyond the evidence
- Distinguish correlation from causation
- Not extrapolate animal/cell findings to humans
- Include caveats and limitations from the original study
- Match claims to actual study design (RCT vs. observational, etc.)
