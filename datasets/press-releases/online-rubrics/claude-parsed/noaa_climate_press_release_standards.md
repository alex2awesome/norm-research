# NOAA Climate / Weather Press Release Standards

## NOAA Communications Function
Staff of public affairs and digital media specialists work with news producers, digital and print reporters, photojournalists, videographers, bloggers and others on behalf of NOAA's diverse scientific mission areas.

## NOAA's Role
As lead federal climate science agency:
- Delivers research, data, services
- Helps communities, tribes, agencies, and international community
- Helps understand, mitigate, prepare for and build resilience to climate change

## Editorial Criteria for Content

### News & Features Articles Must
- Be based on best available science
- Be reviewed by subject matter expert

### Data Sets Must
- Have FGDC-compliant metadata
- Be from validated sources
- Include uncertainty estimates

## Climate vs Weather Distinction
- **Weather**: Short-term state of the atmosphere
- **Climate**: Long-term patterns of weather conditions
- Press releases must clearly differentiate
- Critical for public understanding

## Climate Press Release Required Elements

### Scientific Standards
- Cite peer-reviewed research
- Provide uncertainty estimates
- Use standard climate metrics (temperature, sea level, etc.)
- Reference baseline periods (e.g., 1991-2020)
- Distinguish observed from projected

### Communication Standards
- Plain language
- Define technical terms
- Distinguish weather events from climate trends
- Avoid sensationalism
- Provide context (record-keeping period)

### Action-Oriented Elements (where applicable)
- Public safety information
- Resilience resources
- Adaptation options
- Where to get more information

## Implicit Quality Rubric for Climate/Weather Releases
1. Plain language
2. Cite peer-reviewed source
3. Provide uncertainty/confidence
4. Distinguish weather vs climate
5. Reference baseline period
6. Distinguish observed vs projected
7. Acknowledge uncertainty in projections
8. Use standard metrics
9. Define technical terms
10. Provide context (record period)
11. Multimedia (charts, maps, visualizations)
12. Subject matter expert review
13. Coordinated with NWS/NOS/etc as applicable
14. Public safety info if relevant
15. Adaptation/resilience resources
16. Distinguish from individual weather events

## Special Considerations
- Climate attribution science (event attribution)
- Distinguishing trend from variability
- Communicating probabilistic information
- Avoiding both alarmism and false reassurance
- Cultural sensitivity for affected communities
