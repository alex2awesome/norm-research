# WHO Outbreak Communication Guidelines

## Overview
Risk communication is one of eight core functions WHO Member States must fulfill under International Health Regulations (2005).

## WHO's Five Outbreak Communication Principles

### 1. Trust
Maintaining the public trust must be the over-arching goal of any outbreak communication. Without trust, public will not respond to public health advice.

### 2. Announce Early
Proactive early announcements - even with incomplete information - is essential. Late announcements damage credibility.

### 3. Transparency
Reveal what is known and unknown. Acknowledge uncertainty.

### 4. Public's Existing Knowledge
Account for what the public already understands and believes. Cultural appropriateness.

### 5. Planning
Communication should be planned in advance, not improvised during crisis.

## Press Release Standards During Outbreaks

### Required Elements
- Clear statement of facts known
- Acknowledgment of uncertainty
- What WHO/agency is doing
- What public should do (instructional info)
- Where to get more information
- When next update expected
- Spokesperson contact

### Format Standards
- Plain language (Grade 6-8 reading level)
- Translated for affected populations
- Accessible (screen reader friendly, alt text on images)
- Multilingual when needed
- Released in coordination with other agencies

### Risk Communication Best Practices
- "Know what we don't know" - acknowledge uncertainty
- Express empathy
- Give people something to do
- Be consistent across spokespersons
- Use multiple channels (release + briefing + social)
- Update regularly even with no new info

## Implicit Crisis/Health Press Release Rubric

### A WHO-quality outbreak press release:
1. Released early (not delayed for completeness)
2. States facts clearly
3. Acknowledges what's unknown
4. Provides actionable advice
5. Empathetic in tone
6. Provides update schedule
7. Identifies single trusted spokesperson
8. Coordinated with partner agencies
9. Translated for affected populations
10. Plain language
11. Anticipates and corrects misinformation
12. Provides context (compared to baseline risk)
