# Press Release Optimal Length

## The 400-Word Sweet Spot
The ideal press release length is 300-500 words, with 400 words as the sweet spot that balances comprehensive information with reader attention spans.

## Journalist Reading Behavior (Research-Based)
- **68% of journalists prefer releases under 400 words**
- **5-10 seconds**: Time journalists spend deciding whether to read a release
- **70% stop reading after 200 words**
- The opening (first 200 words) is critical

## Length by Type

### Short Releases (under 300 words)
- Quick updates
- Event reminders
- Simple announcements
- Personnel changes
- Single-fact news

### Standard Releases (300-500 words)
- Most common type
- Product launches
- New partnerships
- Award announcements
- Research findings

### Longer Releases (500-800 words)
- Complex topics
- Mergers/acquisitions
- Major leadership changes
- In-depth research

### Long-form (800-1000+ words)
- Data reports
- Comprehensive industry studies
- Annual reports
- Sustainability/ESG reports

## The 5-Second Rule
Journalists make initial decision in 5-10 seconds. The release must:
- Hook in headline
- Confirm value in subhead
- Deliver core news in first 200 words
- Make finishing the rest "worth it"

## Why Length Matters
A well-sized press release:
- Respects journalists' limited time
- Easier to absorb and act on
- Reduces editing burden
- Increases probability of full reading
- Increases probability of pickup

## Implicit Length Rubric
- Under 200 words: Too brief, lacks substance
- 200-300 words: Brief, fine for simple news
- **300-500 words: Optimal range**
- 500-800 words: Justified for complex stories
- 800-1000 words: Only for major announcements
- Over 1000 words: Probably bloated

## Quality Test
- Does the headline + subhead + first paragraph deliver the news?
- Could a journalist write a story from just first 200 words?
- Does every paragraph add information not in previous?
- Could you cut 20% without losing meaning?
