# Grunig & Hunt (1984) — Four Models of PR applied to press releases

Grunig & Hunt's typology classifies PR practice along two dimensions (one-way vs. two-way; asymmetric vs. symmetric). The model a release implicitly adopts predicts both its likely pickup pattern and its long-run credibility cost.

## The four models, as press-release postures
1. **Press agentry / publicity.** One-way; persuasion-only; accuracy not a priority. The release seeks attention by spectacle (a stunt, an exaggerated claim). High initial pickup risk on tabloid/entertainment beats; low credibility carry-over and burns the source's repeat-pickup capacity.
2. **Public information.** One-way; accurate but undirected. The release reports facts (financial result, appointment, study release) without audience tailoring or feedback. Picks up reliably on dedicated business/science wires; weak on consumer or political beats.
3. **Two-way asymmetric.** Two-way; persuasion-driven; audience research used to *change* the public, not the organisation. The release is pre-tested for which framing will most move readers. High pickup on issues the audience already cares about; moderate credibility risk.
4. **Two-way symmetric.** Two-way; mutual-adjustment posture; the release reflects audience input back into organisational behaviour. Pickup is steady across reputable beats; highest credibility carry-over and best long-run journalist relationships.

## Rubric criteria distilled
1. **Model-fit honesty.** Does the release acknowledge — even implicitly — which model it is operating under? Press-agentry releases that *pretend* to be public-information releases damage source trust.
2. **Symmetric-model markers.** Does the release show evidence of dialogue (cited public input, acknowledgment of opposing concerns, plain admission of uncertainty)?
3. **Avoidance of pure publicity stunts.** Releases positioned in pure press-agentry mode are likely to be flagged by quality outlets as PR-bait and demoted in routine channels.
4. **Public-information completeness.** For factual releases, are the key data, methodology, and source documents attached? Public-information mode requires substantive disclosure or it slides into press agentry.
5. **Asymmetric persuasion transparency.** If the release is two-way asymmetric (issue advocacy), does it disclose its sponsor's stake in the outcome?
6. **Symmetric-mode revisions.** Does the release reference adjustments the issuer has made in response to public input? This signals symmetric-model practice and earns pickup on civic / accountability beats.
7. **Excellence-theory marker (later extension).** Per Grunig's "excellence study," PR units that practice symmetric communication are correlated with organizational outcomes — does the release suggest the issuer integrates communication with management decisions?

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Reputable beats (national papers, NPR, BMJ-tier outlets) systematically prefer public-information and symmetric releases. Trade press and entertainment outlets pick up press-agentry releases more readily but at compounding credibility cost. The "two-way symmetric" posture is the predicted long-run optimum for both credibility and recurring pickup.
