# Jacobi, Welbers et al. — automated analysis of 4,455 Dutch press releases over 10 years

Large-scale automated content analysis: 4,455 press releases vs. all Dutch newspaper and news-agency content over 10 years. Two pickup metrics: (i) was the news article *initiated* by the release? (ii) what fraction of the release's literal content was *reproduced*?

## Headline empirical findings
1. **~10% of newspaper articles** in the sample were initiated by an organizational press release; **higher** for the news agency.
2. **NGO press releases** had higher reproduction rates at the news agency, tabloids, and free papers than at quality papers.
3. **Quality papers reproduce less verbatim**, but still rely on releases as agenda-initiators.
4. **Source type (NGO vs. corporation) matters for reproduction** — quality papers screen for institutional source type and edit accordingly.

## Jacobi et al. rubric criteria
1. **Wire-first targeting.** Reaching the national news agency (ANP in NL; AP / Reuters / PA equivalents) is more pickup-effective than direct-to-paper distribution.
2. **Tabloid-and-free-paper reproduction.** For high literal reproduction, target tabloid and free-distribution outlets — which use NGO content most readily.
3. **Quality-paper agenda hook.** Quality papers do not reproduce verbatim but they do *take agenda cues* from releases. Pickup at quality papers requires releasing the underlying *story* (with detail, data, named expert) that the paper can independently re-report.
4. **Source-type signaling.** NGO releases should make their *sponsorship and methodology* explicit; quality-paper editors apply heavier scrutiny to NGO claims and downgrade releases that hide either.
5. **Repeat-release follow-up.** Jacobi et al.'s temporal analysis showed pickup is highest when a release is part of an ongoing news wave rather than an isolated one-off.
6. **Multi-outlet differential framing.** A single release can be re-versioned for different outlet tiers: tabloid version (short, vivid), agency version (factual, embeddable), quality-paper version (data-rich, named experts willing to speak independently).
7. **Embeddability.** Releases that supply auto-embeddable content blocks (statistics tables, image carousel, pull quote) increase reproduction at agency and tabloid targets.
8. **Originality signal for quality papers.** Quality papers won't reproduce; they will pick up if the release offers them an exclusive interview, a first-look at an embargoed report, or a unique data set.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Jacobi et al.'s decade-scale data formalize what Lewis et al. observed in a UK two-week sample: pickup is dominantly a function of (a) channel routing to the news agency, (b) source-type signaling that matches the receiving outlet's standards, and (c) part-of-a-wave timing rather than isolated novelty.
