# ASBMB Guide to Scientific Press Release Writing

## Length
- 300-500 words ideal
- One page maximum
- Up to 800 words for complex stories

## Structure

### Headline
- Short and enticing
- Active voice
- Avoid scientific detail
- Example BAD: "Microvesicle-derived microRNAs are important for intercellular signaling in tumorigenesis"
- Example GOOD: "Cancer cells communicate through a new molecular messenger"

### Lead Paragraph
- 3-4 sentences
- Who: the authors
- What: the main finding
- When: journal and date of publication
- Why: relevance
- Active voice
- No jargon

### Body Content
- Contextualize the finding
- Describe how finding advances scientific knowledge
- NOT nitty-gritty experimental details
- Plain language explanations
- Explain implications

### Quotes
- Brief quotation from researcher
- Insightful, not promotional
- Authentic researcher voice

### Contact Information
- Corresponding author name
- Email
- Phone
- Institution
- PIO contact too

## Writing Style
- Lead with research impact or innovation
- Include relevant data and statistics
- Quote key researchers
- Tone: objective, not promotional
- Plain language for non-specialist

## Visual Elements
- Compelling visuals: photographs, infographics, diagrams
- Help explain complex concepts
- Clearly labeled
- Captions enhance understanding
- High resolution

## Implicit Quality Rubric for Scientific Press Releases

A scientific press release is high quality if:
1. Headline is plain English (not jargon)
2. Lead answers who/what/when/why in 3-4 sentences
3. Active voice
4. Body contextualizes (doesn't just summarize methods)
5. Has researcher quote
6. Has corresponding author contact
7. Includes journal citation with DOI
8. Has visual aids
9. 300-500 words typical
10. Translates technical terms
11. Discusses implications
12. Notes study scope/limitations
13. Avoids overhype
14. Distinguishes findings from speculation
