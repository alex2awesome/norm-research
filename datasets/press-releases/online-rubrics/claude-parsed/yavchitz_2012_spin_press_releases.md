# Yavchitz et al. (2012) — "Spin" in RCT press releases

Cohort study of 70 two-arm parallel-group RCT press releases on EurekAlert! (Dec 2009–March 2010) and their associated abstracts and news stories. Established the now-standard "spin" rubric for clinical-trial press releases.

## Definition (operational)
> "Spin: specific reporting strategies, intentional or unintentional, emphasizing the beneficial effect of the experimental treatment."

## Headline empirical findings
- **40%** of RCT *abstract* conclusions contained spin.
- **47%** of *press releases* contained spin.
- **51%** of *news stories* contained spin.
- The single strongest predictor of spin in a press release was *spin already present in the abstract*. PR offices propagate the abstract's framing.

## Yavchitz spin categories — release-level rubric
1. **Selective reporting of outcomes.** Does the release report secondary outcomes as if primary, or omit the actual primary outcome when null?
2. **Inappropriate primary-outcome interpretation.** Does the release describe a non-significant primary endpoint as a "trend" or as evidence of effect?
3. **Inappropriate extrapolation.** Does the release generalize the trial's narrow population, dose, or duration to broader clinical settings the trial does not test?
4. **Inappropriate causal language.** Does correlational or association-only data appear with cause-effect verbs ("reduces," "prevents," "improves")?
5. **Inappropriate recommendations.** Does the release advise clinicians or patients to change practice on the basis of the single trial reported?
6. **Beneficial-only emphasis.** Does the release foreground benefits while burying or omitting reported harms, side effects, or attrition?
7. **Mismatch with abstract conclusion.** Does the release's headline claim diverge from what the abstract conclusion actually states?

## Quality flags (positive criteria)
- Pre-registered primary outcome named explicitly.
- Effect sizes given in absolute terms (NNT, absolute risk reduction) rather than relative-risk only.
- Confidence intervals reported.
- Limitations paragraph from the published paper reproduced verbatim.
- Conflicts of interest of investigators disclosed.

## Pickup-worthiness *vs.* fidelity tension
Spin-laden releases get picked up at higher rates by lay outlets but at the cost of downstream correction stories, retraction risk, and journalist trust loss. A pickup-worthy *and* fidelity-preserving release uses the spin-free criteria above and earns repeat coverage from quality outlets (BMJ, NPR, NYT science desks) that explicitly screen for these markers.
