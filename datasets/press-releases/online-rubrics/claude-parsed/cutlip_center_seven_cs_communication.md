# Cutlip & Center — Seven Cs of Communication as a press-release rubric

*Effective Public Relations* (1952) was the first university-level PR textbook. Its "Seven Cs" are the field's most enduring rubric for any communication act, including press releases.

1. **Credibility.** Communication begins in a climate of belief. Does the release come from a source the receiving editor will already trust (track record of accuracy, named expert quote, institutional standing)? Does the *sender* visibly serve the *receiver* (editor / public) rather than only the issuer?
2. **Context.** The release must square with the realities of its environment. Does the announcement fit the current moment — news cycle, political climate, audience expectations? A release that lands without context is invisible.
3. **Content.** Messages must have meaning and relevance for the receiver. Does the release answer "why does this matter to *this* audience?" — i.e., is the relevance to the editor's readers explicit?
4. **Clarity.** Words must carry the same meaning for sender and receiver; complex ideas must be distilled. Is jargon translated? Are sentences short, concrete, and scannable in the inverted-pyramid lead?
5. **Continuity and Consistency.** Communication is an unending process; repetition with variation is required. Does the release fit a sustained narrative arc the issuer has been developing, or does it appear as a one-off out of nowhere?
6. **Channels.** Established channels the receiver already uses are most effective. Is the release distributed through the wire/embargo/email channel the targeted editor actually monitors, in the format that channel expects?
7. **Capability of audience.** Communication works best when it requires least effort from the receiver. Has the release pre-formatted quotes, images, statistics, and contact details so an editor can publish with minimal additional work? (This is also Gandy's "information-subsidy" test; see Gandy 1982.)

## Earlier seven-Cs variant noted by Cutlip & Center

An earlier mnemonic — Completeness, Conciseness, Consideration, Concreteness, Courtesy, Clearness, Correctness — is also given in the textbook tradition. The two lists overlap on clarity/correctness; the credibility/context/channels triad is the distinctively PR-theoretic addition.

## Use as a rubric
A high-pickup release scores positively on all seven; weakness on any single C — most commonly Context (mistimed) or Capability (asks editor to do too much work) — is a typical failure mode.
