# Gandy (1982) — the press release as an *information subsidy*

Gandy's central thesis: a press release is not just communication; it is a *subsidy* — an effort to lower a journalist's cost of producing a story by handing over information in a form pre-shaped for media use. Pickup-worthiness, on this view, is a function of how successfully the release lowers the marginal cost of running the story.

## Gandy's definition (operational)
> "An information subsidy is an effort by means of which information in a form suitable for processing by the mass media is provided to those media in a controlled manner at a subsidized cost."

## Subsidy-quality checklist for a press release
1. **Form-readiness.** Is the release in a form the newsroom can publish with minimal rewriting (inverted-pyramid lead, AP-style, clean quotes, embargoed for the relevant cycle)?
2. **Search-cost reduction.** Does the release supply the underlying study/PDF/dataset so a reporter does not have to hunt for it?
3. **Verification-cost reduction.** Are named sources reachable, with phone/email and time-zone availability windows? Are statistics already attributed to citable sources?
4. **Production-cost reduction.** Are images, graphics, B-roll, broadcast-quality audio, and pull quotes pre-supplied with usage rights cleared?
5. **Cognitive-cost reduction.** Is the angle, the "so what," and the contextualization done — so the reporter doesn't have to invent a frame?
6. **Reduction of editorial uncertainty.** Does the release flag what is *new*, what is *unique*, what makes the story *defensible* to an editor (e.g., peer-reviewed, embargoed, exclusive)?
7. **Targeted distribution.** Was the subsidy directed to *the right* journalist (beat match), at the *right* time (deadline-aligned)?

## Resource-asymmetry caution (Gandy's normative warning)
Subsidy effectiveness is partly a function of the *resources of the source* — well-funded subsidizers (corporations, large universities, government) crowd out resource-poor sources (small NGOs, marginal communities). For a press-release rubric this implies an additional *fairness* dimension: is the subsidy doing journalistic work, or is it manipulating coverage by sheer production-budget advantage?

## Pickup-worthiness implication
A release that fully subsidizes the cost of a story — facts, quotes, images, embargoed exclusive, contactable expert — will be picked up at much higher rates than one that merely announces. This is the operational definition of "pickup-worthy" in the Gandy tradition.
