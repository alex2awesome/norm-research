# BMJ Editorial - Academic Press Release Accountability

## The Problem (Per BMJ)
Exaggerated claims in health-related news stories—including advice not supported by evidence—often have their roots in academic press releases and the scientists who sanction them.

## BMJ Recommendations (Goldacre, Sumner et al.)

### Transparency Requirements
- Press releases should be linked to original papers
- Authors should be publicly named
- Press releases should be posted publicly
- Track exaggeration over time

### Accountability Mechanisms
1. **Public posting of all press releases** with author attribution
2. **Linking** of press releases to specific authors
3. **Tracking** of exaggeration patterns
4. **Public shame** as accountability

### Author Responsibility
- Researchers should review press releases before issue
- Authors should sign off on claims
- Authors named in press release attribution
- Reputation accountability

## BMJ Standards for Academic Communications

### What Press Releases Should Do
- Communicate data accurately
- Allow free and open access
- Be intelligible to specialists
- Be assessable for limitations
- Be usable by other specialists

### What Should Be Avoided
- Causal language for correlational studies
- Generalizing animal/cell research to humans
- Implicit advice not in paper
- "Breakthrough" without basis
- "Cure" language without basis
- Suppressing limitations
- Suppressing conflicts of interest

## Implicit Press Release Rubric (BMJ Editorial)

A press release passes BMJ standards if:
1. Author(s) named (not anonymous PIO)
2. Linked to original peer-reviewed paper
3. Publicly posted (not just sent to media)
4. Match strength of source paper claims
5. Causal vs correlational distinguished
6. Animal/cell vs human distinguished
7. Limitations included
8. Conflicts of interest disclosed
9. Funding sources disclosed
10. Effect sizes in absolute terms
11. Confidence intervals included
12. No "breakthrough" without basis
13. No "cure" without basis
14. Distinguishes hypothetical from observed
15. Researcher reviewed and approved

## Modern Implementation
Some institutions now:
- Post press releases on institutional sites with author attribution
- Use peer-review-style approval before issue
- Track media coverage for exaggeration
- Publish corrections when needed
- Maintain accountability dashboards

## Why This Matters for Pickup
Despite intuition, accuracy doesn't reduce pickup:
- Sumner found no evidence accurate releases get less coverage
- Cautious framing doesn't reduce news interest
- Quality framing builds long-term credibility
- Sources known for accuracy get more (and better) coverage
