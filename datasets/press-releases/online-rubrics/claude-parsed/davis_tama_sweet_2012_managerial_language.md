# Davis & Tama-Sweet (2012) — Asymmetric optimism in earnings releases vs. MD&A

Textual analysis of ~13,000 firm-quarters (1998–2003) comparing the *same* quarter's earnings press release with its MD&A. Finding: managers systematically use *more optimistic* and *less pessimistic* language in press releases than in the SEC-filed MD&A — a strategic disclosure differential.

## Headline empirical findings
1. **Press releases contain significantly less pessimistic language than the same quarter's MD&A.**
2. **Press releases contain significantly more optimistic language than the same quarter's MD&A.**
3. **Pessimistic language in MD&A predicts future negative firm performance** controlling for press-release pessimism.
4. **Strategic language differential is part of an integrated disclosure strategy** — not random variation.

## Davis & Tama-Sweet rubric criteria for earnings press releases
1. **Optimism–pessimism gap discipline.** Sophisticated analysts and algorithms now compute the *gap* between press-release tone and MD&A tone for the same quarter. A gap larger than industry norm flags strategic obfuscation. Pickup-worthy releases keep the gap within the band their analyst community considers normal.
2. **Material-information consistency.** Material adverse facts disclosed in the MD&A must also appear in the press release; selective omission triggers SEC and class-action attention.
3. **Forward-vs-backward language balance.** Earnings releases that lean heavily on forward-looking optimism while burying backward-looking pessimism are flagged by analysts; balance the two.
4. **Symmetric framing of beats and misses.** Both beats and misses should be presented in the same language register; treating misses with extra hedging (and beats with extra emphasis) is flagged.
5. **Pre-MD&A consistency check.** Before release, draft MD&A and press release together so the language differential is monitored.
6. **Industry-comparable optimism band.** Across an industry, peer firms' optimism gaps cluster; releases far from the cluster on the optimism side invite skepticism.
7. **Pre-emptive bad-news framing.** Negative news that *will* appear in MD&A is better introduced in the release than discovered by analysts in the filing.
8. **Litigation-risk awareness.** Davis-Tama-Sweet's data align with later SEC enforcement actions for misleading optimism in 8-K releases; aggressive optimism gaps create securities-law exposure.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Pickup at sophisticated financial outlets (FT, WSJ, Bloomberg, Reuters) and at quality analyst desks is partly a function of *credibility*, which Davis & Tama-Sweet operationalize via the optimism–pessimism gap. A pickup-worthy earnings release matches the language used in its corresponding MD&A within the industry-typical band; outlier releases are downgraded in subsequent coverage and produce correction stories.
