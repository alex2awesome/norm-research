# Data Journalism and Press Release Dependence

## Data Journalism Defined
Journalism based on filtering and analysis of large data sets for creating news stories. Blends journalism with data visualization, computer science, and statistics.

## Empirical Findings on Data Source Dependence

### UK Mainstream Media
- Institutional sources, particularly government agencies, are the most used data sources
- Press releases by research institutes are often referred to in stories on social issues and health
- Data journalism heavily depends on official releases

### Chicago Tribune
- 61% of all data-driven stories in print rely on data from public officials
- Demonstrates strong reliance on government/institutional press releases

## Implications for Press Releases with Data

### What Makes Data-Heavy Press Releases Pickup-Worthy
- Original/proprietary data (not rehashed)
- Statistically significant findings
- Visualizable in chart/map/infographic
- Tells a clear story with the data
- Methodologically transparent
- Comparison points (yoy, peer, baseline)
- Geographic granularity (when relevant)
- Sample size disclosed
- Confidence intervals provided

### Visualization Considerations
- Visualization no longer just complements a story - it IS the story
- Embeddable visualizations help pickup
- Static images for traditional media
- Interactive for digital
- Data file (CSV) for data journalists to remix

## Standard Data-Driven Press Release Components

### Required
- Headline with specific number
- Lead with most striking finding
- Methodology section
- Sample/scope
- Date range
- Comparison to baseline
- Author/researcher
- Funding source

### Multimedia
- Topline chart (one big finding)
- Detailed data tables
- Geographic visualization (where applicable)
- Methodology infographic
- Embeddable widgets

### Pre-Bundled Story Angles
- Top-line finding
- Demographic breakdowns
- Geographic comparisons
- Trend over time
- Surprising sub-findings

## Implicit Rubric for Data-Driven Press Releases
1. Has original/proprietary data
2. Lead finding has clear number
3. Methodology transparent
4. Sample/scope clearly stated
5. Confidence/uncertainty disclosed
6. Comparison points provided
7. Visualizations included (chart, map, infographic)
8. Data file available for re-analysis
9. Multiple story angles pre-identified
10. Geographic/demographic breakdowns
11. Author/expert available
12. Methodology document linked
13. Statistical significance noted
14. Limitations acknowledged
