# Inclusive Language Standards for Press Releases

## Foundation
- APA Inclusive Language Guide (Second Edition)
- C4DISC Guidelines on Inclusive Language and Images in Scholarly Communication
- Multiple style guide updates

## Core Principle
Language is a powerful tool that can bring people together or drive them apart. Words create psychologically safe, inclusive, respectful, and welcoming environments.

## Inclusive Language Definition
Vocabulary and descriptions that:
- Minimize bias
- Exhibit transparency
- Show cultural awareness
- When referencing people, identities, and locations

## Standard Areas

### Race and Ethnicity
- Use specific terms (Mexican American not Hispanic/Latino when accurate)
- Capitalize Black, White, Indigenous, etc.
- "People of color" preferred over "minorities"
- Note distinct ethnic identities

### Gender and Sexual Orientation
- Use chosen names and pronouns
- "LGBTQ+" or current accepted acronyms
- Avoid gendered defaults ("he/him")
- Use gender-neutral terms where appropriate
- Don't assume gender from name

### Disability
- Person-first language ("person with disability") OR identity-first ("disabled person")
- Follow individual/community preference
- Avoid pity language ("suffers from")
- Avoid euphemisms ("differently abled")

### Age
- Specific age ranges
- Avoid "elderly" (preferred: older adults)
- Avoid "youth" generically

### Mental Health and Substance Use
- Person-first ("person with substance use disorder")
- Avoid stigmatizing terms ("addict," "alcoholic")
- "Died by suicide" not "committed suicide"

### Socioeconomic Status
- Avoid "poor" as identity
- "Low-income" community
- Avoid stereotypes
- Specific situations rather than labels

## Image and Visual Inclusivity
- Diverse representation in photos
- Avoid stereotypes in stock images
- Caption people specifically
- Show people in active roles
- Include accessibility (people with disabilities visible)

## Implicit Quality Rubric for Inclusive Press Releases

A press release passes inclusive language standards if:
1. Specific identities used (not generic groupings)
2. Self-identification respected
3. Person-first or identity-first per community preference
4. Capitalization conventions current
5. Gender-neutral where appropriate
6. Pronouns specified per individuals
7. Avoid stigmatizing language (mental health, substance use)
8. Specific age ranges (not "elderly")
9. Avoid "minorities" as default
10. Multiple voices represented
11. Diverse imagery
12. Captions appropriate
13. Accessibility considerations
14. No stereotypes
15. Cultural sensitivity reviewed

## Special Considerations
- Update terminology as language evolves
- Consult community preferences
- Native speaker review
- Sensitivity readers for sensitive topics
- Style guide consistency
- Beyond mere "political correctness" - represents accuracy

## Why This Matters for Pickup
- Major news outlets have inclusive language standards
- Press releases violating standards may be edited heavily or rejected
- Sources flagged for offensive language lose credibility
- Modern audiences expect inclusive framing
