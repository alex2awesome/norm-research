# Nonprofit / NGO Press Release Writing Standards

## Purpose
A non-profit press release seeks to:
- Get people talking about philanthropic initiatives
- Generate publicity for fundraising, disaster management, aid
- Raise awareness about mission and programs
- Attract donors and volunteers
- Build credibility within community

## Key Components

### Headline
- Under 100 characters
- Grab attention while showing impact
- Focus on difference made (not just news)
- Active voice
- Show stakes

### Lead Paragraph
- Five Ws (who, what, when, where, why)
- WHY reader should care
- Mission-connected
- Beneficiaries front and center

### Body
- Goals communicated clearly
- Why story is newsworthy
- Direct quotes from experts
- Statistics on impact
- Success stories
- Beneficiary testimonials (with consent)
- Program details

### Quotes
- Executive director / founder
- Beneficiary (with consent)
- Program partner
- Major donor (where appropriate)

## Common Topics for Nonprofit Releases
- Grant funding received
- Awards received
- New executive hire
- Major donor announcement
- Rebranding
- Program launches
- Annual reports
- Fundraising events
- Partnership announcements
- Crisis response activities

## Charity Event Releases
- Drive attendance and donations
- Create buzz
- Explain why event matters
- Specific date, time, location
- Cost and registration info
- Notable attendees/speakers
- Direct beneficiaries

## Special Considerations for Nonprofits

### Beneficiary Privacy
- Get consent for stories/photos
- Use first names only when needed
- Anonymize when appropriate
- Avoid exploitation
- Show dignity and agency

### Donor Recognition
- Major donors named (with permission)
- Levels of giving
- Anonymous donors respected
- Match challenges promoted

### Mission Connection
- Every release ties to mission
- Statistics on cause
- Long-term goals referenced
- Impact metrics

## Implicit Quality Rubric for Nonprofit Releases
1. Mission-aligned
2. Beneficiary-focused (not org-focused)
3. Specific impact (numbers, stories)
4. Donor recognition appropriate
5. Beneficiary consent obtained
6. Program partner attribution
7. Funding sources transparent
8. Volunteer/donor call-to-action
9. Tax ID / 501(c)(3) noted
10. Annual reports linked
11. Multimedia (beneficiaries with consent)
12. Inclusive language
13. Avoids savior narrative
14. Honest about challenges
15. Provides ways to help
