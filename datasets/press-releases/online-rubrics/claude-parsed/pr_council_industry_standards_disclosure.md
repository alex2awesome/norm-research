# PR Council - Industry Standards for Press Releases

## About PR Council
Trade association representing 150+ premier global, mid-size, regional and specialty PR firms. Sets industry standards and strengthens recognition for PR firms.

## Code of Ethics and Principles
The PR Council Code of Ethics specifically addresses:
- Transparency
- Disclosure
- Honesty
- Client confidentiality
- Conflicts of interest
- Avoiding misleading practices
- Avoiding astroturfing

## Industry Frameworks (PR Council aligned)
- PRSA Code of Ethics
- IPRA Code
- Global Alliance principles
- PR Council guidelines

## Disclosure Requirements

### Sponsor Identification
- Clearly identify the source organization
- Disclose paid relationships
- Disclose client relationships
- Disclose conflicts of interest

### Material Information
- Don't suppress unfavorable information
- Acknowledge limitations
- Provide complete context
- Don't selectively disclose

### Source Attribution
- Real people quoted (not fictional)
- Verified credentials
- Authentic experts
- No paid testimonials disguised as independent

## AI and Modern Standards

### AI Use Disclosure (emerging)
- Disclose AI-generated content where required
- Verify AI-generated facts
- Don't use AI to fabricate quotes
- Don't use AI for sensitive content without review

### Authentic Multimedia
- Real photos (not AI-generated unless disclosed)
- Real testimonials
- Real case studies
- Disclose when illustrations are AI-created

## Implicit Press Release Ethical Rubric (PR Council)

A press release that meets PR Council ethics standards:
1. Source organization clearly named
2. Sponsorship/payment relationships disclosed
3. Conflicts of interest revealed
4. Real people quoted
5. Real photos used
6. AI use disclosed where required
7. No astroturfing
8. No fake testimonials
9. No suppression of material info
10. No selective disclosure
11. Compliant with industry codes
12. Compliant with regulations
13. Verifiable claims
14. Legal review for material content
15. Proper credentials of cited experts

## What Counts as Astroturfing (Unethical)
- Fake grassroots campaigns
- Disguised lobbying
- Hidden corporate sponsorship of "citizen" groups
- Paid commenters posing as customers
- Hidden advertorial
- Undisclosed paid endorsements

## Why This Matters for Press Release Quality
- Long-term credibility
- Avoiding legal risk
- Maintaining journalist trust
- Brand reputation
- Regulatory compliance
- Industry reputation
