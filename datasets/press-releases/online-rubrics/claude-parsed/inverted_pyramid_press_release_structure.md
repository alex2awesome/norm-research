# Inverted Pyramid Press Release Structure

## What is the Inverted Pyramid?

The inverted pyramid is a metaphor used by journalists to illustrate how information should be prioritized and structured in prose. The inverted (upside-down) pyramid is a triangle pointing down: the widest part at the top represents the most substantial, interesting, and important information.

## Structure and Organization

News stories are organized in descending order of importance. The structure has three main sections:

- **The Lead (Top)**: The most newsworthy details, answering who, what, where, when, why, and how. The 5Ws should fit in the first paragraph or the first 30 words.
- **The Details (Middle)**: Supporting facts, background, and context.
- **The Extras (Bottom)**: Quotes, company background, and additional info.

## Historical Origins

The style was first established in the early 1800s when telegraph use encouraged journalists to communicate the most essential facts first: telegraphs were expensive to use and faulty connections were common, so the lead had to be transmitted before the line cut out.

## Key Benefits

### For Press Releases
The structure is journalist-friendly, aligning with how reporters are trained to write. Press releases that follow the inverted pyramid are more likely to be picked up because they require less editing.

### For Readers and Editing
Readers can leave the story at any point and still understand it. Information less vital comes later, where it is easier to edit out for space ("cutting from the bottom").

### For Modern Media
Search engines and AI tools prioritize content with clear information hierarchies. Placing keywords and critical facts early helps releases get indexed and surfaced in search.

## Implicit Rubric

A good press release in inverted pyramid form:
- Front-loads the most important information in the first 30 words
- Answers all 5Ws + H in the first paragraph
- Orders subsequent paragraphs in descending importance
- Can be cut from the bottom without losing essential meaning
- Quotes and background details come last
