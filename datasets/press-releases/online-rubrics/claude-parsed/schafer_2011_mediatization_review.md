# Schäfer (2011) — Mediatization frame for science press releases

Schäfer's review consolidates two competing models in science communication — the public-understanding model and the *mediatization* model — and synthesizes empirical findings on what makes science news. The mediatization model has direct release-rubric implications.

## Schäfer's core diagnostic
**Mediatization** means science coverage increasingly follows *journalistic* news values rather than scientific ones: conflict, novelty, personalization, narrative arc. A science press release that *doesn't* recognize this pattern under-performs even when scientifically excellent.

## Schäfer rubric criteria
1. **Conflict frame.** Does the release surface a tension or controversy (within the field, between fields, with public expectation, with regulation) the journalist can build a story around? Pure consensus releases are picked up less.
2. **Novelty signal.** Does the release explicitly mark *what is new* relative to prior science? "First study to" / "Largest cohort to date" / "First evidence in humans" — not vague claims of importance.
3. **Personalization hook.** Does the release name an individual researcher (with quotable role) or an affected individual (patient, community member)? Mediatized coverage requires personalization.
4. **Narrative arc.** Does the release fit a recognizable story shape (puzzle → discovery; problem → solution; expectation → surprise)? Releases without an arc are harder to publish.
5. **Visibility of controversy.** Does the release acknowledge dissenting views and the scientific debate the finding enters? This satisfies the journalist's balance norm and increases pickup.
6. **Visual-friendly framing.** Mediatized science requires visuals; releases supply images of researchers, sample images, animations, or infographics with clear usage rights.
7. **Disciplinary salience differentiation.** Schäfer's data show certain disciplines (medicine, climate, neuroscience) get systematically more coverage than others (mathematics, theoretical physics). Releases in low-coverage fields need more *novelty* and *narrative* work to compete.
8. **Issue-attention cycle awareness.** Pickup is highest when the release lands on a topic the news cycle is already attending to; releases that anticipate the issue-attention curve outperform.
9. **Avoidance of "deficit-model" framing.** Releases that lecture the public ("the public must understand that...") underperform compared to releases that meet the public on its terms.
10. **Trust-building over single-event impact.** Schäfer's review supports treating each release as a node in a long trust relationship; one-off blockbusters at the expense of accuracy reduce future pickup.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Schäfer's mediatization frame predicts that science releases optimized for *journalistic* news values (conflict, novelty, personalization, narrative) outperform releases optimized for *scientific* importance metrics (citation impact, methodological rigor). The Schäfer-aware release combines both — letting scientific quality reach the journalist via the journalistic-value envelope.
