# Autzen - Press Releases: The New Trend in Science Communication

## Author
Charlotte Autzen, Senior Executive Adviser in Communication at the University of Copenhagen, also conducted PhD research at University of Southern Denmark on science press release strategies.

## Key Empirical Findings
- More than 20,000 press releases posted on EurekAlert! in 2013
- Half of all 2013 EurekAlert! press releases came from universities
- Universities have become major science PR producers
- Enhanced inter-institutional competition has driven PR professionalization

## Strategies Behind Research Press Releases

### Drivers of University Press Release Production
1. Competition for funding
2. Competition for students
3. Visibility for rankings
4. Stakeholder accountability
5. Researcher career incentives
6. Institutional branding

### Common University PR Strategies
- Coordinate with journal embargoes
- Multi-language versions for international media
- Researcher media training
- Pre-arranged interview availability
- Multimedia packages
- Strategic timing around news cycles

## Quality Issues Identified
- PR pressures incentivize hyping findings
- Volume drives selection (only catchy survives)
- "Sexy" topics over fundamental research
- Health/applied over basic
- Press release writers may not have scientific training

## Implicit Press Release Rubric for University Science PR

### A successful (pickup-likely) university science press release:
1. Times release with journal publication
2. Has clear public/applied relevance angle
3. Comes from prestigious institution
4. Has accessible language
5. Has multimedia (images, video, b-roll)
6. Offers researcher for interview
7. Has compelling narrative angle
8. Translates jargon
9. Is short and scannable
10. Has news hook beyond academic interest

### Quality guardrails (anti-hype):
- Press release writer should have scientific competence
- Researcher should review before release
- Caveats and limitations preserved from paper
- Conservative claims tied to actual findings
- Honest about study design (animal, in vitro, observational)
