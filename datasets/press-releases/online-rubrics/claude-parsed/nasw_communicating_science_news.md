# NASW - Communicating Science News Guidelines

## About NASW
The National Association of Science Writers is the oldest and largest professional association for science writers, founded in 1934. Mission: "to improve the craft of science journalism and to promote good science reportage."

## NASW Position on Science Press Releases

### Core Principles
- Funded researchers have an obligation to share their results with the public
- Press releases should facilitate, not replace, good journalism
- Access to scientists and information should be maximized
- Press credentials should be respected

### What NASW Looks for in Science Press Releases

#### Accuracy
- Match claims to evidence in source paper
- Distinguish observed from inferred
- Note study limitations
- Include effect sizes (absolute, not just relative)

#### Accessibility
- Plain language for general audiences
- Define technical terms
- Provide context for results
- Explain why findings matter

#### Sourcing
- Name researchers and affiliations
- Provide journal citation
- Link to peer-reviewed paper
- Disclose funding

#### Multimedia
- High-resolution images cleared for use
- Captions with context
- Video where appropriate
- Diagrams to explain concepts

#### Access
- Researcher available for interviews
- Press kit with background
- Embargo with reasonable lead time
- Direct contact info

## Standards for Science Writers Working WITH Press Releases
- Verify claims against original paper
- Seek independent expert comment
- Read methods, not just abstract
- Disclose if story originated from press release
- Don't rewrite verbatim ("churnalism")

## Implicit Press Release Rubric (NASW perspective)
A press release succeeds with science writers if:
- It's accurate (verifiable against paper)
- It's accessible (plain language)
- It's complete (limitations included)
- It enables independent reporting (offers source access)
- It doesn't oversell (no breakthrough/cure language without basis)
- It provides multimedia
- It comes from credible source
- It respects embargo norms
