# Press Release Tone and Voice Standards

## The Core Principle
A journalistic tone that is neutral, factual, and news-like consistently outperforms an achievement tone that sounds overly excited or promotional.

## News/Factual Approach
A press release should have a professional, journalistic tone that sounds like news rather than advertising:
- Neutral
- Factual language
- Objective presentation
- Third person
- Active voice
- No editorializing

## Press Release vs. News Release Distinction
- **Press release**: Often dual purpose (inform + promote), more persuasive tone
- **News release**: Purely informational, journalistic, neutral
- Best practice: Write press releases like news releases

## Examples

### Bad Tone (overly promotional)
"We are absolutely thrilled to announce the launch of our game-changing, revolutionary SmartWidget Pro! This incredible product will completely transform how businesses operate..."

### Good Tone (journalistic)
"TechCorp today announced the launch of SmartWidget Pro, a business automation tool designed to reduce manual data entry by streamlining workflow processes."

## Best Practices
- Write in third person
- Use active voice
- Avoid jargon and buzzwords
- Stay factual
- Match tone to type of announcement
- Let facts do the persuading

## Words to Avoid (Promotional Red Flags)
- Thrilled, excited, delighted, proud
- Game-changing, revolutionary, groundbreaking, unprecedented
- World-class, leading, premier, best-in-class
- Innovative, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art
- Robust, scalable, seamless
- Synergy, paradigm shift, leverage
- "Solution" (especially with adjectives)
- Superlatives without proof

## Implicit Tone Rubric

A press release passes the journalistic tone test if:
- Could be published as a news article with minimal edits
- No first person ("we")
- No marketing-speak
- No subjective evaluative claims (unsupported)
- Quotes are conversational, not corporate
- Neutral framing of news event
- Subject of sentences = the news, not the company
- Verbs are reportorial (announces, reports, releases) not promotional (transforms, revolutionizes, disrupts)
