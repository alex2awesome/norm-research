# HealthNewsReview.org - 10 Criteria for Rating Health News Releases

Developed by Gary Schwitzer and team. Each criterion scored "Satisfactory," "Unsatisfactory," or "Not Applicable." Total scores expressed as proportions (e.g., 7/10), with star rating from 0-5.

## The 10 Criteria (modified for press releases)

1. **Cost** - Does the news release adequately discuss the costs of the intervention?

2. **Benefits** - Does the news release adequately quantify the benefits of the treatment/test/product/procedure (using absolute numbers, not just relative numbers)?

3. **Harms** - Does the news release adequately explain/quantify the harms of the intervention?

4. **Quality of Evidence** - Does the news release seem to grasp the quality of the evidence (study design, sample size, limitations)?

5. **Disease-mongering** - Does the news release commit disease-mongering (exaggerating disease prevalence/severity to expand market for treatment)?

6. **Funding sources / Conflicts of interest** - Does the news release identify funding sources & disclose conflicts of interest? (Modified for press releases - news stories ask about independent sources used.)

7. **Comparison with existing alternatives** - Does the news release compare the new approach with existing alternatives?

8. **Availability** - Does the news release establish the availability of the product/procedure?

9. **Novelty** - Does the news release establish the true novelty of the approach?

10. **Sensational language / Press release reliance** - For news release reviews: Does the news release include unjustifiable, sensational language, including in the quotes of researchers? (For news stories: Does the story rely solely on a press release?)

## Application
HealthNewsReview applied this 10-criteria rubric to over 2,300 journalism stories since 2006 and approximately 300 PR news releases.

## Implicit Standards a Good Press Release Should Meet
- Quantify benefits in absolute terms
- Disclose costs clearly
- Acknowledge potential harms
- Describe study design and limitations
- Avoid disease-mongering
- Disclose funding sources and conflicts of interest
- Place new findings in context of existing alternatives
- State availability honestly
- Establish (don't oversell) novelty
- Avoid sensational language and overhyped quotes
