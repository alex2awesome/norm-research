# Wilcox & Cameron — News-release chapter pedagogy as a release rubric

Wilcox & Cameron's *Public Relations: Strategies and Tactics* is the most widely-adopted PR textbook in the US and identifies news releases as "the most common tactic used in Public Relations." Chapter 14 codifies the textbook canon for release writing.

## Wilcox-Cameron rubric criteria

### Format conventions
1. **Letterhead identification.** Issuer's logo, address, and contact info on the first page.
2. **Release timing line.** "FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE" or specific embargo date/time at top.
3. **Contact block.** Name, phone, email, time-zone, after-hours availability of media-relations officer.
4. **Headline.** Subject-verb-object; concrete; under 12 words; no all-caps; no marketing adjectives.
5. **Subhead (optional).** One-line elaboration that adds dimension the headline lacks.
6. **Dateline.** "CITY, State (or country) — Day Month Year —" before the lead.
7. **Lead.** Single sentence; under 35 words; answers who/what/when/where/why.
8. **Inverted-pyramid body.** Most important first; next-most second; supporting fourth-fifth; boilerplate at the end.
9. **Quote rule.** At least one direct quote, preferably from a senior named source with title; second quote from independent source where possible.
10. **Boilerplate.** Standardized "About [issuer]" paragraph at the end, ≤100 words.
11. **End mark.** "###" or "—END—" or "—30—" centered after final paragraph.

### Content criteria
12. **News-value alignment.** Each release should be evaluable against classical news values (timeliness, prominence, proximity, conflict, human interest, novelty).
13. **Local-angle option.** For nationally-distributed releases, supply a local-angle version when distributing to community papers.
14. **Single key idea.** One central message; secondary points support, not compete.
15. **Active voice and short sentences.** Average sentence length under 25 words.
16. **Editorial-readability.** Should be readable as journalism, not advertising; no superlatives unless verifiable.
17. **Distinctive opening sentence.** Avoids generic openings ("Today, [issuer] announced...") in favor of substantive lead.

### Distribution criteria
18. **Targeted distribution.** Wilcox-Cameron recommend targeted lists over blast distribution; pickup correlates with relevance to the receiving journalist's beat.
19. **Multi-format availability.** PDF for archival, plain-text for wire intake, HTML for online distribution, social-media snippet variants.
20. **Multimedia bundle.** Photo (with caption), B-roll where applicable, infographic, fact-sheet attachment.

### Ethical / legal criteria
21. **PRSA Code compliance.** Follows PRSA principles (truth, accuracy, disclosure of source, no deception).
22. **Plagiarism check.** Original wording, no uncredited reuse from other organizations.
23. **Trademark / copyright clearance.** All third-party material properly licensed.
24. **Embargo respect.** Embargoed releases follow industry norms (Ingelfinger rule, Nature/Science embargo policy).

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Wilcox-Cameron's textbook canon defines what newsroom intake desks expect. Releases that violate the format (no dateline, no end mark, no contact block) are demoted in routine processing; releases that comply with the canon and add news-value content earn baseline pickup. The textbook also emphasizes that *pickup-worthiness is not just textual* — the targeted-distribution and multi-format-availability criteria are equally load-bearing.
