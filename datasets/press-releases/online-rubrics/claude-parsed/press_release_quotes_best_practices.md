# Press Release Quote Best Practices

## Length and Structure
- Aim for one sentence, at most two
- Average length of quotes in major publications (NYT) is 19-20 words
- Maximum three sentences from any one speaker

## What to Avoid
- "We're pleased to announce..." or "happy to support" statements
- Generic statements devoid of emotion
- Industry jargon and clichés (paradigm shift, pivot, synergy)
- Adjectives that don't add information

## Key Best Practices

### Add Value and Insight
A quote likely to be picked up should be:
- Concise
- Compelling
- Fact-based
- Make a point not made elsewhere in the release

### Focus on Impact
- Focus less on yourself, more on who/what you're impacting
- Skip quotes highlighting leadership's excitement
- Provide perspective the reporter could not generate themselves

### Sound Natural
- Speak the language of the audience
- Conversational tone
- Avoid machine-generated feel
- Should sound like the person actually talks

### Quote Placement
- CEO or key leader should be quoted
- Place at the third or fourth paragraph
- Not the second paragraph (lead should be facts, not quotes)

### Proper Attribution
- After the quote: speaker's full name, title, organization
- Use "said" (AP style)
- Format: "Quote here," said John Smith, CEO of Acme Corp.

## Two-Person Quotes
- If using two quotes, they should make different points
- Often: one from internal leader (CEO), one from external party (customer, partner, expert)
- External quote adds credibility and outside perspective

## Implicit Rubric for Good Press Release Quotes
- Says something that wouldn't be said in marketing copy
- Provides insight only that person could provide
- Has personality
- Could conceivably be quoted in a news article
- Doesn't sound like it was "approved by legal"
