# DOE Communications Style Guide for Press Releases

## Style Hierarchy
1. EERE Style Guide / CMEI Style Guide (DOE-specific)
2. Associated Press Stylebook
3. Merriam-Webster's Dictionary
4. Chicago Manual of Style

## Press Release Standards

### Plain Language Requirements (Plain Language Act of 2010)
- Clear language federal agencies must use
- Active voice
- Avoid acronyms where possible
- Define necessary acronyms on first use
- Reading level: Grade 8 or below
- Short sentences

### Active Voice
- "DOE awarded $X to..." (not "X was awarded by DOE")
- Clear subject in every sentence
- Direct attribution

### DOE-Specific Style
- "Department of Energy" first reference
- "DOE" or "the department" subsequent
- Full names of programs first reference
- Standard acronyms (EIA, NREL, NETL, etc.)
- National laboratory names: full name first

### Press Release Required Elements
- Plain language summary (lead)
- Specific funding amounts
- Cited research papers (if applicable)
- Lab/researcher attribution
- Public benefit articulated
- Section 508 accessibility
- ADA-compliant (alt text, etc.)

## Energy Press Release Specifics

### Required for Funding Announcements
- Specific dollar amount
- Funding source (program, grant)
- Recipient (named)
- Project description
- Expected outcomes
- Performance period
- Total project value (incl. cost share)
- Impact metrics

### Required for Research Releases
- Lab affiliation
- Funding source
- Cite peer-reviewed paper (if published)
- Plain language explanation
- Energy/economic implications
- Photographs of researchers/equipment
- Researcher availability

### Required for Policy/Program Releases
- Specific policy details
- Effective dates
- Affected populations
- Stakeholder benefits
- Implementation plan
- Comment periods (if applicable)

## Implicit Quality Rubric for DOE Releases
1. Plain language (Grade 8)
2. Active voice
3. Specific amounts/dates
4. AP style (with DOE exceptions)
5. National lab/program names correct
6. Acknowledgment language present
7. Section 508 accessible
8. Required disclaimers
9. Cited peer-reviewed source (research)
10. Public benefit clear
11. Authorized spokespersons quoted
12. Coordinated with affected stakeholders
13. Timing aligns with policy/program launch
14. Multimedia (photos of facilities, researchers)
15. Federal communications policy compliant
