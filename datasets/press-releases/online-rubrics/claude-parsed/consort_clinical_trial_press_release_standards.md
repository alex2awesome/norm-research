# CONSORT Standards for Clinical Trial Press Releases

## CONSORT Overview
The Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) was first published in 1996, with major updates in 2001, 2010, and 2025. The CONSORT 2025 statement consists of a 30-item checklist plus a participant flow diagram.

## Adoption
Over 600 journals endorse CONSORT including BMJ, JAMA, Lancet, NEJM, World Association of Medical Editors, ICMJE.

## Six Sections of CONSORT
1. Title and Abstract
2. Introduction
3. Methods
4. Results
5. Discussion
6. Other Information

## Key Items Press Releases About RCTs Should Cover

### From Title/Abstract
- Identify as randomized trial
- Structured summary

### From Methods
- Trial design (parallel, factorial, etc.)
- Eligibility criteria
- Interventions (with sufficient detail)
- Outcomes (primary and secondary)
- Sample size
- Randomization method
- Blinding

### From Results
- Participant flow (CONSORT diagram)
- Recruitment dates
- Baseline data
- Numbers analyzed
- Outcomes and estimation
- Absolute and relative effect sizes
- Confidence intervals
- Harms

### From Discussion
- Limitations
- Generalizability
- Interpretation in context of other evidence

### Other Information
- Trial registration number
- Protocol availability
- Funding sources

## Implicit Press Release Rubric for Clinical Trial Releases

A CONSORT-compliant press release should:
1. Identify the trial as randomized
2. Specify intervention and comparator
3. Report primary outcome with effect size
4. Include absolute (not just relative) numbers
5. Report confidence intervals
6. Note adverse events / harms
7. Acknowledge limitations
8. Specify generalizability
9. Mention trial registration ID
10. Disclose funding
11. Distinguish from observational findings
12. Avoid causal overreach
13. Place findings in context of prior evidence
14. Include link to full trial publication

## Common Violations
- Reporting only relative risk reduction (not absolute)
- Omitting harms
- Generalizing beyond study population
- No mention of limitations
- No funding disclosure
- Treatment effects without confidence intervals
- "Cure" language without basis
- Single-trial findings as definitive
