# Bad Pitch Blog: Critiques From Journalists' Inbox

## The Bad Pitch Hall of Fame

### The Spam Pitch
"Hi [FIRST NAME], I'd like to introduce you to our amazing client..."
*Critique: Template not personalized. Auto-delete.*

### The Wrong Beat Pitch
Pitching consumer product to enterprise reporter
*Critique: Shows no research. Damages future relationship.*

### The Misleading Subject Line
Subject: "EXCLUSIVE: Major Announcement"
Body: Routine product update
*Critique: Inflated language. Reporter trust eroded.*

### The Buzzword Soup
"Our revolutionary, game-changing, AI-powered, blockchain-enabled solution disrupts the paradigm..."
*Critique: All buzzwords, no substance. Auto-filter to spam.*

### The PR-Speak Quote
"'We are excited to bring this innovative solution to market,' said the CEO."
*Critique: Says nothing. Quote should add information.*

### The Anonymous Source
"According to our research..."
*Critique: No methodology, no source. Unverifiable.*

### The 1,000-Word Email
[Long pitch with multiple paragraphs of company background]
*Critique: Reporter scans for news, doesn't find it. Delete.*

### The Aggressive Follow-Up
"Just following up on the email I sent yesterday. And the one before. And..."
*Critique: Annoying. Damages relationship.*

## What Each Critique Teaches

### Personalization Matters
- Use real name, correctly spelled
- Reference specific recent work
- Tailor angle to their beat

### Honesty Matters
- Don't inflate "exclusive" or "breaking"
- Don't claim "first" without proof
- Don't say "revolutionary" without evidence

### Substance Matters
- Real news, not promotional
- Verifiable claims
- Named sources with credentials
- Specific numbers, not vague claims

### Brevity Matters
- Subject line under 60 characters
- Body under 200 words
- One news item per pitch
- Single clear ask

## Quality Rubric (Bad Pitch Blog)
- Personalized to journalist
- Honest framing throughout
- Substantive news only
- Verifiable claims
- Concise (under 200 words)
- Single clear ask
- No aggressive follow-up
