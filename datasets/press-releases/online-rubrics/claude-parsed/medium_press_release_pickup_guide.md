# Medium: How to Write a Press Release That Gets Picked Up

## The Brutal Truth Up Front
- 92% of press releases get ignored
- Journalists receive 200+ pitches per day
- Average time to decide whether to read: 4 seconds
- "Most press releases are written for the company, not for the journalist"

## The Pickup-Worthy Test
Before writing, ask three questions:
1. Would my mom find this interesting at dinner?
2. Does this affect more than just our customers?
3. If I were a reporter on deadline, would I bother my editor about this?

If "no" to all three — kill it. Don't write the release.

## Headline Rules That Drive Pickup
- Front-load the news, not the brand name
- Include numbers when possible ("Raises $20M", "3X faster")
- Avoid "announces", "unveils", "is proud to" — these are red flags
- 80 characters or less
- Pass the "subject line test": would you open this email?

## The Lede Paragraph Formula
- Sentence 1: The single most newsworthy fact
- Sentence 2: Why it matters to the reader (not your customers — the reader)
- Sentence 3: Concrete detail (number, date, person)
- Total: under 50 words

## What Journalists Actually Want
- An angle they can run with, not boilerplate they have to rewrite
- Quotes that say something — not "we are excited to"
- One spokesperson available NOW, not "by appointment"
- Data, screenshots, photos already attached
- Exclusivity is gold; embargo is silver

## Red Flags That Kill Pickup
- "Leading provider of..." in the boilerplate
- "Game-changing", "revolutionary", "disruptive" in headline
- More than 600 words
- Quote from CEO that contains the company name
- No phone number
- PDF-only attachments
- Dateline more than 24 hours old

## The Distribution Hierarchy
1. Direct, personalized email to 5-10 reporters who cover your beat
2. Wire service (only if newsworthy enough to warrant)
3. Newsroom on company site
4. Social syndication
5. Industry-specific aggregators

## Implicit Quality Rubric
- Real news, not vanity announcement
- Headline tested against "subject line test"
- Lede under 50 words with concrete facts
- Quotes that humans actually said
- Spokesperson genuinely available
- Multimedia attached not promised
- Personalized to journalist beat
