# Galtung and Ruge News Values (1965)

## Overview
In 1965, Norwegian social scientists Johan Galtung and Mari Holmboe Ruge made the first in-depth study of news values. Their twelve factors define newsworthiness.

## The Three Hypotheses
1. **Additivity**: The more factors an event satisfies, the higher the probability it becomes news
2. **Complementarity**: The factors will tend to exclude each other
3. **Exclusion**: Events satisfying none or few factors will not become news

## The Twelve News Factors

### Impartial Factors (apply to any culture)
1. **Frequency** - Events that happen quickly fit news cycle
2. **Threshold** - Larger events more likely covered
3. **Unambiguity** - Clear, simple events preferred
4. **Meaningfulness** - Cultural relevance to audience
5. **Consonance** - Fits expectations of audience
6. **Unexpectedness** - Surprising events preferred
7. **Continuity** - Once a story is news, it stays news
8. **Composition** - Mix of news in publication

### Cultural Factors (Western press)
9. **Reference to elite nations**
10. **Reference to elite people**
11. **Personification** - Stories with individual focus
12. **Negativity** - Bad news more newsworthy than good

## Application to Press Releases

### Press releases more likely to be picked up if:
- They reference elite institutions/people
- They have a clear individual or company at center
- They have negativity, conflict, or risk
- They are unexpected or counter-intuitive
- They fit cultural narratives
- They have continuing storylines (sequels)
- They have clarity (no ambiguity)
- They cross threshold (big enough to matter)

## Implicit Press Release Rubric Based on Galtung-Ruge
A press release is more newsworthy if it:
- Concerns a prominent person/org
- Has clear, simple narrative (not ambiguous)
- Has surprise/unexpected element
- Has cultural relevance
- Has emotional pull (negativity, human story)
- Crosses size/scale threshold
- Has scheduled follow-up potential
