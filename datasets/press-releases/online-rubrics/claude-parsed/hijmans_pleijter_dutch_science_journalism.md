# Hijmans, Pleijter & Wester - Dutch Science Journalism (2003)

## Methodology
- Content analysis of Dutch newspapers
- Supplemented with interviews with 10 news editors and science journalists
- 96 newspaper editions analyzed
- 624 reports about scientific research found

## Key Findings

### What Coverage Looks Like
- Quality papers and science sections did NOT supply more or better information on methodological aspects
- Reporting on scientific research means avoidance of complex information, even in science sections
- Heavy reliance on press releases as source

### How Journalists Evaluate Press Releases
From interview data:
- News value ranks higher than scientific significance
- Sources matter (recognized journals, prestigious institutions)
- Practical/local relevance prioritized
- Ease of translation to lay language matters
- Time pressure means complex releases get skipped

## Implications for Press Release Writing

### What Dutch journalists want in press releases
- Simple language, minimal jargon
- Clear "so what" framing
- Local/practical relevance
- Recognizable institution
- Quotable, accessible scientists
- Pre-translated technical concepts
- Story angle, not just facts

### What gets dropped
- Statistical caveats
- Methodological detail
- Sample size discussion
- Limitations
- Conflicts of interest

## Implicit Rubric
A press release reaches Dutch (and similar European) coverage if:
- Lay summary precedes technical detail
- Has accessible scientist quote
- Connects to local audience
- Avoids overly statistical framing
- Has compelling "story"
- Comes from recognized source
