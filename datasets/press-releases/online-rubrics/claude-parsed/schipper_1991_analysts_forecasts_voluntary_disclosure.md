# Schipper (1991) — Analyst-incentive lens on press release reception

Schipper's foundational commentary argues that research on analyst forecasts had focused too narrowly on statistical properties and missed the *decision context and economic incentives* affecting how analysts use disclosed information. The frame applies directly to press releases as one input to the analyst's decision.

## Schipper's diagnostic claims
1. Analysts are not passive receivers; they have economic incentives that shape how they read press releases.
2. Forecast quality depends on the *fit* between disclosed information and analyst decision needs.
3. Selective disclosure pre-Reg-FD created strategic info asymmetries that distorted both releases and forecasts.

## Schipper-derived release rubric criteria
1. **Decision-relevance principle.** Press releases should answer the questions analysts will ask in their forecast revision: revenue trajectory, margin trend, segment mix, capex plans, working-capital changes, guidance.
2. **Forecast-supportive disclosure.** Releases should enable a *bottom-up* forecast (segment-level data) rather than only top-line totals — sophisticated analysts work bottom-up.
3. **Reconciliation to consensus.** Releases should explicitly reference the consensus expectation (or guidance) and explain the variance.
4. **Drivers-not-just-results.** Identify the *drivers* of the result (volume, price, mix, currency, one-time) rather than only reporting the bottom line. Drivers determine the analyst's revision direction.
5. **Forward-looking specificity.** Where guidance is given, give it in a form analysts can model (range, base assumption, sensitivity).
6. **Segment data sufficient for industry comparison.** Provide segment-level data in a form comparable to peer firms' disclosures, so analysts can do peer benchmarking.
7. **Disclosure consistency over time.** Re-defining segments or KPIs without bridging tables degrades analyst trust and triggers downward consensus drift.
8. **Q&A pre-emption.** Anticipate the questions the analyst would ask if they could call (and many can't reach IR) and answer them in the release.
9. **Materiality calibration.** Disclose at a granularity that matches the materiality of the item; over-disclose on what's material, do not bury.
10. **Incentive disclosure.** Compensation tied to specific metrics should be flagged so analysts understand the management-incentive context of the disclosed metric.

## Pickup-worthiness implication
Schipper's frame predicts that press releases meeting analyst decision needs are propagated through the analyst-coverage system into broader media via published reports, model updates, and consensus revisions. A pickup-worthy financial release maximizes the analyst's marginal forecast precision; pickup at financial wires (Bloomberg, Reuters) is partly mediated by analyst desks treating the release as actionable. Releases that fail the decision-relevance test get short coverage and prompt analyst calls for clarification — a tax on pickup.
