# Platformer (Casey Newton): How Tech Startups Should Pitch Press

## What I Look for as a Tech Reporter
- Real product change, not a rebrand
- Numbers I can verify (DAU, revenue, growth rate)
- Source close to the decision (founder, not PR rep)
- Story I haven't seen elsewhere this week
- A villain or stakes (regulatory, competitive)

## On Press Releases vs. Pitches
- Wire-distributed releases mostly ignored
- Personalized email with embargo gets read
- Best releases are ones I never see — exclusive given to ONE outlet
- Wire after exclusive runs is fine for SEO

## What Founders Get Wrong
- They write to journalists like they're talking to investors
- Too much TAM/traction talk, not enough product
- They optimize for "getting covered" instead of telling a true story
- They send the release before the product actually works

## My Personal Quality Filters
- Does this affect platforms (the beat I cover)?
- Is there a power dynamic angle?
- Will this generate followups in 6 months?
- Does it advance a story I'm already telling?
- Is the source going to be a long-term contact?

## The Substack Newsletter Era
- Newsletter writers are pickier than reporters
- Want substantive, not promotional, items
- Will not run "look how cool this company is" pieces
- Always need a contrarian or counter-intuitive frame
- Comments section will roast weak items mercilessly

## Pitch Best Practices for Newsletter Writers
- Subject line must include the news, not the company
- Body under 150 words
- Link to product, screenshots, demo video
- Make founder available for 15-min call same day
- Offer exclusive embargo on news

## Quality Rubric (from a newsletter journalist)
- Substantive product/decision news
- Verifiable numbers
- Founder-level source
- Beat-relevant angle
- Exclusive offered, not blasted
- Demoable / screenshottable
