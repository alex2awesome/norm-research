function createCookie(key, value, date) {
	let expiration = date
		? new Date(date).toUTCString()
		: new Date(new Date().getTime() + (365 * 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000)).toUTCString();
	let cookie = escape(key) + "=" + escape(value) + ";expires=" + expiration + "; path=/";
	document.cookie = cookie;
}

function readCookie(name) {
	let key = name + "=";
	let cookies = document.cookie.split(';');
	for (let i = 0; i < cookies.length; i++) {

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="introduction">Introduction</h2>



<p>The Government Communication Service (GCS) is the professional body for over 6,000 public service communicators working in government departments, agencies and arm’s-length bodies (ALBs).</p>



<p>The GCS Strategy 2022 to 2025 set out the vision for the GCS over the past 3 years, with 30 specific commitments to be achieved.</p>



<p>We built our strategy around3 pillars:</p>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li>Collaboration</li>



<li>Innovation</li>



<li>Great People</li>
</ol>



<p>At the centre of everything is the need to continue to deliver for the public while continually striving to be better. Strategic vision overview:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>World-leading digital capability</li>



<li>Data-driven decision making</li>



<li>Enhanced professional standards</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="collaboration_case">Collaboration case studies</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">1. Revolutionising Crisis Communications</h3>



<p>When the GCS Strategy launched in 2022, crisis communications across government departments often operated in silos, leading to fragmented responses during critical situations. The development of the comprehensive crisis communications toolkit and playbook marked a significant turning point.</p>



<p>The project brought together expertise from across government, creating standardised protocols and response frameworks that could be quickly activated. During its first major test &#8211; a simulated cyber incident involving multiple departments &#8211; the new system demonstrated its value. Teams from different departments seamlessly coordinated their response, sharing real-time updates through the new digital platform and deploying pre-approved messaging frameworks.</p>



<p>&#8220;The difference was night and day,&#8221; recalls a Director of Communications from a major department. &#8220;Previously, we might have spent precious hours aligning messages between departments. With the new toolkit, we had clear protocols and pre-agreed escalation routes. It transformed our ability to respond quickly and coherently.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">2. Streamlining campaign approvals</h3>



<p>Two key changes implemented in 2023/24 saw reforms to government communications planning that significantly improved efficiency whilst maintaining robust standards.</p>



<p>Firstly, the introduction of multi-year campaign approvals transformed long-term planning. In 2023/24, 24% of campaigns received multi-year approval, saving approximately 192 hours of officials&#8217; time. This change particularly benefited campaigns operating on specific cycles, like teacher recruitment, allowing for better resource planning and more strategic media buying.</p>



<p>Secondly, the Professional Assurance and Support Service (PASS) process was overhauled with a new RAG (Red/Amber/Green) classification system. Under this system in 2023/24, 40.5% of campaigns received green ratings (requiring no second stage assurance) and 34.65% received amber ratings (removing the need for additional ministerial review). These improvements saved roughly 410 hours of officials&#8217; time in the first year.</p>



<p>The reforms were praised across government departments. GREAT HQ noted it as &#8220;a real advancement&#8221; while NS&amp;I described it as &#8220;transformative&#8221;. Crucially, these efficiency gains were achieved while maintaining effectiveness &#8211; 99% of campaign value remained under appropriate scrutiny, whilst enabling closer collaboration throughout the campaign planning and delivery cycle.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">3. Fostering cross-government collaboration: Monthly insight</h3>



<p>The introduction of monthly research and insight reports transformed how government communications teams share knowledge and coordinate efforts. By centralising research and insights across key strategy planks, the initiative reached nearly 350 teams and individuals across government, creating an unprecedented level of shared understanding</p>



<p>The impact went beyond simple information sharing. The systematic approach eliminated duplication of effort across departments, delivered consistent views of government communications, and enabled deeper analysis of communication audiences and activities. This coordinated approach proved particularly valuable during major cross-government campaigns, where aligned messaging and shared insights were crucial for success.</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>&#8220;The deeper understanding of our audiences that came from shared insights has transformed how we approach campaign planning,&#8221; noted one department head. &#8220;We&#8217;re no longer working in silos &#8211; we&#8217;re building on each other&#8217;s learnings and successes.&#8221;</p>
</blockquote>



<p>The success of this collaborative approach has led to its evolution into an even more focused weekly reporting system, supporting the delivery of Mission Based campaigns and demonstrating how shared knowledge can drive more effective government communication.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="innovation_case">Innovation case studies</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">1. Leading with innovation: The GCS Ethical AI journey</h3>



<p>The GCS Innovation and Data Strategy &#8216;Innovating with Impact&#8217; marked a watershed moment in public sector communications. As the first government communications service globally to establish a comprehensive Framework for Ethical Innovation, GCS positioned itself at the forefront of responsible AI adoption.</p>



<p>Within months, over 3,500 government communicators across more than 120 organisations were actively using AI tools in their work, guided by clear ethical principles. The launch of GCS Assist &#8211; the first cross-government AI-powered communications tool &#8211; demonstrated how innovation could be both ambitious and responsible.</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>&#8220;It&#8217;s clear GCS is a trail-blazer on this in the public sector globally,&#8221; noted one external innovation expert. &#8220;I&#8217;ve recently got very involved in diving into ethics by design principles &#8211; especially where AI is concerned and these are some of the best documents I&#8217;ve seen.&#8221;</p>
</blockquote>



<p>The strategy&#8217;s success lay in its &#8216;Test, learn, scale, ethically&#8217; approach, which allocated 10% of campaign budgets to testing innovative approaches while maintaining strong ethical guardrails. This is projected to unlock £5 million in annual productivity and efficiency savings across the membership.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">2. Pioneering AI in government comms</h3>



<p>When GCS launched Assist in 2024, it marked a significant milestone as the world&#8217;s first AI assistant specifically designed for public sector communications. What started as an ambitious vision to enhance productivity and innovation has become a transformative tool used by over 3,500 communicators across 125 government organisations.</p>



<p>Assist wasn&#8217;t just about implementing new technology &#8211; it represented our commitment to &#8220;test, learn, scale, ethically.&#8221; The tool was developed alongside the GCS Framework for Ethical Innovation and comprehensive generative AI policy, ensuring responsible AI adoption while maintaining public trust.</p>



<p>Government communicators report increased confidence in using AI tools, with Assist supporting everything from routine tasks to complex crisis communications planning. The tool has been particularly impactful in improving accessibility, with 6 specialised prompts ensuring communications reach all citizens effectively. Using Assist saves a reported average of 2.8 hours each week, equating to a 7% increase in productivity. Based on current usage, to date Assist has delivered around £1.5m of productivity savings.</p>



<p>The success of Assist demonstrates how government communications can embrace innovation while maintaining the highest standards of public service delivery, setting a benchmark for ethical AI adoption in the public sector globally.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">3. Modern standards for modern communications</h3>



<p>The modernisation of GCS professional standards through MCOM 3.0 set a new benchmark for government communications globally. The framework brought clarity and consistency to how communications teams operate, while embedding innovation and digital expertise at its core.</p>



<p>Two years after publication, MCOM 3.0 remains one of the most-viewed pages on the GCS website, with approximately 2,800 views in Q3 24/25 alone. The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) praised the framework as sector-leading.</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>&#8220;GCS guidance and frameworks stand out internationally as an example of sector-leading practice,&#8221; noted the OECD review. &#8220;They raise the bar for public communication efficiency and impact with regard to policy objectives.&#8221;</p>
</blockquote>



<p>The success lay in its practical approach. The &#8220;must, should, could&#8221; framework provided clear guidance while allowing flexibility, while the incorporation of design principles for digital content teams reflected modern communications needs. Delivered entirely in-house, the project demonstrated how GCS could achieve world-class standards through internal expertise.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">4. Project Spark: Making digital accessibility the default</h3>



<p>In early 2024, GCS launched an ambitious initiative to revolutionise how government communicates with all citizens, regardless of their accessibility needs. Project Spark Wave 2 wasn&#8217;t just about meeting minimum standards &#8211; it aimed to make accessibility the starting point of all digital communications.</p>



<p>The project team brought together communicators from across government, including representatives from the Met Office, Defence and Security Accelerator, and the UK Health Security Agency. They engineered six new accessibility-focused prompts for GCS Assist and pioneered innovative British Sign Language solutions for GOV.UK.</p>



<p>Within months, there were 340 views of the Accessible by Default resource page and 876 engagement events with the new resources. But the real success story was in the cultural shift it created. As one team member from the Department for Environment Food &amp; Rural Affairs noted: &#8220;It changed how we think about accessibility &#8211; from an afterthought to the foundation of everything we create.”</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="people_case">Great People case studies</h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">1. GCS Advance: Transforming Professional Development</h3>



<p>The launch of GCS Advance in September 2023 represented a fundamental shift in how government communicators develop their skills. Moving away from traditional external training providers, GCS created a bespoke learning programme that reflected the unique challenges of government communications.</p>



<p>The programme operates across four levels &#8211; Apprentice, Practitioner, Expert, and Leader &#8211; and has already engaged more than 3,500 people from 200 organisations. The impact extends beyond numbers: it&#8217;s creating a new culture of continuous learning and professional excellence.</p>



<p>The success was immediate. The first run of 120 places for the Expert Programme sold out instantly, and all six monthly Practitioner cohorts were fully subscribed, training 1,000 learners per cohort. More importantly, it delivered £1 million in annual savings compared to external training options.</p>



<p>One participant from the Defence Infrastructure Organisation captured the programme&#8217;s impact: &#8220;Great course &#8211; really useful and engaging and made me feel more excited about my role.&#8221; This enthusiasm is reflected in the programme&#8217;s impressive Net Promoter Score of +45, demonstrating its value to government communicators at all levels.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">2. Building community from day one: The GCS New Members&#8217; event</h3>



<p>March 2023 marked a turning point in how GCS welcomed new members to the profession. The launch of face-to-face induction events transformed the onboarding experience for government communicators, creating an immediate sense of community and connection that had been missing during the virtual-only years.</p>



<p>The impact was immediate and sustained. Over four events between March 2023 and October 2024, more than 350 new communicators were introduced to our vision, tools, and most importantly, to each other. The events consistently maintained strong attendance, averaging 85 participants per session, with an impressive 68% of attendees at the first event signing up for additional &#8220;coffee and connect&#8221; networking sessions.</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>Madeleine Clark from Network Rail. &#8220;I&#8217;ve made some great connections with people today that work in different departments and business areas, and I feel really privileged to be part of a wider comms group.&#8221;</p>
</blockquote>



<p>The events go beyond traditional induction formats, featuring panel discussions with senior leaders, skill speed-dating sessions, and interactive introductions to key tools like GCS Connect, which has attracted over 8,000 users.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">3. Building local networks: The GCS Location Strategy</h3>



<p>The GCS Location Strategy is revolutionising how government communicators work across the UK. Starting with three regional networks in 2022, the initiative has grown to 10 thriving professional networks with over 800 members, fundamentally changing how government communications operates outside London.</p>



<p>The impact has been transformative. The percentage of roles based outside London has increased from 48% to 50% across GCS, with ministerial department roles outside London growing from 23% to 25%. Importantly, 67% of members report an increased sense of community through their local GCS network.</p>



<p>Teams like Historic England and HMRC demonstrate how regional presence enhances stakeholder relationships. </p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>&#8220;Being embedded in local communities has transformed our ability to connect with audiences,&#8221; reported one regional team leader. </p>
</blockquote>



<p>The strategy has also fostered partnerships with universities, leading to GCS being named &#8217;employer of choice&#8217; at Manchester Metropolitan University.</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>&#8220;I was proud to be the opening speaker at the first GCS Live Labs,&#8221; shared one regional communicator. &#8220;I&#8217;ve had lovely feedback from students, particularly as seeing someone like me has helped them understand that public servants can come from all backgrounds.&#8221;</p>
</blockquote>



<p>This is just the beginning, as we continue to improve career opportunities and how we connect with local communities across the UK.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="next">Taking it many steps further</h2>



<p>Building on our successful Performance with Purpose strategy, we&#8217;re now looking ahead.</p>



<p>The Government Communications Strategy 2025-2028 represents an ambitious evolution in how we deliver world-class public service communications. We’ll be focusing on three transformative pillars:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Trusted by all: We will revolutionise how government builds authentic connections with communities across the UK, tackling misinformation head-on while fostering transparent, honest dialogue with all our audiences. Our commitment to openness, even during uncertainty, strengthens public trust and social cohesion.</li>



<li>Connected by mission: Through innovative partnerships and strengthened networks, we&#8217;ll create a more agile, responsive communication profession. From enhanced crisis preparedness to seamless collaboration with local government and international partners, we&#8217;ll break down silos to deliver unified, impactful communications.</li>



<li>Expert by design: We&#8217;ll develop tomorrow&#8217;s communication leaders today, embracing cutting-edge technology while maintaining our ethical foundations. Through diverse talent development and AI integration, we&#8217;re building a workforce that truly reflects and serves our communities.</li>
</ul>



<p>This new strategy will empower GCS to deliver exceptional public service communication that makes a real difference.</p>
