<p><i><b>Stanford Social Innovation Review</b></i> (<i><b>SSIR</b></i>) is a magazine and website that covers cross-sector solutions to global problems. <i>SSIR</i> is written by and for social change leaders from around the world and from all sectors of society—nonprofits, foundations, business, government, and engaged citizens. <i>SSIR</i><span class="nowrap" style="padding-left:0.1em;">&#39;s</span> mission is to advance, educate, and inspire the field of social innovation by seeking out, cultivating, and disseminating the best in research- and practice-based knowledge. With print and online articles, webinars, conferences, podcasts, and more, <i>SSIR</i> bridges research, theory, and practice on a wide range of topics, including human rights, <a href="/wiki/Impact_investing" title="Impact investing">impact investing</a>, and nonprofit business models. <i>SSIR</i> is published by the Stanford Center on Philanthropy and Civil Society at <a href="/wiki/Stanford_University" title="Stanford University">Stanford University</a>.
</p><p>The publication was founded in 2003 by the Center for Social Innovation (CSI), a <a href="/wiki/Hewlett_Foundation" title="Hewlett Foundation">Hewlett Foundation</a> grantee<sup id="cite&#95;ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> at the <a href="/wiki/Stanford_Graduate_School_of_Business" title="Stanford Graduate School of Business">Stanford Graduate School of Business</a>. Now, <i>SSIR</i> receives about 2.5 million total unique visitors annually. Outside of the US, the site receives the most traffic from Canada, India, the UK, the Philippines, and Australia.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. <div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Mission">Mission</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanford_Social_Innovation_Review&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Mission"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p><i>SSIR</i> aims to advance, educate, and inspire the field of social innovation by seeking out, cultivating, and disseminating the best in research- and practice-based knowledge.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>8<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="History_and_impact">History and impact</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanford_Social_Innovation_Review&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: History and impact"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p><i>SSIR</i> was launched in 2003 by the Center for Social Innovation (CSI) at the <a href="/wiki/Stanford_Graduate_School_of_Business" title="Stanford Graduate School of Business">Stanford Graduate School of Business</a>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>9<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Beginning in 2010, <i>SSIR</i> has been published at the Stanford Center on Philanthropy and Civil Society (PACS).<sup id="cite&#95;ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>Nonprofit terms such as the nonprofit starvation cycle<sup id="cite&#95;ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Collective_impact" title="Collective impact">collective impact</a><sup id="cite&#95;ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>12<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>13<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>14<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> were first given prominence by <i>SSIR</i> in 2009 and 2011, respectively. The latter term was introduced by John Kania and Mark Kramar in their article "Collective Impact", and became the number two philanthropy buzzword for 2011, according to <i><a href="/wiki/The_Chronicle_of_Philanthropy" title="The Chronicle of Philanthropy">The Chronicle of Philanthropy</a></i>. It has also been recognized by the <a href="/wiki/White_House_Council_for_Community_Solutions" title="White House Council for Community Solutions">White House Council for Community Solutions</a>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>15<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Current_activity">Current activity</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanford_Social_Innovation_Review&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Current activity"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>Since 2006, <i>SSIR</i> has hosted the annual Nonprofit Management Institute<sup id="cite&#95;ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>16<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> conference, a multi-day conference for senior-level nonprofit executives.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>17<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In 2015, <i>SSIR</i> hosted its first annual Data on Purpose conference,<sup id="cite&#95;ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>18<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>19<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> which was then combined with the Do Good Data conference in 2017.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>20<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In 2016, <i>SSIR</i> hosted the inaugural Frontiers of Social Innovation, a forum for global leaders.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>21<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>22<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> <i>SSIR</i> has also hosted a webinar series since 2009. On 10 March 2021 SSIR hosted a webinar on "Creating Impact in a Volatile World — Lessons Learned from the Front Lines",<sup id="cite&#95;ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>23<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> moderated by Eric Nee, Editor-in-Chief SSIR. The presenters were Jim Bildner, CEO of Draper Richards Kaplan Foundation, Kruti Bharucha, founder and CEO of Peepul India, Rebecca Taber Staehelin, cofounder and co-CEO of Merit America, Tatiana Garcia-Granados, cofounder and COO of The Common Market and Claire Chamberlain, managing director for Social Impact at Blackrock.
</p><p>In 2017, SSIR launched a local-language partnership with the <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://en.lepingfoundation.org/">Leping Foundation for Social Entrepreneurship</a>, to produce copies of the magazine in book form translated into simplified Chinese.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>24<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In 2018, they added a South Korean edition in partnership with <a href="/wiki/Hanyang_University" title="Hanyang University">Hanyang University</a>. Since then, they have added editions in Arabic and Spanish (in 2020), and Japanese (2021) with various partners.
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Submission_guidelines">Submission guidelines</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanford_Social_Innovation_Review&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Submission guidelines"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p><i>SSIR</i> seeks to present interesting, original, and important ideas about social innovation to leaders who can put those ideas to work. To that end, <i>SSIR</i> accepts submissions for six types of editorial articles (Book Review, Case Study, Feature, Field Report, Viewpoint, and What's Next) for their quarterly print edition. Additionally, <i>SSIR</i> accepts submissions of shorter works to be published online—typically of 1,200 to 2,000 words.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>25<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
