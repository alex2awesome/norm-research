<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Background">Background</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flat_Earth_News_(book)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>In <i>Flat Earth News</i>, Davies, who has been a journalist since the 1970s,<sup id="cite&#95;ref-National&#95;Geographic&#95;Yong&#95;20090701&#95;5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-National_Geographic_Yong_20090701-5"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> undertook an analysis of daily news media in the United Kingdom from the 1980s to 2008.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-Davies&#95;FlatEarth&#95;2008&#95;4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Davies_FlatEarth_2008-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> From funding raised through the <a href="/wiki/Rowntree_Foundation" class="mw-redirect" title="Rowntree Foundation">Rowntree Foundation</a>, Davies commissioned a <a href="/wiki/Cardiff_School_of_Journalism,_Media_and_Cultural_Studies" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardiff School of Journalism, Media and Cultural Studies">Cardiff School of Journalism, Media and Cultural Studies</a> research project led by <a href="/wiki/Justin_Lewis_(media_scholar)" title="Justin Lewis (media scholar)">Justin Lewis</a> on the United Kingdom's national news coverage<sup id="cite&#95;ref-pressgazette&#95;Davies&#95;20080204&#95;6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pressgazette_Davies_20080204-6"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The researchers examined the origins of 2,000 stories that had been carried by <i><a href="/wiki/The_Times" title="The Times">The Times</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph" title="The Daily Telegraph">The Daily Telegraph</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/The_Guardian" title="The Guardian">The Guardian</a></i>, and <i><a href="/wiki/The_Independent" title="The Independent">The Independent</a></i>, and in some cases−<i><a href="/wiki/The_Daily_Mail" class="mw-redirect" title="The Daily Mail">The Daily Mail</a></i>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-pressgazette&#95;Davies&#95;20080204&#95;6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pressgazette_Davies_20080204-6"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>  The report found that only 12% of the stories were provably based on material that the reporters had fact-checked and investigated themselves.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-pressgazette&#95;Davies&#95;20080204&#95;6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pressgazette_Davies_20080204-6"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-Davies&#95;FlatEarth&#95;2008&#95;4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Davies_FlatEarth_2008-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 95">&#58;&#8202;95&#8202;</span></sup> Based on the data, the research showed that "everyday practices of news judgement, fact-checking, balance, criticising and interrogating sources et cetera, that are, in theory, central to routine, day-to-day journalism" were the exception, not the rule.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-pressgazette&#95;Davies&#95;20080204&#95;6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pressgazette_Davies_20080204-6"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Davies thesis is that journalists themselves are not the reason for the increase in "falsehood, distortion and propaganda"—the problem is structural. Corporations, that operate under a logic of commercialism, have taken over newsrooms.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-National&#95;Geographic&#95;Yong&#95;20090701&#95;5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-National_Geographic_Yong_20090701-5"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Citing <a href="/wiki/Rupert_Murdoch" title="Rupert Murdoch">Rupert Murdoch</a>—the founder and CEO of <a href="/wiki/News_Corp_(2013%E2%80%93present)" class="mw-redirect" title="News Corp (2013–present)">News Corp</a>, as an example, Davies says that under the corporate model, there are fewer journalists working at newspapers and they have increased workloads.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-National&#95;Geographic&#95;Yong&#95;20090701&#95;5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-National_Geographic_Yong_20090701-5"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Reviews">Reviews</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flat_Earth_News_(book)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Reviews"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>A 6 March 2008 <i><a href="/wiki/London_Review_of_Books" title="London Review of Books">London Review of Books</a></i> said that <i>Flat Earth News</i> was "a genuinely important book, one which is likely to change, permanently, the way anyone who reads it looks at the British newspaper industry".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-LRB&#95;20080306&#95;7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LRB_20080306-7"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Mary_Riddell" title="Mary Riddell">Mary Riddell</a> in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Observer" title="The Observer">The Observer</a></i> said that "Much of Davies's analysis is fair, meticulously researched and fascinating, if gloomy".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-theguardian&#95;Riddell&#95;20080203&#95;8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-theguardian_Riddell_20080203-8"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>8<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Peter_Oborne" title="Peter Oborne">Peter Oborne</a> in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Spectator" title="The Spectator">The Spectator</a></i> said that Nick Davies has amassed an overwhelming weight of evidence that the British media lies, distorts facts and routinely breaks the law.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>9<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p><i><a href="/wiki/The_Independent" title="The Independent">The Independent</a></i> called it a "wide-ranging investigation of the shortcomings of the global and British media."<sup id="cite&#95;ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Press Gazette</i> cited a number of reviews—including those that were quite critical of the book.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-pressgazette&#95;Stabe&#95;20080211&#95;11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pressgazette_Stabe_20080211-11"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The British journalist, who was criticized in Davies book—<a href="/wiki/Kamal_Ahmed_(journalist)" title="Kamal Ahmed (journalist)">Kamal Ahmed</a>−published a response in the <i>Observer</i>  on 11 February 2008.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>12<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Davies said that Ahmed had worked with <a href="/wiki/Alastair_Campbell" title="Alastair Campbell">Alastair Campbell</a>, Tony Blair's "spin doctor" and had become a "mouthpiece" for the Blair government, which included the <i>Observer</i> "backing" the Iraq war, which Ahmed denied.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-pressgazette&#95;Stabe&#95;20080211&#95;11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pressgazette_Stabe_20080211-11"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Press_Gazette_series">Press Gazette series</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flat_Earth_News_(book)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Press Gazette series"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>In 2008, <i>Press Gazette</i> serialised <i>Flat Earth News</i> "when every national newspaper – including <i>The Guardian</i> – declined to do so".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>13<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>14<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Many news media outlets were critical of Davies in 2008. The <i>Press Gazette</i> series included references to details such as the copy of a fax that a <i><a href="/wiki/Sunday_Telegraph" class="mw-redirect" title="Sunday Telegraph">Sunday Telegraph</a></i> reporter had given Davies regarding the weapons expert, <a href="/wiki/David_Kelly_(weapons_expert)" title="David Kelly (weapons expert)">David Kelly</a>. On the day Kelly's body was found, a private investigator had sent the <i>Telegraph</i> a list of every phone number Kelly had dialled in the eight weeks leading up to his death.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>15<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-Davies&#95;HackAttack&#95;2014&#95;3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Davies_HackAttack_2014-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 13">&#58;&#8202;13&#8202;</span></sup>
</p>
