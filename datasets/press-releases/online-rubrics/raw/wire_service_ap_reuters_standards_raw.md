<p>A <b>news agency</b><sup id="cite&#95;ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>a<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> is an organization that gathers <a href="/wiki/News" title="News">news</a> reports and sells them to subscribing news organizations, such as <a href="/wiki/Newspaper" title="Newspaper">newspapers</a>, <a href="/wiki/Magazine" title="Magazine">magazines</a> and <a href="/wiki/All-news_radio" title="All-news radio">radio</a> and <a href="/wiki/News_broadcasting" class="mw-redirect" title="News broadcasting">television</a> <a href="/wiki/Broadcasting" title="Broadcasting">broadcasters</a>. Although there are many news agencies around the world, three global news agencies, the <a href="/wiki/Associated_Press" title="Associated Press">Associated Press</a> (AP), <a href="/wiki/Reuters" title="Reuters">Reuters</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Agence_France-Presse" title="Agence France-Presse">Agence France-Presse</a> (AFP) have offices in most countries of the world, cover all areas of media, and provide the majority of international news printed by the world's newspapers.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-:2&#95;2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> All three began with and continue to operate on a basic philosophy of providing a single objective news feed to all subscribers. <a href="/wiki/Jonathan_Fenby" title="Jonathan Fenby">Jonathan Fenby</a> explains the philosophy:
</p>
<blockquote><p>To achieve such wide acceptability, the agencies avoid overt partiality. Demonstrably correct information is their stock in trade. Traditionally, they report at a reduced level of responsibility, attributing their information to a spokesman, the press, or other sources. They avoid making judgments and steer clear of doubt and ambiguity. Though their founders did not use the word, objectivity is the philosophical basis for their enterprises – or failing that, widely acceptable neutrality.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-:1&#95;3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p><a href="/wiki/Newspaper_syndicate" class="mw-redirect" title="Newspaper syndicate">Newspaper syndicates</a> generally sell their material to one client in each territory only, while news agencies distribute news articles to all interested parties.
</p>

<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="History">History</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=News_agency&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>Only a few large newspapers could afford bureaus outside their home city; they relied instead on news agencies, especially <a href="/wiki/Havas" title="Havas">Havas</a> (founded 1835) in France—now known as <a href="/wiki/Agence_France-Presse" title="Agence France-Presse">Agence France-Presse</a> (AFP)—and the <a href="/wiki/Associated_Press" title="Associated Press">Associated Press</a> (founded 1846) in the United States. Former Havas employees founded <a href="/wiki/Reuters_Group" title="Reuters Group">Reuters</a> in 1851 in Britain and <a href="/wiki/Wolffs_Telegraphisches_Bureau" title="Wolffs Telegraphisches Bureau">Wolff</a> in 1849 in Germany.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In 1865, Reuter and Wolff signed agreements with Havas's sons, forming a cartel designating exclusive reporting zones for each of their agencies within Europe.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> For international news, the agencies pooled their resources, so that Havas, for example, covered the French Empire, South America and the Balkans and shared the news with the other national agencies. In France the typical contract with Havas provided a provincial newspaper with 1,800 lines of telegraphed text daily, for an annual subscription rate of 10,000 francs. Other agencies provided features and fiction for their subscribers.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>In the 1830s, France had several specialized agencies. Agence Havas was founded in 1835 by a Parisian translator and advertising agent, <a href="/wiki/Charles-Louis_Havas" title="Charles-Louis Havas">Charles-Louis Havas</a>, to supply news about France to foreign customers. In the 1840s, Havas gradually incorporated other French agencies into his agency. Agence Havas evolved into <a href="/wiki/Agence_France-Presse" title="Agence France-Presse">Agence France-Presse</a> (AFP).<sup id="cite&#95;ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Two of his employees, <a href="/wiki/Bernhard_Wolff" title="Bernhard Wolff">Bernhard Wolff</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_Reuter" title="Paul Reuter">Paul Julius Reuter</a>, later set up rival news agencies, <a href="/wiki/Wolffs_Telegraphisches_Bureau" title="Wolffs Telegraphisches Bureau">Wolffs Telegraphisches Bureau</a> in 1849 in Berlin and Reuters in 1851 in London. <a href="/wiki/Guglielmo_Stefani" title="Guglielmo Stefani">Guglielmo Stefani</a> founded the <a href="/wiki/Agenzia_Stefani" title="Agenzia Stefani">Agenzia Stefani</a>, which became the most important press agency in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy" title="Kingdom of Italy">Italy from the mid-19th century to World War II</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> in 1853.
</p><p>The development of the telegraph in the 1850s led to the creation of strong national agencies in England, Germany, Austria and the United States. But despite the efforts of governments, through telegraph laws such as in 1878 in France, inspired by the British <a href="/wiki/Telegraph_Act_1869" class="mw-redirect" title="Telegraph Act 1869">Telegraph Act 1869</a> which paved the way for the nationalisation of telegraph companies and their operations, the cost of telegraphy remained high.
</p><p>In the United States, the judgment in <i>Inter Ocean Publishing v. Associated Press</i> facilitated competition by requiring agencies to accept all newspapers wishing to join. As a result of the increasing newspapers, the Associated Press was now challenged by the creation of <a href="/wiki/United_Press_Associations" class="mw-redirect" title="United Press Associations">United Press Associations</a> in 1907 and <a href="/wiki/International_News_Service" title="International News Service">International News Service</a> by newspaper publisher <a href="/wiki/William_Randolph_Hearst" title="William Randolph Hearst">William Randolph Hearst</a> in 1909.
</p><p>Driven by the huge U.S. domestic market, boosted by the runaway success of radio, all three major agencies required the dismantling of the "cartel agencies" through the Agreement of 26 August 1927. They were concerned about the success of U.S. agencies from other European countries which sought to create national agencies after the First World War. Reuters had been weakened by war censorship, which promoted the creation of newspaper cooperatives in the Commonwealth and national agencies in Asia, two of its strong areas.
</p><p>After the Second World War, the movement for the creation of national agencies accelerated, when accessing the independence of former colonies, the national agencies were operated by the state. Reuters, became cooperative, managed a breakthrough in finance, and helped to reduce the number of U.S. agencies from three to one, along with the internationalization of the Spanish <a href="/wiki/EFE" title="EFE">EFE</a> and the globalization of Agence France-Presse.
</p><p>In 1924, <a href="/wiki/Benito_Mussolini" title="Benito Mussolini">Benito Mussolini</a> placed Agenzia Stefani under the direction of <a href="/wiki/Manlio_Morgagni" title="Manlio Morgagni">Manlio Morgagni</a>, who expanded the agency's reach significantly both within Italy and abroad. Agenzia Stefani was dissolved in 1945, and its technical structure and organization were transferred to the new <a href="/wiki/Agenzia_Nazionale_Stampa_Associata" title="Agenzia Nazionale Stampa Associata">Agenzia Nazionale Stampa Associata</a> (ANSA). Wolffs was taken over by the Nazi regime in 1934.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/German_Press_Agency" class="mw-redirect" title="German Press Agency">German Press Agency</a> (dpa) in Germany was founded as a co-operative in <a href="/wiki/Goslar" title="Goslar">Goslar</a> on 18 August 1949 and became a <a href="/wiki/Limited_liability_company" title="Limited liability company">limited liability company</a> in 1951. Fritz Sänger was the first <a href="/wiki/Editor-in-chief" title="Editor-in-chief">editor-in-chief</a>. He served as <a href="/wiki/Managing_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Managing director">managing director</a> until 1955 and as <a href="/wiki/Managing_editor" title="Managing editor">managing editor</a> until 1959. The first transmission occurred at 6 a.m. on 1 September 1949.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>8<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> 
</p><p>Since the 1960s, the major agencies were provided with new opportunities in television and magazine, and news agencies delivered specialized production of images and photos, the demand for which is constantly increasing. In France, for example, they account for over two-thirds of national market.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-SgDdai&#95;10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SgDdai-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>9<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Big_Three_agencies">Big Three agencies</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=News_agency&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Big Three agencies"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>Three international news agencies are commonly referred to as the "Big Three" because of their historical prominence, global reach, and central role in the international news ecosystem: <a href="/wiki/Associated_Press" title="Associated Press">Associated Press</a> (AP), <a href="/wiki/Reuters" title="Reuters">Reuters</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Agence_France-Presse" title="Agence France-Presse">Agence France-Presse</a> (AFP).<sup id="cite&#95;ref-:2&#95;2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-:3&#95;12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-12"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> All three trace their origins to the mid-19th century and continue to operate extensive worldwide news-gathering networks.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-:3&#95;12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-12"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Until the 1980s, this group was often expanded to include <a href="/wiki/United_Press_International" title="United Press International">United Press International</a> (UPI), forming what was commonly described as a "Big Four". At that time, these four agencies were estimated to provide more than 90% of the foreign news published by newspapers worldwide.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-:0&#95;13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-13"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>12<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>Together, AP, Reuters, and AFP form the core of the international wholesale news market. They primarily focus on public affairs, politics, and business, while also covering selected areas of sports and entertainment. Their reporting generally follows a neutral editorial style and employs a standardized and non-interpretive approach to news writing, emphasizing key factual elements at the beginning of news stories, followed by supporting details and background information.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-:3&#95;12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-12"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>Beyond the Big Three, two other international news agencies have emerged as leading global actors. <a href="/wiki/Bloomberg_News" title="Bloomberg News">Bloomberg News</a>, founded in New York in 1990, specializes in financial and business reporting and maintains a global network of correspondents concentrated in major financial centers. <a href="/wiki/Xinhua_News_Agency" title="Xinhua News Agency">Xinhua News Agency</a>, a state-owned agency based in China and founded in 1931, has experienced significant expansion in recent decades and now rivals other major international agencies in terms of size, scope, and global reach.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-:3&#95;12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-12"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Organizational_models">Organizational models</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=News_agency&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Organizational models"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Commercial_services">Commercial services</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=News_agency&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Commercial services"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>News agencies can be <a href="/wiki/Corporations" class="mw-redirect" title="Corporations">corporations</a> that sell news (e.g., <a href="/wiki/PA_Media" title="PA Media">PA Media</a>, <a href="/wiki/Thomson_Reuters" title="Thomson Reuters">Thomson Reuters</a>, <a href="/wiki/German_Press_Agency" class="mw-redirect" title="German Press Agency">dpa</a> and <a href="/wiki/United_Press_International" title="United Press International">United Press International</a>). Commercial newswire services charge businesses to distribute their news (e.g., <a href="/wiki/Business_Wire" title="Business Wire">Business Wire</a>,  <a href="/wiki/GlobeNewswire" title="GlobeNewswire">GlobeNewswire</a>, <a href="/wiki/PR_Newswire" title="PR Newswire">PR Newswire</a>, <a href="/wiki/PR_Web" class="mw-redirect" title="PR Web">PR Web</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Cision" title="Cision">Cision</a>).
</p><p>The major news agencies generally prepare hard news stories and feature articles that can be used by other news organizations with little or no modification, and then sell them to other news organizations. They provide these articles in bulk electronically through wire services (originally they used <a href="/wiki/Telegraphy" title="Telegraphy">telegraphy</a>; today they frequently use the <a href="/wiki/Internet" title="Internet">Internet</a>). Corporations, individuals, analysts, and <a href="/wiki/Intelligence_agencies" class="mw-redirect" title="Intelligence agencies">intelligence agencies</a> may also subscribe.
</p><p>Other agencies work cooperatively with large media companies, generating their news centrally and sharing local news stories the major news agencies may choose to pick up and redistribute (e.g., <a href="/wiki/Associated_Press" title="Associated Press">Associated Press</a> (AP), <a href="/wiki/Agence_France-Presse" title="Agence France-Presse">Agence France-Presse</a> (AFP) or the Indian news agency <a href="/wiki/Press_Trust_of_India" title="Press Trust of India">PTI</a>).
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Government_funded">Government funded</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=News_agency&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Government funded"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1320445320" /><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/State_media" title="State media">State media</a></div>
<p>Governments may also control news agencies: China (<a href="/wiki/Xinhua" class="mw-redirect" title="Xinhua">Xinhua</a>), Russia (<a href="/wiki/TASS" title="TASS">TASS</a>), and several other countries have government-funded news agencies which also use information from other agencies as well.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>13<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Alternative_news_agencies">Alternative news agencies</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=News_agency&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Alternative news agencies"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>News sources, collectively, described as <a href="/wiki/Alternative_media" title="Alternative media">alternative media</a> provide reporting which emphasizes a self-defined "non-corporate view" as a contrast to the points of view expressed in corporate media and government-generated news releases. <a href="/wiki/Internet" title="Internet">Internet</a>-based <a href="/wiki/Alternative_news_agency" title="Alternative news agency">alternative news agencies</a> form one component of these sources.
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Associations">Associations</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=News_agency&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Associations"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>There are several different associations of news agencies. <a href="/wiki/European_Alliance_of_News_Agencies" title="European Alliance of News Agencies">EANA</a> is the European Alliance of Press Agencies, while the <a href="/wiki/Organization_of_Asia-Pacific_News_Agencies" title="Organization of Asia-Pacific News Agencies">OANA</a> is an association of news agencies of the Asia-Pacific region. <a href="/wiki/MINDS_International" class="mw-redirect" title="MINDS International">MINDS</a> is a global network of leading news agencies collaborating in new media business.
</p>
