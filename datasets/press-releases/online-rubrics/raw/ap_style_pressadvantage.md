<p><em>Ever wondered why some press releases feel instantly credible while others get tossed aside?</em> The secret isn’t always the story, it’s often the <strong>format</strong>. Specifically, whether it follows <strong>AP style</strong>.</p>



<p>Journalists live and breathe AP style because it keeps content consistent across thousands of publications. If your release doesn’t match those standards, it can scream “amateur,” no matter how groundbreaking your announcement is.</p>



<p>This guide breaks down exactly how to craft an <strong>AP style press release</strong> that earns media trust, looks professional, and maximizes your chances of getting published. Whether you’re a small business owner, marketer, or PR pro, you’ll walk away knowing the <em>why</em> and the <em>how</em>, plus you’ll get templates and real examples.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Is an AP Style Press Release?</strong></h2>



<p>An <strong>AP style press release</strong> follows the formatting rules of the Associated Press Stylebook, the gold standard for journalists and media outlets worldwide.</p>



<p>Unlike a casual blog post or a company newsletter, an AP style release is designed to fit seamlessly into a newsroom. That means:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Consistent grammar, capitalization, and punctuation</strong><strong><br></strong></li>



<li><strong>Standardized date and location formatting</strong><strong><br></strong></li>



<li><strong>Neutral, factual tone</strong><strong><br></strong></li>
</ul>



<p>Think of it this way: you’re not just writing your news, you’re making it easy for a journalist to copy and paste straight into their publication.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why Follow AP Style for Press Releases?</strong></h2>



<p>Here’s the reality: journalists get hundreds of releases daily. They don’t have time to fix formatting or guess at dates and locations. AP style solves that problem for them.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Benefits:</strong></h3>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Builds trust with journalists</strong>: A well-formatted release says, “I understand how the newsroom works.”<br></li>



<li><strong>Ensures consistency</strong>: AP style makes your release look professional no matter where it’s published.<br></li>



<li><strong>Avoids common pitfalls</strong>: Misplaced commas, wrong abbreviations, or inconsistent numbers can instantly flag you as inexperienced.<br></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How Do You Format Your Headline and Subhead?</strong></h2>



<p>Your headline is the first thing a journalist sees, and in AP style, clarity wins over cleverness.</p>



<p><strong>Tips for AP style headlines:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Use <strong>title case</strong> (Capitalize Major Words)<br></li>



<li>Keep it under 80 characters<br></li>



<li>Be factual, not promotional<br></li>



<li>Include the main keyword or event name if relevant<br></li>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Belongs in the First Paragraph?</strong></h2>



<p>The first paragraph (the “lead”) is everything. In AP style, it’s not just about catching attention, it’s about delivering the facts fast.</p>



<p>Include:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">

<li><strong>What</strong> is happening<br></li>

<li><strong>Why</strong> it matters<br></li>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Which AP Style Rules Should You Follow?</strong></h2>



<p>Here are the biggest AP style rules that trip people up:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Numbers</strong>: Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above.<br></li>



<li><strong>Dates</strong>: Write “Aug. 1, 2025” (abbreviate most months, no “th”).<br></li>



<li><strong>States</strong>: Use AP abbreviations (Calif., not CA). Some states are never abbreviated (Texas, Ohio).<br></li>



<li><strong>Quotes</strong>: Always attribute: “Quote,” said Name, Title.<br></li>



<li><strong>Capitalization</strong>: Only proper nouns and formal titles before names are capitalized.<br></li>
</ul>



<p>Using a consistent font like Times New Roman or Arial (12 pt) keeps it newsroom-ready.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>How to Structure the Body of Your Press Release</strong></h2>



<p>After the lead, the rest of your <strong>AP style press release format</strong> should expand logically:</p>



<ol class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Second paragraph</strong>: Supporting details, stats, or background.<br></li>



<li><strong>Quotes</strong>: Adds a human voice and credibility.<br></li>



<li><strong>Call-to-action</strong>: What should readers do next? Visit a website, register, contact you?<br></li>



<li><strong>Boilerplate</strong>: A 2-3 sentence “About Us” section at the bottom.<br></li>



<li><strong>Media contact</strong>: Name, email, phone number.<br></li>
</ol>



<p>Aim for <strong>400–600 words</strong>. Enough to give context, not so much it feels like an essay.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>AP-Style Press Release Templates and Examples</strong></h2>



<p>Here’s a quick template you can adapt:</p>



<p><strong>FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE</strong></p>



<p><strong>CITY, State – Month Day, Year</strong></p>

<p>CITY, State – [Opening sentence with who, what, when, where, why]. [Additional context or stat to support the announcement.]</p>



<p>“[Insert compelling quote from CEO or spokesperson],” said [Name], [Title] of [Company].</p>



<p>More supporting details here: partnerships, product specs, event agenda, etc. End with a call-to-action: “Visit [website] to register” or “Contact [media email] for more information.”</p>



<p><strong>About [Company Name]</strong><strong><br></strong> [2–3 sentence boilerplate about the company’s mission and background.]</p>



<p><strong>Media Contact:</strong><strong><br></strong>Name<br>Email<br>Phone</p>

<p><strong>FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE</strong><strong><br></strong> <strong>NEW YORK – July 10, 2025</strong></p>



<p><strong>Local Nonprofit Launches Summer Coding Bootcamp for Teens</strong></p>
