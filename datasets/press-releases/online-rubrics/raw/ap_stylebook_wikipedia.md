template">e</abbr></a></li></ul></div></td></tr></tbody></table><p><i><b>The Associated Press Stylebook</b></i> (generally called the <i><b>AP Stylebook</b></i>), alternatively titled <i><b>The Associated Press Stylebook and Briefing on Media Law</b></i>, is a <a href="/wiki/Style_guide" title="Style guide">style and usage guide</a> for <a href="/wiki/American_English" title="American English">American English</a> grammar created by American <a href="/wiki/Journalist" title="Journalist">journalists</a> working for or connected with the <a href="/wiki/Associated_Press" title="Associated Press">Associated Press</a> journalism cooperative based in <a href="/wiki/New_Yorkers_in_journalism" title="New Yorkers in journalism">New York City</a>. The <i>Stylebook</i> offers a basic reference to American English grammar, punctuation, and principles of reporting, including many definitions and rules for usage as well as styles for <a href="/wiki/Capitalization" title="Capitalization">capitalization</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abbreviation" title="Abbreviation">abbreviation</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spelling" title="Spelling">spelling</a>, and numerals.
</p><p>The first publicly available edition of the book was published in 1953. The first modern edition was published in August 1977 by <a href="/wiki/Lorenz_Press" class="mw-redirect" title="Lorenz Press">Lorenz Press</a>. Afterwards, various paperback editions were published by different publishers, including, among others, Turtleback Books, <a href="/wiki/Penguin_Random_House" title="Penguin Random House">Penguin</a>'s Laurel Press, <a href="/wiki/Pearson_plc" title="Pearson plc">Pearson</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Addison-Wesley" title="Addison-Wesley">Addison-Wesley</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Hachette_Books" title="Hachette Books">Hachette</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Perseus_Books_Group" title="Perseus Books Group">Perseus Books</a> and <a href="/wiki/Basic_Books" title="Basic Books">Basic Books</a>. Recent editions are released in several formats, including <a href="/wiki/Paperback" title="Paperback">paperback</a> and flat-lying <a href="/wiki/Coil_binding" title="Coil binding">spiral-bound</a> editions, as well as a digital <a href="/wiki/E-book" class="mw-redirect" title="E-book">e-book</a> edition and an online subscription version. Additionally, the <i>AP Stylebook</i> also provides English grammar recommendations through social media, including <a href="/wiki/Twitter" class="mw-redirect" title="Twitter">Twitter</a>,<sup id="cite&#95;ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>1<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Facebook" title="Facebook">Facebook</a>,<sup id="cite&#95;ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>2<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pinterest" title="Pinterest">Pinterest</a>,<sup id="cite&#95;ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>3<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Instagram" title="Instagram">Instagram</a>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>4<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>From 1977 to 2005, more than two million copies of the <i>AP Stylebook</i> were sold worldwide, with that number climbing to 2.5 million by 2011.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>5<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-AP&#95;2005&#95;6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AP_2005-6"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Writers in <a href="/wiki/Broadcasting" title="Broadcasting">broadcasting</a>, news, magazine publishing, marketing departments, and <a href="/wiki/Public_relations" title="Public relations">public relations</a> firms traditionally adopt and apply AP grammar and punctuation styles.
</p>

<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Organization">Organization</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=AP_Stylebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Organization"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>The <i>AP Stylebook</i> is organized into sections:
</p>
<dl><dt>Business Guidelines</dt>
<dd>A reference section for reporters covering business and financial news including general knowledge of accounting, bankruptcy, mergers, and international bureaus. For instance, it includes explanations of five different chapters of bankruptcy.</dd></dl>
<dl><dt>Sports Guidelines and Style</dt>
<dd>Includes terminology, statistics, organization rules and guidelines commonly referenced by sports reporters, such as the correct way to spell and use basketball terminology like half-court pass, field goal and goal-tending.</dd></dl>
<dl><dt>Guide to Punctuation</dt>
<dd>A specific guide on how to use punctuation in journalistic materials. This section includes rules regarding hyphens, commas, parentheses, and quotations.</dd></dl>
<dl><dt>Briefing on Media Law</dt>
<dd>An overview of legal issues and ethical expectations for those working in journalism, including the difference between slander and libel. Slander is spoken; libel is written.</dd></dl>
<dl><dt>Photo Captions</dt>
<dd>The simple formula of what to include when writing a photo caption, usually called a cutline in newspapers.</dd></dl>
<dl><dt>Editing Marks</dt>
<dd>A key with editing symbols to assist the journalist with the proofreading process.</dd></dl>
<dl><dt>Digital Security</dt>
<dd>A guide to protect journalists, their work, sources, online accounts, and avoid online harassment.</dd></dl>
<dl><dt>Bibliography</dt>
<dd>This provides second reference materials for information not included in the book. For example, it says to use <i>Webster's New World College Dictionary</i> as a reference after the <i>AP Stylebook</i> for spelling, style, usage and foreign geographic names.</dd></dl>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Title">Title</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=AP_Stylebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Title"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>From 1909, when the first company-wide stylebook-like guide was released internally under the title: <i>"The Associate Press Rules Regulations and General Orders"</i>, and until 1953, the stylebook was published under different titles including, among others, <i>Instructions for Correspondents of the Associated Press</i>, <i>The Associated Press. Regulations Traffic Department</i>, <i>A Guide for Filing Editors. The Associated Press</i>, <i>A Guide for Foreign Correspondents. The Associated Press</i>, <i>A Guide for Writers. The Associated Press</i>, <i>The AP Copy Book</i>, and <i>AP Writing Handbook</i>.
</p><p>By the end of WWII, pressures from a growing number of non-journalistic business sectors, already referencing copied or confiscated copies of the guide for years, greatly increased the stylebook's demand. The first publicly available edition of <i>AP Stylebook</i> was published in 1953 under the title <i>"The Associated Press Style Book"</i>. Since 1953, the stylebook has been published under different titles, including  <i>Writing for The AP</i>; <i>AP Stylebook</i>; and <i>The Associated Press Stylebook and Libel Manual</i>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>7<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>Some journalists have referred to <i>The AP Stylebook</i> as the 'journalist bible'.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>8<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>In 2000, the guide was renamed <i>The Associated Press Stylebook and Briefing on Media Law</i> and the paperback edition has been published under this title since then.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-Luckie&#95;9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Luckie-9"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>9<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>10<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Some editions, such as the spiral-bound and <a href="/wiki/E-book" class="mw-redirect" title="E-book">e-book</a> editions, use the shorter title <i>The Associated Press Stylebook</i> on their covers.
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="History">History</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=AP_Stylebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: History"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>The Associated Press organization was first created in 1846. The first company-wide AP "guide" did not cover English grammar. It was more of a brochure with 24 pages of various titles and corporate structures of the Associated Press organization and was first published in 1900 under the title "The Associated Press".
</p><p>Although a formal English grammar style guide did not exist across the organization through the 1800s, individual bureaus were known to have maintained similar internal style guides as early as the late 1870s. The first corporate-wide style guide, with a complete reference to American English words and grammar, was released in 1909, under the title: <i>"The Associate Press Rules Regulations and General Orders"</i>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>a<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-cjr&#95;1&#95;12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cjr_1-12"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>11<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-AP&#95;Stylebook&#95;origins&#95;13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AP_Stylebook_origins-13"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>12<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>13<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>
By the early 1950s the publication was formalized into the <i>AP Stylebook</i> and became the leading professional English grammar reference by most member and non-member news bureaus throughout the world. Due to growing demand by non-member journalists and writers working in public-facing corporate communications, the AP published their first official "stylebook" for the general public in 1953 under the title <i>Associated Press Style Book</i>; the first publication focused on "where the wire set a specific style".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-poynter&#95;1&#95;15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poynter_1-15"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>14<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-AP&#95;2013&#95;16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AP_2013-16"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>15<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>16<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-usc1&#95;18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-usc1-18"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>17<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> For nearly a quarter century it assumed its reader had a "solid grounding in language and a good reference library" and thus omitted any guidelines in those broader areas.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-usc1&#95;18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-usc1-18"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>17<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In 1977, prompted by AP Executive News Editor <a href="/wiki/Louis_Boccardi" title="Louis Boccardi">Lou Boccardi</a>'s request for "more of a reference work", the organization started expanding the book and in 1977 produced a book that was different in a few fundamental regards.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-usc1&#95;18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-usc1-18"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>17<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Firstly, the structure was changed and entries were organized in alphabetical order so that users could find what they need in a timely manner.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>18<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Secondly, in 1977 the book was published for the first time by a 3rd party publisher – <a href="/wiki/Lorenz_Press" class="mw-redirect" title="Lorenz Press">Lorenz Press</a>.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>19<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Thirdly, in 1977, <a href="/wiki/United_Press_International" title="United Press International">United Press International</a> and AP cooperated to produce stylebooks for each organization based on revisions and guidelines jointly agreed to by editors of both <i>UPI Stylebook</i> (Bobby Ray Miller) and <i>AP Stylebook</i> (Howard Angione).<sup id="cite&#95;ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>20<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-Dorfman&#95;Fuller&#95;1982&#95;22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dorfman_Fuller_1982-22"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>21<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In 1982, Eileen Alt Powell, a co-editor of <i>AP Stylebook</i> 1980 edition, stated that: <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1244412712">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 32px}.mw-parser-output .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;margin-top:0}@media(min-width:500px){.mw-parser-output .templatequotecite{padding-left:1.6em}}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Howard Angione... at times thought the task he and UPI counterpart Bobby Ray Miller had undertaken resembled the quest of Don Quixote. It was "an impossible dream", Angione said, to find style rules that pleased everyone, especially since even grammarians couldn't agree among themselves.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-Dorfman&#95;Fuller&#95;1982&#95;22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dorfman_Fuller_1982-22"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>21<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup></p></blockquote><p>
In 1989, Norm Goldstein became the <i>AP Stylebook</i> lead editor, a job he held until the 2007 edition.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-usc1&#95;18-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-usc1-18"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>17<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> After publishing the final edition under his editorship, Goldstein commented on the future of the <i>AP Stylebook</i><span class="nowrap" style="padding-left:0.1em;">&#39;</span>s section on name references: <link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1244412712" /></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>I think the difference... now is that there is more information available on the Internet, and I'm not sure, and at least our executive editor is not sure, how much of a reference book we ought to be anymore. I think some of our historical background material like on previous hurricanes and earthquakes, that kind of encyclopedic material that's so easily available on the Internet now, might be cut back.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-usc1&#95;18-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-usc1-18"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>17<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>After Norm Goldstein stepped down as lead editor in 2007, in bibliographical records for all subsequent editions starting from 2008 lead editors' names are usually not explicitly called out and the author is simply referred to as <i>Associated Press</i> or <i>AP Editors</i>. In 2009 and 2011 the <i>Stylebook</i> was released as an app called <i>AP Stylebook Mobile</i> edition for iOS and BlackBerry, respectively,<sup id="cite&#95;ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>22<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>23<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>24<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> however it was later discontinued in 2015 in favor of users simply accessing the <i>AP Stylebook</i> online edition through their desktop or mobile browsers.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-AP&#95;2015&#95;26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AP_2015_-26"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>25<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>26<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In March 2019 AP created an <i>Archived AP Stylebooks</i> section on its apstylebook.com website where anyone can access previous versions of the <i>AP Stylebook</i> starting from 1900 "brochure on AP corporate structure" and all the way to 1977 edition.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-poynter&#95;1&#95;15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poynter_1-15"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>14<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The first Spanish AP stylebook was created in 2012, after requests from the AP Mexico City bureau and others to develop such a stylebook. The bureau at the time was looking for ways to expand into Latin America while bridging the language barrier. In 2013 the AP Spanish Stylebook came into fruition and is now available to everyone.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>27<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The Spanish AP stylebook is also referred to as the <i>Manual de Estilo</i>.
</p><p>The most recent print edition is the 2020–2022 <i>AP Stylebook</i>, available spiral-bound directly from AP, and as a <a href="/wiki/Bookbinding#Thermally_activated_binding" title="Bookbinding">perfect-bound</a> paperback sold by <a href="/wiki/Basic_Books" title="Basic Books">Basic Books</a>. Creation of <i>AP Stylebook</i> has been helmed by lead editor Paula Froke since 2016.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>28<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>29<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> In early 2023, the stylebook attracted attention for suggesting that "<a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">the French</a>" could be an offensive term in a tweet promoting <a href="/wiki/People-first_language" title="People-first language">people-first language</a>; there was considerable mockery of the suggestion, and the AP subsequently retracted it.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>30<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>31<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>After American president <a href="/wiki/Donald_Trump" title="Donald Trump">Donald Trump</a> issued <a href="/wiki/Executive_Order_14172" title="Executive Order 14172">Executive Order 14172</a> to rename the <a href="/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico" title="Gulf of Mexico">Gulf of Mexico</a> as the 'Gulf of America', the Associated Press style recommended both names were to be used, as "Mexico, as well as other countries and international bodies, do not have to recognize the name change", and "the Gulf of Mexico has carried that name for more than 400 years.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>32<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Following this, Associated Press journalists were prevented from covering several events in the White House, due to the news agency's use of the 'Gulf of Mexico' name.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-Bauder&#95;34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bauder-34"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>33<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>34<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The White House then banned the Associated Press indefinitely from the <a href="/wiki/Oval_Office" title="Oval Office">Oval Office</a> and <a href="/wiki/Air_Force_One" title="Air Force One">Air Force One</a> due to their reporting over the gulf's name.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>35<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Influence_on_American_English">Influence on American English</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=AP_Stylebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Influence on American English"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>The influence of the AP and similar news service styles has reached beyond the news writing community.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>36<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>37<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Many other North American sectors disseminating information to the public began to adopt news styles as early as the late 1800s. Many other sectors now also have developed their own similar style guides and also continue to reference the <i>AP Stylebook</i> for general American grammar, more than any other style guide available.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>38<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>39<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Edition">Edition</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=AP_Stylebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Edition"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Edition_number:_English_edition">Edition number: English edition</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=AP_Stylebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Edition number: English edition"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>The first publicly available English edition of the book was released in 1953.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-poynter&#95;1&#95;15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poynter_1-15"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>14<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> However, all editions prior to 1977 are not included in the editions count and the first modern edition is considered to be the August 1977 edition released for the first time by <a href="/wiki/Lorenz_Press" class="mw-redirect" title="Lorenz Press">Lorenz Press</a>. The latest, 2020 version, is the 55th edition and can be used until<sup id="cite&#95;ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>40<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> 2022. The Associated Press has reduced the frequency in print publication due to the popularity of the online version of the <i>AP Stylebook</i>. The print version is expected to be available, unless otherwise stated, biennially.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>41<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Edition_number:_Spanish_edition">Edition number: Spanish edition</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=AP_Stylebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Edition number: Spanish edition"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>Due to the rising influence of the Spanish language worldwide, in November 2012 Associated Press added, in addition to American English, its first ever Spanish edition of its stylebook.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>42<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>43<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The Spanish edition is separate from the English edition and has a different website, as well as Twitter and Facebook accounts.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>44<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>45<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Unlike the English edition which currently has both online and print versions, the Spanish edition only has an online edition. The Spanish edition does not have an 'edition number' since it only exists as an online service.
</p>
<div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Revision_process">Revision process</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=AP_Stylebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Revision process"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p>From 1980 to 1984, the English edition was updated biennially; then from 1985 to 2020, the English edition was updated annually, usually in May, at which time edits and new entries were added to keep the stylebook up to date with technological and cultural changes. As of the middle of 2024, the most recent edition is the 2024–2026 edition (57th<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>edition).<sup id="cite&#95;ref-57th&#95;47-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57th-47"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>46<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>In 2005, dozens of new or revised entries were added, including "<a href="/wiki/September_11_attacks" title="September 11 attacks">Sept.<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>11</a>", "<a href="/wiki/List_of_Latin_phrases#E" title="List of Latin phrases">e.g.</a>", "<a href="/wiki/List_of_Latin_phrases#I" title="List of Latin phrases">i.e.</a>", "<a href="/wiki/FedEx" title="FedEx">FedEx</a>", and "<a href="/wiki/Midwestern_United_States" title="Midwestern United States">Midwest region</a>".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-AP&#95;2005&#95;6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AP_2005-6"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>6<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>In 2008, about 200 new or revised entries were added, including "<a href="/wiki/IPhone" title="IPhone">iPhone</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Antivirus_software" title="Antivirus software">anti-virus</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Outsourcing" title="Outsourcing">outsourcing</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Podcast" title="Podcast">podcast</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Text_messaging" title="Text messaging">text messaging</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Social_network" title="Social network">social networking</a>", "<a href="/wiki/High-definition_television" title="High-definition television">high-definition</a>", and "<a href="/wiki/Wikipedia" title="Wikipedia">Wikipedia</a>".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-AP&#95;2008&#95;48-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AP_2008-48"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>47<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>In 2009, about 60 new or revised entries were added, including "<a href="/wiki/Twitter" class="mw-redirect" title="Twitter">Twitter</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Baba_ghanoush" title="Baba ghanoush">baba ghanoush</a>", and "<a href="/wiki/Text_messaging" title="Text messaging">texting</a>".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-AP&#95;2009&#95;49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AP_2009-49"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>48<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>In 2013, about 90 new or revised entries were added, including "<a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictine</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Grand_Marnier" title="Grand Marnier">Grand Marnier</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Madeleine_(cake)" title="Madeleine (cake)">madeleine</a>" and "<a href="/wiki/Upside-down_cake" title="Upside-down cake">upside-down cake</a>", "<a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/chichi" class="extiw" title="wikt:chichi">chichi</a>" and "<a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/frou-frou" class="extiw" title="wikt:frou-frou">froufrou</a>".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-AP&#95;2013&#95;16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AP_2013-16"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>15<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Journalistic usage of "illegal immigrant" was no longer sanctioned. The use of 'illegal' to describe a person became regulated.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>49<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>  The decision was part of a wider AP move away from labeling people.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>50<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>In 2018, <i>AP Stylebook</i> included a chapter on polling and surveys.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>51<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Recommends the use of "<a href="/wiki/Transgender_pregnancy#Trans_men" title="Transgender pregnancy">birthing people</a>" and "pregnant people", which was clarified in 2022.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>52<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>In 2019, about 200 new or revised entries were added, including "<a href="/wiki/Budtender" title="Budtender">budtender</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Deepfake" title="Deepfake">deepfake</a>", and "<a href="/wiki/Cryptocurrency" title="Cryptocurrency">cryptocurrency</a>".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>53<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> <i>AP Style</i> recommended removal of the hyphen in Asian&#x2011;American, African-American, or Irish-American as common <a href="/wiki/Microaggression" title="Microaggression">microaggression</a> for more than a century.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>54<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The 2020–2022 edition was released on May 21, 2020. About 90 new or revised technology-related entries were added, including "internet privacy", "digital wallet"<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>/<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>"mobile wallet", "smart devices", and "lidar". A new chapter was added about digital security for journalists.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>55<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>56<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability" title="Wikipedia:Verifiability"><span title="The material near this tag failed verification of its source citation(s). (August 2021)">failed verification</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> <i>AP<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>stylebook</i> moved to capitalized Black and lowercase white.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>57<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p><p>The 2022–2024 edition includes more than 300 new and revised entries, including a new chapter on "inclusive storytelling", "where possible" usage of "<a href="/wiki/Singular_they" title="Singular they">they/them/their</a>" singular pronouns, revised guidance on the use of the term "female", immigration and new entries for "<a href="/wiki/Critical_race_theory" title="Critical race theory">critical race theory</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Anti-vaccine_activism" title="Anti-vaccine activism">anti&#x2011;vaxxer</a>".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-59" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-59"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>58<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> A controversial change was referring to <a href="/wiki/Twitter" class="mw-redirect" title="Twitter">X</a> as "X, formerly known as Twitter".<sup id="cite&#95;ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>59<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> Cautions use of the word "female" in the context of describing women, as some people object to emphasizing biology and reproductive capacity.
</p><p>The 2024–2026 edition includes a new criminal justice chapter.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-57th&#95;47-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57th-47"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>46<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup> The edition also marked a switch from <i><a href="/wiki/Webster%27s_New_World_Dictionary" title="Webster&#39;s New World Dictionary">Webster's New World College Dictionary</a></i> to <a href="/wiki/Merriam-Webster" title="Merriam-Webster">Merriam-Webster</a> as the AP Stylebook's official dictionary.<sup id="cite&#95;ref-61" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-61"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>60<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup><sup id="cite&#95;ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62"><span class="cite-bracket">&#91;</span>61<span class="cite-bracket">&#93;</span></a></sup>
</p>
