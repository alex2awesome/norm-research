<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What Is the Tone of Voice in a Press Release?</strong></h2>



<p>Tone of voice refers to how your press release sounds and feels to readers. It isn&#8217;t just what you say; it is how you say it. Two press releases can announce the exact same news, but one might sound confident and credible while the other comes across as pushy and sales-driven. The difference is in tone.</p>



<p>Think of tone as the personality behind your words. It encompasses your word choices, sentence structure, level of formality, and the overall impression your writing creates. When a journalist reads your press release, they form an instant judgment about your organization based on how the announcement is written, not just what it contains.</p>



<p>Getting the tone right can mean the difference between media coverage and being ignored. Your message might be &#8220;We&#8217;re launching a new product next month.&#8221; Your tone determines whether that sounds like legitimate news worth covering or like an advertisement that belongs in someone&#8217;s spam folder.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Why Tone of Voice Matters in Press Releases</strong></h2>



<p>Your tone determines whether your press release achieves its purpose. <a href="https://presscable.com/insights/how-to-send-a-press-release-to-a-journalist-email-examples-alternatives/">Journalists</a> often receive hundreds of pitches daily and make split-second decisions about what deserves attention.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Tone plays a significant role in those decisions.</p>



<p>First impressions matter enormously. Within the first few sentences, a journalist forms an opinion about your credibility. If your tone feels too casual, too salesy, or too filled with hype, they&#8217;ll move on without reading further.</p>



<p>Tone also reflects your brand identity. Every communication represents your organization. A <a href="https://presscable.com/insights/4-common-types-of-press-releases-examples-how-to-use/">press release</a> with an inappropriate tone signals that your company may lack professionalism, while a well-toned release suggests competence and effective communication skills.</p>



<p>Beyond first impressions, tone builds credibility and trust. Journalists are naturally skeptical of promotional content. When your press release reads like news rather than <a href="https://presscable.com/insights/press-release-vs-advertising-differences-uses-benefits/">advertising</a>, it signals that you respect their role. This makes them more likely to consider covering your announcement.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Two Approaches: Journalistic vs. Achievement Tone</strong></h2>



<p>When writing press releases, there are two main tonal approaches: journalistic and achievement.</p>



<p>The journalistic tone is neutral, descriptive, and focused on facts. It follows conventions you&#8217;d find in a news article: objective language, clear statements, and emphasis on the story rather than praising the organization. A press release written in a journalistic tone reads like something a reporter might have written themselves.</p>



<p>The achievement tone is upbeat and celebratory. It relies on enthusiastic language and superlatives like &#8220;groundbreaking&#8221; or &#8220;industry-leading&#8221; to carry the message. While this tone might feel natural when you&#8217;re excited about your news, it rarely translates well to <a href="https://presscable.com/insights/how-to-get-media-coverage-for-your-startup-tips-strategies/">media coverage</a>.</p>



<p>Here&#8217;s the reality: journalistic tone consistently outperforms achievement tone. When your release sounds like news, journalists treat it like news. When it sounds like marketing, they ignore it. Save the achievement tone for your company blog and stick to a journalistic tone for press releases.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Best Practices for Press Release Tone of Voice</strong></h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>1. Keep It Professional but Human</strong></h3>



<p>Professional tone doesn&#8217;t mean robotic or stiff. The goal is to sound credible and authoritative while still being readable. Your press release should feel like it was written by a competent professional, not generated by a legal department.</p>



<p>Avoid overly formal constructions that nobody uses in conversation, but don&#8217;t swing too far casual either. Read your press release aloud before sending it. If it sounds like something you&#8217;d hear on a credible news broadcast, you&#8217;re on the right track.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>2. Write in Third Person</strong></h3>



<p>Press releases should refer to your organization by name rather than using &#8220;we,&#8221; &#8220;us,&#8221; or &#8220;our.&#8221; This creates distance that makes the announcement sound more objective and news-like.</p>



<p>Compare: &#8220;TechCorp announced today that it will expand operations into three new markets&#8221; versus &#8220;We&#8217;re thrilled to announce that we&#8217;re expanding into three new markets!&#8221;</p>



<p>The first sounds like news. The second sounds like self-promotion. The exception is direct quotes, which naturally use the first person since they represent someone speaking.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>3. Use Active Voice</strong></h3>



<p>Active voice makes your writing direct and engaging. It puts the actor before the action, creating stronger sentences.</p>



<p>Compare: &#8220;The company launched a new sustainability initiative&#8221; versus &#8220;A new sustainability initiative was launched by the company.&#8221;</p>



<p>The active version is shorter, clearer, and more dynamic. Journalists prefer active constructions because they&#8217;re easier to quote and adapt.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>4. Avoid Jargon and Buzzwords</strong></h3>



<p>Nothing undermines credibility faster than stuffing a release with empty buzzwords. Terms like &#8220;synergy,&#8221; &#8220;cutting-edge,&#8221; &#8220;leverage,&#8221; and &#8220;best-in-class&#8221; have been so overused that they&#8217;ve lost all meaning. When journalists encounter this language, they assume you&#8217;re hiding a lack of substance.</p>



<p>Write in plain language that anyone can understand. Instead of &#8220;leveraging innovative solutions to optimize operational efficiency,&#8221; write &#8220;using new software to reduce processing time.&#8221; The second version communicates the same idea with actual meaning.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>5. Stay Factual, Not Promotional</strong></h3>



<p>A press release is not an advertisement. Its purpose is to inform, not sell. Promotional phrases like &#8220;the best product on the market&#8221; or &#8220;truly revolutionary technology&#8221; are opinions disguised as facts that journalists can&#8217;t verify or include in responsible reporting.</p>



<p>Instead of claiming your product is &#8220;the best,&#8221; describe what it does and let readers draw conclusions. Instead of calling your technology &#8220;revolutionary,&#8221; explain specifically what makes it different. Let the facts speak for themselves.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>6. Match Tone to the Announcement Type</strong></h3>



<p>While your overall tone should remain professional, subtle adjustments are appropriate for different announcements. A product launch can carry slightly more energy than a quarterly earnings report. A crisis response requires a more serious tone than a partnership announcement.</p>



<p>Whatever adjustments you make, maintain consistency with your brand voice. Your press releases should feel like they come from the same organization, even when covering different topics.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Press Release Tone Examples: Good vs. Bad</strong></h2>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Example 1: Product Launch</strong></h3>



<p>Bad tone (overly promotional): &#8220;We are absolutely thrilled to announce the launch of our game-changing, revolutionary SmartWidget Pro! This incredible product will completely transform how businesses operate and deliver an unparalleled experience that crushes the competition. Don&#8217;t miss out on this groundbreaking innovation!&#8221;</p>



<p>Good tone (professional and factual): &#8220;TechCorp today announced the launch of SmartWidget Pro, a business automation tool designed to reduce manual data entry by streamlining workflow processes. The product integrates with existing enterprise software and is available immediately through the company&#8217;s website.&#8221;</p>



<p>The bad example reads like an advertisement, filled with unsupported superlatives and exclamation points. The good example reads like news, presenting clear information that a journalist could quote directly.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Example 2: Company News</strong></h3>



<p>Bad tone (jargon-filled): &#8220;Leveraging our synergistic capabilities and best-in-class methodologies, we are pivoting to optimize our go-to-market strategy and deliver enhanced value propositions to key stakeholders across multiple verticals.&#8221;</p>



<p>Good tone (clear and direct): &#8220;GrowthCo announced a strategic shift to focus on healthcare and education markets, aiming to better serve customers in industries where the company sees strong demand for its project management software.&#8221;</p>



<p>The bad example is nearly incomprehensible, hiding simple information behind a wall of buzzwords. The good example communicates the same news in plain language that anyone can understand and that journalists can easily work with.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Limitations of Perfecting Tone for a Single Format</strong></h2>



<p>Even a perfectly crafted press release relies entirely on a journalist deciding your news is worth covering. You can follow every best practice and present genuinely newsworthy information, and still find that reporters ignore your announcement because they&#8217;re overwhelmed with other priorities.</p>



<p><a href="https://presscable.com/insights/traditional-vs-digital-press-release-differences-examples/">Traditional press releases</a> also reach only one audience through one format. But your potential customers aren&#8217;t only reading news articles. They&#8217;re watching videos on YouTube, listening to podcasts, scrolling through social media, and searching for information across dozens of platforms.</p>



<p>A single press release, no matter how well-toned, cannot reach audiences across all these channels. And adapting your message for each platform requires significant time and specialized skills that most organizations don&#8217;t have.</p>
