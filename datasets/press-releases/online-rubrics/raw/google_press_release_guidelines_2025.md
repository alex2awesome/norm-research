<p>As <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/how-search-works" data-type="link" data-id="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/how-search-works" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google’s search algorithms</a> continue to evolve, so do the rules for press releases and their impact on SEO. Over the years, Google has cracked down on manipulative link-building strategies, and in 2025, the guidelines have become even more stringent.</p>



<p>Press releases can still be a powerful tool for brand exposure, media coverage, and audience engagement—but only if used correctly. Google’s focus is now on authenticity, credibility, and value-driven content, meaning press releases should be crafted with the primary goal of informing, not manipulating search rankings.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="press-releases-are-not-a-direct-seo-strategy-anymore">Press Releases Are Not a Direct SEO Strategy Anymore</h3>



<p>Historically, many businesses used press releases as a way to gain backlinks and boost search rankings. In 2025, however, Google’s algorithms treat press releases as promotional content rather than editorially-given links.</p>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-google-penalizes-in-press-releases">What Google Penalizes in Press Releases:</h4>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Press releases stuffed with keyword-rich anchor text links for SEO manipulation.</li>



<li>Mass-syndicated, duplicate press releases across multiple low-quality press release sites.</li>



<li>AI-generated, low-quality content with little journalistic or informational value.</li>



<li>Overuse of dofollow links, which Google considers an attempt to manipulate PageRank.</li>
</ul>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-google-approves-in-press-releases">What Google Approves in Press Releases:</h4>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Informative, well-written, and newsworthy content that provides real value.</li>



<li>Press releases distributed through high-quality, authoritative platforms rather than spammy PR directories.</li>



<li>No-follow or sponsored links to prevent link scheme penalties.</li>



<li>Proper attributions and citations to ensure credibility.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="do-press-releases-still-help-seo-in-2025">Do Press Releases Still Help SEO in 2025?</h2>



<p>The big question on every marketer’s mind: Do press releases still contribute to SEO rankings in 2025?</p>



<p>The answer: Yes, but not in the traditional way. While press releases no longer directly boost SEO through link-building, they still play a vital role in brand visibility, authority, and organic discovery.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="the-indirect-seo-benefits-of-press-releases">The Indirect SEO Benefits of Press Releases</h3>



<p>Even though Google has devalued backlinks from press releases, they can still enhance SEO in the following ways:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Boosts Brand Awareness &amp; Authority</strong> – If your press release is picked up by high-authority news sites, your brand gains credibility, which can increase branded search queries (an important ranking factor).</li>



<li><strong>Improves Search Visibility in Google News</strong> – Google still ranks press releases in Google News and sometimes in Featured Snippets if they provide original, authoritative content.</li>



<li><strong>Drives Referral Traffic</strong> – While backlinks from press releases don’t pass SEO value, a well-placed press release on a major publication can drive real traffic to your website.</li>



<li><strong>Encourages Organic Backlinks</strong> – If journalists, bloggers, or influencers discover your press release and write about it on their own platforms, you can earn natural, editorially-given backlinks—which are extremely valuable for SEO.</li>
</ul>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="what-makes-a-press-release-rank-well-in-google-in-2025">What Makes a Press Release Rank Well in Google in 2025?</h3>



<p>Google’s Search Generative Experience (SGE) and AI algorithms now prioritize the following factors when ranking press releases:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong><a href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/google-raters-guidelines-e-e-a-t" data-type="link" data-id="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/google-raters-guidelines-e-e-a-t" target="_blank" rel="noopener">E-E-A-T Compliance</a></strong> – Google gives higher visibility to press releases from brands with strong Expertise, Experience, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness (E-E-A-T).</li>



<li><strong>High-Quality, Original Content</strong> – Press releases that provide unique insights, newsworthy content, and avoid promotional fluff rank better.</li>



<li><strong>Google News Optimization</strong> – Structuring press releases with proper headlines, meta descriptions, and schema markup improves their chances of appearing in Google News results.</li>



<li><strong>Engagement Signals</strong> – If a press release gets shared widely on social media or gains traction through brand mentions, Google recognizes it as valuable content.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="common-seo-mistakes-that-can-get-you-penalized">Common SEO Mistakes That Can Get You Penalized</h2>



<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-large"><img decoding="async" width="1024" height="538" src="https://globalwavepr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Common-SEO-Mistakes-That-Can-Get-You-Penalized-1024x538.webp" alt="Common SEO Mistakes That Can Get You Penalized" class="wp-image-4886" srcset="https://globalwavepr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Common-SEO-Mistakes-That-Can-Get-You-Penalized-1024x538.webp 1024w, https://globalwavepr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Common-SEO-Mistakes-That-Can-Get-You-Penalized-300x158.webp 300w, https://globalwavepr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Common-SEO-Mistakes-That-Can-Get-You-Penalized-768x403.webp 768w, https://globalwavepr.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Common-SEO-Mistakes-That-Can-Get-You-Penalized.webp 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" /></figure>



<p>Google’s search algorithms have become increasingly sophisticated in detecting manipulative tactics, and press releases are no exception. Many businesses still make critical SEO mistakes when using press releases, which can lead to penalties, ranking drops, and wasted PR efforts.</p>



<p>If you’re using press releases as part of your digital marketing strategy, avoid these common mistakes to stay compliant with Google’s guidelines in 2025.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="the-biggest-seo-mistakes-in-press-releases">The Biggest SEO Mistakes in Press Releases</h3>



<h4 class="wp-block-heading" id="1-over-optimized-anchor-text-in-links">1. Over-Optimized Anchor Text in Links</h4>



<p>One of the biggest SEO mistakes businesses make in press releases is using exact-match keyword anchor text to link back to their website.</p>



<p><strong>Example of bad practice:</strong></p>



<p><em>“Our best digital marketing agency in New York is now offering new services”</em> (linked to the homepage).</p>



<p><strong>Why this is bad:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Google identifies excessive keyword-stuffed anchor text as manipulative.</li>



<li>It violates Google’s link scheme policies, leading to ranking penalties.</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Solution:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Use branded or natural anchor text instead of keyword stuffing.</li>



<li>Apply nofollow or sponsored attributes to links to avoid Google penalties.</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>2. Mass Press Release Syndication on Low-Quality Sites</strong></p>



<p>Many companies distribute the same press release across dozens of low-quality PR sites, hoping for widespread exposure and backlinks.</p>



<p><strong>Why this is bad:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Google considers this duplicate content, diluting your SEO value.</li>



<li>Many low-authority PR sites are ignored by Google’s algorithms.</li>



<li>Press releases that appear word-for-word on multiple sites don’t rank well.</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Solution:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Distribute press releases selectively to authoritative news sources instead of relying on mass distribution.</li>



<li>Use unique headlines and summaries when submitting to different outlets.</li>



<li>Focus on earned media coverage—getting real journalists to write about your news.</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>3. AI-Generated Press Releases Without Human Review</strong></p>



<p>With the rise of AI writing tools like ChatGPT and Google Bard, many businesses are now automating press release writing. However, Google’s AI-detection algorithms are becoming stricter, and low-quality AI-generated press releases can be penalized.</p>



<p><strong>Why this is bad:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>AI-generated content often lacks originality and depth.</li>



<li>Google’s 2025 update prioritizes human expertise (E-E-A-T) over auto-generated content.</li>



<li>Press releases filled with repetitive phrasing and generic information won’t rank well.</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Solution:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Use AI tools to assist writing but always have human oversight.</li>



<li>Ensure fact-checking, personalization, and editorial quality control.</li>



<li>Avoid publishing fully automated press releases without edits.</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>4. Excessive Use of Dofollow Links</strong></p>



<p>Another common mistake is adding multiple dofollow links in a press release, hoping to pass SEO value to their website.</p>



<p><strong>Why this is bad:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Google flags dofollow links in press releases as unnatural link-building.</li>



<li>Too many dofollow links can result in Google penalties or manual actions.</li>
</ul>



<p><strong>Solution:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Use “nofollow” or “sponsored” attributes for all press release links.</li>



<li>Limit the number of links (one or two maximum per release).</li>



<li>Prioritize earned backlinks (from journalists covering your story) instead of relying on press release links.</li>
