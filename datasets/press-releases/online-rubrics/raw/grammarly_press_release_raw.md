In this guide, we’ll walk you through how to write press releases for important events or announcements that stand out and gain media attention. </span>

<h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">What is a press release, and what is its purpose?</span></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A press release is an official statement that an organization distributes to news outlets or the public. It’s a formal way to collect all information relevant to a story into a one-page source. A press release’s function is to attract media attention. </span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">When should a press release be created or used? </span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Press releases should be created any time an organization wants media attention for a major event. They can also be used to shape the narrative when a company or organization is facing scrutiny. These events could include:</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">1</span>
 Changes in executive-level leadership </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">2</span>
 </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Launch of a new product</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">3</span>
 Public and private events </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">4</span>
 Funding announcements</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">5</span>
 Breaking news</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">6</span>
 Groundbreakings and grand openings </span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">How is a press release different from other company announcements?</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Press releases are more than formal announcements. In journalism and historically accurate </span><a href="https://www.grammarly.com/writing"><span style="font-weight: 400;">writing</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a press release is considered a </span><a href="https://www.grammarly.com/blog/citations/primary-and-secondary-sources/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">primary source</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. They are original documents containing official information. Press releases are also different from other announcements because they’re written in a specific way, with the objective of being picked up and reported on by media outlets. </span></p>
<h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">4 dos and 3 don’ts of writing a press release</span></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">When you write a press release, it’s best to follow traditional guidelines. If you want your newsworthy press release to be picked up by a media outlet, you should follow these suggestions. </span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Do consider the angle of your press release</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Focus on how your announcement provides value to your target audience. That’s your angle (and your hook). When it serves people, it’s worth sharing. Conveying a clear perspective or angle will attract more press attention. Consider the outlet you’re sending your release to—a television station may respond to a different angle than a public radio station or a newspaper. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Consider how your story impacts the community. Is there any controversy around this event? Is there progress being made to address a specific problem? How might your press release evoke an emotional response from the reader? </span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Do include the five Ws</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The five Ws are </span><b>who</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><b>what</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><b>when</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><b>where</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">, and </span><b>why</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">. This is the most important information in the press release. Important information should be shared early in your press release so that the journalist reading it doesn’t have to search for vital details, such as when and where the announcement or event is taking place and who will be there. </span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Do use keywords</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If your press release is sent out by a distribution service, it will usually be searchable across the web. These distribution services also send your press release directly to journalists’ inboxes. Using keywords in your headline and summary will make your press release show up on search engines and make it easier to find key information.</span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Do hyperlink or source any references you cite</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you use any references in your press release, use anchor text to hyperlink the source. You might choose to reference data, quotes, or other information that needs to be cited. Journalists will often ask where you got your information or for the source of your data, so putting that information up-front will answer those questions before they’re asked.</span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Don’t go long* </span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Press releases should be clear and concise. </span><a href="https://www.grammarly.com/blog/writing-tips/writing-careers/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Journalists</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> don’t have time to read multiple pages, especially when they receive dozens of press releases a day. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">*An exception to this rule includes press releases whose goal is to convey complicated and nuanced information, such as those announcing a medical study, a technological breakthrough, etc.</span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Don’t forget to edit</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Press releases are formal, professional documents. You don’t want your press release to have any mistakes. Thoroughly revise and edit your release before sending it, keeping an eye out for typos, misspelled names, incorrect dates, and missing information. </span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Don’t be redundant</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Each component of your press release is another chance to convey your angle and show that your event is newsworthy. Avoid making your headline, subhead, and first sentence the same. The paragraphs in your press release should each contain unique information and should easily flow together. </span></p>
<h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">How to write a press release</span></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Adhering to the typical press release outline shows media outlets that your organization is professional. The standard structure also makes it easier for journalists to quickly find the information that they’re looking for. Let’s dive into the types of press releases and what each kind should include. </span></p>
<h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">Press release outline</span></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A press release is essentially one page of news that follows a traditional outline. It should be between 200 and 500 words. Here’s a standard press release outline. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">1</span>
 Headline</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">2</span>
 Date of publication</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">3</span>
 Contact information</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">4</span>
 Summary</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">5</span>
 Intro paragraph</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">6</span>
 Detail paragraphs</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">7</span>
 About</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">8</span>

<h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">What are the different types of press releases?  </span></h2>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Product launches and updates</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A product launch is a great time to create a press release. It allows a company to control which information about their product goes out to the public and generates excitement for their latest innovation. Always include a high-quality photo of the product in a product press release.</span></p>
<p><b>Examples: </b></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Apple Unveils New Apple Watch Pride Edition Bands</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Coding Dojo Launches Part-Time Online UX/UI Program to Upskill Working Professionals</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Genesis Delivers First GV60 Electric Car in the US</span></li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Event announcements or invitations </span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Press releases can be used to announce both public and private events. Whether it’s a summary of a private charity event or a public announcement of an upcoming grand opening, an event press release can contribute to an organization’s image and brand recognition. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If the event is open to the public, include a clear call to action and distribute your press release at least two weeks before the event. Play up highly visual aspects of the event through images to garner more interest. </span></p>
<p><b>Examples: </b></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Jeni’s Splendid Ice Creams—a Midwestern Favorite—Opens Its First San Diego Shop</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bonnaroo Releases Full Schedule</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lupus Charity Walk Raises $1M for Research</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Organization Helps Housing-Insecure Families Find Permanent Homes</span></li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Branding updates</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A branding-update press release shows the public how and why your organization rebranded. Include a quote from your organization’s leadership to help convey the intentions of the rebrand. You can take a rebrand press release one step further by making it interactive. Don’t forget to include images of the new brand identity. </span></p>
<p><b>Examples: </b></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dunkin’ Donuts Is Now Dunkin’</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Google Becomes “Alphabet” in Massive Rebranding</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cystic Fibrosis Foundation Gets New Logo</span></li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Organizational changes</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Press releases for organizational changes may include new C-suite leadership, partnerships with other companies, mergers, and acquisitions. Organizational changes can be perceived in different ways depending on the organization’s history and coverage surrounding the change. A press release is the easiest way to tell your organization’s story. Include quotes from key players in the change. </span></p>
<p><b>Examples: </b></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">M&amp;M’s and Adidas Team Up for New Sneaker Drop</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Blueprint Medicines Elevates Two Talented Leaders to Its Executive Team</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft to Acquire Activision Blizzard to Bring the Joy and Community of Gaming to Everyone</span></li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Awards </span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Award announcements bolster your company’s reputation. Use keywords and superlative</span><a href="https://www.grammarly.com/blog/parts-of-speech/superlative-adjectives/"><span style="font-weight: 400;"> adjectives</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to emphasize your accomplishments in your headline. Award press releases can also be used to create buzz if your organization is seeking or has recently been awarded funding. </span></p>
<p><b>Examples: </b></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ologies with Alie Ward</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Wins Webby’s Best Podcast Host</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Korean Auto Manufacturers Lead the Way in Vehicle Dependability, J.D. Power Finds</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fresh Air with Terry Gross</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Wins Peabody Institution Award</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Plastic Upcycling Startup Novoloop Raises $10M</span></li>
</ul>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">Breaking news</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Press releases’ succinct, formal format encourages you to simply state the facts and provide clear explanations without using overly emotional language, even when announcing difficult breaking news. In fact, the first press release ever distributed back in 1906 covered a tragic railroad accident. </span></p>
<p><b>Examples: </b></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">EPA Announces Coronavirus (COVID-19) Resources for State, Local, and Tribal Governments</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Powerful Earthquake Shakes Southern Peru</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Japan to Resume Tourism in June</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Sophie Masloff, Former Mayor of Pittsburgh, Dies at 96</span></li>
</ul>
<h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">8 things all press releases should have</span></h2>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">1</span>
 Headline</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Since media outlets receive hundreds of stories per day, you’ll need a killer </span><b>headline</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for your press release to stand out. To create a standout headline, try some of these approaches:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Reference interesting data</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Speak directly to the reader</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ask a question</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Include keywords</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Use numbers</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Answer a question</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Apply alliteration</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Add value</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><div class="tool__tip"><b>Here’s a tip:</b> Crafting the perfect headline doesn’t have to be a challenge. Grammarly’s </span><a href="https://www.grammarly.com/ai/ai-writing-tools/headline-generator"><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI headline generator</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> helps you create attention-grabbing headlines in seconds. </div>
</span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">2</span>
 Date of publication</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Most press releases simply put “for immediate release” in the </span><b>date of publication</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> field, but that isn’t your only option. If you have an upcoming event, you can always send out your press release early and instead list the date you’d like it to be released.</span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">3</span>
 Contact information </span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><b>contact information</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> field on the press release should list your organization’s public relations liaison. This field should include their name, job title, email, and phone number.</span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">4</span>
 Summary</span><b> </b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><b>summary</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> falls above the body of the press release but below the headline, contact information, and date. The summary is one to three bullet points or an italicized paragraph of two to three sentences relaying the most important information. Keep it succinct.</span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">5</span>
 Intro paragraph</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The first paragraph, or </span><b>intro paragraph</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">, should start with the location of the event and introduce what the press release is about. Media outlets are looking for a newsworthy announcement and for three of the five Ws—when, where, and why. </span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">6</span>
 Detail paragraphs </span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">You can include one or two </span><b>detail paragraphs</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> that share facts and figures, more details, or a quote from a relevant organization member. Pick only one or two crucial quotes or statistics that are truly newsworthy. You can use the third paragraph to also relate your press release to a current news event to make it timelier and more relevant. </span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">7</span>
 About </span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Also called the boilerplate, the </span><b>about </b><span style="font-weight: 400;">section is where you tell the press about your company. This section should be two to four sentences and include the last two of the five Ws—who and what. Include the name of your organization and its mission statement, founding dates, and company size. </span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;"><span class="tool__number">8</span>
 ### </span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Traditionally, three pound signs have been included at the end of a press release to indicate the end of the press release content. The signs were used to let wire services know that there wasn’t another page and tell journalists that they didn’t need to wait around for another page. <a href="https://www.grammarly.com/blog/commonly-confused-words/nowadays-or-now-days/">Nowadays</a>, these symbols aren’t technically necessary, but many writers still follow this format out of respect for </span><a href="https://www.thehalogroup.com/what-does-mean-at-the-end-of-a-press-release/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><span style="font-weight: 400;">the tradition</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span></p>
<h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">Press release FAQs</span></h2>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">What is a press release?</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A press release is a formal statement distributed to media outlets to provide official information to the public. A press release is considered a primary source. </span></p>
<h3><span style="font-weight: 400;">When should you write a press release?</span></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">You should write a press release for important, newsworthy events that you want publicized. </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Product launches and updates</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Event announcements or invitations </span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Branding updates</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Organizational changes</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Awards </span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Breaking news</span></li>
</ul>
