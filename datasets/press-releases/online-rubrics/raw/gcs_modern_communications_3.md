<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Introduction: how to use MCOM 3.0</h2>
</div></div>



<p>The purpose of this new Modern Communications Operating Model (MCOM) is to provide simplicity and clarity about the expectations of teams and leaders within the Government Communication Service (GCS).</p>



<p>MCOM brings together all the policies and guidance needed to build and lead a team that delivers the GCS vision of exceptional communications that make a difference.</p>



<p>This updated MCOM uses a must*, should, could framework to provide complete clarity on: the policies teams must follow; those that we recommend they should follow; and guidance that is available to consult and apply where needed. </p>



<p>The GCS Strategy and Government Communications Plan set the overarching strategy framework for government communications. The MCOM ‘house’ sits underneath this with three pillars: People &amp; Structure, Policies, and Guidance &amp; Tools.</p>



<p>Whether you are new to GCS, or an established leader who wants an accessible guide to best practice, this MCOM is for you. It is a living document and will be updated regularly, so we welcome ongoing feedback to ensure it remains relevant to you.</p>



<p>We hope that this updated approach enables you to use the recommendations and supporting guidance within MCOM to its best and fullest effect. We look forward to working together to continue delivering world class communications.</p>



<p><em>*The Communication Functional Standard (GovS011) uses the term Shall for mandatory elements. In this document, for simplicity we use Must rather than Shall.</em></p>





<h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="gcs-strat-approach">GCS strategic approach </h2>



<p>The <a href="https://strategy.communications.gov.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">GCS Strategy 2022-25 ‘Performance with Purpose’</a> and the <a href="https://communication-plan.communications.gov.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">annual Government Co</a><a href="https://development.communications.gov.uk/news/gcs-launches-the-2023-24-uk-government-communication-plan/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">mmunication Plan</a> set out <strong>what </strong>we deliver. MCOM’s policies and guidance set out <strong>how</strong> to deliver exceptional communications, develop your people and build great teams. </p>



<p>All departmental or organisational communication plans <strong>must </strong>reflect the strategic priorities in the Government Communication Plan. The plans <strong>must </strong>also include clear metrics to support the delivery of the cross-government and organisational objectives, approved by the organisation’s board (or equivalent).&nbsp;</p>



<p>For team leaders:</p>



<p>Strong leadership skills are critical to the successful delivery of effective and efficient government communications. As leaders, Directors and Heads of Communication set the vision for their teams, a vision which <strong>must </strong>support a shared vision of communications across government.&nbsp; Directors and Heads of Communication are expected to make a corporate contribution to GCS by working on cross-government projects such as the delivery of the Strategy Commitments.&nbsp; The following leadership objective <strong>must</strong> be included in annual performance and development plans of all SCS leaders:</p>



<p><em>“GCS SCS to play an active role in championing the three pillars of the GCS Strategy 2022-25 by promoting collaboration, increasing efficiency and innovation, supporting great people and raising professional standards, and contributing to the delivery of the GCS strategy commitments.”</em></p>



<p>We will support SCS leaders in doing this by setting out clear expectations, a new leadership capability framework (to be launched Autumn 2024) and targeted key skills development to ensure they stay up to date in areas such as the latest trends in technology. Further guidance on Leadership, including the GCS Leadership Framework, is available from <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/guidance/leadership/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Leadership &#8211; GCS</a>.</p>

        Team design principles
    </button>

    <div id="team-design_long_desc">

        <p>Directors and Heads of Communication <strong>must </strong>clearly set out roles and accountabilities (e.g. in an organogram).</p>
<p>Directors and Heads of Communication <strong>must </strong>take the following principles into account when designing their teams:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ensure, through tiered accountability, that it is clear who makes decisions and owns outcomes and processes;</li>
<li>Work collaboratively in order to achieve clear, simple and consistent communication on behalf of government; manage duplication and minimise complexity;</li>
<li>Keep processes simple, proportionate and user-focused, and;</li>
<li>Define roles to suit the needs of the activity being undertaken.</li>
</ul>
<p>The purpose and size of organisations will differ, but every communications team <strong>should</strong> apply the common, gold standard principle of using a multi-disciplinary approach when structuring their teams.</p>
<p>Every communication team <strong>should</strong> have the core seven MCOM disciplines represented to differing degrees depending on organisational need, size and focus. This does not mean needing seven separate siloed teams. In the spirit of a multi-disciplinary approach, it means having those skills somewhere in the team and available to others.</p>
<p>New design principles for Digital content teams have been developed to reflect the creation of Digital as a new discipline. <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/guidance/">Guidance on the design principles for Media, Strategic Communications, External Affairs, Internal Comms and Marketing are available from the GCS website</a>.</p>
<p>Design principles for Data &amp; Insight will be launched in Autumn 2024.</p>
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote">
<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Digital team design principles</h4>
<p>Digital teams <strong>should </strong>include generalist digital experts with a deep understanding of the digital channels, platforms and content formats required to grow and engage audiences, as well as specialists in the fields of digital content creation and data analytics.</p>
<p>There are four core functions of a modern digital communications team around which your digital team <strong>should</strong> be structured:</p>
<ul>
<li>Digital Strategy &amp; Leadership</li>
<li>Data and insight analysis</li>
<li>Technical content creation including these specialisms: Graphic design and animation; Videography; Photography</li>
<li>Copywriting, editing, channel management &amp; strategic platform publishing, including all digital channels.</li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The full </span><a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/guidance/digital-communication/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Digital discipline guide</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is available from the GCS website.</span><br />

<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><p><b>Structuring a small team: the Scotland Office:</b></p>
<ul>
<li>Smaller departments offer a good opportunity for strong cross-functional agile working, skills development and collaboration.</li>
<li>Each member of the team will have their identified specialism, alongside being upskilled on basics such as social media management, ministerial visit logistics and managing incoming media enquiries. This allows work to flow to peaks and a fairer balance across a very small team. It also helps to ensure that everyone has an appreciation of the wider strategy, context and positioning of Scotland Office communications.</li>
<li>This does not mean everyone does some press work or social media engagement; people’s specialisms are respected and the team&#8217;s broader, practitioner level skills are developed.</li>

        Working across your organisation
    </button>

    <div id="across-org_long_desc">

        <p>Directors or Heads of Communication are responsible for managing the communication function (all communications work, not just the comms team itself) for your organisation. They are accountable for ensuring their whole organisation, including ALBs and embedded communicators, complies with the <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/government-communication-functional-standard/">Functional Standard GovS011: Communication</a>. They are also accountable for their whole organisation aligning to the vision and strategies of GCS, and for building their professional communications capability and skills.</p>
<p>Directors and Heads of Communication <strong>should </strong>have oversight of relevant recruitment across their whole organisation, including embedded communicators and sponsored ALBs. For example, this would include agreeing job descriptions and participating in selection panels.</p>
<p>This section contains the principles which Directors and Heads of Communication <strong>should </strong>follow to establish collaborative partnerships between ALBs and sponsoring departments.</p>
<p>A helpful overview of how ALBs <strong>should </strong>link to Directors of Communication and the Head of Profession can be found in <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/government-functional-standard-govs-001-government-functions">the overall Government Functional Standard &#8211; GovS 001</a> (figure 6 on page 17).</p>
<h4 id="working-with-albs" class="wp-block-heading">Effective working between ALBs and Sponsor Departments</h4>
<p>The principles in the <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/alb-sponsor-department-working-principles/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">ALB/Sponsor department working guidance </a>set out how Directors and Heads of Communication <strong>should </strong>approach building strong links between ALBs and Sponsor departments, namely:</p>
<ul>
<li>Where an organisation is part of an ALB cluster, duplication <strong>should </strong>be removed, and closer, integrated working should be the norm.</li>
<li>Organisations do not necessarily need every capability in-house, and <strong>should </strong>look at sharing service opportunities across a group, or across the GCS.</li>
<li>Sponsor departments should make sure the right processes are in place to quality assure all ALBs &#8211; without inhibiting ALB autonomy and expertise &#8211; and encourage and enable access to the GCS learning offer.</li>
</ul>
<p>Sensible collaboration doesn’t preclude operational independence. It means coming together where there are shared interests without limiting the ability to pursue individual responsibilities.</p>

        Data handling &#038; data protection
    </button>

    <div id="data_long_desc">

        <p>The day-to-day work of government communicators<strong> must</strong> be understood in the wider context of the legislative and regulatory environment.  Whilst innovation in technology provides huge opportunities for government communication, it is accompanied by significant ethical and legal considerations around data handling and data protection which all government communicators <strong>must</strong> have awareness of.</p>
<p>Directors and Heads of Communication <strong>must </strong>ensure that the work of their communications team conforms to the principles of the <a href="https://www.gov.uk/data-protection">Data Protection Act 2018</a>. This act controls how personal information is used by organisations, businesses and government, and every government communicator<strong> must</strong> have an understanding of the eight data protection principles. This includes when it is necessary to complete a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA). Please refer to <a href="https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/when-do-we-need-to-do-a-dpia/">ICO guidance</a> on when this is required.</p>
<p>In summary, personal data should be:</p>
<ul>
<li>Processed fairly and lawfully and not unless certain conditions are met</li>
<li>Obtained for specific and lawful purposes and not further processed in a manner incompatible with that purpose</li>
<li>Adequate, relevant and not excessive for that purpose</li>
<li>Accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date</li>
<li>Processed in accordance with the rights of the individual</li>
<li>Kept no longer than is necessary for the purpose</li>
<li>Protected by appropriate security</li>
<li>Not transferred without adequate protection.</li>
</ul>
<p>You <strong>should</strong> refer to your organisation’s Data Protection Officer to ensure compliance, get approval on wording relating to Data Protection and for further guidance.</p>
<p>Directors and Heads of Communication <strong>must</strong> be aware of the introduction of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in the Data Protection Act 2018, and ensure compliance when planning and implementing government campaigns.  Put simply, ‘privacy by design’ <strong>must</strong> be a key objective.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://ico.org.uk/">Information Commissioner’s Office website</a> provides comprehensive information on the <a href="https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-data-protection/">Data Protection Act and GDPR</a> and you should refer to your Data Protection Officer for further guidance.</p>
<p>Alongside Data Protection principles which all government communicators <strong>must</strong> know is more specialist guidance which Directors and Heads of Communication must be aware of concerning data protection and Direct Marketing, Campaign Platform development, Programmatic Real Time Bidding, and Facebook related privacy notices and cookie banners:</p>
<h4 class="wp-block-heading">GDPR and Direct Marketing</h4>
<p>The majority of communications from government to individuals are unlikely to constitute direct marketing.  However, campaign teams <strong>must</strong> demonstrate a lawful basis for processing personal data under UK GDPR within their Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) if they are planning to reach audiences through this approach. Further guidance is available from the GCS website &#8211; <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/guidance/marketing/delivering-government-campaigns/ico-campaign-guidance/">ICO Direct Marketing campaign guidance</a> and from the Information Commissioner Officer’s website &#8211; <a href="https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing/direct-marketing-and-the-public-sector/">Compliant use of Direct Marketing approaches</a>.  Campaign leads <strong>should</strong> consult their organisation’s Data Protection Officer.</p>
<h4 class="wp-block-heading">Data Protection and Data Management guidance in Social Media Campaigns and website development</h4>
<p>Guidance on ensuring compliance with GDPR when building campaign platforms <strong>must</strong> be consulted &#8211; <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/guidance/marketing/how-to-set-up-a-new-government-campaign-online/#General-Data-Protection-Regulation-(GDPR)-and-cookies">GCS website development GDPR guidance</a></p>
<p>Departments <strong>must</strong> adopt a ‘Least Data by Default’ approach when using programmatic real-time bidding advertising (RTB) to deliver digital media.  This guidance provides the practical steps departments <strong>must</strong> take to ensure compliant use of RTB &#8211; <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/guidance/marketing/delivering-government-campaigns/real-time-bidding-rtb-guidance/">Compliant use of Programmatic Real-Time-Bidding </a></p>

        Accessible communication
    </button>

    <div id="accessible_long_desc">

        <p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Equality Act 2010 requires the government to make reasonable adjustments to ensure that everyone has equal access to information, goods and services. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">If your communication is about something that will affect disabled people, or they are entitled to know or be included in, you </span><b>must</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> ensure that you have done as much as can be reasonably expected to ensure they can access and respond to the information.  Please refer to the GCS guidance on </span><a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/guidance/accessible-communications/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">accessible and inclusive communications. </span></a></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">GCS has set out </span><a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/guidance/accessible-communications/accessibility-standards/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">accessibility standards</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> which all GCS communications content </span><b>should</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> meet. The standards also help you to think through how you can make your content inclusive to different audiences, so that it has a wider and more effective impact.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In 2022, the </span><a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/34/contents/enacted"><span style="font-weight: 400;">British Sign Language (BSL) Act</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> became law, meaning that British Sign Language (BSL) is a recognised language of the UK. The BSL Act requires ministerial departments to report on their use and promotion of BSL in public announcements on policy or changes to the law.  There is no statutory requirement for all government communications to be translated into BSL. But, all government departments </span><b>should</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> consider where the use of BSL will be of most interest and importance to Deaf BSL users.</span></p>
<p><a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/british-sign-language-act/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">GCS has published guidance</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to support government communicators, policy officials and others to provide BSL content to those who need it.</span></p>

    </button>

    <div id="oasis_long_desc">

        <p>A campaign is a planned sequence of communications and interactions that uses a compelling narrative over time to deliver a defined and measurable outcome.</p>
<p>All government communications should be viewed in the context of a wider campaign, for example, what do we want to achieve and what is the strategic context? This way we can ensure that all our work links to a clear objective and we can evaluate the impact of everything we do.</p>
<p>All government communication campaigns <strong>should</strong> be developed and delivered according to the principles set out in the <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/guidance/marketing/delivering-government-campaigns/guide-to-campaign-planning-oasis/">Guide to campaign planning: OASIS</a></p>

        Evaluation Cycle
    </button>

    <div id="evaluation_long_desc">

        <div>
<p dir="ltr">Evaluation is the cornerstone of effective communication, enabling communicators to reach target audiences effectively and do so in a meaningful and cost-effective way.  It should be seen as a continuous process that fuels ongoing learning and development, supporting us all in making informed decisions which are fuelled by evidence and insight.  Evaluation should be seen as an intrinsic element of the communications planning process from the very outset, with activities built into each campaign stage so learning can be incorporated and reflected in real-time, as well as to improve future activities.</p>
<p dir="ltr">Building on the foundations set out by the Evaluation Framework 2.0, the new <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/gcs-evaluation-cycle/">Evaluation Cycle</a> sets out a six-step process to support communicators in meaningfully measuring the impact of their work.  The Cycle brings together the latest best practice guidance and thinking on evaluation, including digital techniques which enable real-time evaluation. It also reflects changing audience demographics and is rooted in principles of inclusivity. It is designed to be an agile framework, flexible enough to be used for all types of communications, from low cost/no cost activities to larger-scale paid-for campaigns.  The Evaluation Cycle document clearly maps out the connections with OASIS, COM-B and the Theory of Change, enabling communicators to take an integrated, holistic approach when planning activities.</p>

        Crisis communication
    </button>

    <div id="crisis_long_desc">

        <p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Effective communication is a vital component of good crisis management.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As set out in </span><a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/crisis-communication-a-behavioural-approach/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Crisis communication: A behavioural approach</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, during a crisis government communicators </span><b>must</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> act quickly to ensure the public has access to accurate information, knows the actions and behaviours they can adopt to protect themselves and others, and understands what the government is doing and why.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Directors of Communication </span><b>must </b><span style="font-weight: 400;">be responsible for preparing and managing crisis communications associated with the risks that their department owns and is the ‘Lead Government Department’ for (as identified in the National Security Risk Assessment). The GCS Crisis Team (<a href="mailto:crisiscomms@cabinetoffice.gov.uk">crisiscomms@cabinetoffice.gov.uk</a>) will support departments to develop robust communication contingency plans in place before a crisis, and ensure they have the right capabilities in place to implement that plan. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Directors of Communication </span><b>should</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> ensure their teams use the </span><a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/crisis-comms-planning-guide/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Crisis Communication Planning Guide</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in the creation of their crisis communication plans. The guide uses the &#8216;STOP&#8217; framework (considering Strategy, Tactics, Organisation and People) to outline the essential components of an effective crisis comms plan. It builds on the wider planning cycle outlined in the </span><a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/emergency-planning-framework/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Emergency Planning Framework</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The</span><a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/crisis-communications-operating-model/"><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Crisis Communications Operating Model</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> sets out what the communication function </span><b>must</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> do to prepare, respond and recover from crisis situations. It aims to:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Clarify roles and responsibilities</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Embed comms into crisis management &amp; preparedness structures</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Grow a crisis comms community and help us work together</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Remove operational barriers</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Build our crisis comms expertise</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Keep up to date with the latest lessons</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Directors of Communication </span><b>must </b><span style="font-weight: 400;">ensure they are familiar with the </span><a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/crisis-communications-operating-model/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Crisis Communications Operating Model</span></a></p>

    </button>

    <div id="PITCH_long_desc">

        <p>Modern communication is borderless and vital to influencing and shaping perceptions among global audiences. If the UK is to be successful in achieving its international goals, it needs to fully use its strategic communication capabilities in terms of allies, assets and abilities. The guidance and approach contained within the <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/pitch-strategic-communication-as-a-tool-of-global-influence">PITCH toolkit</a> will enable you to develop and deliver effective global communication campaigns which are credible, proactive and effective, and which cut through a crowded information environment. The PITCH framework is centred on: working in partnership; using insight; gathering trusted voices; delivering compelling content; and maintaining the highest ethical standards, in line with our democratic values.</p>
<p>All Directors and Heads of Communications working with international audiences <strong>should</strong> be familiar with the tools and best practices set out within PITCH, and share them proactively with their teams. PITCH <strong>should</strong> be read in tandem with the <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/resist-3-building-resilience-to-information-threats/">RESIST 3 framework</a> as it builds on the ‘Strategic Communications’ step of RESIST. You <strong>should</strong> refer to the PITCH checklist contained within Annex A to the guidance when developing your international campaigns.</p>

        Guidelines for influencer marketing
    </button>

    <div id="influencers_long_desc">

        <p>Influencer Marketing is when brands partner with individuals who have an engaged online following.  This includes social media influencers, podcasters and professional experts. It can be an effective way to reach audiences that the Government and traditional marketing channels find hard to reach. When used in a transparent way, influencers can create personalised and engaging content for your campaign.</p>
<p>However, if used incorrectly, public confidence in government communications risks being eroded. Therefore, all government communicators <b>should</b> read and follow the guidance set out in the <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/guidance/marketing/guidelines-for-influencer-marketing/">guidelines for influencer marketing</a> (member-only content).</p>
<p>All government communicators considering using influencers as part of a campaign<b> should</b> understand the need for rigorous checks to take place before an activity goes live. This is to ensure any partnership does not represent a reputational risk to the Government. As per the Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000 (RIPA), these checks <b>should</b> be managed by an agency. The principles set out in the <a href="https://www.communications.gov.uk/publications/safer-framework-for-digital-brand-safety/">SAFER framework</a> <b>should</b> also be used as a guide.</p>
<p>Government communicators <b>should </b>refer to their organisation&#8217;s guidelines on Influencer Marketing.</p>
