# The Associated Press Statement of News Values and Principles — Anonymous Sources, Attribution, Quotations
SOURCE_URL: https://accountablejournalism.org/ethics-codes/news-values-and-principles
DOMAIN: journalism

## Core commitment

We always strive to identify all the sources of our information, shielding them with anonymity only when they insist upon it and when they provide vital information — not opinion or speculation; when there is no other way to obtain that information; and when we know the source is knowledgeable and reliable.

## Anonymous sources — the three-part test

Under AP's rules, material from anonymous sources may be used only if **all three** conditions hold:
1. The material is information, not opinion or speculation, and is vital to the news report.
2. The information is not available except under the conditions of anonymity imposed by the source.
3. The source is reliable and in a position to have accurate information.

Process requirements:
- Reporters must get approval from their news manager **before** sending the story to the desk. The manager vets the material for compliance, must know the source's identity, and is obligated (like the reporter) to keep it confidential. Only after vetting should editors allow transmission.
- Reporters should proceed with interviews assuming they are on the record; if a source wants conditions, negotiate them at the start, and try again at the end of the interview to move information back on the record.
- Before agreeing to use anonymous material, the reporter should ask **how the source knows** the information is accurate, ensuring the source has direct knowledge. Reporters may not agree to a source's request that AP not pursue additional comment or information.
- **The AP routinely seeks and requires more than one source.** Stories should be held while attempts are made to reach additional sources for confirmation or elaboration. In rare cases, one source suffices — when the material comes from an authoritative figure providing information so detailed there is no question of its accuracy.
- The story must explain **why** the source requested anonymity, and, when relevant, the source's motive for disclosing the information. If a story hinges on documents rather than interviews, the reporter must describe how the documents were obtained, to the extent possible.
- The story must provide attribution that establishes the source's credibility — simply "a source" is not allowed. Be as descriptive as possible (e.g., "according to top White House aides," "a senior official in the British Foreign Office"). A source's description must never be altered without consulting the reporter.
- Do not say a person "declined comment" if they are already quoted anonymously; do not attribute information to anonymous sources when it is obvious or well known — state it as fact instead.
- Stories using anonymous sources must carry a reporter's byline. Any complaints/questions about the authenticity of anonymous material must be promptly brought to the news manager's attention.

## Definitions (ground rules for attribution)

- **On the record:** Information usable with no caveats, quoting the source by name.
- **Off the record:** Information that cannot be used for publication at all.
- **Background:** Information publishable only under conditions negotiated with the source — generally without the source's name, but with an agreed description of their position. AP reporters should push back on group background briefings and try to move them on the record.
- **Deep background:** Information usable but without any attribution — the source doesn't want to be identified even anonymously.

Information obtained under any of these can still be pursued with other sources for placement on the record.

## Pickups from other news organizations (claims sourced to a third-party report)

Reports from other news organizations based on anonymous sources require the **most careful scrutiny** before being used in AP's own report. The same three-part rule applies: material must be factual and obtainable no other way; the story must be truly significant and newsworthy; use must be authorized by a manager; the story must be balanced and comment must be sought. Before picking up such a story, AP must make a bona fide effort to get it on the record or, at minimum, confirm it through AP's own sources — and should not hesitate to hold the story if there are doubts. If used, source material must be attributed to the originating outlet, including that outlet's own description of its sources.

## Providing attribution generally

Give the full name of a source and as much information as needed to identify the source and explain why they are credible (age, title, organization, hometown where appropriate). If quoting someone from a written document (report, email, news release), say so explicitly. Information from the internet must be vetted to AP's accuracy standards and attributed to the original source. For lengthy stories, attribution can be consolidated in an extended editor's note detailing interviews, research and methodology — the goal is to give the reader enough information to have full confidence in the story's veracity.

## Quotations

The same care used to ensure quotes are accurate should ensure quotes are not taken out of context. Quotations are never altered, even to fix grammar. If a quote is too murky to paraphrase accurately, it should not be used. Stories should provide the setting in which a quotation was obtained (press conference, phone interview, hallway conversation) since that context bears on how the claim should be weighed.

## Corrections

Staffers must notify supervisory editors as soon as possible of errors or potential errors. A correction must always be explicitly labeled as a correction — euphemisms like "recasts," "fixes," "clarifies" are not used for factual-error corrections.

## Responses (fairness as a corroboration/verification step)

AP must make significant efforts to reach anyone portrayed negatively in a story, and give them reasonable time to respond before moving the story; if the parties involved cannot be reached, the story must explain what efforts were made to reach them.
