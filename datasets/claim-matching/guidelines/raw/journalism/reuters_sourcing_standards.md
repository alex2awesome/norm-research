## Sourcing (core principle)

Accuracy entails honesty in sourcing. Reuters' reputation for accuracy, and for freedom from bias, rests on the credibility of its sources.

- A Reuters journalist or camera is always the best source on a witnessed event.
- A named source is always preferable to an unnamed source.
- Reuters should never deliberately mislead in sourcing, quote a source saying one thing on the record and something contradictory on background, or cite sources in the plural when only one exists.
- Anonymous sources are the weakest sources.

Practical rules:
- Use named sources wherever possible; they are responsible for the information they provide (though Reuters remains liable for accuracy, balance and legal risk). Press sources to go on the record.
- Unnamed sources may be used only when they provide information of market or public interest not available on the record. Reuters alone is responsible for the accuracy of such information.
- Cross-check information wherever possible: two or more sources are better than one. In assessing unnamed-source information, weigh the source's track record, position and motive.
- Talk to sources on all sides of a deal, dispute, negotiation or conflict.
- Give as much context and detail as possible about sources — named or anonymous — to let the information be authenticated. Be explicit about what is not known.
- Reuters will publish news from a single, anonymous source only in exceptional cases, when it is credible information from a trusted source with direct knowledge of the situation; single-source stories require special authorisation.

## Gradation and use of unnamed sources (from "The Essentials of Reuters Sourcing")

**When to source:** Every statement in every story must be sourced unless it is an established fact or clearly in the public domain (e.g., court documents) or the reporter/photographer witnessed it directly.

**Location of sourcing:** Aim to source every story within the first two paragraphs; stories of great importance should be sourced in the first paragraph. A contentious statement must be sourced every time it appears — qualifying it with "alleged" is not protection against defamation. If a story is inflammatory or an allegation, the source is given *before* the claim (e.g., "X accused Y of..." not "Y did...").

**Next best is a named source:** A named source doesn't remove responsibility for what is quoted — even named-source information should normally be checked and balanced, especially in conflict or negotiation. Naming gives more protection if a story is challenged.

**Anonymous sourcing rules:**
- Unnamed sources must have direct knowledge of the information, or represent an authority with direct knowledge. Reliability declines the further the source is from the event.
- Vague descriptions ("a source," "sources," "observers," "informed sources," "reliable sources") are unacceptable — specificity is required (e.g., "a company executive close to the transaction").
- Stories based on anonymous sources require particularly rigorous cross-checking — normally two or three sources.
- Unnamed sources rank, in order of strength: **authoritative source** (real authority on the specific issue) > **official source** (access in an official capacity, limited to their field) > **designated source** (e.g., diplomatic, conference, intelligence sources with access to reliable information on the topic).

**Single-source stories:** Must be the exception and require approval by an immediate supervisor (bureau chief, unit head, or editor in charge), who must be satisfied the source is authoritative. For a single-source story, the informant must be an actual policymaker or participant with first-hand knowledge, or an official spokesperson speaking on background — subject to particular scrutiny against manipulation. Supervisor approval is noted on the outgoing copy.

**Honesty in sourcing:** Never use pseudonyms. Never cite sources in the plural when there is only one. Do not quote a source as declining comment if that same source gave information off the record; if on-record comment is still needed, seek it from another individual.

**Avoiding manipulation:** Every source has a motive; identify it and weigh it against other evidence. Two sources are always better than one — seek at least one from each side of a dispute. Give greatest weight to sources with a track record of accurate, unmisleading information. Try to disprove as well as prove a story; consult independent third parties to gauge reliability (e.g., a banker not involved in a deal, a think-tank analyst).

**Checking back with sources:** Reuters never submits stories, scripts or images to sources for pre-publication vetting — this breaches independence. Reuters may check back with a source only to verify a quote or satisfy itself of factual reliability, without giving the source a chance to retract or materially alter the information.

**Allegations and contentious attacks:** Sources cannot make allegations or vituperative attacks behind a cloak of anonymity. Unnamed sources from one side of a conflict may be quoted only on facts, not opinions, about the other side. A source wanting to attack an individual, organization or country must speak on the record, unless a senior official is making a considered, clearly newsworthy policy statement — and the story must state both that the informant volunteered the information and that they are an official.

## Reporting rumours

Reuters aims to report facts, not rumours, but must sometimes explain why a market is moving. Normal procedure:
1. Confirm from at least two sources what the rumour actually is, without spreading it.
2. Attempt to determine whether it is true.
3. Write a report noting that comment was sought (and whether it was obtained), and that checking continues.

In fast-moving exceptional circumstances where a story must move before verification: have a second reporter check the rumour while the story is written; keep the description of the rumour vague so as not to lend it credibility; state explicitly that Reuters is checking; if confirmed or denied, issue a follow-up at equal priority. Never write of "unconfirmed rumours" as if that phrase makes them reportable — a rumour is by definition unconfirmed.

## Pickups from competing/other news organizations

Pickups must always name the originating source (newspaper, website, broadcaster, rival agency); using someone else's report does not exonerate Reuters from responsibility for accuracy, balance and legal soundness. Reporters must make every effort to independently check a pickup; if confirmation is not possible, the story must say so explicitly. Reuters must not rely on secondhand accounts of another outlet's report (e.g., from a trader) — it must see, hear or read the original report itself before running a story about it.

Do not use "reports" or "unconfirmed reports" as the basis for a story on their own — quote an acceptable source commenting on them instead. Avoid vague constructions like "it was not known..." — attribute the not-knowing to a named source (e.g., "Police said they did not know...").

## Attribution

Proper attribution is required for any material not originated by Reuters (text, photograph, video). It is insufficient to label material simply "handout" — the specific source must be identified (e.g., "Greenpeace Video," "U.S. Army Photo"). Failure to attribute clearly may expose Reuters to charges of plagiarism.

## Social media / user-generated content verification

Twitter and similar sites are not sources in themselves — it is wrong to say a story was "sourced to Twitter." Verification of social-media material is a major issue: content might not be what it purports to be, due to sloppy posting or deliberate deceit. Strict criteria apply before using such material, and any residual uncertainty about provenance must be made explicit to the reader. Checks include confirming an account is official (e.g., cross-referencing with other reputable coverage) and watching for "sleeper" or "shadow" accounts designed to spread misinformation. If a claim cannot be verified, Reuters may report only that the claim was posted, note what is/isn't known, and continue trying to confirm or knock it down independently.
