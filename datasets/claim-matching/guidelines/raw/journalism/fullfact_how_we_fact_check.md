# How we fact check (Full Fact)
SOURCE_URL: https://fullfact.org/about/how-we-fact-check/
DOMAIN: journalism

Full Fact is the UK's independent fact-checking charity. Its published methodology describes a claim-verification pipeline built around understanding the full implication of a claim (not just its literal words), contacting the claimant, gathering a broad evidence base weighted toward primary sources, and requiring independent review before publication.

## Step 1: Understand what has actually been said

Before evidence-gathering begins, Full Fact's process starts by carefully establishing **what claim is actually being made** — including the claim's underlying assumption, not merely its surface wording. The methodology is explicit that a claim can be built from factually correct individual facts while still being **misleading or incorrect as an overall point** — i.e., the truth of the constituent facts does not guarantee the truth of the claim they are assembled to support. This means the checking process must evaluate both:
- **The evidence cited in the claim itself** (are the specific numbers/facts asserted actually correct), and
- **The underlying assumption or inference the claim relies on** (does the correct evidence actually establish the broader point being made using it).

This two-layer test — factual accuracy of the components, plus validity of the inference drawn from them — is a recurring structural feature across fact-checking methodologies (compare PolitiFact's "Half True: partially accurate but takes things out of context" category) and is directly relevant to claim-matching: evidence can "match" a claim's literal sub-facts while still failing to support the claim's overall thrust.

## Step 2: Contact the claimant

Unless the source of a claim is self-evident, Full Fact's process is to **contact the claimant directly** to ask about their source and to gather any additional information needed to understand it, and to give them a right of reply before publication. This mirrors PolitiFact's and FactCheck.org's equivalent step, and reflects a general norm across the fact-checking field: the person who made the claim should be given the opportunity to substantiate it before the checking organization proceeds to independent research and publishes a verdict.

## Step 3: Gather evidence, weighted toward primary sources

Full Fact's process gathers a wide range of evidence relevant to the claim, and applies explicit source-quality preferences:
- It aims to provide **links to primary sources for all factual assertions wherever possible.**
- It aims to provide **at least two sources to verify the central claim** of any given fact-check, **unless there is only one relevant source available** (in which case that limitation is implicit in the single citation, rather than papered over).
- It explicitly **prefers linking to data tables, legal documents, or other primary sources rather than to press releases or executive summaries** of statistical releases — i.e., when a press release characterizes an underlying dataset, Full Fact's practice is to go around the press release and cite the dataset itself, since a press release is itself only a secondary characterization of the primary evidence.
- When a topic falls outside the researching staff's own expertise, they contact outside subject-matter experts rather than relying solely on their own reading of primary documents.

This gives a concrete, generalizable **source-count and source-tier rule**: (a) prefer two independent sources over one wherever more than one exists; (b) within available sources, always prefer the primary document (data table, statute, transcript) over any secondary characterization of it (press release, summary, news write-up).

## Step 4: Independent review before publication

**Every piece Full Fact publishes is reviewed by another researcher** before it goes live, specifically to check that it is correct, impartial, and clearly written — an internal second-rater check analogous to PolitiFact's multi-editor vote, though structured as a single independent reviewer rather than a panel. For politically sensitive topics, Full Fact's Director additionally reviews the piece before publication, adding a second, more senior layer of review precisely where the risk of bias or reputational consequence is highest.

## Claim selection and prioritization

Full Fact fact-checks claims that are in public debate and of public interest — this includes, but is not limited to, claims made by politicians and by the media. Selection is prioritized toward claims that have **the most potential to cause harm to people's lives** if left unchecked, and core subject areas are informed by public-concern polling (the methodology references the Ipsos MORI Issues Index) covering areas such as crime, immigration, the law, education, health and social care, and the economy — i.e., claim selection is driven by a combination of public salience and potential real-world harm, similar in spirit to PolitiFact's "reach" and "public interest" filters.

## Transparency and accountability after publication

Full Fact states that it provides **links to all sources so that readers can check what has been said for themselves and draw their own conclusions** — the same "replicability" principle codified formally in the IFCN Code of Principles (of which Full Fact is a signatory). Beyond the individual fact-check, Full Fact also follows up on its findings by pressing public figures and institutions for corrections or retractions, and by publicly naming those who fail to respond or correct the record — extending the verification act beyond a single published verdict into an ongoing accountability process.

## Relevance to claim-matching

Full Fact's pipeline contributes three reusable elements to a claim-matching methodology: (1) an explicit **two-layer evaluation** — check the literal sub-facts, then separately check whether the inference/assumption built on those sub-facts actually holds; (2) a **quantitative source rule** — require at least two independent corroborating sources for the central claim when more than one exists, and always prefer a primary document over a secondary characterization of it (e.g., a dataset over a press release quoting the dataset); and (3) a **mandatory independent second-reviewer step** before any verdict is finalized, with an escalated review tier for higher-stakes/sensitive claims.
