# Guidelines for Reviewers & Criteria for Publication | PLOS ONE
SOURCE_URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/reviewer-guidelines
DOMAIN: academic

PLOS ONE's review model is explicitly designed to separate "importance"/impact judgments (not assessed) from technical and evidentiary soundness (the actual review criteria). This makes it one of the clearest published statements of what "claims are supported by evidence" means as a checkable criterion, independent of subjective significance.

## The seven criteria for publication (from https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/criteria-for-publication)

"Unlike many journals which attempt to use the peer review process to determine whether or not an article reaches the level of 'importance' required by a given journal, PLOS ONE uses peer review to determine whether a paper is technically rigorous and meets the scientific and ethical standard for inclusion in the published scientific record."

1. The study presents the results of original research.
2. Results reported have not been published elsewhere.
3. **Experiments, statistics, and other analyses are performed to a high technical standard and are described in sufficient detail.** "Experiments must have been conducted rigorously, with appropriate controls and replication. Sample sizes must be large enough to produce robust results, where applicable. Methods and reagents must be described in sufficient detail for another researcher to reproduce the experiments described."
4. **Conclusions are presented in an appropriate fashion and are supported by the data.** This is the core claim-matching criterion, quoted here in full: "The data presented in the manuscript must support the conclusions drawn. Submissions will be rejected if the interpretation of results is unjustified or inappropriate, so authors should avoid overstating their conclusions. Authors may discuss possible implications for their results as long as these are clearly identified as hypotheses instead of conclusions." Note the explicit hedging rule: speculative implications are permitted only if labeled as hypotheses, not asserted as findings.
5. The article is presented in an intelligible fashion and is written in standard English.
6. The research meets all applicable standards for the ethics of experimentation and research integrity.
7. The article adheres to appropriate reporting guidelines and community standards for data availability.

## "What to Assess" — the reviewer's working checklist

When writing the review itself, reviewers are told to consider the following questions (verbatim):

- "What are the main claims of the paper and how significant are they for the discipline?"
- "Are the claims properly placed in the context of the previous literature? Have the authors treated the literature fairly?" — this is a direct instruction to check whether novelty/priority claims are honest about what prior work already showed.
- "Do the data and analyses fully support the claims? If not, what other evidence is required?" — the single most direct statement of the claim-evidence matching task: reviewers must identify the *gap* between what is claimed and what is shown, and specify what additional evidence would close it.
- Where a protocol is provided (e.g., for a randomized controlled trial), "are there any important deviations from it? If so, have the authors explained adequately why the deviations occurred?" — i.e., claims of adherence to a pre-registered plan must be checked against the actual conduct.
- "Are original data deposited in appropriate repositories and accession/version numbers provided...?"
- "Does the study conform to any relevant guidelines such as CONSORT, MIAME, QUORUM, STROBE, and the Fort Lauderdale agreement?"
- "Are details of the methodology sufficient to allow the experiments to be reproduced?"
- "Is any software created by the authors freely available?"
- "Is the manuscript well organized and written clearly enough to be accessible to non-specialists?"

## Registered Reports: a stage-2 check that isolates claim-matching from other judgments

Because Registered Reports are pre-approved on rationale and methodology *before* results exist, the stage-2 review (after data collection) isolates the claim-evidence question almost completely from novelty/importance judgments. Reviewers are told to consider:

- "Have the authors followed the procedures outlined in their Registered Report Protocol? If not, do the changes make sense?"
- "Are there any additional experiments or exploratory results not outlined in the Registered Report Protocol? If so, were they reasonable and methodologically sound?"
- "**Are the conclusions supported by the data, and do they address the hypothesis from the original Registered Report Protocol?**" — this makes explicit that a conclusion must both (a) be supported by the data presented and (b) actually answer the question the study set out to ask; a well-supported but off-target conclusion still fails.

## Study Protocols (pre-registration review, no results yet)

Even before data exist, PLOS ONE reviewers check whether the *planned* analysis would be capable of supporting the intended claims: "Is the protocol technically sound and planned in a manner that will lead to a meaningful outcome and allow testing of the stated hypotheses?" This is a forward-looking version of claim-matching — checking whether the design, if executed as planned, could in principle produce evidence adequate to the hypothesis.

## Process notes relevant to claim assessment

- PLOS ONE uses a structured reviewer form; the free-text review ("Review Comments to the Author") is where the claim-evidence analysis above is expected to appear.
- Reviewers may consult COPE's position statement and guidelines on editing/suppressing reviewer reports; PLOS states that reviewer comments intended for authors are not edited except for inappropriate language or confidentiality breaches, which mirrors Nature's non-censorship policy.
- Editors, not reviewers, make the final publication decision, weighing reviewer assessments of the criteria above alongside the authors' rebuttal.
