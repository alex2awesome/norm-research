#3 by Oskatat
From the FAQ:

No. We've been over the issue multiple times. Here is a summary of why not, copied from the forums:
There are several problems with making the review content compulsory. First of all, it would significantly reduce the number of ratings on the site. This would pose several problems, including:
  • We'd need to remove existing anonymous ratings, as the reduced number of reviews would mean that top list entries would be very, very hard to overcome, if not virtually impossible unless the site's viewership suddenly increased tenfold.
  • At the same time, removing existing ratings would mean that we'd make a gigantic mess of the site. The top list would get completely scrambled, and manipulating the top list would become much easier due to the significantly reduced number of reviews to judge rankings by.
  • Removing reviews, actually, does not stop drive-bys. An excellent example of this is Amazon. I recommend doing a quick research on the topic, as "spam reviews" are a significant problem with the system either way.
Additionally, most users that do not review, according to statistics, would not rate if they had to write a review to do so. Another problem with forcing reviews is that drive-by 0.5-star ratings would amount into a significant level of written hate reviews, which looks really bad and isn’t fair to the author. This would both make moderation increasingly hard and authors more likely to quit due to the negative feedback.
____________________________________________________________


People who don't like your story are more likely to rate it and more likely to leave a review. However, people who don't like your story and don't have the option to leave a rating are much more likely to instead write you a negative review. More so than people who like your story are likely to give you a good review instead of a good rating. Overall, it would have a negative effect on the averages of most writers.

If you suspect someone is creating additional accounts just to manipulate your ratings, send a support ticket - that's what they're for

These complaints are neither impressive or at all novel. The general popularity for some authors to go complaining about the rating system speaks to how uncommon it is for them to follow the advice of never looking too hard at the statistics. For their own sake as well as ours.

These complaints are flawed on several counts. Firstly is that while the system has some observable flaws, there is basically no effective way to mitigate or eliminate them more than is already being done. Some alternative rating suggestions have been made more out of a frustration with the current system rather than them actually being superior, just different, and usually an understanding of the underlying maths can allow anyone to conclude the suggested systems are easier not a significant improvement, the act of change having a larger negative impact on the site efficacy and community than any conceivable benefit, and the actual mathematical outcomes being effectively identical. There has been no lack in due diligence in designing the site features and algorithms.

The so called unfair “weight” of 0.5 ratings is in many ways false. It has the same weight on rating a 0 would have if that and 1 were the only rating options. It’s a downvote, with the option for some people to downvote less intensely by using something between 0.5 and the average rating instead of a straight 0.5. Additionally your rating are only sensitive to it as much as you are obsessed with the average rating instead of realizing that a higher rating count is better no matter what those ratings are. Rating mea s popularity, even low ratings. Even 0.5 ratings are contributing to the statistics of a fiction in a positive way.

Also, complaints about bot ratings are highly speculative. People don’t actually know how many bots are going around, and I know for a fact that the mods deal with bots relatively well because the mods for this site are highly responsive and attentive to the community. RR is actually relatively small enough for a lot of stuff to be manually administrated by human mods, as evidenced by the approval process for fictions. We don’t really suffer for any lack of oversight. Any influence bots might have overall should be minor, and attributing “bombing” ratings to bots speaks more to unhealthy frustration of certain authors than what is actually most likely to be happening.

I don’t get it why people think it strange that a book might suddenly attract a lot more criticism when breaking into a high ranking or into Trending. You got more attention, and that means more serious analysis and standards. It’s the top 200 for a reason and if it was easy to get in there I might question the validity of the entire rankings list if they were so easily supplanted. If you’re not on a major ranking readers will tend to care a lot less whether your book is something they like or not, and the only people likely to rate you are people who like you, but when you break into the rankings you catch the eye of people in more general and diverse preference circles, who aren’t predisposed to liking your book.

Anonymous internet people are the perfect way to rate fictions on mass because large sets of data points always trend towards an appropriate result. Though people might tend to rate things closer to what the ratings already are just because of how mob mentality works. The biggest thing though is that it’s a highly chaotic system. While things do trend towards an appropriate equilibrium in the long term wild fluctuation in the short terms will always have more sway on the presented value at any given time. The ratings effectively orbit around approximately where they should be rather than resting where they should be. They’ll likely always be some distance away from their “true” value because of these short term variations, so complaining about small shifts in the value is ill advised.

Most of all, authors are ultimately completely responsible for marketing their own books. RR is a platform, not your personal publishing agency. They do not represent you personally, and they aren’t invested in your particular success. You should be marketing yourself instead of leaving all the responsibility for accurately portraying the supposed quality of your work on a neutral third party. RR is marketing your work just by hosting it, but they are an independent party who don’t owe entitled authors anything. They do work to make this site fair, active, popular, and even supportive, but they do this out of voluntary cooperative with their community rather than out of some kind of servitude. Their platform works with you, not for you.

Lastly, the biggest issue of authors complaining about some minor grievances about ratings and reviews, is that they mostly complain on the basis of struggling to mirror the success of the most visible examples on the platform. Those are the most superficial sample of the RR fiction pool. If you see them, they are out of the ordinary and cannot be reasonably used for a baseline statistical comparison for success. People define their image of success using these exemplars but ignore the fact that there are likely dozens of variations of being decently “successful” that likely aren’t obvious to them. Books that seem to be doing fine in their own weird corner despite being hard to find. Authors that are completely content with their fanbase despite whatever ratings they may have. Books that hardly ever update and still have an enjoyable community waiting for them on every new post. I just really don’t like the sound of whining when it’s always about the same old argument that says more about the people complaining than the thing they’re complain about.
