Episodic Program

Episodic

Program

The upcoming Episodic Lab will take place in May 2026!

The Sundance Institute Episodic Program provides artists with the tools and training to develop their original series and pilot script. Offering one of the few training grounds for independent voices, the program is designed to help creators move their projects and careers forward. Episodic Program Calendar

Submissions for the 2026 Lab are now closed. The next submission season will open in Summer of 2026 for an in-person Lab taking place in 2027.

The Sundance Institute Episodic Lab is a six-day program that offers writers the opportunity to workshop a pilot script, while developing their writing. Working with accomplished showrunners, Fellows will participate in one-on-one story meetings, craft workshops, and simulated writers’ rooms. Together, these meetings provide an elevated creative strategy for artists to advance their projects. Still have questions about the Episodic Lab? Read our FAQ

Continuum of Support

A unique attribute of Sundance Institute Labs is the high touch mentoring and support our Fellows receive long after the Lab has completed. This allows Fellows to fully realize the potential of their work, hone their skills in a dynamic and supportive environment, and position themselves for the best possible outcomes for their projects. This includes ongoing creative and strategic advice from the Episodic team and Advisors in addition to targeted introductions to agents and managers.

Spring Intensive

Recognizing that artists approach us at all stages of their early careers, we will design and lead an inaugural two-day intensive workshop serving an additional cohort of 10-12 diverse writers who were finalists for the Lab.

Year-round Gatherings

The Episodic Program hosts a series of events throughout the year to build a community of working writers in the episodic space. These include alumni and industry mixers; craft workshops; and sessions focused on pitching, staffing, and building creative skills.

Annual Pitch Parlor

Taking place annually each Spring in Los Angeles, Pitch Parlor will feature select Fellows and alumni from the most recent Labs who will present seven-minute pitches in front of invited TV producers, showrunners, managers, agents, and studio and network executives with the intention to celebrate and further introduce the work and writers with the wider industry.

Still have questions about the Episodic Lab? Read our FAQ

  1. When was the most recent submission period?
    • Open submission period for the 2026 Lab was October 6 – November 9, 2025.
  2.  Can I still apply for the May 2026 Lab after November 9, 2025?
    • Only if you secured a Deadline Waiver Code, yes.
  3. How do I use my Deadline Waiver Code to apply after November 9, 2025?
    • After November 9th, on the FilmFreeway Submission Page, the “SUBMIT” button will be replaced with a “WAIVERS ONLY” button. You enter here to begin your application and proceed as usual. 
  4.  When is the next submission season?
    • The next submission season will open in Summer of 2026 for an in-person Lab taking place in 2027.
  5. When is the 2026 Episodic Lab taking place?
    • May 15 – 20, 2026.
    • In April 2026, the Episodic Fellows begin with a few weeks of pre-lab development that includes virtual workshops preparing the writers and their materials leading up to the in-person Lab.
  6. Who is eligible for the lab?
    • Applicants must be 18 years of age or older at time of application.
    • Applicants of any nationality or residency are eligible.
    • International series writers at all levels are eligible.
  7. Who is not eligible?
    • Applicants who have sold a scripted episodic project (pilot or pitch) that has been produced for US television or streaming and shot with a major US studio or network are not eligible. 
    • Writers who have surpassed co-producer level in a writers room on a US based scripted adult series is not eligible.
  8. Will you consider writing teams?
    Yes. We will consider writing teams of two people, no more.
  9. Can I apply with a director, producer, or other attachment?
    This lab is designed for writers currently seeking creative development support and collaboration. Projects with existing exclusive producing partners are not eligible. If you have a non-exclusive creative attachment, only the 2 co-writers are eligible to apply to participate in the Lab. 
  10. What types of projects are eligible?
    • Full pilot drafts of a scripted series are eligible; we currently are not supporting unscripted nor documentary series at this year’s Lab.
    • Projects of any genre, intended for episodic series on visual platforms.
    • On-going, anthology, or limited series are eligible.
    • Live action and animated series are both eligible.
    • We currently are not supporting sketch format at this year’s Lab.
    • We currently are not supporting audio series at this year’s Lab.
    • Projects cannot be currently set up or pitching in the US.
    • Projects cannot have already found distribution, online or otherwise.
    • The draft must be submitted in the English language for evaluation but the project does not need to be intended to be produced in English.
    • The project should be new, or if previously submitted, it must be materially different from any and all versions previously submitted to the Episodic Lab.
    • The project must be the applicant’s original creation or adapted from optioned source material or material in the public domain.
    • The project shall not infringe the copyright or any other proprietary right of another individual or entity. The creator/applicant is responsible for all rights pertaining to the project.
    • Projects cannot currently have contractual ownership with a third party.
  11. Can I submit more than one project?
    • Yes. You must submit a separate submission for each project. Please note each submission will incur a separate $50 fee through FilmFreeway. 
  12. What do I need to submit in the application process?
    • An Original Pilot Script (formatted as a PDF with dated cover page)
    • Contact Information for writer(s): We allow for up to 2 writers per project; however, you must choose one person to be the primary applicant and your designated point of contact.
    • Bio(s) of the writer(s) (approximately 250 words per person).
    • Series Logline: a one-sentence description of your series (no more than 50 words) that conveys what your project is about. Please include running length of episodes ie: ½ hour or 1 hr.
    • Pilot Synopsis (no more than 500 words): a summary of your pilot episode.
    • Series Overview (no more than 500 words): This is a one-page description of your series that gives the reader a feel for its main characters, the basic plot and themes, and the tone and format. Imagine you’re describing your series to a friend in a way that conveys your excitement, clearly explains what your series is about, and gives them a reason to want to watch it.
    • Personal Statement (no more than 250 words): A brief written response to the following questions:
      • What is your personal connection to the material?
      • Why are you the best person to tell this story?
      • Why tell this story now?
    • Thematic Statement (no more than 250 words): a brief written response to the following questions:
      • What is the theme of your series? That is, what is the central idea or big question you are exploring? (For example, “In the wake of the ultimate betrayal, how does one continue to trust others?” OR “Are criminals born or are they made?” OR “Can a modern family still function like a traditional family?”)
      • How is this thematic question explored on an episode-to-episode basis in your series? (For example, “Each episode, the betrayal is seen through the eyes of a different character as its ripple effect permeates more and more lives,” OR “Each episode, we focus on a different criminal and the path that led to their crimes,” OR “Each episode, an unconventional family struggles to overcome conventional problems.”)
      • How does this theme inform each of your characters and their journeys over the course of the series? How does the theme connect emotionally to what your characters are going through?
    • Video Samples of Previous Work are optional: Provide one to three URL links to video samples of your previous work (not to exceed 10 minutes total in length). Samples may include short films, web series episodes, stand up sets, or other relevant video content. Please include passwords as needed.
    • Demographics are optional: To be filled out by the primary applicant and the additional applicant if applicable.
    • Application Terms: By submitting an application, applicants agree to the terms set forth by FilmFreeway and listed on the FilmFreeway page.
    • A $50 Non Refundable Processing Fee (payable by credit card via the online application).
  13. Is there an application fee to apply for the Episodic Lab?
    • Yes, there is a $50 nonrefundable processing fee, payable by credit card via the online application, FilmFreeway.
  14. How complete should the pilot script be?
    • You must submit a full, completed original pilot script in order to be considered.
    • Do not submit the feature, theatrical play adaptation, or any other version of your project as the primary draft for consideration.
  15. How do I upload my pilot script to FilmFreeway?
    • You must first create a FilmFreeway profile, then upload a pilot draft for each ‘project’ that you will select to submit. There will be a field for you to upload a PDF file and SAVE each project. Once you are ready to submit, FilmFreeway will prompt you to select one of your existing projects to submit for consideration. If your project is missing a file, FilmFreeway will label your submission as Incomplete. Not to worry; you can still upload a script file by logging in to FilmFreeway, go to ‘My Projects,’ and select the pilot after submitting your application.
  16. Will you consider series intended for international production?
    • Yes, although the project draft must be submitted in English.
  17. Will you consider adaptations of other material such as novels, magazine articles, films, etc.?
    • Yes. We will consider adaptations so long as the writer (or writers) has secured the rights to the original source material and can disclose the legal rights for the Sundance Institute to review.
  18. Do participants pay for travel and accommodation?
    • No. The Sundance Institute will pay for economy class airfare and provide accommodations for all participants. 
  19. What does the program cost?
    • Aside from an application fee of $50, participation in the Episodic Lab, including travel, food, and accommodations, is no cost to the Artists.
  20. Does the Sundance Institute own my pilot?
    • No. The Sundance Institute is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization and does not have an ownership stake in any projects that are submitted to, or selected for, the lab, nor are we attached in any producorial capacity. All lab Fellows must own or have approval from the project owner to participate.
  21. When will final decisions be made?
    • Applicants will receive email notification approximately mid April 2026 through FilmFreeway. If you do not hear about your status by April 30, you may reach out to the Episodic Program Team by emailing: episodicprogram@sundance.org

If you are interested in learning more about writing for the episodic space with Sundance Institute, visit collab.sundance.org. Sundance Collab is Sundance Institute’s digital space for artists to learn from experts & build a global filmmaking community with self-guided writing courses and resources.

Still don’t see your question answered here? Email episodicprogram@sundance.org with further questions.

Have technical questions regarding the submission site? Email support@filmfreeway.com

Episodic Lab

Application

The 2026 Episodic Lab accepts application from October 6 – November 9, 2025

DatesMay 15 – 20, 2026
Size of Lab8 projects per lab
Eligibility
  • Applicants must be 18 years of age or older at time of application.
  • The project must be the applicant’s original creation or adapted from optioned source material.
  • The project must be written in the English language, but it does not need to be intended to be fully produced in English.
  • The project should be new, or if previously submitted, it must be materially different from any and all versions previously submitted to Sundance Institute. Projects may not be already produced, sold to a third party, or distributed online or otherwise.
  • Applicants cannot have sold an episodic project (pilot or pitch) to a studio or network in the past that has been shot.
  • The project shall not infringe the copyright or any other proprietary right of another individual or entity. The creator/applicant is responsible for all rights pertaining to the project.
The Application ProcessApplicants are required to submit an original pilot script, their bio, a series logline, a series overview, their personal statement, a thematic statement, additional creative questions, and (optional) links to samples of previous work, along with a $50 nonrefundable processing fee. For further details on the application process, please see our FAQ section below.
DescriptionThe Sundance Institute Episodic Lab is a six-day program that offers writers the opportunity to workshop a pilot script, while developing their writing and pitching skills. Working with accomplished showrunners, non-writing creative producers, and executives, the Fellows participate in one-on-one story meetings, craft workshops, and writers rooms, which together provide an elevated creative strategy for their material from key industry players in positions to advance their projects and careers.

For more information about the Episodic Lab, please contact episodicprogram@sundance.org.
