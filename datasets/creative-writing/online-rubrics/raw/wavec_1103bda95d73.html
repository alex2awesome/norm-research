Learning to Read with Beverly Cleary: Beloved Books for Early Readers

readingcom
March 17, 2025March 13, 2025

Have you ever watched a child’s eyes light up when they meet Ramona Quimby for the first time? Beverly Cleary, one of America’s most cherished children’s authors, created characters that generations of young readers have befriended during their journey to reading fluency. 

What makes Cleary’s works so special isn’t just their humor and relatability—it’s how perfectly they align with what science tells us about how children develop reading skills. Her carefully crafted sentences, relatable scenarios, and memorable characters create the perfect bridge between learning to read and reading to learn.

Beverly Cleary’s Literary Legacy and Impact

Beverly Cleary’s contribution to children’s literature is nothing short of extraordinary. With over 91 million copies of her books sold worldwide in 25 different languages, her impact on early literacy spans multiple generations. Born in 1916 and passing away in 2021 at the age of 104, Cleary’s career as an author spanned over 50 years, producing more than 40 books that continue to resonate with children today.

What makes Cleary’s work particularly remarkable is her understanding of her audience. Before becoming an author, Cleary worked as a children’s librarian, where she encountered many young readers struggling to find books about “kids like us.” This experience informed her writing approach, creating characters and situations that reflect the everyday experiences of children.

According to a 2023 study by the National Literacy Trust, books featuring relatable characters and everyday scenarios significantly increase reading engagement among children aged 6-10, with 73% of children more likely to finish a book when they identify with the main character. This explains the enduring popularity of Cleary’s works, as characters like Ramona, Henry Huggins, and Ralph S. Mouse present authentic childhood experiences that young readers immediately recognize.

Cleary’s contribution to literacy is also evidenced by her numerous accolades, including the National Book Award, the Newbery Medal, and being named a “Living Legend” by the Library of Congress in 2000. According to the American Library Association, her books continue to be among the most frequently borrowed children’s titles in public libraries nationwide, with a 12% increase in circulation noted during the pandemic years when parents sought quality reading material for children at home.

Beverly Cleary’s works naturally contain a beneficial balance of familiar vocabulary and new terms for literacy development, which is important because high-frequency words are foundational to reading fluency.

A small subset of these words constitutes a significant portion of the text encountered by young readers:​

  • 12 high-frequency words make up approximately 25% of the words we read and write.​
  • 100 high-frequency words account for about 50% of the words in common texts.
  • Approximately 300 high-frequency words represent 75% of the words we read and write.

Beverly Cleary Books for Beginning Readers

Beverly Cleary’s books span a range of reading levels, making them suitable for children at various stages of literacy development. While some books may be more challenging for early readers, they offer excellent opportunities for read-aloud sessions with parents. These stories are perfect for building vocabulary, comprehension, and a love of storytelling, whether read independently or shared aloud.

Read-aloud Books For Emergent Readers (Ages 4-6)

  • The Ramona Books Picture Books – Simplified versions of the beloved Ramona stories, featuring more illustrations and shorter text, making them accessible for young listeners.
  • Two Dog Biscuits – A charming story about twins Jimmy and Janet who receive dog biscuits as gifts, ideal for early vocabulary development.
  • Janet’s Thingamajigs – A delightful picture book about a little girl who collects treasures, encouraging curiosity and language skills.

These books are perfect for parents to share with their children, enhancing their listening skills while fostering a deeper connection to reading.

For Developing Readers (Ages 6-8)

  • “Henry Huggins” (simplified editions) – The adventures of Henry and his dog Ribsy
  • “Ramona the Pest” – Ramona’s experiences in kindergarten
  • “The Mouse and the Motorcycle” – Ralph S. Mouse’s first adventure

These books contain slightly more complex sentence structures but still provide ample picture support. They’re ideal for children who have mastered basic phonics and are building reading fluency.

For Confident Readers (Ages 8-10)

  • The Complete Ramona Series – Following Ramona from age 4 through fourth grade
  • The Henry Huggins Series – Henry’s adventures on Klickitat Street
  • “Dear Mr. Henshaw” – A more advanced book dealing with family changes

The graduated reading levels across Cleary’s collection align well with literacy development principles, allowing children to advance their reading skills naturally while enjoying stories about familiar characters they’ve come to love.

How Beverly Cleary’s Writing Supports Reading Development

Beverly Cleary’s books align remarkably well with the science of reading. Her writing style incorporates many elements that research has shown to facilitate reading development and comprehension.

Decodable Text Structure

While Cleary’s books may not fit the strict definition of “decodable texts” used in early reading instruction, they feature a high frequency of regular phonetic patterns. This makes them an excellent choice for children who are beginning to gain confidence in reading. The repetition of common phonograms and sight words supports fluency, helping young readers gain comfort with the rhythm and flow of language.

Sequential Vocabulary Development

Cleary’s books introduce new vocabulary in context, often with supportive clues or explanations embedded in the narrative. This scaffolded approach to vocabulary acquisition reflects current best practices in literacy instruction.

Syntactic Complexity Progression

As children move through Cleary’s books from early readers to chapter books, they encounter increasingly complex sentence structures and narrative devices. This gradual progression supports the development of syntactic awareness, an essential component of reading comprehension.

Character Development and Inference Training

Cleary’s rich character development requires readers to make inferences about characters’ thoughts, feelings, and motivations—a crucial comprehension skill. By experiencing these inferential demands in the context of engaging stories, children develop their comprehension abilities without explicit instruction.

Use Beverly Cleary Books in Reading Practice

Incorporating Beverly Cleary’s books into structured reading practice can enhance their educational value. Here are practical ways to use her works to support reading development:

Read-Aloud First, Independent Reading Later

For younger children or those still developing fluency, start by reading Cleary’s books aloud. This builds vocabulary, comprehension, and motivation before asking children to read independently. 

Buddy Reading with Ramona

For children in the early stages of reading chapter books, try alternating pages or chapters with your child. This provides support while building confidence and fluency. The Ramona series is particularly well-suited for this approach, as the chapters often function as semi-independent episodes.

Character Journals

Have children keep a simple journal where they respond to the book from the perspective of Ramona, Henry, or other characters. This builds comprehension while providing writing practice.

Word Collection Activities

Encourage children to collect and record interesting words from Cleary’s books. This vocabulary-building activity helps children notice and remember new words. For example, from “Ramona the Pest,” children might collect words like “possessive,” “triumphant,” or “nuisance.”

Text-to-Self Connections

Cleary’s realistic fiction provides abundant opportunities for children to make connections between the text and their own experiences. 

Beyond the Beverly Cleary Books: Adaptations and Extensions

Beverly Cleary’s works have expanded beyond their original format, offering additional ways to engage with her characters and stories.

Audiobook Versions

Professional audiobook recordings of Cleary’s books, many narrated by acclaimed voice actors like Stockard Channing and Neil Patrick Harris, provide excellent models of fluent, expressive reading. 

Film and Television Adaptations

Several of Cleary’s books have been adapted for screen, including the “Ramona and Beezus” movie and PBS’s “Ramona” series. These adaptations can serve as valuable complements to the reading experience, particularly for visual learners.

Digital Extensions

Modern extensions of Cleary’s work include interactive e-books and educational games that build on her characters and settings. These digital resources can provide additional practice with specific reading skills while maintaining engagement with familiar characters.

Cross-Curricular Connections

Beverly Cleary’s books offer natural connections to social studies (changes in American childhood over decades), social-emotional learning (dealing with siblings, school challenges), and even mathematics (many of Ramona’s adventures involve concepts like time and money).

Bring Beverly Cleary Into Your Reading Journey

Beverly Cleary’s timeless stories continue to delight and support young readers decades after they were first published. Their balance of engaging content and accessible text makes them perfect companions on the journey to reading proficiency. By integrating these beloved books into a structured approach to reading instruction, parents and educators can harness both the joy and the educational value of Cleary’s work.
