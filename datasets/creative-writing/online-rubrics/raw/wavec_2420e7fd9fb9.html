Accurate ratings

#1 by jcbutterrtoast
A long time ago, I ran into a weird problem rating a fiction. I wanted to rate everything middle-of-the-road but give the overall score a 5-star rating. Didn't make sense. Why rate the overall experience better than the pieces of the experience (characters/story/etc.)? So, yeah. It's been on my mind for a while. Stewing. Today, I figured it out.

RoyalRoadl ratings don't actually measure what matters most to readers. Like. Style/story/grammar/character matters, but only because they hint at what matters more, which is "was is worth it." RoyalRoadl doesn't have a "worth it" score. So, I used the "Overall" score as a placeholder when writing my review.

another way of putting it: a lot of stories with bad grammar and meandering stories I'd give a perfect score on "was it worth it" because I found them meaningful, somehow. I hope this makes sense.



btw, I'm not suggesting RoyalRoadl change up ratings or anything. Just want to point out that these rating categories are probs more useful to writers than readers. Writers can use the style/story/grammar/character ratings to make those parts of their stories better, after all.

Idk what a perfect rating system for readers would look like. Maybe split an overall "worth it" score into two categories, meaningfulness (was the content good) and readability (was it easy to read). Something. Thoughts?

I've done the same thing a couple of times.  Found their prose middling and their grammar lacking, but still gave them relatively high marks for 'overall' because, despite those deficits, I actually genuinely enjoyed the content.  Figure anything I'm genuinely interested in, something that hooks me and makes me actively want to keep reading, deserves a subjective rating of at least 4 stars and usually 4.5 or 5.
