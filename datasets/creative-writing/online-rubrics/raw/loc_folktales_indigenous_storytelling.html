Indigenous storytelling in the US and Canada

Introduction

The following resources pertain to Indigenous stories and storytellers in the US and Canada. Many of the stories included are subject to restrictions based on seasonality, occasion, topic, teller, audience, or other criteria. The AFC works to ensure that access to these materials via its digital collections and onsite in the Reading Room is available only in accordance with these restrictions. Please contact the tribal communities to which these stories and storytellers belong to ensure their proper usage.

Digital Collections

  • Ancestral Voices
    This first presentation in the Ancestral Voices project is the result of a collaborative venture among the AFC, the Passamaquoddy Tribe of Maine, and the creators of TK Labels and Mukurtu CMS. Passamaquoddy elders have provided cultural narratives and added traditional knowledge about Passamaquoddy recordings, which were spoken in a French-influenced dialect of the Passamaquoddy language in 1890. Storytelling materials are available under subject headings "tales" "trickster tales" and "legends."

Selected Collections from the American Folklife Center

  • Curtis Cook Zuni Pueblo storytelling collection
    Call Number: AFC 2004/010
    Published/Created: 1961-1985, 2005
    Collection of field recordings, photographs, and notes by linguist Curtis Cook, who learned the Zuni language and documented the stories of elderly Zuni speakers while undertaking a translation of the Bible into the Zuni language during the 1960s and 1970s. Sound recordings (1964-1967) include narratives told by Zuni storytellers Longkeena Nash and Tom Ideque and others. Manuscripts include Zuni stories and translation exercises in Zuni, and some interlineal translations in English of Zuni recordings from the Doris Duke collection.
  • Evelyn Robe Finkbeiner collection of Dakota songs and stories
    Call Number: AFC 1977/021
    Published/Created: 1947-1948
    Field recordings of Dakota, Lakota, Omaha, and Cheyenne songs, dance songs, stories, prayers, and recordings of children reading samples from primary readers recorded by Evelyn Robe Finkbeiner from July 22 - August 29, 1947. The collection also includes correspondence from the collector, Evelyn Robe Finkbeiner (1946-1978) about the recordings, and a letter from the donor, Rosebud Yellow Robe (August 1976).
  • Frederica de Laguna, Mary Jane Downs Lenz, and Catharine McClellan collection of Tlingit, Atna Athabaskan, and Southern Tutchone recordings, 1954
    Call Number: AFC 1962/014
    Copies of audio recordings as well as field notes and transcripts made by Frederica de Laguna with assistance of Mary Jane Downs Lenz and Catharine McClellan in Yakutat, Chitina, and Chistochina, Alaska and Kluskshu, Yukon in 1954. The recordings in this collection contain songs and stories primarily by Tlingit and Ahtena of the Yakutat and Dry Bay area of Southeast Alaska and some of Aleut, Haida, or Southern Tutchone origin. The collection includes songs and stories on Tlingit history as well as ceremonial and cultural protocols. Also included with many songs and narratives are introductions, discussions, vocabulary, explanations and/or translations by the performers or by Frederica de Laguna.
  • Frederica de Laguna and Catharine McClellan Tlingit Collection, 1952
    Call Number: AFC 1962/015
    Copies of audio recordings of Tlingit songs and stories recorded by Frederica de Laguna and Catharine McClellan on fieldwork expeditions in Yakutat, Alaska, June-August 1952. Tlingit recordings include songs, and traditional narratives including stories of Raven. Songs and stories are primarily in Tlingit, with a few songs in Southern Tuchone and Yukon Athabaskan.
  • Fred Lukoff collection of Onondaga wire recordings
    Call Number: AFC 1970/007
    Published/Created: 1948
    Collection of field recordings of Onondaga material, originally part of Mss. Rec. 3, American Philosophical Society. Includes Onondaga ceremonies, welfare and Buffalo dances, condolence ceremonies; dances, songs, stories, texts, recipes, and Oneida hymns and other songs recorded by Fred Lukoff, July-August 1948.
  • Jane Willets collection of Ottawa language wire recordings
    Call Number: AFC 1970/005
    Published/Created: 1947
    Collection of field recordings of Ottawa material, including hymns, tales, word list, interviews, Nanabojo myths, legends, interview on history of Ottawa, etc. Recorded by Jane Willets, 1947. Copies of the notes and translations were provided by Frank Ettawageshik (Jane Willets' son).
  • Kenneth Croft collection of Cheyenne wire recordings
    Call Number: AFC 1970/008
    Published/Created: 1948-1949
    Collection of field recordings of Northern and Southern Cheyenne conversations, instructions to children, songs, and stories performed by Blue Elk, Run Away, Charles Redbird, William Guerrier, John Standing (or Stands-in) Timber, Turkey Legs, Mortimer Dreamer, and unidentified others. Recorded in Montana and Oklahoma by Kenneth Croft, 1948-1949 with a grant from the American Philosophical Society.
  • Maine Folklife Center collection
    Call Number: AFC 2012/047
    Published/Created: 1960-2011
    Collection of field recordings, primarily of interviews with various residents of Maine and the Atlantic Maritime region on topics including boats and boat building, lobstering, fishing and commercial fisheries, environmental issues, labor relations, railroads, lumbering, agriculture -- including potato farming, horses and horse racing, maple sugar making, foodways, folksongs, folklore, folk medicine, folk music, folk music festivals, homemaking, paper industry, politics, town meetings, local histories, cross-border migration, wildlife and hunting, smuggling, American Indians, sporting camps, schools, tall tales, vernacular architecture, material culture, beliefs, religion, holidays, various immigrant and ethnic groups, women in Maine, and other topics recorded from about 1960 to 2011 by Edward "Sandy" Ives and others, including students at the University of Maine. The collection includes the Northeast Archives of Folklore and Oral History.
  • Mary Louise Defender Wilson and Keith Bear concert and interview collection, 2006 August 16
    Call Number: AFC 2006/031
    Collection of video recordings of a performance of storytelling by National Heritage Fellowship Award winner Mary Louise Defender Wilson, a Dakotah/Hidatsa elder enrolled with the Standing Rock Indian Reservation in North Dakota and by Keith Bear, a Mandan/Hidatsa flute player and storyteller enrolled with the Fort Berthold Indian Reservation, North Dakota, performed in the Coolidge Auditorium, Library of Congress on August 16, 2006, as part of the Homegrown concert series sponsored by the American Folklife Center. Each performer was interviewed on the same day by Troyd Geist, folklorist, North Dakota Council on the Arts and these interviews (about 46 minutes each) form part of this collection.
  • Telapna:we -- Zuni verbal art in performance collection
    Call Number: AFC 1996/073
    Published/Created: 1966-1967
    Copies of field recordings of 15 Zuni storytellers recorded in Zuni, New Mexico in August 1988 and in January 1989 (24 audio cassettes); Zuni language storytelling programs produced for radio aired on KSHI-FM radio in December 1989 (19 audio cassettes); and English language versions of Zuni storytelling programs (10 audio cassettes).
  • Zuni Pueblo storytelling collection
    Call Number: AFC 1996/073
    Published/Created: 1966-1967
    Collection was initiated in the 1960s when the Zuni tribal government received funding to record Zuni oral history and collective memories. Recordings were made in 1966-1967 under the direction of the American West Center of the University of Utah, sponsored by the American Indian Oral History Project, funded by the Doris Duke Foundation. The Zuni Tribal council and its governor, Robert E. Lewis, approved the transfer of the master tapes to the American Folklife Center in 1996.
  • D. J. Battiest-Tomasi interview / conducted by Rodger Harris, 2011 May 11.
    Call Number: AFC 2011/031
    DJ (Deborah Jan) Battiest-Tomasi is a Choctaw - Chickasaw woman who has served in the Sundance Ceremonies of the Lakotas. She plays the flute for clients and patients in her practice for the Oklahoma City Indian Health Clinic and has worked in the mental health field for 22 years. Oral history interview with Homegrown 2011 performer, Choctaw storyteller, and flute player, D. J. Battiest-Tomasi. Interview conducted by Rodger Grimes Harris, coordinator, Oklahoma Folklife Council, on May 11, 2011 at the Oklahoma City Indian Health Clinic.
  • Tim Tingle interview conducted by Rodger Harris, 2011 May 10.
    Call Number: AFC 2011/032
    Video oral history interview with author and storyteller Tim Tingle, conducted by Rodger Harris, for the Oklahoma Historical Society and the Library of Congress on May 10, 2011. Tingle talks about his upbringing in Houston, Texas; playing basketball; race relations; studying literature in college; his theater and dance experience; work experience; the Texas Folklore Society and J. Frank Dobie; his Choctaw family stories; literary influences; writing; and how he began storytelling and research on Choctaws. He walked a re-enactment of the Trail of Tears with Charlie Jones, listening to Charlie's stories. He talks about the border, ethnicity, and storytelling in Spanish. The interview was conducted in advance of his performance in the Homegrown 2011 series at the Library of Congress on June 29, 2011.

Selected Public Programming from the American Folklife Center

Since its inception in 1976, the American Folklife Center has routinely hosted public programs at the Library of Congress in the form of concerts, lectures, panels, and symposia. From 2006 on, most of these public programs have been video recorded and made available online. Below, find videos of public programming related to Indigenous stories and storytelling in the US and Canada.

Mary Louise Defender Wilson and Keith Bear perform Sioux and Mandan Hidatsa storytelling and music from North Dakota, another in the Homegrown Concert Series sponsored by the American Folklife Center.

Both D.J. Battiest-Tomasi and Tim Tingle are enrolled as members of the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma and regularly participate in tribal events. Both also share relatives who came on the Trail of Tears from the original Choctaw homelands in Mississippi. They speak Choctaw as a part of their performances. They normally perform individually, but have been asked to perform together on this occasion.

Noted Native American scholars, authors, and civil rights activists Walter Echo-Hawk, Malinda Maynor Lowery, LaDonna Harris, and Tim Tingle look back at the long Native American struggle for equality, examine current barriers for sustaining community ways of life and identity, and address the path ahead for Native nations and communities. The event is moderated by Letitia Chambers and co-sponsored by the Association of Tribal Archives, Libraries & Museums, the Institute for Museum & Library Services, the Ak-Chin Community Council and the Yocha Dehe Wintun Nation. Watch from 1:14:30-1:26:30 to hear Tim Tingle tell personal narratives.

Other Resources in the Library of Congress

  • Wopila by Dovie Thomason (Performed by)
    Call Number: RYL 2118
    ISBN: 0938756443
    Published/Created: 1993-06-01
    Seven stories from the Lakota tradition filled with insights on how to live with respect for all peoples. With traditional flute music by Kevin Locke. Cover art by Ben Franklin Award winning artist, Murv Jacob. Stories include: Introduction -- The pet donkey -- The spirit wife -- The rabbit people -- Iya (camp eater) -- Introduction -- Iktomi & buzzard -- Iktomi & ducks & rock -- Iktomi changes his mind.
  • Lessons from the Animal People by Dovie Thomason; Ulali (Music by)
    Call Number: RYE 9386
    ISBN: 0938756508
    Published/Created: 1996-01-01
    This record brings together two premier Native American artists to tell nine stories drawn from different tribes. Contents include: Creation [music] / Ulalí -- The making of the Animals (Algonquian/Iroquois) -- Rabbit's lament [music] / Soni -- Ant dances for light (Grandma's story) -- Ant's shuffle / Ulalí -- The beaver woman & the eagle (Lakota/Dakota) -- Shhh... [music] / Jennifer -- Mouse & the moon (Lakota/Dakota) -- Ink pa tdah [music] / Ulalí -- The war party (Lakota/Dakota) -- Turtle goes mad [music] / Pura Fé -- Turtle learns to fly (Lakota/Dakota) -- North Carolina ease on down the road song / Ulalí -- Two chipmunks (Catawba) -- A lonesome song / Ulalí -- The bear child (Penobscot/Passamaquoddy/Iroquois) --A lonesome song / Ulalí -- Frog & turtle (Lakota/Dakota) -- To be a human being / Ulalí.
  • Morning star whispered [sound recording] : traditional stories and flute music by Keith Bear / Makoché
    Call Number: SDC 12355
    ISBN: 0911205098
    Published/Created: 2007
    Mandan-Hidatsa storyteller and musician Keith Bear performs stories including: Introduction (1:11) -- Northern lights (5:23) -- Sticks a feather in his head (24:59) -- Sticks a feather in his head (flute) (2:48) -- Turtle and pretty crane (22:20) -- All through the night (3:10) -- Night dreamer (4:55).
  • Un de' Che Cha Pi by Mary Louise Defender Wilson (Artist); North Dakota Council on The Arts (Produced by)
    Call Number: SDB 58522
    ISBN: 0911205063
    Published/Created: 2003-01-01
    Collection of traditional Dakotah stories about Unktomi, or Spiderman, a trickster who had difficulties with gluttony, violence, anger, and sex. Each story illustrates one of these universal struggles and demonstrates the need for civilized human beings to live a balanced life. Contents: Introduction (2:59) -- The ducks dance with their eyes closed (12:02) -- The spiderman meets the giant (9:35) -- How the meadowlark won the race (18:05) -- The painted turtle who went after horses (7:24) -- Spiderman tries to marry his own daughter (14:18) -- Closing/Prayer (4:00).
  • My Relatives Say by Mary Louise Wilson
    Call Number: SDA 80617
    ISBN: 0965087271
    Published/Created: 2001-01-01
    Shows the natural forces that shaped these wisdom tales of Dakotah Sioux. In the opening story "The World Never Ends," she reflects on the importance of storytelling as each generation passes along its knowledge. Included among the eight stories are transformation myths such as "The Dakotah Have Had Horses for a Long Time," "How Snake Creek Came to Be", and "Why the fawn Has Spots." The value of a caring community is emphasized in stories such as "Earth Beans." and "The Blue Heron Who Stayed for the Winter."
  • Under Mount Saint Elias: the history and culture of the Yakutat Tlingit by De Laguna, Frederica
    Call Number: GN1 .S54 vol. 7
    Published/Created: 1972
    A 3-part book detailing de Laguna's fieldwork with Tlingit communities. The book includes extensive discussion of the folktales and epics she heard. Request in the Jefferson or Adams Buildings Reading Rooms.
  • Crossing Bok Chitto by Tim Tingle; Jeanne Rorex Bridges (Illustrator)
    Call Number: PZ7.T489 Cro 2006 FT MEADE
    ISBN: 9780938317777
    Published/Created: 2006-04-01
    There is a river called Bok Chitto that cuts through Mississippi. In the days before the War Between the States, in the days before the Trail of Tears, Bok Chitto was a boundary. On one side of the river lived the Choctaws. On the other side lived the plantation owners and their slaves. If a slave escaped and made his way across Bok Chitto, the slave was free. Thus begins Crossing Bok Chitto, told by award-winning Choctaw storyteller Tim Tingle and brought to life with the rich illustrations of Jeanne Rorex Bridges. Request in the Jefferson or Adams Buildings Reading Rooms.
  • How I Became a Ghost by Tim Tingle
    Call Number: PZ7.T489 Hc 2013 LANDOVR
    ISBN: 9781937054533
    Published/Created: 2013-06-18
    A Choctaw boy tells the story of his tribe's removal from its Mississippi homeland, and how its exodus to the American West led him to become a ghost -- one able to help those left behind. Request in the Jefferson or Adams Buildings Reading Rooms.
