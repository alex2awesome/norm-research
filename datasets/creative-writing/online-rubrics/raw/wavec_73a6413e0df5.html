Writing History: A Guide to Crafting Historical Fiction

Historical Fiction EditorBook Writing CoachingManuscript Editing
Mar 28
Written By Gilliam Writers Group

Writing historical fiction is an art form that challenges the creative and intellectual faculties of authors, demanding a nuanced balance between the authenticity of the past and the vibrancy of narrative storytelling. This genre stands at the crossroads of imagination and historical precision, inviting readers to traverse the landscapes of bygone eras while engaging with the timeless human experiences of struggle, ambition, love, and resilience. To craft stories that resonate with both the truth of history and the heart of narrative fiction is no small feat; it requires a deep commitment to research, character development, and plot craftsmanship. Historical fiction writers embark on a journey through time, endeavoring to breathe life into the shadowy realms of history, making them tangible and relatable to contemporary readers. Their work is a testament to the power of literature to bridge the chasm between the past and the present, illuminating the human condition in all its complexity.

At the heart of this endeavor lies the symbiotic relationship between the writer and the historical fiction editor. The editor's role transcends the conventional boundaries of proofreading and grammar correction, venturing into the realms of historical verification, narrative enhancement, and character development. They serve as the writer's ally in navigating the intricate dance between fact and fiction, ensuring that the tapestry of the story is woven with both accuracy and artistry. Historical fiction editors are custodians of authenticity, guiding authors in the delicate task of portraying historical periods with integrity while crafting engaging and relatable narratives. Their expertise in balancing historical fidelity with storytelling ensures that the final manuscript not only captivates readers but also honors the truth of the eras it depicts. Through their collaborative efforts, writers and historical fiction editors forge works of fiction that stand as vivid portals to the past, offering insights into the lives of those who walked before us and reflecting the universal themes that continue to shape our world today.

For writers eager to embark on this journey, getting started in historical fiction requires a balance of research, creativity, and narrative skill. Here’s how to begin:

Choose Your Era Wisely

The first step is to select a time period that not only interests you but also has enough historical information available to support your story. Whether it’s the flamboyant courts of Renaissance Italy, the turbulent times of the American Civil War, or the complex social web of 19th-century London, your chosen era should be one you’re passionate about. This passion will fuel the countless hours of research required to bring authenticity to your work.

Dive Deep into Research

Research is the cornerstone of historical fiction. It’s essential to immerse yourself in the period you’re writing about. This means going beyond just dates and events; you should understand the social customs, language, fashion, economic conditions, and daily lives of the people. Primary sources like letters, diaries, and newspaper articles from the era can provide invaluable insights. Additionally, historical texts, academic papers, and even other historical fiction novels can offer context and depth to your understanding.

Develop Authentic Characters

Characters should be a product of their time, yet relatable to modern readers. While historical figures can play roles in your story, creating fictional characters allows you greater freedom to explore the nuances of the era through their eyes. It’s important that their beliefs, decisions, and conflicts are true to the time period while still resonating with contemporary themes of love, loss, ambition, and resilience.

Create a Compelling Plot That Weaves in Historical Elements

Your plot should skillfully intertwine fictional narrative with historical events and settings. While the historical backdrop provides a rich tapestry, the heart of your story lies in its characters and their journeys. Balancing historical accuracy with compelling storytelling can be challenging, but it’s crucial for engaging your readers and immersing them in the world you’ve recreated.

Pay Attention to Detail, But Don't Get Lost in It

While historical accuracy is important, getting too bogged down in the minutiae can detract from the narrative flow. Use historical details to enhance the setting and mood, but remember that the story’s primary aim is to engage readers. Sometimes, this means knowing when to prioritize narrative clarity and character development over strict adherence to historical fact.

Seek Feedback and Be Open to Revision

Once your draft is complete, seeking feedback from beta readers, particularly those with knowledge of the era, can be incredibly helpful. They can point out inaccuracies or anachronisms you might have missed and provide insights on how to deepen your story’s historical context. Be open to revisions; crafting a compelling historical fiction novel often requires refining your narrative to balance historical detail with storytelling.

Never Stop Learning

The journey of writing historical fiction doesn’t end with publication. Each story is an opportunity to learn more about the past and refine your craft. Stay curious, continue researching, and always be open to discovering new aspects of the periods you write about.

A historical fiction editor plays a crucial role in refining and enhancing a writer’s manuscript, ensuring both historical accuracy and compelling storytelling. Their expertise extends beyond basic editing tasks to include a deep understanding of the historical context, character development, and narrative structure tailored to this genre. Here’s how a historical fiction editor can assist a writer in polishing their work:

Ensuring Historical Accuracy

One of the primary roles of a historical fiction editor is to verify the historical accuracy of the manuscript. This includes fact-checking dates, events, settings, language, and customs of the period depicted. Editors often have a keen eye for anachronisms or inaccuracies that might elude the writer. By ensuring that the historical backdrop of the story is as accurate as possible, the editor helps to create a believable and immersive world for the reader.

Balancing Fact with Fiction

A key challenge in historical fiction is striking the right balance between factual accuracy and engaging storytelling. An editor can guide the writer in weaving historical facts seamlessly into the narrative, ensuring that the story flows naturally without being bogged down by excessive detail. They can suggest ways to integrate historical events or figures into the plot in a manner that enriches the story without compromising historical integrity.

Character Development

Characters in historical fiction must be authentic to their time period while remaining relatable to contemporary readers. Editors can provide insights into character development, helping writers create complex characters who are true to their historical context yet exhibit universal emotions and motivations. They can also advise on dialogue, ensuring that it reflects the era’s speech patterns without alienating modern readers.

Enhancing Narrative Structure

Historical fiction often involves complex plots that include a mix of real events and fictional elements. An editor can assist in refining the narrative structure, ensuring that the story maintains a compelling pace, and that historical events are integrated smoothly into the personal journeys of the characters. They can help identify areas where the story may lag or become confusing and suggest revisions to enhance clarity and engagement.

Providing Genre-Specific Feedback

Given their familiarity with historical fiction, these editors are well-equipped to provide genre-specific feedback. They understand market trends, reader expectations, and the elements that make historical fiction resonate with audiences. This insight can be invaluable in positioning the book to stand out in a crowded market.

Encouraging Authenticity and Sensitivity

Editors also play a critical role in ensuring that historical fiction is written with sensitivity and respect for the people and cultures depicted. They can guide writers in addressing historical controversies, representations of marginalized groups, and the portrayal of sensitive topics with care and authenticity.

Crafting a Cohesive Manuscript

Finally, beyond the unique demands of historical fiction, editors assist in all the traditional aspects of editing—improving grammar, punctuation, and syntax, enhancing the manuscript’s overall coherence, and ensuring that the writer’s style is consistent throughout the book.

The journey of creating historical fiction is a profound exercise in empathy and imagination, demanding a meticulous blend of research, creativity, and narrative skill. For authors and editors alike, it is a labor of love that seeks to make the past accessible and engaging for a new generation of readers. As they navigate the challenges of accurately representing historical periods while telling compelling stories, they contribute to a rich literary tradition that enriches our understanding of both history and humanity. The craft of historical fiction, supported by the keen insights of historical fiction editors, stands as a vibrant testament to the enduring power of storytelling to connect us with the vast tapestry of human experience.
