# Save the Cat! Beat Sheet — 15 Beats

Blake Snyder's framework for story structure (originally screenplay; adopted for fiction).

## The 15 Beats (with typical % position in story)

1. **Opening Image (1%)** — Snapshot of the protagonist's "before" world; tone-setting.
2. **Theme Stated (5%)** — A character (often not the protagonist) hints at the story's deeper meaning.
3. **Set-up (1–10%)** — Establish protagonist, world, status quo, what's missing/broken.
4. **Catalyst (10%)** — The inciting incident; life is disrupted.
5. **Debate (10–20%)** — Protagonist hesitates; "should I or shouldn't I?"
6. **Break Into Two (20%)** — Protagonist commits to the new world / quest.
7. **B Story (22%)** — A secondary plot (often love story or mentor) introduced; carries the theme.
8. **Fun and Games (20–50%)** — "Promise of the premise"; what the genre/title delivers.
9. **Midpoint (50%)** — False victory or false defeat; stakes raised; clock starts.
10. **Bad Guys Close In (50–75%)** — External and internal antagonists ramp up.
11. **All Is Lost (75%)** — Lowest point; "whiff of death."
12. **Dark Night of the Soul (75–80%)** — Protagonist absorbs the loss and grows.
13. **Break Into Three (80%)** — Solution found, often combining A and B stories.
14. **Finale (80–99%)** — Five-point finale: gathering team, executing plan, high tower surprise, dig deep down, execution of new plan.
15. **Final Image (99–100%)** — Mirror of opening image, showing transformation.

## Snyder's Genre Categories
Save the Cat! also classifies all stories into 10 genres (e.g., Monster in the House, Golden Fleece, Out of the Bottle, Dude with a Problem, Rites of Passage, Buddy Love, Whydunit, The Fool Triumphant, Institutionalized, Superhero), each with conventions a story must hit.

## "Save the Cat" Moment
The titular technique: early in the story, give the protagonist a moment of likable agency (literally "saving a cat") so the audience roots for them.
