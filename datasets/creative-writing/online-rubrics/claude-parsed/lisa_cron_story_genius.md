# Lisa Cron — Story Genius & Wired for Story (Brain-Science Storytelling)

## Core Premise
Storytelling craft can be informed by *neuroscience*. Our brains evolved to be hooked by certain story patterns; great stories deliver what the brain is wired to crave.

## Key Principles

### Stories Are About the Internal Struggle
Plot is the *external* manifestation of the protagonist's *internal* struggle. The change inside is what readers feel. External action without internal change = events without story.

### Emotion First, Always
"Emotions engage the brain faster than anything else." Information without emotional valence doesn't stick. Quality fiction makes the reader feel before they think.

### The Story Question
Every scene must answer: *what does the protagonist want, and what's keeping them from getting it?* Internal stakes amplify external action.

### "Misbelief" — The Lie the Protagonist Believes
Similar to Weiland's Lie. The protagonist enters the story holding a false belief about themselves/the world that has shaped their life. The plot tests this belief.

### Cause and Effect ("And So…")
Every scene must connect to the next via "and so" not "and then." The brain craves causality.

### The Backstory Counts
Cron urges deep work on the protagonist's backstory before drafting — not as info-dump but as the foundation for the misbelief and current behavior.

### Anticipation Engages the Brain
Setting up expectations (and meeting/subverting them) keeps readers engaged. "What happens next?" is the brain's default mode.

### Specificity Triggers Imagination
Generic detail bores; specific detail (Cron quotes the science) lights up the brain's sensory cortices.

## Process Steps (Story Genius)
1. **The "What If"** — initial story spark
2. **The "Why" — protagonist's misbelief**
3. **The "Aha" — the truth they need**
4. **Backstory development**
5. **Plot built scene-by-scene around internal arc**
6. **Each scene: cause / effect / realization**

## Quality Tests
- Does the reader know what the protagonist wants from page 1?
- Is there an *internal* struggle, not just external obstacles?
- Does each scene cause the next?
- Do specific details engage the senses?
- Does the ending pay off the misbelief→aha trajectory?

## Critique
Some craft writers find Cron too prescriptive — the misbelief framework can over-determine character. Defenders argue it's a useful diagnostic for stories that "feel like nothing's happening" despite events.
