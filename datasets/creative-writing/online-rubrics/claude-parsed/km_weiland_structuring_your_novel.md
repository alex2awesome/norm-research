# K.M. Weiland — Structuring Your Novel

Influential craft book + blog (Helping Writers Become Authors) on novel structure.

## The Major Beats

### The Hook
Opening that promises the story; pulls the reader in immediately.

### The Inciting Event (~12%)
Disrupts the protagonist's status quo; raises the central question.

### The First Plot Point (~25%)
The "doorway of no return." Protagonist crosses a threshold and commits to the journey.

### The First Pinch Point (~37%)
A reminder of the antagonistic force; raises stakes.

### The Midpoint / Second Plot Point (~50%)
Centerpiece of the novel; "moment of truth"; protagonist sees the conflict clearly. Often a false victory or false defeat. Reactive → proactive shift.

### The Second Pinch Point (~62%)
Antagonist applies more pressure; protagonist's plan is failing.

### The Third Plot Point (~75%)
The "low moment" / "all is lost"; protagonist faces apparent defeat. Working from False Victory (midpoint) to this Low Moment.

### The Climax (~88–98%)
Final confrontation; sacrifice and victory/failure.

### The Climactic Moment
The single decisive action.

### The Resolution (~98–100%)
Aftermath; new equilibrium.

## Weiland's "5 Secrets" of Story Structure
1. **Hook the reader**
2. **Set up the inciting event** (introduce stakes)
3. **Cross the threshold** at first plot point
4. **Time the midpoint** (centerpiece)
5. **Earn the climax** (consequences for choices)

## Quality Criteria
- Each beat lands at the right narrative position
- Beats are *causal*, not arbitrary
- The midpoint is genuinely transformative (not just a midbook scene)
- Pinch points keep antagonist present
- The third plot point's despair is earned
- Climax answers the inciting question

## Why This Framework Matters
Weiland synthesizes Field, Snyder, Truby into a 9-beat shape that's more granular than three-act and less Hollywood-specific than Save the Cat. Widely used by indie/genre novelists.

## Companion Frameworks Weiland Uses
- Character arc types (positive change, negative, flat) — saved separately
- Scene structure (goal/conflict/disaster + reaction/dilemma/decision)
- Theme integration via character arc
