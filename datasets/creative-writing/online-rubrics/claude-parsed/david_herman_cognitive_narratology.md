# David Herman — Cognitive Narratology / Storyworlds (2002, 2009, 2013)

Founder of cognitive narratology. Frames narrative not as a sequence of events but as a **storyworld** that interpreters mentally inhabit.

## Storyworld

"The world evoked implicitly as well as explicitly by a narrative." A storyworld is not a list of events; it is a mentally and emotionally projected environment in which interpreters live out cognitive and imaginative responses.

## Two Foundational Questions

1. How do stories interlock with interpreters' mental states and processes to give rise to narrative experiences?
2. How does narrative scaffold our efforts to make sense of experience itself?

## Four Basic Elements of Narrative

Herman's four-part definition:

### 1. Situatedness
"Narrative is situated in — must be interpreted in light of — a specific discourse context or occasion for telling." Every story is told **somewhere**, **to someone**, **for a reason**.

### 2. Event Sequencing
Narrative cues interpreters to draw inferences about a structured time-course of particularized events.

### 3. Worldmaking / World Disruption
Narrative represents not just events but a **world** that the events disrupt and reconfigure. The reader builds and updates this world during reading.

### 4. What It's Like (Qualia)
Narrative conveys what it is like for someone to experience the events — the phenomenal quality of experience. Without this, you have a chronicle, not a story.

## Storyworld Construction

Readers do not passively reconstruct events; they actively build a storyworld using:
- Frame knowledge (schemas about restaurants, weddings, hospitals)
- Inference (filling explicit gaps)
- Emotional engagement (caring about what happens to whom)
- Imagined embodiment (sensing the storyworld as a physical place)

## Implication for Fiction Evaluation

A story is more than a sequence — it is a habitable world. Successful fiction:
- Cues the reader to build a coherent storyworld
- Activates appropriate frames without over-explanation
- Conveys phenomenal quality (qualia)
- Gives the reader a stake — emotional investment in the world
- Sustains internal consistency

## Failure Modes

A narrative fails as story when:
- The discourse context is unmotivated (why is this being told?)
- Events accumulate without world-making (chronicle, not story)
- Qualia are absent (events without anyone to experience them)
- Frame cues are missing or contradictory (the reader cannot build the world)

## Evaluative Criteria

A piece of fiction can be assessed on:
- Strength of situatedness (occasion of telling)
- Coherence of event-sequence inference
- Richness of worldmaking
- Presence of qualia / experiential character
- Reader's ability to inhabit the storyworld
