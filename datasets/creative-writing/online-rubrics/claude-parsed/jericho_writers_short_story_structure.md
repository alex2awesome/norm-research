# Jericho Writers — Short Story Structure and Craft

UK author-services platform founded 2004 by novelist Harry Bingham. Their craft blog distills practical short-story principles.

## Definition

A short story is **1,000–4,000 words** (can extend to 15,000), readable in **one sitting**. Beyond 15,000 words, the demands change — the form becomes novella.

## Five Structural Components

### 1. Exposition
Introduce characters and setting; establish the protagonist's ordinary world. In a short story, this must be done in paragraphs, not chapters.

### 2. Rising Action
The inciting incident propels the story; complications mount. Pressure increases on the protagonist.

### 3. Climax
The pivotal moment that tests the protagonist. Decision under pressure. The story's reason for being.

### 4. Falling Action
Consequences flow from the climax. Briefer than the rising action.

### 5. Resolution / Denouement
Three possible outcomes:
- **Protagonist succeeds** at their stated goal
- **Protagonist fails** at their stated goal
- **Protagonist discovers something more valuable** than the goal (transformation ending)

## Core Craft Principles

### Simplicity and Focus
- **Theme should be simple** — one central concern
- **Plot should be linear** — flashbacks used sparingly
- **Avoid complicated subplots** — no room
- **Limit character count** — 3–5 named characters typical
- **Single major setting**, or at most a few

### Structure vs. Plot (James Scott Bell distinction)
- **Plot** = what happens (the elements)
- **Structure** = the orderly arrangement (the timing)
A story can have eventful plot but bad structure, or modest plot with great structure.

### Dual Reader Requirements
The reader must understand both:
- **What happens** (events, plot)
- **What it means** (theme, significance)

Failure on either dimension produces a defective story.

## Recommended Frameworks

Jericho recommends combining short-story structure with one of:
- **Hero's Journey** (Vogler's simplification)
- **Three-Act Structure**
- **Seven-Point Story Structure** (Dan Wells)
- **Save the Cat Beat Sheet**

Use a known framework — not because formula is virtue, but because the mind organizes around scaffolds.

## Common Short-Story Failures

- Trying to fit a novel's worth of plot into 4,000 words
- Multiple subplots
- Too many named characters
- Theme stated rather than embodied
- No clear climax (story drifts)
- Resolution that fails to connect to the climax

## Evaluative Criteria

A short story can be assessed on:
- Word count fit (within 1k–15k)
- All five structural components present
- Single, focused theme
- Linear (or motivated nonlinear) plot
- Limited named characters
- Reader gets both **what** and **what it means**
- Climax tests protagonist meaningfully
- Resolution earns the climax
