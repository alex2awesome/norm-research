# Northrop Frye — Anatomy of Criticism (1957)

Systematic taxonomy of literary criticism — modes, symbols, mythoi, genres.

## Five Modes of Fiction (by Hero's Power of Action)

1. **Mythic** — Hero is a god, superior in kind to other men and the environment
2. **Romantic** — Hero is superior in degree to other men and the environment (legends, fairy tales)
3. **High Mimetic** — Hero superior in degree to other men but not the environment (epic, tragedy: kings, princes)
4. **Low Mimetic** — Hero is one of us (most realist novels, comedy)
5. **Ironic** — Hero is inferior in power or intelligence to ourselves; we look down on them

Western literature has shifted progressively from mythic to ironic over its history.

## Four Mythoi (Plots Tied to Seasons)

1. **Comedy / Spring** — myth of birth, renewal, integration; ends in marriage/feast
2. **Romance / Summer** — myth of triumph, adventure, quest, harmony
3. **Tragedy / Autumn** — myth of fall, decay, isolation, sacrifice
4. **Irony and Satire / Winter** — myth of chaos, dissolution, the absurd

These four mythoi together form a circular pattern matching the seasonal cycle.

## Theory of Symbols (Five Phases)

1. **Literal / Descriptive** — symbol as motif (within the work) or sign (pointing outward)
2. **Formal** — symbol as image; structural play of imagery
3. **Mythical / Archetypal** — symbol as connector across literary tradition
4. **Anagogic** — symbol as monad; literature as a unified imaginative universe
5. (Ethical phase implicit between formal and mythical)

## Archetypal Imagery

- **Apocalyptic imagery**: paradise, the Lamb, the Tree of Life, bread/wine, the city, the garden
- **Demonic imagery**: hell, the predator, the wasteland, sterile city, dragon
- **Analogical imagery**: states resembling but not identical to either pole

Organized by **Great Chain of Being**: divine, human, animal, vegetable, mineral, water.

## Theory of Genres (Four Radicals of Presentation)

1. **Epos** — author addresses visible audience directly (oral, recited)
2. **Fiction** — author and audience hidden from each other (the novel)
3. **Drama** — author hidden; audience experiences action directly
4. **Lyric** — speaker overheard by hidden audience

Each genre has a distinctive rhythm: recurrence (epos), continuity (fiction/prose), decorum (drama), associative (lyric).

## Evaluative Use

A work can be classified by mode, mythos, symbolic phase, and genre — and judged by how richly it engages archetypal patterns and how coherently it inhabits its mode.
