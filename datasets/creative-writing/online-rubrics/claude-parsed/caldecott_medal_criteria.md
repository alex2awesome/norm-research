# Caldecott Medal — Official Evaluation Criteria

Awarded annually by ALSC for "the most distinguished American picture book for children." The award goes to the **illustrator**, but text-illustration integration is considered.

## Five Core Criteria

### 1. Excellence of Execution in the Artistic Technique Employed
Whatever medium or technique the illustrator uses (watercolor, collage, digital, mixed media), it must be executed at a high level of craft.

### 2. Excellence of Pictorial Interpretation of Story, Theme, or Concept
The illustrations must interpret — not merely accompany — the text. They should advance, deepen, or counterpoint the story's meaning.

### 3. Appropriateness of Style of Illustration to Story, Theme, or Concept
Style must fit subject. A horror story illustrated like a baby book fails this criterion; so does the reverse.

### 4. Delineation of Plot, Theme, Characters, Setting, Mood, or Information through the Pictures
Pictures must carry narrative weight. They should communicate plot beats, evoke mood, characterize figures, establish place, or transmit information — not merely decorate.

### 5. Excellence of Presentation in Recognition of a Child Audience
The book must be **for children** — visually navigable, age-appropriate, inviting. Adults' aesthetic admiration alone does not qualify a book.

## Components Other than Illustration

Text, design, typography, and overall book object **may** be considered as they affect the effectiveness of the illustrations, but the primary judgment is on the pictures.

## Implication for Picture-Book Rubric

A picture book can be evaluated on:
- Technical execution of the chosen medium
- How much the pictures interpret (not just illustrate)
- Fit between pictorial style and story
- The narrative weight pictures carry
- Whether the book truly addresses children
- Integration of text and image as a unified object
