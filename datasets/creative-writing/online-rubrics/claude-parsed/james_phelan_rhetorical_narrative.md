# James Phelan — Rhetorical Theory of Narrative

Third-generation Neo-Aristotelian / Chicago School theorist; extends Wayne Booth. Foundational concepts in *Narrative as Rhetoric*, *Living to Tell About It*, and *Experiencing Fiction*.

## Two Foundational Principles

1. **Narrative is rhetoric**: "Someone telling someone else, on some occasion and for some purpose, that something happened."
2. **Reading is multidimensional**: simultaneously engaging intellect, emotion, ideology, and ethics.

## Narrative Progression

Progression = the synthesis of textual dynamics + reader dynamics.

### Textual Dynamics
- **Instabilities**: tensions among characters in the storyworld
- **Tensions**: tensions among the implied author, narrator, and audience (i.e., differences in knowledge, value, perception)

A narrative moves forward by introducing instabilities/tensions, complicating them, and resolving (or refusing to resolve) them.

### Reader Dynamics
- **Interpretive judgments**: about what is happening
- **Ethical judgments**: about characters' actions and the narrator's reliability
- **Aesthetic judgments**: about the work's form and execution

## The Synthesizing Move

"The progression of instability is accompanied by narrative judgments." Reading is the simultaneous tracking of textual dynamics and the formation of judgments about them.

## Three Components of Character

Phelan analyzes characters along three dimensions:
1. **Mimetic** — the character as person (psychology, plausibility)
2. **Thematic** — the character as representative of an idea
3. **Synthetic** — the character as artifact (constructed for the work's purpose)

A successful work integrates all three; imbalance produces flatness or didacticism.

## Six Types of Unreliable Narration

Refining Booth, Phelan distinguishes:
1. Misreporting
2. Misreading (misinterpreting)
3. Misregarding (mis-evaluating ethically)
4. Underreporting
5. Underreading
6. Underregarding

This framework allows precise diagnosis of unreliable narrators.

## Ethical Position

Every narrative situates the reader in an ethical position — by inviting certain judgments about characters and asking the reader to share, resist, or revise them. Ethical criticism is not optional: every reading is an ethical encounter.

## Evaluative Criteria

A narrative can be analyzed on:
- Strength of instabilities (in storyworld)
- Strength of tensions (in narration)
- Coherence of progression (introduce → complicate → resolve)
- Integration of mimetic / thematic / synthetic character functions
- Honesty of the ethical position the work implies
- Specificity of any unreliable narration (which type?)
