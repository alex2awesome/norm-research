# Three-Act Structure (Syd Field)

Codified in *Screenplay: The Foundations of Screenwriting* (1979). The dominant story-architecture in English-language fiction and film.

## The Three Acts

### Act 1 — Setup (~25%)
- Exposition: introduce protagonist, world, status quo
- **Inciting Incident** (~12%): the catalyst that disrupts equilibrium
- **Plot Point 1** (~25%): protagonist commits to a new course

### Act 2 — Confrontation / Rising Action (~50%)
- Protagonist tries to resolve the problem; situations worsen
- **Midpoint** (~50%): major reversal or commitment
- **Plot Point 2** (~75%): a setback that demands a new plan

### Act 3 — Resolution (~25%)
- Climax — the decisive confrontation
- Falling action
- Resolution / new equilibrium

## Quality Criteria
- **Each act has its own beginning/middle/end** (fractal structure)
- **Plot points are real reversals**, not just events
- **Act 2 is the longest** — and the hardest; it must keep escalating
- **The midpoint is load-bearing** — false victory or false defeat
- **The climax must answer the question raised by the inciting incident**
- **Act 1 promises; Act 3 pays off**

## Variants
- **Field's "paradigm"** — explicit page targets per act in screenwriting
- **Syd Field's "pinch points"** — additional structural beats added in his later work
- **Snyder's Save the Cat** — 15 beats inside three acts
- **Truby's 22 steps** — refined three-act with character/moral arc weave
- **Vogler's Hero's Journey** — 12 steps mapped onto three acts
