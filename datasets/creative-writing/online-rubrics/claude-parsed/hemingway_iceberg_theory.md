# Hemingway's Iceberg Theory (Theory of Omission)

## Core Principle
"The dignity of movement of an iceberg is due to only one-eighth of it being above water." Most of a story's meaning lies beneath the visible surface. The reader feels the unseen mass.

## The Rule of Knowledge + Omission
"If a writer of prose knows enough of what he is writing about he may omit things that he knows and the reader, if the writer is writing truly enough, will have a feeling of those things as strongly as though the writer had stated them."

But: "If you leave or skip something because you do not know it, the story will be worthless." Omission only works when the writer fully knows what is omitted.

## Craft Implications
- **Subtext over text** — what's implied carries more weight than what's said
- **Trust the reader** — readers complete meaning from gaps
- **Concrete surface** — focus on observable action, dialogue, gesture
- **No explanation** of feelings — show the action that produced them
- **Spare prose** — short, concrete sentences; few adjectives/adverbs
- **Plot fragments** — start in middle, end before resolution
- **Symbol via repetition** rather than declaration

## Example
"Hills Like White Elephants" — entire story is two characters drinking at a train station; word "abortion" never appears; reader understands.

## Quality Tests
- Does the surface action imply more than it says?
- Has the writer earned the omission by knowing what's omitted?
- Does the reader feel the unsaid weight?
- Are the visible details *the right* one-eighth?

## Related Principles
- **Defamiliarization** (Shklovsky) — making the familiar strange
- **Subtext** (Stanislavski) — what characters mean beneath what they say
- **Negative space** (visual arts analog)
- **Suggestion vs. Statement** (poetry's lyric mode)

## "Second Iceberg Theory" (Matthew Duffus)
The reader's emotional response should also be partly hidden — story should provoke feelings the reader doesn't immediately understand, prompting return reading.
