# Shawn Coyne — The Story Grid (2015)

Editor's framework for diagnosing story problems. Coyne edited hundreds of books; the Story Grid is his systematic toolkit.

## The Five Commandments of Storytelling

Every functional unit of story (beat, scene, sequence, act, global story) must contain all five.

### 1. Inciting Incident
An unexpected event that destabilizes the protagonist by upsetting the balance of their life.
- **Causal**: the protagonist actively chose
- **Coincidental**: it happened to them

### 2. Turning Point Progressive Complication
The protagonist's strategies fail; conditions worsen until a final turning point — a revelation or action that forces a decision. The "things get worse" pressure.

### 3. Crisis
A binary choice with meaningful stakes. Two forms:
- **Best Bad Choice** — two terrible options
- **Irreconcilable Goods** — two desirable options that cannot coexist

### 4. Climax
The active answer to the question raised by the crisis. The decision under pressure that reveals true character.

### 5. Resolution
The meaningful consequences of the climactic choice. Must validate the climax without being repetitious — focus on aftereffects, not restatement.

## Story Levels (Fractal Application)

The Five Commandments embed at every level:
- **Beat** (smallest unit of change)
- **Scene** (single location/time)
- **Sequence** (collection of related scenes)
- **Act / Quadrant**
- **Global Story**

## The Controlling Idea

Every effective story communicates a single controlling idea — typically: "[Value] arises in human life when [cause]." This is the truth the story argues.

## Genre

Story Grid distinguishes:
- **External Genre** (action, war, society, performance)
- **Internal Genre** (worldview, status, morality, education)

A story must succeed in both its external and internal genres.

## The Genre Five-Leaf Clover

Five mandatory components of any genre identification:
- Time (long form, short form, etc.)
- Reality (realism, fantasy, etc.)
- Style (drama, comedy, etc.)
- Structure (arch-plot, miniplot, antiplot)
- Content (the value at stake)

## The Story Grid Spreadsheet

A diagnostic table tracking, for every scene:
- Word count
- Story event
- Value shift (positive to negative, or vice versa)
- Polarity shift
- Turning point
- Point of view
- Period / duration / location
- Onstage characters
- The five commandments present in that scene

## Evaluative Use

A scene fails if it lacks any of the Five Commandments or if no value shift occurs. A story fails if external + internal genres are not both fulfilled, or if the controlling idea is incoherent.
