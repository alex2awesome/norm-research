# PEN/Faulkner Award for Fiction — Judging Criteria

Largest peer-juried fiction award in the U.S. Three writer-judges select from 300+ submissions per year. Award honors the best fiction by American permanent residents annually.

## Process

- Three judges (writers) chosen annually by the Board to ensure independence from commercial influence
- Judges select longlist of 10
- Then shortlist of 5 finalists
- Then one winner ("first among equals")

## Implicit Evaluative Standards (from Juror Citations)

The Foundation does not publish formal criteria; jurors articulate standards through public citations. Recurring qualities cited:

### Beauty
"Vital, brilliant" prose; sentence-level distinction; the work as art-object.

### Compassion
The capacity to extend imaginative empathy across difference. Repeatedly cited as a binding quality of finalists.

### Imagination
Originality of conception. Not novelty for its own sake but genuine inventive power.

### Quiet Artistry
A recurring phrase. Distinguishes the works the panel selects from those that announce themselves loudly. The PEN/Faulkner aesthetic favors restraint and depth over spectacle.

### Humanity
Engagement with what is fundamentally human. "Drawing us into humanity's complex core of beauty, violence, joy, intelligence, and imagination."

### Complexity
Refusal of simple moral or political resolutions. The capacity to hold contradictions.

## Eligibility (procedural, not aesthetic)

- Author must be American citizen or permanent resident
- Published in the calendar year
- Any fictional form (novel, novella, story collection)

## Implication for Rubric

A work of literary fiction can be evaluated by the PEN/Faulkner standards of:
- Sentence-level beauty
- Compassion / imaginative empathy
- Originality of conception
- Restraint and depth (vs. announcement and spectacle)
- Engagement with the fundamentally human
- Capacity to hold complexity without resolving it cheaply

## Distinction from Other Awards

The PEN/Faulkner is **peer-juried** by writers (not editors or critics). This produces a craft-emphatic standard — the judges read as practitioners. Compared to the National Book Award (which uses critics, librarians, booksellers as well as writers), PEN/Faulkner tends toward the formally distinguished and the quietly profound.
