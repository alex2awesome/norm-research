# John Gardner — The Art of Fiction: Principles for Good Writing

## Central Principle: The Vivid and Continuous Dream
Fiction is "a vivid and continuous dream" for readers. The writer's primary obligation is to avoid disrupting this dream with any false note.

## Essential Elements
- **Commitment** — total artistic commitment to the work
- **Truth** — fiction must be believable; not "perceived by the reader as a lie"; must "affirm moral truths about human existence"
- **Precise Details** — concrete, specific sensory detail
- **Profluence** — the principle of what moves narrative forward and holds it together
- **Subject, Plot, Character, Setting, Theme, Style** — the components

## Common Faults That Break the Dream
1. **Clumsy prose** — Awkward sentences that pull the reader out
2. **Needless explanation** — Telling instead of showing; excess exposition
3. **Sentimentality** — Unearned emotion; manipulating reader feeling
4. **Mannerism** — Self-conscious stylistic tics
5. **Frigidity** — Coldness; failure to take subject seriously
6. **Faulty rhythm** — Sentence rhythm that doesn't match content
7. **Inappropriate diction** — Wrong word choices for context
8. **Misuse of point of view** — Inconsistent or violated POV
9. **Unmotivated character action** — Behavior not earned
10. **Overuse of metaphor / fancy language** — Showy prose that distracts

## Moral Fiction
Gardner's broader thesis: serious fiction has a moral function — to model human nuance, develop empathy, and engage truth at a deep level.

## Two-Part Structure
- Part 1: What fiction *is* (aesthetics, the dream, moral function)
- Part 2: Technical matters (sentence structure, mechanics, craft)
