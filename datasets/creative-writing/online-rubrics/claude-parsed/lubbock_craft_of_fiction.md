# Percy Lubbock — The Craft of Fiction (1921)

The first systematic English-language work of novel criticism. Influenced Henry James criticism and the modernist defense of the novel as art form.

## Central Distinction: Picture vs. Drama

Lubbock divides narrative method into two basic functions:

### Pictorial / Panoramic
- Encompasses large swathes of place and time
- Author summarizes; reader sees through narrator's organizing intelligence
- Economical of words but **relatively thin** — events are reported, not enacted
- Used for backstory, transitions, broad sweeps

### Scenic / Dramatic
- The reader views action as if watching a play
- Characters speak for themselves
- Bound to a specific time and place
- **Far less economical** of words
- **Much more vivid** — the reader experiences events directly

A novel is fundamentally a fluctuation between picture and drama. The art lies in choosing when each is appropriate.

## Third Category: The Mixed Method

Most great novels combine the two. The "stream" of pictorial summary alternates with "scenes" of dramatic immediacy. Pace and emphasis are produced by the alternation.

## Point of View

Lubbock was a pioneer of POV analysis. He examines:
- **Omniscient authorial voice** — wide control, but distance
- **Retrospective first person** — strong voice, but constrained by what the narrator knows
- **Close third person** — interiority of one character, with narrator's diction
- **Dramatic / objective third person** — visible behavior only, no interior

Each POV has different "powers" — and the **crucial principle** is whether the chosen POV serves the book's core subject matter.

## Show vs. Tell (Pioneer Account)

Lubbock anticipates Booth and the modernist debate. He privileges **dramatic showing** over **pictorial telling** — the novel is more powerful when the action plays out in scene than when it is summarized. (This view was later challenged by Booth.)

## The Subject

For Lubbock, every novel has a "subject" — the central matter the book is about. The craft test: do all the methodological choices (POV, picture/drama balance, scene selection) serve that subject?

A novel that is technically masterful but dispersed across multiple subjects fails the unity test.

## Evaluation Criteria

A novel can be analyzed by:
- Identifying its subject (single, double, dispersed?)
- The picture/drama ratio and its appropriateness
- Choice of POV and its fitness for the subject
- Whether scenes are selected for maximal yield to the subject
- Coherence of methodological choices across the whole

## Significance

Lubbock established the analytical vocabulary for novel criticism in English. His picture/drama distinction is still in active use; his POV analysis was the seed of all later studies.
