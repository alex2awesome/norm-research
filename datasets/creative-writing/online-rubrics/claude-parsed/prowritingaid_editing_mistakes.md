# ProWritingAid — Top 10 Editing Mistakes Authors Make

Editing software platform's checklist of common manuscript failures. Each mistake is itself an evaluative axis.

## The 10 Mistakes

### 1. Grammar Mistakes
Wrong word use, vague pronoun references, faulty sentence structure. Even great prose collapses on grammatical errors that disrupt clarity.

### 2. Misspelling and Typos
Slip through despite spell-check. Verify language variant settings (British vs. American). The reader registers carelessness immediately.

### 3. Punctuation Errors
Comma misuse, apostrophe mistakes, missing/wrong hyphens, quotation errors. Punctuation controls pacing and clarity; errors corrupt both.

### 4. Citation/Source Inaccuracy
For research-backed fiction or nonfiction. Inconsistent citation style undermines credibility.

### 5. Writing Weaknesses
- Repetitive words
- Clichés
- Overused adverbs
- Limited sentence variety
Run analysis identifying these patterns; eliminate systematically.

### 6. Word Omissions / Wrong Words
Missing words; correctly-spelled but incorrect homonyms (their/there, lead/led, principle/principal). Use homonym reports.

### 7. Structural Blunders
Poor organization at sentence, paragraph, scene, or manuscript levels. Address structure through **developmental editing** before line-editing.

### 8. Over-Reliance on Software
Tools cannot replace human judgment. Combine automated tools with professional editor review.

### 9. Editing While Writing
Mixing creative and technical processes. "Stop editing while writing. Write creatively first, and edit logically afterwards."

### 10. Over-Reliance on Self-Editing
Authors miss their own errors consistently. The brain reads what it intended to write. Hire an outside editor.

## As Evaluation Rubric

A manuscript can be assessed for:
- Grammar correctness
- Spelling/typo density
- Punctuation precision
- Citation accuracy (where applicable)
- Word repetition rate, cliché count, adverb count, sentence variety
- Word omission / homonym error count
- Structural coherence at multiple scales
- Evidence of professional editing pass

## Editing Process Order

The order itself is a craft principle:
1. **Developmental editing** (structure, character, plot)
2. **Line editing** (sentence-level)
3. **Copy editing** (grammar, punctuation, consistency)
4. **Proofreading** (final typo sweep)

Skipping a stage, or doing them out of order, produces predictable failures.
