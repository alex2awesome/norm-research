# Rasa Theory — Bharata Muni's Natyashastra (c. 200 BCE – 200 CE)

Indian aesthetic theory of emotional flavors evoked by literature and drama. The fundamental criterion: does the work successfully evoke a rasa in the cultivated reader (sahṛdaya)?

## The Nine Rasas (Navarasa)

1. **Śṛṅgāra (शृङ्गार)** — Love, romance, eroticism. Presiding deity: Vishnu.
2. **Hāsya (हास्य)** — Laughter, mirth, comedy. Presiding deity: Shiva.
3. **Karuṇa (करुण)** — Compassion, sorrow, pathos. Presiding deity: Yama.
4. **Raudra (रौद्र)** — Fury, wrath, destructive anger. Presiding deity: Rudra.
5. **Vīra (वीर)** — Heroism, valor, courage. Presiding deity: Indra.
6. **Bhayānaka (भयानक)** — Fear, terror, horror. Presiding deity: Kāla.
7. **Bībhatsa (बीभत्स)** — Disgust, aversion. Presiding deity: Mahakala.
8. **Adbhuta (अद्भुत)** — Wonder, amazement. Presiding deity: Brahma.
9. **Śānta (शान्त)** — Peace, tranquility (added later by Abhinavagupta). Presiding deity: Vishnu.

## The Bhavas (Emotional States) — Four Categories

### Sthayibhava (Stable Emotions)
Eight permanent mental states underlying each rasa: rati (love), hāsa (mirth), śoka (sorrow), krodha (anger), utsāha (energy), bhaya (terror), jugupsā (disgust), vismaya (astonishment).

### Vibhava (Determinants)
The causes producing the emotion:
- **Ālambana** — the human object/substratum (the beloved, the enemy)
- **Uddīpana** — the excitants (moonlight, garden, weapon)

### Anubhava (Consequents)
Observable effects of the emotion: gestures, expressions, voice changes. Includes eight **sāttvika bhāvas** — involuntary physical signs (sweating, trembling, tears, voice break, hair standing on end, fainting, change of color, paralysis).

### Vyabhicāribhāva (Transient States)
33 fleeting ancillary feelings that color and support the dominant emotion (e.g., shame, weariness, anxiety, pride, jealousy).

## The Formula for Rasa

Rasa arises from the **combination of vibhāva + anubhāva + vyabhicāribhāva** acting upon the sthayibhava in a cultivated audience.

## Required Audience: The Sahṛdaya

The "one with heart" — a spectator with aesthetic cultivation, emotional attunement, and artistic literacy. Without the sahṛdaya, no rasa arises. Aesthetic experience is co-produced.

## Evaluative Criteria

- Does the work evoke a clear, dominant rasa?
- Are determinants and consequents skillfully integrated?
- Does emotional authenticity emerge from technique, not stated assertion?
- Does the work transport the spectator beyond entertainment toward the expression of ultimate reality?
