# Italo Calvino — Six Memos for the Next Millennium

Calvino's planned 1985 Norton Lectures at Harvard. He wrote 5 of the planned 6 before his death.

## The Six Literary Values
Calvino identified the qualities of literature he wanted to advocate for the new millennium:

### 1. Lightness
"I have tried to remove weight, sometimes from people, sometimes from heavenly bodies, sometimes from cities; above all, I have tried to remove weight from the structure of stories and from language." Lightness is not frivolousness; it's a way of rising above the noise of life. Examples: Cavalcanti's poetry, Kundera's *The Unbearable Lightness of Being*.

### 2. Quickness
The art of narrative compression. The ability to compress vast experience into a few words. Quality writers make every word count toward speed of meaning. Folktales as the model.

### 3. Exactitude
Three primary aspects:
- A well-defined and well-calculated plan for the work
- Evocation of clear, incisive, memorable visual images
- A language as precise as possible both in choice of words and in expression of subtleties of thought and imagination

### 4. Visibility
The "visual imagination" — the writer's ability to make the reader see. Mental images are made of words; the writer must select words that conjure pictures. Calvino worried this faculty was endangered by media saturation.

### 5. Multiplicity
The novel as multiple worlds, multiple perspectives, multiple knowledge systems woven together. Quality fiction holds multiplicities without collapsing them. (Examples: Borges, Perec, Calvino's own *Invisible Cities*.)

### 6. Consistency (unwritten)
Calvino died before completing this memo. Speculation: he intended to discuss the way a great work coheres despite (or via) multiplicity.

## Quality Implications

### Lightness as Quality
The story doesn't have to be heavy to be serious. Lightness can carry the heaviest themes.

### Quickness as Discipline
Compression is craft. The folktale's economy is something modern fiction can recover.

### Exactitude as Precision
Both at the level of overall design and at word choice.

### Visibility as Sensory Imagination
Quality fiction is *seen* by the reader, not merely processed.

### Multiplicity as Range
A great work holds many systems, voices, knowledges in tension.

## Why It Matters
Calvino's memos remain among the most influential modern statements of what literature *is for*. The five completed memos function as both diagnostic (what's missing in much contemporary work) and prescription (what to aim for).

## Quality Tests
- Is the work *light* without being trivial?
- Is the work *quick* in its essential moments?
- Is the work *exact* — both architecturally and word-by-word?
- Does the work make the reader *see*?
- Does the work hold *multiplicity* without dissolving?
