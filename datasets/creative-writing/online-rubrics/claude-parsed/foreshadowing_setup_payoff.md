# Foreshadowing — Setup & Payoff

## The Three-Beat Rhythm
**Plant → Patience → Payoff**

Every piece of foreshadowing requires:
1. **Plant** — early presence (often disguised as ordinary detail)
2. **Patience** — distance between plant and payoff (often watered with subtle reminders)
3. **Payoff** — the moment of recognition

## The Reverse-Engineering Principle
Setup and payoff are written in reverse — the writer comes up with the *payoff* first, then goes back to find the optimum place to plant the seed.

## Quality Criteria
- **Subtle** — too obvious telegraphs the twist; too hidden feels arbitrary
- **Functional in the moment** — best plants do double duty (have meaning right now AND set up later)
- **"Watered" along the way** — subtle reminders keep the seed alive without spotlighting
- **Inevitable in retrospect** — readers should think "Oh, THAT'S what that meant"
- **Doesn't reduce surprise** — readers know the *twist* is coming because it's earned, not because they've solved it

## Famous Example (The Sixth Sense)
Scenes where Malcolm seems ignored take on perfect new meaning when we learn he's dead. Each scene was both meaningful then AND payoff-ready.

## Types of Foreshadowing
1. **Direct (heralds)** — explicit warning ("Beware the Ides of March")
2. **Indirect (hints)** — implication via image, dialogue, action
3. **Symbolic** — recurring symbol that prefigures
4. **Atmospheric** — mood/weather that signals coming events
5. **Chekhov's Gun** — object placed early, used later
6. **Foreshadowing by analogy** — earlier event mirrors later one
7. **Negative foreshadowing** — character says "this will never happen" (then it does)
8. **Prophecy** — explicit prediction that comes true (oracular)

## Common Failure Modes
- Setup without payoff (broken promise / Chekhov's gun unfired)
- Payoff without setup (deus ex machina / unearned twist)
- Heavy-handed setup that telegraphs everything
- Buried setup that feels random when paid off
- Excessive setup ahead of small payoff (front-loaded)

## The Balance
"Great foreshadowing feels inevitable in hindsight but surprising in the moment."

## Quality Tests
- After the payoff, do earlier scenes recontextualize?
- Could the reader, on re-read, identify the plants?
- Was the patience period long enough to forget the seed?
- Does the watering keep the seed alive without making it obvious?
