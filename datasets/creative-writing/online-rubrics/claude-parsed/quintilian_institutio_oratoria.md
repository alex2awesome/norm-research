# Quintilian — Institutio Oratoria (c. 95 CE)

12-volume Roman textbook on rhetoric. Foundational source for figures of speech and figures of thought; provides the classical rubric for verbal craft.

## Three Forms of Narrative

### 1. Fictitious (Mythos)
The fully invented — found in tragedies and poems. No requirement of factual truth; truth is artistic.

### 2. Realistic (Argumentum)
What is not true but **could be true** — comedy. Maintains verisimilitude. The realm of the realistic novel and contemporary fiction.

### 3. Historical (Historia)
Exposition of actual fact. The rhetorician should begin training here, "whose force is in proportion to its truth."

A storyteller crosses these categories knowingly; failures occur when the wrong mode is invoked for the matter.

## Tropes vs. Figures (Books 8–9)

### Tropes
A trope substitutes one word for another:
- **Metaphor** — implicit comparison
- **Metonymy** — substitution by association
- **Synecdoche** — part for whole
- **Antonomasia** — epithet for name
- **Onomatopoeia** — sound mimicking sense
- **Catachresis** — strained metaphor (sometimes a fault)
- **Allegory** — extended metaphor
- **Hyperbole** — exaggeration
- **Litotes** — affirmation by negation of contrary
- **Periphrasis** — circumlocution
- **Irony** — saying the opposite of what is meant

### Figures of Thought
Modify the manner of expression without substituting words:
- **Apostrophe** — direct address
- **Antithesis** — opposition
- **Aporia** — feigned doubt
- **Praeteritio** — mentioning by saying you won't mention
- **Erotema** — rhetorical question
- **Anaphora** — repetition at start of clauses
- **Epistrophe** — repetition at end
- **Chiasmus** — reversed parallelism
- **Asyndeton** — omission of conjunctions
- **Polysyndeton** — addition of conjunctions

## Five Canons of Rhetoric (background)

The classical training framework:
1. **Inventio** — finding what to say
2. **Dispositio** — arranging it
3. **Elocutio** — style; word choice; figures
4. **Memoria** — memorization
5. **Pronuntiatio** — delivery

## Style as Three Levels

Quintilian (following Cicero) distinguishes:
- **Plain style** — for instruction; minimal ornament
- **Middle style** — for pleasure; balanced
- **Grand style** — for moving the audience; full rhetorical resources

A skilled writer modulates among the three within a single work.

## Evaluative Criteria

A piece of prose can be evaluated on:
- Identification of narrative mode (fictitious / realistic / historical) and fitness
- Catalog of tropes and figures employed
- Whether figures serve meaning or merely decorate
- Style level (plain / middle / grand) and modulation
- Inventio (richness of material), dispositio (order), elocutio (style)

## Significance

Quintilian's terminological apparatus (most of the trope/figure names) is still the standard vocabulary of Western rhetoric, used in everything from law school to MFA programs. It supplies the scalpel-precise vocabulary by which prose can be described and judged at the verbal level.
