# Romance Writers of America — Genre Criteria

## RWA's Two Required Criteria
1. **A central love story** — "the main plot centers around individuals falling in love and struggling to make the relationship work."
2. **An emotionally satisfying and optimistic ending** — "lovers who risk and struggle for each other and their relationship are rewarded with emotional justice and unconditional love." Often abbreviated **HEA** (Happily Ever After) or **HFN** (Happy For Now).

## Sub-Genre Conventions (informally evaluated)
- **Contemporary romance** — modern setting, real-world stakes
- **Historical romance** — period-accurate setting, customs
- **Paranormal romance** — speculative element + romance arc
- **Romantic suspense** — thriller plot interwoven
- **Inspirational/Christian romance** — faith-anchored relationships
- **LGBTQ+ romance** — queer protagonists; same HEA/HFN requirement

## Common Quality Criteria for Romance (from RWA awards & industry)
- **Believable chemistry** between leads
- **Conflict that organically threatens the relationship** (not contrived misunderstandings)
- **Both characters change/grow** through the relationship
- **Emotional payoff** — reader should feel the satisfaction
- **Pacing of the romance arc** — meet-cute, attraction, obstacle, dark moment, declaration
- **Genre conventions respected** — readers expect HEA/HFN; subverting it disqualifies the work as romance
- **Heat level appropriate to category** (sweet → erotic, all valid)

## RWA Awards
- **Vivian Award** (formerly RITA, renamed 2021) — highest honor for published romance
