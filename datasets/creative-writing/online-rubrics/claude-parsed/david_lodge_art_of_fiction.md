# David Lodge — The Art of Fiction (1992)

50 short essays, each on one fiction technique, illustrated with a classic/modern passage. Originally newspaper columns.

## ~50 Topics Covered (Selected)

### Universal Aspects of Fiction
- Beginning
- Ending
- Point of View
- Introducing a Character
- Suspense
- Names
- The Sense of the Past
- Telephoning (in fiction)
- The Title
- A Sense of Place

### Specific Techniques
- **Stream of Consciousness** (Joyce, Woolf)
- **Interior Monologue** (Joyce again)
- **Repetition** (Hemingway)
- **Defamiliarisation** (Shklovsky concept; Tolstoy "Kholstomer")
- **Time-Shift / Analepsis** (Ford Madox Ford)
- **The Intrusive Author** (Fielding, George Eliot)
- **The Unreliable Narrator** (Booth, Ishiguro)
- **The Epistolary Novel** (Richardson, Walker)
- **Free Indirect Style** (Austen, Flaubert, James)
- **Showing and Telling**
- **Different Voices** (multi-perspective novels)
- **Coincidence**
- **Symbolism**
- **Allegory**
- **Irony**
- **Metafiction**
- **Intertextuality**
- **The Comic Novel**
- **Surrealism**
- **Magical Realism**
- **The Nonfiction Novel**
- **Implication**
- **The Title**
- **Ideas** (novels of ideas)
- **The Experimental Novel**

## Lodge's Method
Each chapter:
1. Quotes a passage exemplifying the technique
2. Defines the term
3. Traces its history briefly
4. Discusses examples
5. Suggests when/why a writer might use it

## Quality Criteria Embedded in Lodge's Selection
- Technique is *one of many* available — no single right approach
- Each technique has best-fit contexts
- Technical vocabulary sharpens reader and writer
- Tradition is a toolbox, not a constraint

## Why Useful as a Reference Rubric
Lodge's 50 categories form perhaps the most comprehensive technique-by-technique inventory of fiction craft. Each is testable: "Does this story use defamiliarization? Free indirect style? An intrusive narrator?"

## Companion Volume
*The Art of Fiction (1884)* by Henry James — earlier and more philosophical.
