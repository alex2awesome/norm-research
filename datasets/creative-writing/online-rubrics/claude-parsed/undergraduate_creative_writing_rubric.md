# Undergraduate Creative Writing Assessment Rubric (Indiana University South Bend)

## Standard Categories (typical of US undergraduate CW rubrics)

### Facility with Language
- Diction precision
- Sentence rhythm
- Syntactic variety
- Control of register
- Avoidance of cliché

### Style and Voice
- Distinctive
- Consistent
- Appropriate to subject
- Recognizable
- Earned (not imitative)

### Sense of Genre and Tradition
- Knowledge of conventions
- Awareness of predecessors
- Productive engagement with form
- Originality within tradition

### Creativity and Originality
- Unexpected angles
- Fresh treatment of familiar material
- Risk-taking with form/voice/subject
- Avoidance of the obvious

### Thematic Insight
- Substantive depth
- Issues addressed with seriousness
- Complexity of moral/philosophical engagement

### Narrative Development
- Plot architecture
- Causal momentum
- Pacing
- Resolution

### Establishment of Lyric Meditation (for poetry/CNF)
- Sustained reflective stance
- Image patterns
- Resonance

### Reflection on Craft
- Self-awareness of choices
- Articulation of decisions
- Evidence of revision

### Mechanics
- Grammar
- Punctuation
- Spelling
- Format
(Less weight than craft categories — but still required)

## Common Scoring Scale
4–6 levels: Beginning / Developing / Proficient / Strong / Exemplary
OR letter-grade-aligned: F / D / C / B / A

## Critical Note
Many academic rubrics weight non-creative elements (mechanics, organization, structure) at >50% of the score, even though "the primary purpose of creative writing is creativity." This reflects a tension in the field between teaching evaluation (mechanical proficiency provable) and artistic evaluation (creativity hard to grade).

## A Better Balance (per academic literature)
- 40–60% creativity/voice/originality
- 20–30% craft execution
- 10–20% mechanics/format
- 10% revision evidence
