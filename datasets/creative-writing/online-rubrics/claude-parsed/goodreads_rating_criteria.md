# Goodreads — Reader Rating & Review Criteria

## The 5-Star Scale (Official)
- 5 stars — "It was amazing"
- 4 stars — "Really liked it"
- 3 stars — "Liked it"
- 2 stars — "It was OK"
- 1 star — "Did not like it"

## Common Reader Rating Categories (Crowdsourced from Goodreads communities)
Many readers explicitly use multi-dimensional rubrics:

### Plot
- Captivating, full of twists, with stakes that matter
- Pacing — no sag in the middle
- Resolution that earns reader investment

### Characters
- Distinct personalities
- Growth/transformation across the book
- Believable motivations
- Compelling protagonist + worthy antagonist

### Writing
- Prose enjoyable to read
- Descriptive without being purple
- Voice distinctive
- Few typos / errors

### World-building (genre-relevant)
- Internally consistent
- Sensorily rich
- Original yet legible

### Emotional impact
- Made me feel something
- Stayed with me after reading
- Re-readability

### Personal enjoyment
- Did I want to keep reading?
- Did I recommend it?
- Did the book deliver on its promise?

## Goodreads Quirk
Most popular books cluster between 3.5–4.5; ratings reflect both quality and audience match. A 5-star "amazing" rating is generously given, often for an emotional connection more than craft mastery.

## Implications for Crowd-Sourced Reddit Story Ratings
- Reader ratings reflect emotional resonance + craft + genre match
- Outliers (1-star) often stem from genre/expectation mismatch, not just craft failure
- Aggregate scores cluster narrowly; signal often lies in distribution, not mean
