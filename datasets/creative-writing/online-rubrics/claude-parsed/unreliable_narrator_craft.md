# Unreliable Narrator — Types & Craft Principles

## Definition
A storyteller whose account is deceptive, subjective, or flawed (intentionally or not) — creating ambiguity and tension. Coined by Wayne Booth in *The Rhetoric of Fiction* (1961).

## Types

### 1. The Deliberate Liar
Consciously misleads readers (e.g., Humbert Humbert in *Lolita*; the narrator of Gillian Flynn's *Gone Girl*)

### 2. The Self-Deceiver
Believes their own false narrative; narrating in good faith but deluded (e.g., Stevens in *The Remains of the Day*)

### 3. The Mentally Compromised
Perception affected by illness, trauma, or substance (Christopher in *The Curious Incident…*; the narrator in *Memento*)

### 4. The Naïve Observer
Lacks experience to understand what they're seeing — children, sheltered narrators (Scout in *To Kill a Mockingbird*; Holden Caulfield)

### 5. The Morally Ambiguous
Values skew their interpretation (Patrick Bateman in *American Psycho*; the narrator of *A Clockwork Orange*)

### 6. The Unwitting (Plot-Level)
Doesn't know key facts the reader/other characters do; asymmetric information

### 7. The Ironic / Implied-Author Distant
Narrator's worldview is not the implied author's; reader sees through

## Craft Techniques
1. **Establish trust first** — settle reader into believing
2. **Plant subtle inconsistencies** — small enough to miss first read, recognizable in hindsight
3. **Mostly accurate details** — outright lies from the start break trust
4. **Motivated unreliability** — must have a reason in character or plot
5. **Strategic reveal** — dramatic single moment OR gradual aha OR never confirmed
6. **Ironic distance** — implied author signals what narrator misses

## Quality Criteria
- The unreliability is *purposeful*, not just a gimmick
- Reader's discovery of unreliability *deepens* meaning, doesn't merely surprise
- Re-reading rewards new perspective
- Narrator's voice is consistent within their own logic
- Author and reader maintain a knowing relationship behind narrator's back

## Common Failure Modes
- Narrator simply lies for plot mechanics (no character logic)
- Reveal of unreliability erases everything before (cheating)
- Inconsistencies are author errors, not character traits
- Reader can never tell if anything is true (unmoored ambiguity)
- Reveal is "Aha! It was all a dream!" (cliché)

## Booth's Distinction
Reliable narrator = speaks/acts in accord with norms of the work
Unreliable narrator = does not
The implied author is the standard against which we judge reliability.
