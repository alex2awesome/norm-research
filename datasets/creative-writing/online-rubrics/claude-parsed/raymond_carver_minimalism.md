# Raymond Carver — Minimalism in Short Fiction

## Core Aesthetic
"Less is more." Sparse narrative; simple, straightforward language; *more* unsaid than said. No elaborate plot or verbose detailing.

## Influences
- Hemingway's iceberg theory (omission with knowledge)
- Editor Gordon Lish (controversially heavy editing of early Carver stories — many "Carver" trademark-minimalist pieces were Lish-shaped)

## Key Craft Techniques
1. **Elliptical dialogue** — characters talk past each other; conversations end before they conclude; silences carry weight
2. **Scene-jump structure** — elliptical transitions between scenes leaving the reader to fill gaps
3. **Defamiliarization** — making the ordinary strange
4. **Juxtaposition** — meanings emerge from clashing scenes/images
5. **Ambiguity** — endings refuse closure
6. **Parallel structure** — recurring phrasing, mirrored events
7. **Suggestive imagery** — small, charged details
8. **Pared-back diction** — Anglo-Saxon over Latinate vocabulary
9. **Withheld interiority** — limited access to thoughts; behavior over psychology
10. **Working-class subject matter** — bartenders, mechanics, broken marriages, alcoholism

## Quality Criteria for Minimalism
- Every word must do work
- Surface implies depth (iceberg)
- Reader does the meaning-making
- Specificity at moment of action
- Silence as eloquence
- Limited vocabulary that intensifies attention

## Famous Carver Stories
- "What We Talk About When We Talk About Love"
- "Cathedral"
- "A Small, Good Thing"
- "Where I'm Calling From"
- "Will You Please Be Quiet, Please?"

## "Dirty Realism"
Term coined by Bill Buford (*Granta*) for Carver-school fiction. Plain language, blue-collar settings, emotional reticence, secular grief.

## Carver's Own Statements
- "Get in. Get out. Don't linger."
- "Use no superfluous word."
- A short story is "a glimpse" — small but devastating.
- The writer must "stand at his own window and see the world freshly."

## Minimalist Vs. Maximalist
- **Minimalist** (Carver, Hempel, Tobias Wolff, Mary Robison) — restraint, ellipsis
- **Maximalist** (Pynchon, DeLillo, Wallace, Saunders, Diaz) — abundance, density, voice
Both produce great work; opposite means.
