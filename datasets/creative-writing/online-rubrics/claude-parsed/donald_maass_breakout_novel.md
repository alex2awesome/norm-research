# Donald Maass — Writing the Breakout Novel

Maass is a long-time literary agent (Donald Maass Literary Agency).

## Core Principle: Tension on Every Page
"Tension on every page is the secret of great storytelling." Even quiet scenes need micro-tension.

## Breakout Fundamentals
1. **Strong characters** — three-dimensional, contradictory
2. **Inner conflict** — characters at war with themselves, not just outside obstacles
3. **Personal stakes** — protagonist must risk something *they* care about (not just "save the world")
4. **Plot layers** — multiple storylines that interweave and amplify
5. **Powerful scenes** — every scene must turn a value
6. **Micro-tension** — line-by-line tension within and between scenes

## Tension Sources
- Conflict (external)
- Inner conflict
- Bridging conflict (small tension between major beats)
- Anticipation
- Dread
- Curiosity
- Withheld information
- Time pressure

## High Stakes Test
- Could the protagonist lose something irreplaceable?
- Are the goals/dreams/life genuinely at risk?
- Are emotional, physical, and spiritual stakes all engaged?

## Pacing Principle
Vary pace deliberately — alternate high-tension scenes with reflective ones. Pure intensity exhausts; pure reflection bores.

## Breakout vs. Solid
- A solid novel: competent execution of genre conventions
- A breakout novel: breaks beyond convention through:
  - Unique voice
  - Larger-than-genre themes
  - Surprising character depth
  - Unforgettable scenes
  - Resonant emotional truth
- Maass argues breakouts are *engineered*, not born — through deliberate craft choices
