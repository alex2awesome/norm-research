# 25 Things You Should Know About Character

1. **The Character As Fulcrum** — Characters form the foundation; all story elements rest upon them.
2. **The Cure For All That Ails** — A compelling character can compensate for weak plotting or themes.
3. **Connected To Everything** — Characters are "the DNA in every goddamn cell" of the narrative.
4. **You Are The Dealer; Character Is Drug** — Audiences become addicted to great characters.
5. **Tell Us What She Wants** — Establish clear objectives early; audiences must grasp motivation.
6. **Not About Likability But Livability** — Audiences needn't like characters; they must want to spend time with them.
7. **The Give-A-Fuck Factor** — Identify upfront why audiences should care.
8. **Rub Up Against Remarkability** — Characters must prove worthy of their own story.
9. **Act Upon The World** — Proactive characters drive engagement; passivity creates dullness.
10. **Bad Decisions Are Good Decisions** — Flawed, reactive choices create authenticity and tension.
11. **This Is Why Jesus Invented Suspense** — Beloved characters making poor choices create tension.
12. **How You Succeed By Not Having Them Succeed** — Become an antagonistic force frustrating their path.
13. **The Code** — Each character maintains an internal moral compass; violations feel inauthentic unless earned.
14. **A B C** — Map three character transformations across your narrative.
15. **Boom Goes The Dynamite** — Provide moments where audiences pump their fists.
16. **Beware The Everyman, Fear The Chosen One** — Avoid bland ordinariness and unearned specialness.
17. **Nobody Sees Themselves As Supporting** — Secondary characters inhabit full lives where they're protagonists.
18. **The Main MC, DJ Protag** — Your protagonist should remain the most compelling presence.
19. **You Are Not Your Character, Except When You Are** — Don't create self-inserts, yet infuse personal emotional experiences.
20. **Fugged Up** — Every character needs flaws and darkness.
21. **A Tornado Beneath Cool Breeze** — Balance surface simplicity with underlying complexity.
22. **On The Subject Of Archetypes** — Begin with familiar types, then transcend them.
23. **Dialogue Over Description, Action Over Rumination** — Show character through speech and deeds.
24. **Take The Test Drive** — Experiment with characters in informal writing first.
25. **Get All Up In Them Guts** — Develop intimate knowledge so choices feel inevitable.
