# Russian Formalism (1910s–1930s)

Movement that systematized analysis of literary devices. Central to modern narrative theory.

## Key Concepts

### Literariness (Literaturnost)
The properties that make a text literary rather than practical communication. The proper object of literary study is not biographical or sociological context but **what makes language literary**.

### Defamiliarization (Ostranenie / Остранение)
**Viktor Shklovsky's** central concept. Art makes the ordinary strange, restoring the freshness of perception that habit dulls. "The technique of art is to make objects unfamiliar, to make forms difficult, to increase the difficulty and length of perception, because the process of perception is an aesthetic end in itself."

A test of literary craft: does the work renew our perception of its subject?

### Fabula vs. Syuzhet
- **Fabula** (story): the chronological raw events
- **Syuzhet** (plot/discourse): the artistic ordering of those events as the reader encounters them

The distinction is foundational to all subsequent narratology.

### The Device (Priyom)
Art is the sum of devices the artist manipulates — repetition, parallelism, retardation, framing, displacement, inversion. The device is the unit of literary craft.

### Motivation
The justification for the inclusion of a device or motif within the narrative — realistic, compositional, or artistic motivation.

### Bound vs. Free Motifs
- **Bound motifs**: cannot be omitted without destroying the causal-temporal sequence
- **Free motifs**: digressions, asides, tangents that contribute aesthetically but not causally

### The Dominant
**Roman Jakobson**. The structuring component of a work — the element that governs and transforms the others. Identifying the dominant is central to genre and style analysis.

### Skaz
A first-person narrator with a strongly oral, vernacular voice — the narrator's manner of speaking is itself a device.

## Key Figures

- **Viktor Shklovsky** — defamiliarization, plot devices
- **Boris Eichenbaum** — formal method, skaz
- **Yuri Tynyanov** — literary evolution, parody
- **Roman Jakobson** — poetic function, the dominant
- **Boris Tomashevsky** — motifs (bound/free), thematics
- **Vladimir Propp** — morphology of folktale (associated)

## Evaluative Implications

A text can be analyzed by:
- Its catalog of devices
- The relation of fabula to syuzhet (how the artistic ordering distorts the chronological)
- Whether it defamiliarizes its subject
- Identifying the dominant element
- The proportion and integration of bound/free motifs
