# Short Story Literary Analysis — Elements & Devices

Standard college-level framework used to evaluate short fiction in English departments.

## Literary Elements (How a Story is Built)
1. **Plot** — exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution
2. **Character** — protagonist, antagonist, foils, round vs. flat, dynamic vs. static
3. **Setting** — location, time period, time of day, weather, social atmosphere, economic conditions
4. **Theme** — main idea, lesson, message about human condition / society / life
5. **Point of View** — first/second/third; limited/omniscient/objective; reliable/unreliable
6. **Tone** — author's attitude toward subject
7. **Mood** — emotional atmosphere created in reader
8. **Style / Diction** — word choice, sentence structure, register
9. **Conflict** — person vs. person, self, society, nature, supernatural, technology

## Literary Techniques (How Language Is Manipulated)
- **Metaphor / Simile** — comparison
- **Personification** — non-human gets human traits
- **Symbolism** — object/character represents larger idea
- **Imagery** — sensory description
- **Foreshadowing** — early hints of later events
- **Flashback** — earlier event inserted into present
- **Allusion** — reference to other text/event
- **Irony** — verbal, situational, dramatic
- **Hyperbole** — exaggeration
- **Understatement / Litotes** — opposite of hyperbole
- **Onomatopoeia** — sound-imitating words
- **Alliteration / Assonance / Consonance** — sound patterning
- **Motif** — recurring image/idea
- **Juxtaposition** — meaningful contrast
- **Parallelism** — structural repetition
- **Synecdoche / Metonymy** — part for whole / related thing for thing
- **Anaphora / Epistrophe** — repetition at start/end
- **Chiasmus** — inverted parallelism
- **Pathos / Ethos / Logos** — emotional/credible/logical appeals
- **Defamiliarization** — making the familiar strange (Shklovsky)
- **Verisimilitude** — appearance of reality

## Quality Analysis Questions
- How do plot/character/setting interact to develop theme?
- Does the POV reveal more than it states?
- Are literary devices integrated with meaning, or decorative?
- Does the title comment on the story?
- Does the ending recontextualize what came before?
- Are gaps in the text purposeful (iceberg) or sloppy?
