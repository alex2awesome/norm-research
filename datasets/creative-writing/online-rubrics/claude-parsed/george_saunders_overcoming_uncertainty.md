# George Saunders on Overcoming Uncertainty in Writing

## Key Craft Principles

**Reframe Problems as Potential.** Rough patches are "avoidance moments" where your subconscious is working through something important. "A rough patch in a story is not an error or a defect or evidence of our lack of talent."

**Stories Develop in Waves.** A good story occurs in waves; a piece typically requires numerous drafts before reaching final form. Imperfection at an intermediate stage is normal and productive.

**Practice Active Waiting.** When you encounter unclear moments, the solution isn't immediate fixing. Keep revising that section and surrounding material "with affection and hope, until it relents and pops into clarity."

**Treat Problematic Passages as Load-Bearing.** Confusing spots often contain the story's core identity. Approach them "like a future all-star" — they lag because they carry significant potential.

**Recognize Interconnected Problems.** Avoidance moments operate as "a co-dependent system" — solving one often helps resolve others.

## Revision Method (Story Club)
Saunders revises by imagining a meter with "P" (Positive) and "N" (Negative), reading his story the way he would read someone else's, noting honest reactions before editing — thousands of "micro-decisions" that are intuitive: simply preferring one thing to another.

## On Plot Through Character
"If I make a person in enough detail, this creates plot. That is: if, over the course of a few pages, I make a vivid, funny person that the reader feels some connection with, anything that happens to that person is going to feel meaningful."
