# Jane Smiley — 13 Ways of Looking at the Novel

Pulitzer-winning novelist's exploration of the novel as form, after reading 100 novels in three years.

## Smiley's Definition of the Novel
A novel is "a (1) lengthy, (2) written, (3) prose, (4) narrative with a (5) protagonist."

## The 12 Styles of Discourse a Novel May Contain
Great novels contain many of these:
1. **Travel**
2. **History**
3. **Biography**
4. **Tale**
5. **Joke**
6. **Gossip**
7. **Diary or Letter**
8. **Confession**
9. **Polemic**
10. **Essay**
11. **Epic**
12. **Romance**

## The Circle of the Novel
12 aspects in a circular relation:
- Exposition
- Rising action
- Climax
- Denouement
- Plus turning points and structural moments

## Smiley's Core Claims

### The Novel as Freedom
The novel is "perhaps the ultimate expression of freedom" — artistic, personal, political. Reading novels develops the ability to see multiple POVs.

### The Novel as Subversive
Novel-reading "incubates an ability to see multiple points of view, thus leading to a more liberal view of the world." The form itself is politically and morally formative.

### What Makes a Novel "Work"
- Sustained engagement
- Multi-stylistic richness
- A protagonist worth following
- Length sufficient for development
- Prose (not verse) as the medium

## Quality Implications for Fiction
- Range of "discourse modes" within a single work signals ambition and depth
- A novel that operates in only one mode is impoverished
- Length should be earned by content; arbitrary brevity OR length both fail
- The protagonist should be *worth* the time investment

## Critical Note
Smiley doesn't actually deliver 13 well-defined "ways of looking" as the title suggests; the book is a discursive exploration of novel-form rather than a strict taxonomy.
