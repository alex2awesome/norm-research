# Gabriel García Márquez — On the Craft of Magical Realism

Compiled from interviews, essays, and the body of critical analysis on his technique. Provides a craft rubric distinct from realist fiction.

## Core Technique: The Deadpan Magical

The omniscient narrator reports impossible events with **deadpan objectivity**. The story's power comes from extraordinary details rendered with the same matter-of-factness as ordinary ones. Levitation is described in the same tone as sweeping the floor.

## Authorial Reticence

The narrator does **not explain** the magical. No marvelling, no asides, no signaling of the unusual. The reader must accept the magical because the text gives them no permission to question it.

García Márquez learned this from his grandmother, whose tone never varied whether she was telling him about ghosts or about the price of corn.

## Numerically Specific Exaggeration

Hyperbole gains reality when it is **precise**.
- Colonel Aureliano Buendía's "thirty-two armed uprisings, all of which he lost"
- The rainstorm that lasts "four years, eleven months, and two days"
- A hundred years of solitude

Vague hyperbole reads as vague; specific hyperbole reads as fact.

## Native Beliefs as Knowledge, Not Folklore

García Márquez's distinctive contribution: native and popular beliefs (ghosts, premonitions, miracles) are **valid knowledge** within the narrative — not exotic decoration, not folklore to be explained. The narrator holds the same epistemology as the characters.

This breaks the colonial framing in which Western rationality is the implicit standard against which "magic" is measured.

## Saturation

Magical events are not rare punctuations — they saturate the world. Macondo is a place where strange things constantly happen. Strangeness becomes the background.

## Sensory Overload Coupled with Restraint

Descriptions are sensory-rich (smells, colors, weather, fruit) but the **narrator's emotional commentary is restrained**. The reader feels through the senses without being told what to feel.

## Cyclical Time

Latin American magical realism often uses cyclical or recursive time — the same names recur across generations, events rhyme, history repeats. Linear narrative is a Western imposition.

## Inheritance from Faulkner and Borges

García Márquez named both as ancestors. Faulkner's mythologized Yoknapatawpha taught him to make a place into a cosmos; Borges's deadpan handling of impossibilities taught him the tone.

## Evaluative Criteria for Magical Realism

A magical-realist work succeeds when:
- The deadpan narrator's tone is unbroken across natural and supernatural
- Hyperbole is numerically specific
- Magical events arise from the same epistemology as ordinary events (not exoticized)
- Native beliefs operate as knowledge, not as primitivism to be explained
- Sensory description is rich without emotional commentary
- The world is saturated with strangeness, not punctuated by it

## Failure Modes

Magical realism fails when:
- The narrator winks (signals the magical as magical)
- Hyperbole is vague rather than precise
- Beliefs are framed as quaint or exotic
- The work reads as fantasy rather than magical realism (the magical becomes the rule, not the woven texture)
