# Newbery Medal — Official Evaluation Criteria

Awarded annually by the Association for Library Service to Children (ALSC, a division of the American Library Association) for "the most distinguished contribution to American literature for children."

## Six Core Criteria

The committee considers all six dimensions; a book need not excel in every area but should be distinguished in those relevant to the work.

### 1. Theme / Concept
The intellectual or moral substance of the book — what it explores, the ideas it puts in motion. Considered "the backbone of the Newbery award" — without strong thematic development, no work is remembered.

### 2. Plot Development
How events unfold in service of meaning. Plot is evaluated for how it impacts other elements — especially character and theme — not as event sequence alone.

### 3. Character Development
The depth, dimensionality, and authenticity of characters. How well do they grow, change, or persist? Are they recognizable as human (or as the kind of being they are)?

### 4. Setting
Delineation of time period, location, and any world-building. The setting must be specific, integrated with theme, and serve the work's emotional and intellectual goals.

### 5. Appropriateness of Style
The "how" — diction, syntax, voice, pacing. Does the style match the matter, the age range, the theme? Style is judged not by "good prose" abstractly but by fitness for this particular work.

### 6. Accuracy, Clarity, and Organization of Information
For nonfiction or fiction with factual elements. Does the work present information accurately? Is it organized in a way the child reader can follow?

## Child Audience Criterion

A book must be evaluated as a contribution to **children's** literature — appropriate to its intended age range. This includes:
- Conceptual accessibility
- Emotional fitness
- Imaginative invitation

A book that is technically distinguished but inaccessible to children does not qualify.

## Eligibility Constraints (procedural, not aesthetic)

- Original work
- Author must be U.S. citizen or resident
- Published in English in the U.S. during the prior year

## Voting Procedure

Committee members rank top 3:
- 1st place: 4 points
- 2nd place: 3 points
- 3rd place: 2 points

Winner must receive ≥8 first-place votes AND be ≥8 points ahead of second place.

## Implication for Rubric

A book of children's fiction can be evaluated by:
- Strength of theme/concept
- Plot's service of theme/character
- Character development
- Specificity of setting
- Stylistic fitness
- Information accuracy (where relevant)
- Appropriateness for child audience
