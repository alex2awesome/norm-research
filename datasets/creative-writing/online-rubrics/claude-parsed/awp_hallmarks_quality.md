# AWP — Hallmarks of Quality (Creative Writing)

The Association of Writers & Writing Programs is the dominant US professional organization for academic creative writing, serving ~50,000 writers and 500 programs.

## Mission Statement
"Amplifies the voices of writers and the academic programs and organizations that serve them while championing diversity and excellence in creative writing."

## Document Series ("Hallmarks")
AWP publishes "Hallmarks" guideline documents for various program types:
- Hallmarks for Diversity, Equity, Inclusion, Accessibility
- Hallmarks of a Successful MFA Program
- Hallmarks of an Effective Low-Residency MFA Program
- Hallmarks of an Effective BFA / BA Major
- Hallmarks of an Effective Minor
- Hallmarks of an Effective Two-Year College Program

(Many full documents are members-only, but the principles are published widely.)

## Standards Embedded in AWP Pedagogy

### Workshop-Based Instruction
- Peer critique
- Iterative revision
- Practical craft over theoretical analysis
- Manuscript-focused discussion

### Curricular Diversity Requirements
- Multiple literary periods
- Multicultural American (and global) traditions
- Varied craft topics
- Genre breadth (fiction, poetry, CNF, drama, screenwriting)

### Faculty Standards
- Excel at both holistic and line-specific revision suggestions
- Active publishing/practicing writers
- Mentorship rather than purely lecture-based

### Student Standards (for graduation)
- Produce a publishable literary work
- Demonstrate expertise in primary genre
- Engage with secondary genres

## Implicit Quality Criteria for "Publishable" Work
From the convergent practice of AWP-affiliated programs:
- Distinctive voice
- Sentence-level mastery
- Earned formal control
- Ambition of subject
- Craft choices that fit the work
- Originality balanced with tradition-awareness
- Revision-evidenced (multiple drafts visible in process)

## Why AWP Matters
AWP is the dominant gatekeeper of *what counts as serious creative writing* in US academia. Its workshop model and aesthetic preferences (often called "MFA aesthetic") shape:
- What gets taught
- What gets published in literary journals
- What gets reviewed favorably
- What gets nominated for prizes
- And, downstream, what crowd-sourced readers compare new work *to*
