# John Truby — The Anatomy of Story (22 Steps)

Truby is a Hollywood story consultant; *The Anatomy of Story* (2007) is one of the most thorough modern story-structure books.

## The Designing Premise
"All stories have a premise, but only good stories have a *designing* premise."
- Premise = your story in one line
- Designing premise = the premise that *creates* the story; the seed from which everything grows

## The 22 Steps

### Phase 1: Foundation (Setup)
1. **Self-revelation, need, and desire** — protagonist's inner psychology
2. **Ghost and story world** — backstory wound + the world that shaped them
3. **Weakness and need** — moral and psychological flaws + what they must learn
4. **Inciting event** — disrupts the world
5. **Desire** — what protagonist consciously wants (the goal)

### Phase 2: Cast & Conflict
6. **Ally or allies** — who helps
7. **Opponent and/or mystery** — antagonist (or the puzzle)
8. **Fake-ally opponent** — apparent friend who's actually a foe
9. **First revelation and decision: changed desire and motive** — protagonist learns something, adjusts
10. **Plan** — strategy to win

### Phase 3: Conflict Builds
11. **Opponent's plan and main counterattack**
12. **Drive** — protagonist takes action against opponent
13. **Attack by ally** — protagonist's allies challenge them
14. **Apparent defeat** — looks like all is lost
15. **Second revelation and decision: obsessive drive, changed desire and motive** — protagonist re-commits

### Phase 4: Climax & Resolution
16. **Audience revelation** — readers learn key info
17. **Third revelation and decision** — protagonist learns more
18. **Gate, gauntlet, visit to death** — the trial
19. **Battle** — the climax
20. **Self-revelation** — protagonist realizes the truth about themselves
21. **Moral decision** — based on self-revelation, makes a moral choice
22. **New equilibrium** — new state of being

## Truby's Key Innovations
- **Moral argument** — the story is a argument about the right way to live; opposed by antagonist's counter-argument
- **Designing premise** — premise that organizes everything
- **Self-revelation as climax** — the inner change that the outer plot drives
- **Character web** — interlocking flaws/needs across all major characters
- **Story-as-organism** — every part connected to every other

## Quality Tests
- Is there a designing premise?
- Does the protagonist have a clear ghost, weakness, need, desire?
- Does the antagonist embody the opposing moral argument?
- Does the climax produce self-revelation?
- Does self-revelation drive the moral decision?
- Are allies and opponents organized into a web of flaws?

## Truby vs. Other Frameworks
- More granular than McKee or Snyder
- More moral than Field
- Less mystical than Campbell
- More integrated character + plot than most
