# Chuck Wendig Interview — The Creative Penn

## Memorable Characters
- Characters must be active and interesting; "they have to do stuff."
- Avoid the passive "everyman vibe" — it tends toward boredom.
- Use the "Save the Cat" moment to show ethos, wants, desires in action.
- Example: Miriam Black perceives how people will die upon touch — psychological tension drives every choice.

## Crafting Effective Metaphors
- Metaphors are "like fingerprints" — they reveal how the author's mind works.
- Strong metaphors combine "two things that don't belong together."
- Metaphors must work both visually and emotionally.
- Wendig's anecdote: mistaking a rock for a wounded bird due to poor eyesight illustrates how seeing things differently fuels creative metaphor.
- Practice the metaphor "muscle" regularly to strengthen overall writing.

## Writing Dark Content
- Fear naturally drives compelling narratives.
- "Every novel is, in its core, a horror story" — writers help audiences confront universal anxieties through characters' struggles.
