# Mary Karr — The Art of Memoir

(Memoir is technically nonfiction, but craft principles apply to first-person literary fiction too.)

## Core Principles

### Voice Is Everything
"Each great memoir lives or dies based 100 percent on voice." Establishing authentic literary voice requires confronting both your good and bad qualities. "Whatever people like about you will manifest on the page, and what drives them crazy will keep you humble; you'll need both sides of yourself to hold a reader's attention."

### The Truth Contract
With the reader. Memoir relies on memory, which is fallible. The memoirist must strive for truth within those limits. (For first-person fiction: parallel implicit contract about authenticity of internal experience.)

### Carnality / Sensory Detail
The text's "carnality" — its sensual data, leveraged to create multidimensional reader experience. Karr argues sensory detail is *the* path into emotional truth, not adjacent to it.

### The Inner Enemy
Each memoirist (each first-person narrator) has an inner antagonist — a self-deception, blind spot, or moral failing. The book becomes interesting when the writer faces it.

### Memory's Texture
Show how memory works — its gaps, its sensory hooks, its contradictions. Don't pretend to remember everything; lean into the partial, hauntological quality of recall.

### Selecting Memory
The memoirist (and novelist working from life) curates radically. The 5 minutes that make the chapter are chosen from 10,000.

### The Risk of Honesty
Karr argues the great memoirs require risking the writer's own image — admitting what's shameful, ridiculous, or pathetic. Same for fiction with significant interiority.

## Quality Tests for Memoir / First-Person Fiction
- Does the voice feel inhabited, not performed?
- Are scenes specific (place, time, sense)?
- Does the narrator confront uncomfortable truth?
- Does the work avoid score-settling and self-mythologizing?
- Is the structure shaped, not just chronological?
- Are the small details load-bearing?

## Karr's Memoir Pantheon
*Speak, Memory* (Nabokov), *This Boy's Life* (Wolff), *The Liars' Club* (Karr), *Dispatches* (Herr), *Stop-Time* (Conroy), *The Color of Water* (McBride), *Just Kids* (Patti Smith).
