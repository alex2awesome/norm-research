# Setting & Worldbuilding — Craft Principles

## Definition
Worldbuilding constructs an imaginary setting — geography, history, cultures, power structures, rules — so the story feels grounded in a living, breathing place.

## Quality Criteria for Setting
1. **Sensory richness** — engages all 5 senses, not just sight
2. **Specific over generic** — particular details over abstract scene-setting
3. **Mood/atmosphere coherent** — setting feeds emotional tone
4. **Setting affects character** — landscape shapes psychology
5. **Setting affects plot** — place generates events
6. **Internal consistency** — invented rules followed
7. **Implied rather than explained** — no infodumps; details earn their place
8. **Contrast** — different locations have different feels (Hogwarts vs. Gringotts)
9. **Living, evolving world** — culture, economy, politics that move
10. **Verisimilitude** — even fantasy worlds feel internally true

## Elements of Setting
- **Time**: era, year, season, day, hour
- **Place**: country, region, city, building, room
- **Weather and climate**
- **Social context**: class, community, customs, language
- **Cultural context**: religion, art, food, dress
- **Economic context**: wealth, scarcity, work
- **Political context**: power structures, conflicts
- **Historical layers**: what came before this moment

## Worldbuilding Techniques
- **Iceberg principle** — know far more than you write
- **Reveal organically** — show consequences of history rather than recounting it
- **Specific names and details** — "the marketplace at Tasrekh" is more vivid than "the marketplace"
- **Sensory anchors** — smell of fish, sound of bells, taste of yam porridge
- **Telling objects** — a single household item can imply a culture
- **Character-mediated POV** — what the character notices reveals their world

## Common Failure Modes
- Travelogue scenes that pause story for description
- Generic "medieval European" fantasy
- World rules invoked only when convenient
- Map-without-people fantasy
- Future tech that doesn't change the human stakes
- Foreign settings as "exotic backdrop" without lived texture

## Setting as Character
Many great works treat setting as a character — Yoknapatawpha (Faulkner), Macondo (García Márquez), Brixton (Smith), Kerala (Roy). The place has agency, mood, history.
