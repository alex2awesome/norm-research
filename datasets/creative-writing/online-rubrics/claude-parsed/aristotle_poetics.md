# Aristotle — Poetics (c. 335 BCE)

The foundational Western treatise on dramatic and narrative theory.

## Six Qualitative Elements (in order of importance)

### 1. Plot (Mythos)
The "organization of incidents." The soul of tragedy. Should imitate an action evoking pity and fear. Plot transitions characters from good to bad fortune (or vice versa). **Complex plots** (involving reversal and recognition) are superior to **simple plots**.

### 2. Character (Ethos)
Characters must be:
- **Good** (between moral extremes)
- **Appropriate** (matching age, station, gender)
- **Consistent** (or "consistently inconsistent" if change is explained)
- Reveal themselves through actions and choices

### 3. Thought (Dianoia)
The "spoken reasoning" of characters; how they articulate ideas, motives, and arguments through dialogue.

### 4. Diction (Lexis)
Quality of speech and word choice. Speeches should reflect character and express meaning clearly.

### 5. Melody (Melos)
Musical/choreographic dimension; the chorus as an integrated character.

### 6. Spectacle (Opsis)
Visual apparatus (set, costumes, props). Aristotle's least-valued element — "least connected to the playwright's craft."

## Key Concepts

### Mimesis
Imitation/representation/expression of action. All art imitates.

### Catharsis
Purgation, purification, or clarification of the emotions of pity and terror experienced by spectators.

### Hamartia
"Miscalculation" or tragic error — a good character pushed toward undeserved misfortune through a mistake.

### Peripeteia
Reversal of fortune within the plot.

### Anagnorisis
Recognition or identification — moment when ignorance turns to knowledge.

## Quality Criteria for Plots

- **Completeness**: must have beginning, middle, and end
- **Magnitude**: sufficient scope for emotional change
- **Unity**: each event must follow logically from prior events
- **Probability and Necessity**: actions must be plausible; even surprises must seem necessary in retrospect
- **Complexity**: complex plots with reversals and recognitions are superior

## Definition of Tragedy

"Representation of a serious, complete action of magnitude, in embellished speech... by people acting and not by narration, accomplishing through pity and terror the catharsis of such emotions."
