# Stephen King's 20 Rules for Writers

Drawn from Stephen King's *On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft* (2000).

1. **Prioritize yourself first** — Write for personal satisfaction first; tell yourself the story initially. Rewriting focuses on removing non-essential elements.
2. **Avoid passive voice** — "Timid writers like passive verbs for the same reason that timid lovers like passive partners. The passive voice is safe."
3. **Minimize adverbs** — "The adverb is not your friend."
4. **Skip adverbs after dialogue tags** — Especially after "he said" and "she said."
5. **Don't obsess over grammar** — Focus on welcoming readers and telling engaging stories over perfect syntax.
6. **Trust your instincts / banish fear** — "I'm convinced that fear is at the root of most bad writing."
7. **Read extensively** — "If you don't have time to read, you don't have the time (or the tools) to write."
8. **Write truthfully** — Authentic expression matters more than pleasing others.
9. **Eliminate TV / distractions** — "TV…really is about the last thing an aspiring writer needs."
10. **Maintain a deadline** — Complete first drafts within three months, the length of a season.
11. **Sustain personal wellness** — Physical health and stable relationships support productivity.
12. **Work incrementally** — "The work is always accomplished one word at a time."
13. **Create a focused environment** — Remove phones, screens, games from your writing space.
14. **Develop your own voice / style** — Don't imitate other writers.
15. **Excavate stories** — "Stories are relics, part of an undiscovered pre-existing world." Lean on intuition over plot.
16. **Take breaks** — Six-week layoffs before revision provide fresh perspective.
17. **Edit ruthlessly / kill your darlings** — "Kill your darlings, kill your darlings…even when it breaks your egocentric little scribbler's heart."
18. **Background research appropriately** — Keep research unobtrusive and in backstory.
19. **Learn through practice** — Reading and writing extensively are the schoolmasters.
20. **Embrace creative joy** — "Writing is magic." Pursue intrinsic fulfillment.

## Additional Craft Principles
- **Description**: "A good description usually consists of a few well chosen details that will stand for everything else. Description should begin in the writer's imagination, but should finish in the readers."
- **Plot vs Story**: King distrusts plot — "our lives are largely plotless" — preferring intuition/discovery over plotting.
- **Opening line**: An opening line should invite the reader to begin the story.
- **2nd Draft = 1st Draft – 10%**: King's revision formula.
