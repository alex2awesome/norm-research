# Joseph Campbell — The Hero's Journey (Monomyth) — 17 Stages

From *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949). Three Acts: Departure, Initiation, Return.

## Departure
1. **The Call to Adventure** — Hero faces a problem/challenge in the ordinary world.
2. **Refusal of the Call** — Reluctance/fear; refuses initially.
3. **Supernatural Aid** — A mentor/guide provides assistance or talisman.
4. **The Crossing of the First Threshold** — Hero leaves ordinary world, commits to journey.
5. **Belly of the Whale** — Final separation from known world; symbolic death/rebirth.

## Initiation
6. **The Road of Trials** — Series of tests, tasks, ordeals.
7. **The Meeting with the Goddess** — Experience of unconditional love.
8. **Woman as Temptress** — Temptations away from the quest.
9. **Atonement with the Father** — Confrontation with ultimate power/authority.
10. **Apotheosis** — Spiritual death/rebirth; new understanding.
11. **The Ultimate Boon** — Achievement of the goal/treasure/wisdom.

## Return
12. **Refusal of the Return** — Reluctance to bring boon back.
13. **The Magic Flight** — Escape from the special world.
14. **Rescue from Without** — Help needed to return.
15. **The Crossing of the Return Threshold** — Re-entering ordinary world.
16. **Master of Two Worlds** — Balance between material and spiritual.
17. **Freedom to Live** — Ability to live in present moment without fear of death.

## Christopher Vogler's 12-Step Adaptation (used in Hollywood)
1. Ordinary World; 2. Call to Adventure; 3. Refusal of the Call; 4. Meeting the Mentor; 5. Crossing the Threshold; 6. Tests/Allies/Enemies; 7. Approach to Inmost Cave; 8. The Ordeal; 9. Reward (Seizing the Sword); 10. The Road Back; 11. Resurrection; 12. Return with the Elixir.

## Quality Implications
A "good" hero-journey story:
- Has a meaningful transformation
- Shows the protagonist faced real loss/sacrifice
- Returns the hero changed in a way that matters to others
- Honors archetypal patterns without slavishly imitating them
