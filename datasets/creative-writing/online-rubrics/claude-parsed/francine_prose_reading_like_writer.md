# Francine Prose — Reading Like a Writer

## Premise
The best teacher of writing is close reading of great writing. Each chapter focuses on a unit of craft, illustrated with passages from canonical authors.

## Chapter / Craft Element Sequence
1. **Close Reading** — Slow down; pay attention; the surface of the prose is everything
2. **Words** — The raw material of literature; precision matters
3. **Sentences** — "The well-made sentence transcends time and genre."
4. **Paragraphs** — Units of thought and rhythm
5. **Narration** — POV, distance, tense
6. **Character** — Action, dialogue, physical description as characterization tools
7. **Dialogue** — Don't "clean up" speech; capture its rhythm; learn from Le Carré on advancing plot via talk
8. **Details** — One or two telling details > paragraph of description (Flannery O'Connor)
9. **Gesture** — Character actions reveal character; not "physical clichés" but illuminations (Joyce, Mansfield)
10. **Learning from Chekhov** — The master of restraint, compassion, ambiguity
11. **Reading for Courage** — The encounter with great work emboldens you to try difficult things

## Quality Criteria from Prose
- **Sentence quality matters** at the line level — lazy sentences = lazy story
- **Telling detail beats description**
- **Gesture is one of fiction's most underused tools**
- **Dialogue reveals character through diction, rhythm, evasion**
- **POV is always a deliberate ethical choice** — what is being seen, by whom, with what knowledge
- **Read closely, then read more**

## Authors Prose Highlights
- Flaubert (Madame Bovary's eyes)
- Tolstoy (the small detail that does the work)
- Chekhov (compassion + restraint)
- Joyce (gesture as character)
- Mansfield (psychological precision)
- Flannery O'Connor (the cunning telling detail)
- Le Carré (plot through dialogue)
- Heinrich von Kleist (sudden movement)

## Mantra
"There are no general rules; there are only specific examples."
