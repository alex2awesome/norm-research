# Ursula K. Le Guin — 10 Essential Writing Tips

(Drawn from her essay responding to John Rechy's writing rules.)

## The 10 Tips

1. **'Show, Don't Tell' is for Beginners.** Useful as guide for inexperienced writers; bad as universal law. Mature writers know when to tell.
2. **So Is 'Write What You Know'.** Useful corrective against vagueness; limiting if absolute. You can write what you imagine, research, or invent.
3. **Do Your Job as a Writer, and Do it Really Well.** Craft is craft; honor the work.
4. **Shoot for the Top, Always.** Compare yourself to the best, not the mediocre.
5. **Write Like Who You Are.** Embrace your particular sensibility; pastiche is a phase, not a goal.
6. **Learn From the Greats.** Read deeply, absorb, then digress.
7. **Writing Is All About Learning to See.** Pay attention; the writer's first job is observation.
8. **Begin Your Story With a Voice.** A story starts when a voice begins to speak — not with a premise.
9. **Focus on the Rhythm of the Story.** The shape, pace, music. Listen as you write.
10. **Don't Waste Time.** Cut the unnecessary; respect the reader's hours.

## Le Guin's Meta-Principle on "Rules"
"As guides for beginners, [show-don't-tell, write-what-you-know] are useful. Expanded into laws, they are nonsense."

## How to Become a Writer
"You become a writer by writing. It is exactly the same with writing as with playing the tuba — you sit down and you do it, and you do it, and you do it, until you have learned how to do it."

## On Rule-Breaking
You can only break a rule meaningfully if you know it. "In order to perform a sacrifice, we must know what to sacrifice." (Quoting Tzvetan Todorov.)

## On Making Something Good
"The way to make something good is to make it well." There's no other path — no shortcut, no trick, no magic. The work itself is the way.
