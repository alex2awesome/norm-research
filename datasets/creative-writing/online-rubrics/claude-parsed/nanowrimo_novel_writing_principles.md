# NaNoWriMo — National Novel Writing Month Principles

## The Challenge
Write 50,000 words in November (1,667 words/day average). 50K is the loose minimum for a novel.

## Core Principles

### "Don't Edit While You Write"
"NaNoWriMo's golden rule." The month is for getting story onto page; revision comes later. Premature editing kills momentum.

### Plotter vs. Pantser
- **Plotter** — outlines extensively before writing
- **Pantser** — "writes by the seat of their pants"; discovery-drafts
- Most writers are somewhere between
- NaNoWriMo accommodates both styles

### Daily Word Count Discipline
The work happens through showing up. 1,667 words/day is achievable; consistency beats inspiration.

### Plan Ahead (for Plotters)
- Outline structure
- Brief cast of characters
- Research locations
- Have premise + ending in mind

### Lower Initial Quality Bar
First drafts are supposed to be rough. Lamott's "shitty first drafts" applied at industrial scale.

### Community Accountability
Public word counts, regional groups, write-ins. Social pressure helps.

## Quality Implications
- A polished story is rarely produced by NaNoWriMo alone — but a *first draft* is
- Volume of writing (across many years' participants) generates massive corpus useful for understanding what beginners produce
- 50K novels written in 30 days have characteristic structural problems: rushed endings, undercooked middles, voice inconsistencies, plot contradictions

## Implications for Quality Modeling
NaNoWriMo-style writing is a key training and reference corpus for understanding *amateur* fiction. The community's own discussions about what works/doesn't form a folk craft taxonomy.
