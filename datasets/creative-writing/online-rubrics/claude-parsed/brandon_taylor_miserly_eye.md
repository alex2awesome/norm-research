# Brandon Taylor — The Miserly Eye

## What He Advocates For
- **Selective pressure and focalization** — filter descriptions through a character's perspective rather than presenting "objective" visual data.
- Compressed imagery and linkage of moments, not exposition.
- "The power of negative capability" — readers construct meaning from gaps and silences.
- "The right image in the right place in a sequence" creates accumulated meaning.
- Modulate interiority carefully — keep it "quite tight" and "close to the incident."
- Exemplars: Hemingway, Greene, Highsmith.

## What He Argues Against
- **"Trite physicality"** — passages that function as mere "transcript of the moving image."
- Uncontrolled cinematic technique can "sap writing of vitality."
- Privileging incident so heavily that literature loses its **discursive power** — its ability to synthesize meaning alongside action.
- Loss of "fiction's great synthetic capacity to both portray and through portrayal comment upon the moments, structures, events, places, and things that make up life."
