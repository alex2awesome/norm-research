# Reedsy — 7 Elements of a Story

Major author-services platform's standard rubric for evaluating fiction craft.

## The Seven Elements

### 1. Theme
The underlying lesson or message driving the narrative. **Weave** theme into other elements rather than stating it. Readers should discover theme through inference, not exposition.

### 2. Characters
"Your characters give your story the depth it needs to keep readers wanting more — they are, quite literally, the life force of your story." Personality, relationships, and motivations form the story's foundation.

### 3. Setting
World, location, and time period. **Avoid excessive description**; weave exposition little by little. Setting should be felt, not described in blocks.

### 4. Plot
Sequence of events. Critical principle: each plot point must follow from character actions using **cause and effect** — "this happened, **therefore** that happened" — not "this happened, **then** that happened." Random sequence kills story.

### 5. Conflict
"Conflict is story." The obstacles and tension driving the protagonist forward. Without conflict, no narrative pulse. Conflict tests whether the protagonist achieves their goal.

### 6. Point of View
The narrative perspective (first person, third person omniscient, third person limited, second person). Affects:
- Tone of the story
- Reader's sympathy with characters
- What can and cannot be revealed

### 7. Style
The distinctive voice — pacing, tone, word choice. "No style is better than another — as long as you're being true to yourself."

## Implicit Evaluative Criteria

A story succeeds when:
- Theme emerges, never lectures
- Characters carry "life force"
- Setting is woven, not dumped
- Events follow causally (therefore, not then)
- Conflict drives every scene
- POV serves the story's contract with the reader
- Style is consistent and honest
