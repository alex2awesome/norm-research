# Janice Hardy — Fiction University (Narrative Drive Principles)

Long-running craft blog by YA fantasy novelist (*The Healing Wars* trilogy). Particularly known for systematic scene-craft checklists.

## Definition of a Scene

"A story-building block that consists of a goal, the attempt to resolve that goal, and the resolution of that goal."

## The Three-Part Scene Test

Every scene must contain:
1. **Goal** — the protagonist wants something (concrete, in this scene)
2. **Conflict** — something stands in the way
3. **Stakes** — something bad will happen if they fail

A scene that lacks any of the three is "not a scene" — it's exposition, vignette, or dead weight.

## The Red-Flag Question

"If you can't answer why a scene is in your novel, it's a huge red flag that the scene might not be serving the story."

Diagnostic test: ask of every scene, "Why is this here?" If the answer is "because that's what happened" or "because I wanted to show the character," cut it or rebuild it around goal/conflict/stakes.

## Narrative Drive

A "strong narrative drive" is what keeps readers turning pages. Drive comes from:
- **Active protagonist** — pursuing a goal, not waiting
- **Escalating stakes** — each scene raises what is at risk
- **Question generation** — the reader is always asking "what now? what next?"
- **Causal chain** — scene B is caused by scene A
- **Withheld information** — the reader senses something more

## Scene-Sequence Pattern (from Swain via Hardy)

Hardy uses the Scene/Sequel pattern:
- **Scene** = goal → conflict → setback (or partial victory)
- **Sequel** = reaction → dilemma → decision (sets up next scene's goal)

This produces continuous forward motion: every scene leaves the protagonist in a worse situation, and every sequel produces the new goal that drives the next scene.

## Common Failures Hardy Diagnoses

- Scenes without goals (filler)
- Goals without conflict (easy victory)
- Conflict without stakes (pointless)
- Resolution that simply ends without setting up the next goal (drift)
- Multiple parallel goals diluting drive
- Protagonist waiting for things to happen (passive)

## Self-Editing Tips

Hardy's revision checklist for narrative drive:
- Mark every scene with its **goal**
- Identify the **conflict**
- Identify the **stakes**
- Identify the **value shift** (positive to negative or vice versa)
- Check whether the **next scene flows** from this one's end

If any cell is empty, fix or cut.

## Evaluative Criteria

A scene can be assessed on:
- Goal clearly present
- Conflict clearly present
- Stakes clearly present
- Value shift accomplished
- Causal connection to prior and next scene
- Necessary to the larger story (would removing it break anything?)
- Forward momentum (reader wants to continue)
