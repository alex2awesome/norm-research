# Pulitzer Prize for Fiction — Selection Criteria

## Stated Criterion
"Distinguished fiction by an American author, preferably dealing with American life."

That's it — explicitly *no* fixed criteria. Each year's juries and the Pulitzer Prize Board determine what makes a work "distinguished."

## Process
- Jury of 5 reads ~300+ books
- Submits 3 nominations to Board
- Board selects winner by majority vote (or chooses different entry by 75% supermajority)
- Board may vote no award (has happened)

## Implicit Criteria (from history of winners)
- **American subject** strongly preferred (national life, history, identity)
- **Literary ambition** — Pulitzer rewards capital-L Literary Fiction
- **Significant scope** — even short novels (Strout, Brooks) tend to address substantial themes
- **Mastery of prose** at the line and page level
- **Critical consensus** matters — winners often have strong reviews already
- **Diversity** of voices increasingly emphasized in 21st century
- **Often historical / generational** — sweep matters
- **Moral seriousness** — engagement with ethics, identity, community

## Notable Pattern
Pulitzer often diverges from Booker and NBA — sometimes rewards more accessible literary fiction; sometimes rewards experimental work (Whitehead's *Underground Railroad*, Saunders's *Lincoln in the Bardo*).

## Process Requires Three Affirmations
1. Jury must nominate
2. Board majority must approve OR
3. 75% supermajority can override jury
This means consensus-building shapes the prize toward "high quality but reasonable to many readers" works.
