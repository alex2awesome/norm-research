# Literary Fiction vs. Genre Fiction — Distinguishing Criteria

## Literary Fiction
"Fiction that cannot be categorized by any specific genre conventions, and which seeks to describe real-life reactions to complex events using well-developed characters, themes, literary devices, and experimentations in prose."

### Hallmarks
- **Character over plot** — interiority, psychology, complexity
- **Stylistic ambition** — sentence-level craft is foreground
- **Thematic depth** — engages "the human condition"
- **Literary devices** — metaphor, symbolism, allegory, defamiliarization
- **Possibly non-conventional structure** — fragmented, multi-perspective, time-shifted
- **No guarantee of satisfying ending** — ambiguity is acceptable / often valued
- **Prose foregrounded** — language is itself a focus

## Genre Fiction
Stories that operate within conventions of a recognized category (sci-fi, fantasy, mystery, romance, thriller, horror, YA, etc.).

### Hallmarks
- **Plot over interiority** (often)
- **Conventions honored** — readers buy genre expecting genre
- **Pacing prioritized** — story momentum
- **Resolution expected** — closed endings standard (HEA in romance, solved case in mystery)
- **Worldbuilding** — extensive in fantasy/SF
- **Genre-specific tropes** — known and (sometimes) subverted

## The Spectrum (Not Binary)
- **Pure literary**: Toni Morrison, Jenny Erpenbeck, W.G. Sebald
- **Literary with genre elements**: Kazuo Ishiguro (sci-fi-tinted), George Saunders (fabulist), Colson Whitehead (alt-history)
- **High-craft genre**: Ursula Le Guin, Octavia Butler, Tana French
- **Mid-genre**: well-executed Stephen King, Lee Child, Diana Gabaldon
- **Pulp/commercial genre**: high-concept thrillers, mass-market romance

## Quality Standards Differ
- **Literary**: judged on prose, theme, originality, depth, complexity
- **Genre**: judged on convention-execution, pace, satisfaction, world-building, tropes deployed/subverted
- A "great" literary novel and a "great" thriller succeed on *different* axes

## Why It Matters for Reader Voting
- Reddit/Goodreads readers judge often by genre expectations
- A literary story posted in a genre-friendly community gets penalized
- A genre story judged by literary criteria looks "shallow"
- Quality models risk privileging one tradition's criteria over another

## "Why Literary Fiction Is a Genre" (Mythcreants)
Mythcreants and others argue "literary fiction" is itself a genre with conventions:
- MFA-trained voice
- Quiet domestic drama
- Open endings
- Workshop-epiphany structure
- Privileged class settings
- Anti-plot bias
This perspective challenges the assumption that "literary" = "objectively better."
