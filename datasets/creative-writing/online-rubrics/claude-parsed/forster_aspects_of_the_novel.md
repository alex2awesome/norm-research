# E.M. Forster — Aspects of the Novel (1927)

Lectures delivered at Trinity College, Cambridge. Identifies seven universal aspects by which novels can be evaluated.

## The Seven Aspects

### 1. Story
"A narrative of events arranged in their time-sequence." The lowest, most primitive aspect — what answers "and then? and then?" Story appeals to the reader's curiosity about what happens next.

### 2. People (Characters)
- **Flat characters**: built around a single idea or quality; can be summed up in one sentence; do not change. Useful as foils, easy to remember.
- **Round characters**: complex, capable of surprising the reader convincingly; develop and change.
A round character is one "capable of surprising in a convincing way."

### 3. Plot
Distinguished sharply from story. A plot is a narrative of events arranged with emphasis on **causality**.
- **Story**: "The king died, and then the queen died."
- **Plot**: "The king died, and then the queen died of grief."
Plot demands intelligence and memory from the reader.

### 4. Fantasy
The element of the marvellous, the supernatural, the impossible. Asks the reader to grant something extra — a willing suspension of disbelief in the impossible.

### 5. Prophecy
Not foretelling the future but a tone of voice — a song, a sense that the novelist is gesturing toward something universal beyond the literal events. Found in Dostoevsky, Melville, Brontë, Lawrence.

### 6. Pattern
The aesthetic shape of the whole novel — its design as a unified object. Patterns can be hourglass-shaped (Henry James, *The Ambassadors*) or chain-shaped. Pattern arises from plot but is more abstract.

### 7. Rhythm
Recurrent themes, motifs, phrases that create internal music — like leitmotifs in Wagner. Easy rhythms (recurrence within a book) and difficult rhythms (the novel as a whole producing a symphonic effect, as in Tolstoy's *War and Peace*).

## Evaluative Criterion

The greatest novels deploy **all** these aspects in balance, especially achieving rhythm and pattern without sacrificing the human truth of round characters.
