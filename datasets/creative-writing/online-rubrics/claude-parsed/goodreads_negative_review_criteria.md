# Goodreads — What Makes a Story Bad (Reader Criteria)

## 1-Star: "Did Not Like It"
Common reasons cited:
- DNF (did not finish)
- Offensive content
- Internally inconsistent
- Major plot holes
- Boring throughout
- Insulting to reader's intelligence
- Bad prose at the line level
- Characters all sound alike or unbelievable
- Predictable to the point of pointlessness
- Heavy-handed messaging / "preachy"
- Cultural insensitivity / harmful representation
- Plot resolved by deus ex machina
- Stupid romance / contrived couple

## 2-Star: "It Was OK"
- Not actively bad but unimpressed
- Could finish but no enthusiasm
- Lacks "personality and storyline"
- Forgettable
- Wasted time
- Wrong genre/expectation match
- Many small issues that accumulate

## 3-Star: "Liked It"
- Decent and readable
- Pleasant but not remarkable
- "Mostly enjoyed but didn't leave you with the 'wow, that was good' feeling"
- Basic characters
- Simple storylines
- Obvious endings
- Lacks personality

## 4-Star: "Really Liked It"
- Genuinely enjoyed
- Recommend with caveat
- One major flaw or genre quibble
- Will reread or seek more from author

## 5-Star: "It Was Amazing"
- Stayed with the reader
- Strong emotional impact
- Rare; some reviewers reserve for top 5%
- Will reread; will give as gift; will recommend widely

## Common Negative Trigger Categories (synthesized from review analysis)

### Craft Failures
- Telling instead of showing
- Pacing problems (slow or breakneck without rhythm)
- Inconsistent POV
- Dialogue that doesn't sound like real people
- Info-dumps
- Purple prose
- Generic descriptions

### Character Failures
- Mary Sue / Gary Stu (overpowered, perfect protagonists)
- Inconsistent motivation
- Romance based on nothing
- Misogyny or other -isms in characterization
- Insta-love
- Underdeveloped antagonists

### Plot Failures
- Predictable
- Plot holes
- Unmotivated coincidence
- Unsatisfying ending
- Cliffhanger without payoff
- Magical solution to complex problem

### Worldbuilding (Genre)
- Inconsistent rules
- Generic medieval/dystopia
- Unconvincing future
- "Magic" that solves whatever the plot needs
- Cultural appropriation handled poorly

### Marketing / Expectation Mismatch
- Cover/blurb misrepresents content
- Genre conventions not honored
- Hype that didn't deliver

## Implications for LitBench / Reader Voting
Reader judgments aren't just craft-evaluations; they're craft + expectation + identity-political alignment + mood + genre fit. Quality models trained on aggregate ratings inherit all these biases.
