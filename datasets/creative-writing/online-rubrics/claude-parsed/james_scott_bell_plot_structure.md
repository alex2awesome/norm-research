# James Scott Bell — Plot & Structure

A bestselling craft book on commercial fiction structure.

## The LOCK System
Bell's signature framework:
- **L**ead — A protagonist worth following (sympathetic, vulnerable, with a problem)
- **O**bjective — What the lead wants (clear, urgent goal)
- **C**onfrontation — Forces of opposition (must be sufficient to test the lead)
- **K**nockout — A satisfying, decisive ending that resolves the central conflict

## Three-Act Structure (Bell's Version)
- Act 1 (1/4): Establish lead + world; inciting incident; pass through "doorway of no return"
- Act 2 (1/2): Confrontation; rising obstacles; midpoint reversal; second "doorway"
- Act 3 (1/4): Climax; resolution

## Two "Doorways of No Return"
- **Doorway 1** (~20-25%): Lead crosses into the main story; can't go back
- **Doorway 2** (~75-80%): Lead commits to final action; only forward

## Key Craft Principles

### Open with Action or Conflict
Don't waste the opening on weather or backstory. Start with the lead in trouble.

### Make the Lead Sympathetic
Readers must care; without sympathy, no engagement.

### Make the Stakes Personal
Save-the-world plots without personal stakes feel hollow.

### Build Conflict Constantly
Every scene needs tension. Even "quiet" scenes have undercurrents.

### Resolve, Don't Trail Off
The knockout — Bell insists on a real, satisfying ending. No anti-climax in commercial fiction.

### Pacing through Scene/Sequel
- **Scene** = goal/conflict/setback (action)
- **Sequel** = reaction/dilemma/decision (reflection)
Alternate to keep pacing varied.

## Quality Tests
- Is the lead sympathetic?
- Is the objective clear and urgent?
- Are the forces of opposition strong enough?
- Does the knockout deliver?
- Are scenes structured (goal/conflict/setback)?
- Are sequels structured (reaction/dilemma/decision)?
- Have we crossed both doorways of no return?

## Bell's Genre Lean
Commercial fiction (especially thriller). Less applicable to literary fiction's ambiguity-prizing tradition. But the LOCK system + doorways apply broadly.

## Bell's Writing Habits Advice
- 350 words/day = a novel a year
- "Write hot" then "edit cold"
- Read in your genre + outside it
