# Flash Fiction — Quality Criteria

## Word Count Conventions
- **Twitterature / six-word stories** — 6 words (Hemingway: "For sale: baby shoes, never worn.")
- **Drabble / microfiction** — exactly 100 words
- **Dribble / minisaga** — 50 words
- **Sudden fiction** — up to 750 words
- **Flash fiction** — up to 1,000–1,500 words depending on market
- **Short short story** — up to 2,000 words

## Quality Criteria
1. **Completeness** — must be a complete story, not a fragment. Beginning, middle, end (however compressed).
2. **Word economy** — every word load-bearing; no warm-up
3. **Strong opening** — orient the reader in one sentence
4. **Strong ending** — the punch lands; perfect openers + weak endings fail
5. **Implied larger story** — suggests a world beyond the page
6. **Single scene / moment focus** — usually no subplot, single character arc
7. **Resonance** — the piece lingers despite brevity
8. **Title as third leg** — title carries 1/3 of meaning in flash
9. **Surprise + inevitability** — like a poem's turn
10. **Sound and rhythm** — closer to lyric than to extended narrative

## Forms of Flash
- The list essay/story
- The single-image story
- The two-character vignette
- The hermit-crab story (borrowed form: recipe, instruction manual, obituary)
- The braided micro
- The single-sentence story
- The found-object story
- The catalogue story
- The dictionary entry / Wikipedia parody

## Quality Tests
- Can you remove any word?
- Does the title contribute meaning?
- Does the ending recontextualize the opening?
- Does the reader want to re-read?
- Does it suggest more than it shows?
