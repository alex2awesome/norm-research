# Clarion Workshop — SF/F Workshop Method and Implicit Craft Criteria

Six-week intensive science fiction and fantasy writers' workshop, founded 1968 by Robin Scott Wilson at Clarion State College, modeled on Damon Knight's Milford Workshop. Clarion West (Seattle), Clarion South (defunct), and the original Clarion (now at UCSD) all derive. Has trained much of the SF/F field for 60 years.

## The Six-Week Format

- 6 different instructors (one per week), each a published SF/F professional
- 17 students; 4–6 stories produced per student
- Daily critique sessions covering all student stories
- Author silence during critique (Milford Method) followed by reply

## Critique Dimensions (Clarion Tradition)

The implicit rubric used by Clarion instructors and peers:

### Concept / Idea
- Is the central science-fictional or fantasy idea **fresh**?
- Is it productively complicated, or merely thrown in?
- Has it been done before, and if so, what is added?

### Worldbuilding
- Is the imagined world **internally consistent**?
- Are the rules clear (or productively ambiguous)?
- Does the world's strangeness emerge through detail, not exposition?
- Lore-dumping is the universal critique.

### Plot
- Does the story have a **shape**?
- Is the conflict productive?
- Does the protagonist drive events or merely observe?
- Is the ending earned?

### Character
- Are characters distinct in voice, body, motivation?
- Are protagonists active?
- Do secondary characters serve the story without being flat?
- Does the protagonist change?

### Prose
- Sentence-level competence
- Voice consistency
- POV discipline (no head-hopping)
- Show vs. tell judgment
- Cliché elimination

### Pacing
- Does the story hold attention?
- Are there sagging middles?
- Is the climax timed appropriately?

### Genre Engagement
- Does the story know its genre conventions?
- Does it deploy or knowingly violate them?
- Is it accessible to genre readers without being pandering?

## Recurring Critiques (the "Clarion Trinity" of common faults)

1. **The infodump** — backstory, worldbuilding, or tech explained in chunks
2. **The passive protagonist** — events happen TO the character rather than from their choices
3. **The unearned ending** — climax not set up by what came before; deus ex machina; "and then they woke up"

## Recent Critiques and Reforms

Clarion West (2020–2022 review) identified that the traditional Milford-style workshop:
- Privileges Western/Anglophone craft norms
- Is hostile to writers from underrepresented communities
- Performs critique-confidence as ritual
- Silences cultural framing the work needs

Reforms include:
- Author-centered dialogue (not pure silence)
- Cultural-context briefings
- Critique of work on its own terms, not against external genre norms
- Time for the writer to set their own evaluation criteria

## Implication as Rubric

The Clarion implicit rubric for SF/F short fiction:

1. **Idea freshness**
2. **World internal consistency**
3. **Plot shape and earned ending**
4. **Character distinctness and agency**
5. **Sentence-level prose**
6. **Voice and POV discipline**
7. **Pacing**
8. **Genre engagement** (knowing, not naïve)
9. **Absence of infodumps, passivity, unearned endings**

A story passing all nine is workshop-strong; one failing several gets the harshest critiques.
