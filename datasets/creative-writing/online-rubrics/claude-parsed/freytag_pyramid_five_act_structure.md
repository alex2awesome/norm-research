# Freytag's Pyramid — Five-Act Dramatic Structure

Gustav Freytag (*Die Technik des Dramas*, 1863). The classic shape of dramatic narrative — and the implicit standard against which most short stories' structural quality is judged.

## The Five Acts

### Act 1: Exposition
- Establishes setting (time/place/atmosphere)
- Introduces main characters
- Inciting incident ("exciting force") suggests the primary conflict

### Act 2: Rising Action
- Conflict escalates as the protagonist pursues a goal
- Series of complications, each raising stakes
- Tension increases

### Act 3: Climax
- The story's turning point (highest point of the pyramid)
- Protagonist's fate is sealed (success in comedy, downfall in tragedy)
- Single moment of greatest intensity

### Act 4: Falling Action
- Consequences of the climax play out
- "Final suspense" — possibility of an unexpected outcome
- Movement toward resolution

### Act 5: Resolution / Denouement / Catastrophe
- Loose ends tied
- New equilibrium (happy in comedy; "catastrophe" — often deaths — in tragedy)

## Quality Criteria Implied by the Pyramid
- Each act has clear function — no narrative drag
- Rising action genuinely *rises* (escalation, not repetition)
- Climax is *earned* by everything before it
- Denouement isn't truncated or over-extended
- Inciting incident appears early enough to grab the reader
- Falling action maintains tension despite movement toward conclusion

## Variants
- **Three-Act**: Setup / Confrontation / Resolution
- **Four-Act**: Setup / Conflict / Crisis / Resolution
- **Seven-Point Story Structure** (Dan Wells): Hook / Plot Turn 1 / Pinch 1 / Midpoint / Pinch 2 / Plot Turn 2 / Resolution
- **Fichtean Curve**: Multiple rising-action mini-crises before the climax (no exposition; in medias res)
