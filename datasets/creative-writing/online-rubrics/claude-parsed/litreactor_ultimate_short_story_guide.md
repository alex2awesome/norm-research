# LitReactor — Ultimate Guide to Writing Short Stories

## Definition & Fundamentals
1,000–7,000 words; 2–3 characters; "a single powerful message or theme." Every element must work harder than in longer formats.

## Key Elements

**Characters:** 2–3 maximum. "Characterization should be almost entirely implied." A single detail (e.g., $5 tip on a $10 ride) can convey generosity, privilege, and naïveté simultaneously.

**Conflict:** One primary conflict, often internal with external manifestations. Avoid subplots.

**Theme:** Explore an aspect of human experience thoughtfully. "You don't have to provide a definitive moral ruling — in fact, it's usually better to keep things ambivalent."

## Generating Ideas
- Use writing prompts filtered by genre.
- Consider POV and structure carefully — they dramatically affect impact.
- Popular prompts: running away, obsession, yearning, facing fears, dialogue-only narratives.

## Structure & Writing Process

**Opening:** Start "as close to the end as possible." Use *in medias res*, intriguing questions, or vivid imagery to hook immediately.

**Development:** Alternate between significant events and exposition; gradually increase pacing toward climax.

**Ending:** "Find the natural conclusion"; end with a standout line; "drop the mic and get out" — avoid falling action.

## Editing
1. Wait days or weeks before revising.
2. Examine each scene's purpose; eliminate redundancy.
3. Polish sentence-level prose for variety and impact.
4. Proofread thoroughly.
