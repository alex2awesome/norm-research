# Susan Sontag — "Against Interpretation" (1966)

Critical essay arguing that the dominant interpretive tradition reduces art and that we need an "erotics of art" instead of a hermeneutics.

## Central Thesis

The contemporary critical impulse to ask "what does it mean?" — to translate art into content, message, meaning — is a flight from the actual sensory presence of the work. "In place of a hermeneutics we need an erotics of art."

## Form vs Content

The artificial separation of form and content has impoverished criticism. Form **is** content. Style and surface carry the work's meaning; one cannot peel them away to reach a "real meaning" beneath.

## Transparence

Sontag's positive criterion: "Transparence is the highest, most liberating value in art and in criticism today. Transparence means experiencing the luminousness of the thing in itself, of things being what they are."

A successful work allows itself to be seen, not decoded.

## Against Allegory

Modern criticism's tendency to treat every work as an allegory for something else (Freud, Marx, biography, history) is reductive. The work is not a clue to a hidden truth; it is the truth, on its surface.

## Sensory Engagement

Aesthetic experience is bodily, immediate, erotic. The mistake of the interpretive reader is to insulate themselves from the actual sensory power of the work via abstraction.

## "Notes on Camp" (companion essay)

Camp as a sensibility privileges artifice, exaggeration, surface over depth — a corrective to high-modernist seriousness. Validates aesthetic responses outside the canonical.

## Evaluative Criteria

A work of art succeeds when:
- It resists easy interpretation
- It maintains sensory intensity
- It is transparent — the thing itself, not a coded message
- It privileges form (which is content)
- It permits an erotic, not merely intellectual, response

## Implication for Fiction

A short story should be evaluated for what it *does* on the page — its sentences, images, rhythms — not for what it "means." Fiction reduced to message is fiction destroyed.
