# Sentence-Level Craft — Line Editing Principles

## What Line Editing Targets
Line editing polishes:
- Word choice
- Rhythm
- Tone
- Interiority
- Transitions
- Show vs. tell
- Concision
- Diction precision

A line editor reads for *flow, clarity, and impact* — marking where prose stumbles, energy flags, or word choice muddles meaning.

## Sentence Rhythm Principles

### Vary Sentence Length
- The #1 rhythm rule
- Short sentences hit hard.
- Longer, flowing sentences carry the reader forward, accumulating clauses and momentum.
- Average 10–20 words for "workhorse" sentences
- Genre signals: thriller writers shorter; literary writers longer

### Short, Short, Long
A reliable rhythm pattern. The short sentences hit; the long sentence carries.

### Sentence Fragments
Used purposefully. Create:
- Emphasis
- Speed
- Intimacy
- Interrupted thought
(Used too often, they become a tic.)

### Punctuation as Rhythm
Just as composers use rests and dynamics, writers use commas, semicolons, dashes, ellipses, and periods to control pace.

### Sound Patterning
- Alliteration sparingly
- Internal rhyme (often unconsciously)
- Consonance / assonance
- Avoid unintentional rhymes that distract

### Read Aloud Test
If it stumbles or feels monotonous when read aloud, fix it.

### Match Rhythm to Content
- Action: short, punchy
- Reflection: longer, more complex
- Tension: clipped
- Lyric description: musical, flowing

## Quality Indicators (Sentence Level)
- No two consecutive sentences have the same length/structure
- Word choice precise; no easier word would do
- Active verbs predominate
- No filler ("there is", "it is")
- Modifiers used sparingly
- Sound and meaning align
- The page has a *texture* you can hear

## Common Failure Modes
- All sentences average 12 words (monotony)
- Reliance on compound-complex sentences (sludge)
- Action scenes in long, qualified sentences (drains energy)
- Reflective passages in choppy short sentences (no breath)
- Repetitive openings ("She walked. She turned. She looked.")
- Adverb clusters
- "To be" verb chains
