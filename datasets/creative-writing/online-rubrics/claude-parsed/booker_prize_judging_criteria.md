# The Booker Prize — Judging Criteria

## Stated Criterion
"The best sustained work of fiction written in English by an author of any nationality, and published in the UK and/or Ireland."

## What Judges Look For (synthesized from chair statements over years)
- **Literary merit** — craft and ambition above all
- **Sustained quality** — not just brilliant scenes but a whole book that holds
- **Originality of vision** — fresh perspective, voice, form
- **Distinctive prose** — recognizable, controlled, alive sentences
- **Resonance** — lingers; rewards re-reading
- **Engagement with the present moment** — many years' winners speak to contemporary anxieties
- **Formal innovation** — Booker often rewards experimental structure (e.g., *Lincoln in the Bardo*, *Milkman*)
- **Moral seriousness** — willing to take on difficult subjects with care

## Process
- 5 judges read 150+ submitted novels
- Longlist (~13) → Shortlist (6) → Winner
- Judges change yearly — chair sets tone

## Critique (Hornby)
"If you have a short-list of six Booker Prize books, people read the one that wins…"; the Booker can narrow what counts as literary culture.

## International Booker Prize
Same structure for translated fiction; judges fiction in English translation, prize split between author and translator.
