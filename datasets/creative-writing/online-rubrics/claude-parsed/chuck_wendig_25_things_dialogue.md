# 25 Things You Should Know About Dialogue

1. **Dialogue Is Easy Like Sunday Morning** — Readers flow through dialogue quickly.
2. **Easy Isn't The Same As Uncomplicated** — Dialogue conveys complex narrative elements.
3. **Sweet Minimalism** — Rely on "said" and "asked"; avoid excessive adverbs.
4. **Uh, You Do Know The Rules, Right?** — Master proper formatting and punctuation.
5. **Use It To Set Pace** — Dialogue accelerates reading speed.
6. **Shape Determines Speed** — Short, sharp dialogue is fast; long exchanges slow tempo.
7. **Expository Dialogue Is A Pair Of Cement Shoes** — Avoid info dumps.
8. **Showing Through Telling** — Dialogue conveys info while preserving "show, don't tell."
9. **The Wart On The End Of The Nose** — Characters shouldn't state exact feelings.
10. **The Words Beneath The Words** — Subtext matters more than literal meaning.
11. **Pay No Attention To The Dead Man Behind The Curtain** — Dialogue conceals as much as it reveals.
12. **Where Tension, Suspense And Mystery Have A Big Crazy Gang-Bang** — Dialogue generates suspense.
13. **Quid Pro Quo, Clarice** — Voice and personality emerge through how characters speak.
14. **Let The Character Sign Their Own Work** — Speech patterns reveal distinct identity.
15. **Dialogue Is A Theme Park** — Characters can orbit thematic elements through conversation.
16. **Dialogue Is Action** — Speaking constitutes action; characters move while talking.
17. **The Real World Is Not Your Friend** — Real speech ("um", "like") doesn't translate.
18. **For The Record, You're Not David Mamet** — Don't mimic overlapping dialogue without skill.
19. **Again: Not A Ping-Pong Match** — Characters interrupt and pursue agendas.
20. **Conversation Is Conflict** — Effective dialogue from conflicting goals.
21. **Authenticity Trumps Reality** — Dialogue should feel genuine, not literally accurate.
22. **Sometimes, You Just Gotta Babble That Shit Out** — Allow rambling first; refine in revision.
23. **Nothing Wrong With Banter** — Casual interaction reveals personality.
24. **The Greatest Crime Against Humanity Is Writing Boring Dialogue** — Maintain sharp, engaging exchanges.
25. **Double-Duty Dialogueing** — Dialogue develops character, theme, plot, tension simultaneously.
