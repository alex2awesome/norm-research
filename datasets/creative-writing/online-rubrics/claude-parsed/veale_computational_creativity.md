# Tony Veale — Computational Creativity: The Philosophy and Engineering of Autonomously Creative Systems (2017)

Veale (UCD) wrote (with collaborators) the canonical text for computational creativity. Provides systematic dimensions for evaluating creative output, including narrative.

## Definition Inherited

Computational creativity is "the capacity of machines to both generate and evaluate novel outputs that would, if produced by a human, be considered creative."

Generation **and** evaluation — both are necessary.

## Two Pillars from Guilford

Veale draws on J.P. Guilford's theory of divergent production:
- **Fluency** — the ability to generate many different ideas
- **Flexibility** — the ability to generate different **kinds** of ideas

A creative system (or writer) is judged on both axes. Fluent but inflexible = many similar outputs. Flexible but infrequent = few outputs across diverse kinds.

## Two Necessary Conditions for Creativity

1. **Novelty** — output deviates from norm
2. **Value** — output has utility/quality for an audience

A novel output that lacks value is junk. A valuable output that lacks novelty is template.

## Topics in Veale's Framework

### Autonomous Intentionality
A creative system must have its own goals and pursue them — not merely execute external instructions. Applied to fiction: characters with intentionality (Riedl) AND systems/writers with intentionality (Veale).

### Conceptual Blending
Following Fauconnier and Turner: creativity arises from blending elements of two unrelated mental spaces into a third novel space. A creative metaphor blends source and target. A creative story blends genres, plot devices, character types.

### Literature Mining
Computational creativity systems mine large textual corpora for patterns, tropes, conventions — to know what is conventional and therefore what would be novel.

### Models of Novelty
Quantitative: similarity-based, statistical, information-theoretic measures.
Qualitative: surprise, defamiliarization, originality.

### Evaluation
The hardest problem. Multiple proposed methods:
- Human ratings (gold standard but expensive)
- Comparison to a corpus (relative novelty)
- System self-evaluation (the system judges its own outputs against criteria)
- Hybrid (system filters; human evaluates final candidates)

### Common-Sense Knowledge
Creative outputs must satisfy common-sense norms unless deliberately violating them. A creative metaphor is recognizable as a metaphor.

### Social Creativity
Creativity is not solitary. Influence, dialogue, co-creation produce more interesting outputs than isolated systems.

## Narrative Jokes — A Diagnostic Case

Veale's analysis of jokes: a narrative joke contains an unexpected "kink" or discontinuity — a surprising gap in the logic that the punchline retroactively repairs (or does not repair, in absurdist humor).

This kink/repair structure generalizes: many short stories operate on a smaller, subtler version of the same dynamic. The reader expects X; the story delivers Y; the surprise is recognized as meaningful in retrospect.

## Implications for Story Evaluation

A story can be assessed on:
- **Fluency** of the writer's output (do they generate many different stories?)
- **Flexibility** (different kinds of stories, not many of one kind)
- **Novelty** of conceptual blends (what spaces are blended?)
- **Value** delivered to the reader
- **Surprise + repair structure** (does the story produce a productive kink?)
- **Autonomous intentionality** (does the work seem written from inner necessity, not from a template?)
- **Common-sense fit** (does the work earn its violations?)

## Scéalability

Veale's narrative system; demonstrates that plotting, characterization, and rendering can be modularly engineered. The system's design constraints reveal axes of evaluation: any story can be analyzed along the same modular dimensions.
