# Writers.com Short Story Writing Guide

## Core Elements (Foundation)
- Protagonist with a clear desire or need
- Well-defined dilemma
- Decision made by protagonist
- Climactic moment where tension peaks
- Outcome showing protagonist transformation

## 6-Step Process
1. **Generate an Idea** — Originality presents itself in storytelling, not in ideas.
2. **Outline OR Develop Characters** — Background, motivations, fatal flaw; small cast.
3. **Write Scenes Around Conflict** — Every scene relates to central struggle.
4. **Complete First Draft** — Accept imperfection; build tension toward climax.
5. **Revise with Fresh Eyes** — Step away; ensure each word serves the story.
6. **Compare Against Checklist** — Use criteria for final assessment.

## Detailed Craft Checklist

### Length & Setting
- 1,000–7,500 words optimal.
- Single time period; gaps only for travel/sleep.
- Each scene = one location or continuous transit.
- Space breaks between time jumps.

### POV
- One POV character (except some Romance structures).
- Never head-hop.
- Use "apparently," "obviously," "supposedly" for other characters' thoughts.
- Consistent throughout regarding internal access.

### Protagonist & Antagonist
- One clear protagonist who typically transforms.
- Defined antagonist creating obstacles.
- Protagonist begins trapped in a flaw.
- "Want" vs "Need" distinction: Want often contradicts best interests; Need is authentic benefit.

### Motivation & Goals
- Each scene shows main character's goal.
- Scenes have "charge" — progress or regression.
- Most scenes end negatively until climax.
- Inciting Incident triggers protagonist's story goal.
- Crisis forces character to change approach.

### Character Development
- Characters speak distinctly.
- Dialogue conveys subtext.
- Characters feel real, not illustrative of author's beliefs.
- Flat supporting characters acceptable.

### Prose Style
- Language: succinct, fresh, exciting, specific, colorful.
- Avoid clichés.
- Vary sentence structures.
- Genre: simple language, short sentences, direct symbolism.
- Literary: sophisticated vocabulary, metaphor, implied motifs allowed.

### Story Structure
- Three Act or Freytag's Pyramid.
- Genre: tie up threads.
- Literary: ambiguity allowed.
- Experimental forms okay if internal logic is clear.

### Reader Engagement
- Hook at beginning: mystery, voice, vivid location, humor.
- Minimal exposition.
- Avoid distancing words ("used to," "would often").
- Backstory earned only after reader wants answers.
- High stakes.
- "Every sentence is compelling enough to urge the reader to read the next one."

## Key Principle
Short stories require economy — no subplots. Each element serves the central conflict.
