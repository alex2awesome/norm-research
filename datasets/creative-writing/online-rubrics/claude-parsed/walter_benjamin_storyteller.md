# Walter Benjamin — "The Storyteller: Reflections on the Works of Nikolai Leskov" (1936)

Foundational essay distinguishing the storyteller from the novelist and the journalist.

## Storytelling vs. Information

Information "lays claim to prompt verifiability" and is bound to its moment. The story, by contrast, "preserves and concentrates its strength and is capable of releasing it even after a long time." Information dies when it is consumed; a story is renewed in retelling.

## Stories Carry Counsel (Rat)

A true story always carries something useful — "either a moral, a practical tip, a proverb, or a maxim." This counsel is **not extracted as a lesson** but woven into the fabric of the tale. Storytelling preserves wisdom; information transmits facts.

## The Storyteller as Craftsman

Two archetypal storytellers:
- **The settled craftsman** (deep knowledge of one place)
- **The traveling journeyman** (knowledge of distant lands)

The greatest storytellers fuse both. The story is a craft object, shaped slowly, polished by retelling. "The storyteller takes what he tells from experience — his own or that reported by others. And he in turn makes it the experience of those who are listening to his tale."

## Boredom and Listening

"Boredom is the dream bird that hatches the egg of experience." A culture without spaces of receptive boredom (rural labor, traveling, weaving) loses the conditions for storytelling. Modern distraction kills story.

## The Half-Art of Restraint

"Half the art of storytelling is to keep a story free from explanation as one reproduces it." Leave gaps. Trust the listener. Explanation kills the story. The listener should be free to interpret things their own way.

## Death as the Source of Authority

"It is, however, characteristic that not only a man's knowledge or wisdom, but above all his real life — and this is the stuff that stories are made of — first assumes transmissible form at the moment of his death." The dying impart authority; this is why traditional stories often pass through deathbed scenes.

## The Unfinished Quality

A story remains open — "free of explanation," available for continuation. The novel concludes definitively; the story invites retelling.

## Chronicle vs. History

The chronicler narrates events without distinguishing major from minor — every event is part of God's plan. The historian explains. The chronicler is closer to the storyteller than the historian is.

## Evaluative Criteria

A piece of fiction succeeds as story (in Benjamin's sense) when it:
- Carries counsel without preaching it
- Refuses unnecessary explanation
- Is shaped for retelling
- Preserves transmissible experience
- Stays open at its end
- Speaks from the authority of the lived (or the dying)
