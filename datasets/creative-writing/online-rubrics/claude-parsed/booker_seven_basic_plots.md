# Christopher Booker — The Seven Basic Plots

## The Seven Plots
1. **Overcoming the Monster** — Hero confronts a great evil that threatens the community (Beowulf, Star Wars, Jaws)
2. **Rags to Riches** — Mean/obscure protagonist rises to greatness (Cinderella, David Copperfield, Aladdin)
3. **The Quest** — Hero travels to acquire an object/place against obstacles (Odyssey, Lord of the Rings, Raiders)
4. **Voyage and Return** — Hero visits a strange world, returns home with experience (Alice, Robinson Crusoe, Wizard of Oz)
5. **Comedy** — Confusion, light tone, resolution in marriages/reconciliation (Midsummer Night's Dream, Bridget Jones)
6. **Tragedy** — Flawed hero whose downfall results from character flaw (Macbeth, Anna Karenina)
7. **Rebirth** — Hero falls under shadow then is redeemed (Scrooge, Sleeping Beauty, Beauty and the Beast)

## Two Additional (later)
- **Rebellion** — Against oppressive force (1984)
- **Mystery** — Discovery of a hidden truth

## The "Meta-Plot" (Stages within each)
1. **Anticipation Stage** — Hero called to adventure
2. **Dream Stage** — Initial success; illusion of invincibility
3. **Frustration Stage** — Obstacles mount; reversal
4. **Nightmare Stage** — Apparent defeat
5. **Resolution / Miraculous Escape** — Hero overcomes through transformation

## Quality Criteria Implied
- **Recognizable plot pattern** — even subverted, the underlying shape is felt
- **Character matched to plot type** — protagonist's qualities suit the story shape
- **Catharsis appropriate to plot** — comedy delivers reconciliation, tragedy delivers grief
- **Moral coherence** — Booker argues stories work when they affirm a moral order

## Critique
Many scholars find Booker too rigid; not every story fits cleanly. But the framework remains influential as a *reader expectation* model — readers recognize and respond to these archetypal shapes.
