# BBC National Short Story Award

UK's most prestigious single-story prize. Open to British nationals/UK residents (18+); stories up to 8,000 words.

## Judging Criteria

Judges weigh three primary dimensions:

### 1. Originality
A genuine claim to newness — fresh subject, fresh approach, or fresh language. Distinguishes the prize-winner from competent execution.

### 2. Emotional Resonance
The story must move the reader. Affective power is decisive in the compressed form. Judge Di Speirs has cited "compassion and understanding" as the binding quality of selected stories.

### 3. Technical Craft
Mastery of the short form's particular demands — economy, suggestion, structural unity, last-line landing.

## Balancing Innovation and Accessibility

Judges explicitly balance **innovation** (formal risk, originality of voice) against **accessibility** (the story must reach a serious reading audience, not only specialist readers). A story that is innovative but unreadable does not win; a story that is accessible but conventional does not win.

## Recurring Thematic Notes from Judges

Successful shortlisted stories in recent years have engaged:
- The collision of cultures followed by new comprehension
- The need to adapt and make the most of life whatever the difficulties
- Compassion across difference

These are not selection criteria per se but indicate the judges' affinity for stories with moral and cultural weight.

## Procedural

- Blind submissions (mitigates bias)
- Five-judge panel, rotated annually
- Shortlist of five; winner announced at BBC Radio 4 broadcast
- Prize: £15,000

## Implication for Short-Story Rubric

A story can be evaluated on:
- Genuine originality (not novelty for its own sake)
- Emotional resonance (does it move the reader?)
- Technical mastery of the short form
- Balance of innovation and accessibility
- Engagement with serious moral/cultural matter
