# Story Endings — Types & Quality Criteria

## Major Ending Types

### 1. Resolution / Closed Ending
Conflict resolved; loose ends tied; protagonist's arc complete. Most common in commercial fiction.

### 2. Twist Ending
A revelation reframes earlier events. Quality requires foreshadowing + earned surprise. Risks: cheating, unmotivated, telegraph-able.

### 3. Open / Ambiguous Ending
Key questions left for the reader. Aligns with story's tone and theme. Common in literary fiction.

### 4. Cliffhanger Ending
Suspends mid-action. Needs sequel/payoff to feel honest. Risky in standalone short story (often perceived as cheating).

### 5. Full-Circle / Frame Ending
Returns to opening (image, location, idea) but transformed by what happened. Save the Cat's "Final Image."

### 6. Epilogue / Time-Skip Ending
Shows the future after main events. Useful for addressing "what happened to…" but can feel tacked on.

### 7. Dying-Fall / Diminuendo Ending
Quiet, deliberately anticlimactic; tone of acceptance/melancholy (Chekhov, Carver). Common in literary stories.

### 8. Tragic Ending
Protagonist fails, dies, suffers. Aristotelian. Catharsis.

### 9. Iron / Twist of Knife Ending
Cruel revelation that recontextualizes everything. (Du Maurier "Don't Look Now.")

### 10. Refused Ending / Anti-Climax
Story explicitly denies expected resolution. Often experimental.

## Quality Criteria for Endings

### Earned, Not Arbitrary
The ending must follow from setup. Reader should feel "of course."

### Surprising AND Inevitable
The ideal: surprise in the moment, inevitability in retrospect.

### Resonant
Lingers; rewards reflection; invites re-reading.

### Tonally Coherent
Matches the story's overall mood — a happy ending in a bleak story feels false.

### Right Length
Not rushed (climax cut short); not over-extended (denouement drags).

### Doesn't Explain
Lets the reader feel meaning rather than have it stated.

### Recontextualizes the Beginning
Best endings change how the opening reads.

### Specific, Not Generic
A unique image, line, or moment — not "and they all lived happily ever after."

## Common Failure Modes
- Deus ex machina (rescue from outside the story's logic)
- Author's-out endings (character wakes up, it was a dream)
- Unearned reconciliation
- Information-dump explaining what happened
- Stop without resolution (different from open ending; this is laziness)
- "Then everyone died" cynical defaults
- Sequel-bait that prevents this story from working alone

## Notable Quality Tests
- Does the ending feel chosen, not stumbled into?
- Could this story have ended differently and lost something essential?
- Does the last sentence resonate?
- Would the reader want to re-read knowing the ending?
