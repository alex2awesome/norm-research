# Stephen Koch — The Modern Library Writer's Workshop

Koch chaired Columbia's MFA program for 21 years; this is his distilled craft guide.

## Chapters / Stages of the Process
1. **Beginnings** — How to start; what to capture in early drafts
2. **The Writing Life** — Daily practice, notebooks, reading habits
3. **Shaping the Story** — Plot, structure, dramatic question
4. **Making Characters Live** — Interior + exterior; desire + obstacle
5. **Inventing Your Style** — Voice; sentence rhythm; diction
6. **The Story of the Self** — Memoir, autobiographical fiction, distance
7. **Working and Reworking** — Revision as the real work
8. **Finishing** — When and how to stop; recognizing completion

## Core Craft Principles
- **Notebook discipline** — capture everywhere, all the time
- **Fast first drafts** — get the bones down; revise later
- **Plot serves story, not vice versa** — story is what you discover; plot is the structure that delivers it
- **Read widely and deeply** — esp. masters of your form
- **Trust the process of revision** — early drafts are exploration; final form emerges through cutting
- **Style emerges, doesn't get imposed**

## Writers Quoted
Edith Wharton, Trollope, García Márquez, Stephen King, Tolstoy, Chekhov, Henry James, Faulkner, Welty, etc.

## Implicit Quality Criteria
- Story has a discoverable spine
- Characters have inner lives that exceed plot function
- Style is consistent and unforced
- Revision visible in tightness and resonance
- Ending feels arrived at, not stopped
