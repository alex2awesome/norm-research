# Kishōtenketsu (起承転結)

Four-act narrative structure originating in Classical Chinese poetry, adopted by Japanese and Korean storytelling traditions. Distinguished from Western three-act structure by **lack of conflict-centeredness**.

## The Four Acts

### 1. Ki (起 — Introduction)
Establishes characters, setting, era, and context. Introduces the world without conflict. Translates as "rouse" or "wake up."

### 2. Shō (承 — Development)
Expands and develops what was introduced. No major changes; deepens situation and character. Sets up what the twist will pivot against.

### 3. Ten (転 — Twist / Turn)
The crux. An unexpected development reframes everything. Often introduces an apparently unrelated element that recontextualizes the prior acts. **The structure is deliberately asymmetric** — major story content is held back until the third of four acts.

### 4. Ketsu (結 — Conclusion)
Resolution; brings the disparate elements together to produce meaning. Translates as "result, consequence, outcome."

## Distinguishing Features vs. Western Structure

- **No requirement of conflict** as the engine of plot
- **Asymmetric placement** of the turning point (3 of 4 acts, not 2 of 3)
- **Juxtaposition over causation** — the twist often shifts the meaning of prior acts rather than escalating them
- Allows for **slice-of-life and contemplative narratives** that would seem "plotless" by Western criteria

## Evaluation Criteria

- Whether each act fulfills its structural role
- Whether the *ten* genuinely reframes (not merely escalates)
- Whether the *ketsu* synthesizes rather than resolves a conflict
- Coherence between juxtaposed elements
