# Turning Off the TV in Your Mind — Lincoln Michel (Counter Craft)

## Core Problem
Contemporary fiction often exhibits "TV brain" — authors mentally visualizing scenes like film and translating that directly to prose, abandoning the unique strengths of written narrative.

## Primary Distinctions Between Mediums

**Prose advantages:**
- Direct access to character interiority and consciousness
- Complete control over temporal manipulation (compress centuries or expand seconds)
- Subjective filtering through POV rather than objective camera observation

**Visual media advantages:**
- Human faces and bodies are inherently compelling
- Processes visual information instantaneously
- Naturally conveys small physical movements

## Specific Revision Techniques

1. **Eliminate unnecessary movement description.** Skip banal actions unless they serve characterization or theme. "She got a glass of water" suffices; you needn't catalog each gesture.
2. **Develop robust interiority.** Prose's prime advantage. Move beyond dialogue and action that merely repeats itself — explore genuine internal conflict and complexity.
3. **Recalibrate POV as subjective.** Details should reflect what characters would realistically notice and be colored by their perspective, not present as neutral camera documentation.
4. **Embrace temporal flexibility.** Use summarization, compression, and expansion strategically rather than defaulting to real-time scene playing.

## Key Principle
Lean into what your medium does best. Prose is not film; do not write fiction as if describing a video playing in your mind.
