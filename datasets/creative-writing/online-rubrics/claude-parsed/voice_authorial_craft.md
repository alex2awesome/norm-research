# Voice — Authorial, Narrator, Character

## Three Levels of Voice
1. **Author voice** — The writer's recognizable signature across works (Hemingway, Atwood, Saunders)
2. **Narrator voice** — The voice telling this particular story (may differ from author)
3. **Character voice** — How specific characters speak/think in dialogue and (sometimes) interior monologue

## What Constitutes Voice
- **Vocabulary** — word choice, register, repeated words
- **Syntax** — sentence length, structure, rhythm
- **Tone** — attitude toward subject (ironic, earnest, melancholic)
- **Point of view stance** — distance, sympathy, judgment
- **Worldview / sensibility** — what the writer finds important, funny, sad
- **Imagery patterns** — recurring metaphors, sensory preferences
- **Pacing** — how fast or slow the prose moves
- **Use of silence** — what's left unsaid

## Quality Criteria for Voice
- **Distinctive** — recognizable on the page
- **Consistent** — sustained across the work
- **Appropriate** — fits subject and form
- **Earned** — not affected or imitative
- **Audible** — the prose has a sound you could read aloud
- **Personal** — emerges from real engagement with the subject

## How Voice Develops
- Read widely and notice what resonates
- Write a lot — voice emerges through volume
- Trust your obsessions — what you actually care about appears
- Break rules consciously — knowing when to deviate
- Avoid pastiche — imitation is a phase, not a goal
- Embrace specificity — generic voice = no voice

## Anti-Patterns
- Workshop-bland voice (no recognizable character)
- Imitation of one admired writer (pastiche)
- Voice over-tuned to genre conventions
- Voice that fights the subject matter
- Voice inconsistent within a single work
- Voice that draws attention to itself instead of the story

## Saunders on Voice
"Voice is a particular style of speech, a way of putting things, a set of phrasings, tics, and cadences that suggest a particular mindset behind it." Voice and worldview are inseparable.
