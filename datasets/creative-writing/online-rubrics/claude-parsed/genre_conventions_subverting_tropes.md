# Genre Conventions & Subverting Tropes

## What Tropes Are
Recurring motifs, themes, conventional devices used in storytelling. Building blocks readers recognize and (often) crave.

## Why Tropes Matter
- Reader expectations
- Marketing/discoverability
- Inherited craft wisdom
- Shared cultural vocabulary
- Quality benchmark within a genre

## Genre-Specific Conventions

### Mystery/Detective
- Discovery of crime/body at start
- Investigation (evidence, suspects, witnesses)
- Red herrings
- Solution at the end
- Fair-play clueing (golden-age) or psychological focus (modern)
- The detective figure (amateur, professional, unconventional)

### Thriller
- Two storylines: "Stop!" (prevent catastrophe) or "Run!" (flee)
- Time pressure
- Escalating stakes
- Antagonist threat present from early
- Hero with specific skill set
- Action sequences

### Sci-Fi
- Speculative premise extrapolated rigorously
- "What if" question driving the plot
- Sense of wonder
- Ideas as content
- Worldbuilding (technology, society, ecology)

### Fantasy
- Hero's Journey common
- Magic system with internal rules
- Worldbuilding (history, geography, peoples)
- Quest narrative often
- Good/evil moral architecture (often inverted in modern work)

### Romance (RWA conventions)
- Central love story
- HEA or HFN ending
- Meet-cute / antagonism / dark moment / declaration
- Heat level appropriate to subgenre
- Two protagonists with mutual transformation

### Horror
- Dread building
- The threat (monster, ghost, idea, illness)
- Vulnerable protagonist
- Moments of sudden intensity
- Often ambiguous resolution

### YA
- Teen protagonist + voice
- Coming-of-age themes
- Internal + external integrated
- Hopeful ending

## Quality Criteria for Genre Work
1. **Knows its conventions** — reader trust earned by competent execution
2. **Honors what readers come for** — never breaks the contract
3. **Subverts thoughtfully** — when subverting, knows what's being subverted and why
4. **Moves beyond cliché** — uses convention as scaffold, not crutch
5. **Specificity within convention** — fresh rendering of familiar pattern

## Subverting Tropes Successfully
- Be intimately familiar with the trope first
- Subversions: gender-swap, role-reversal, expectation-flip
- "Princess in distress who saves herself"
- "Villain as protagonist"
- The subversion must serve the *story*, not just demonstrate cleverness

## Genre Blending
Many great works combine:
- Sci-fi + thriller (*Jurassic Park*)
- Fantasy + romance (*Outlander*)
- Mystery + literary (*The Secret History*)
- Horror + literary (*Beloved*)

## Failure Modes
- Slavishly following tropes (formulaic)
- Subverting without understanding (broken expectation, unfulfilled promise)
- Genre-mismatched marketing (alienates intended readers)
- Genre snobbery — refusing to learn from popular conventions

## Implication for Quality Models
- A "genre" rubric must encode genre-specific quality criteria, not just "literary" ones
- Reader voting in mixed-genre communities (like r/WritingPrompts) reflects genre-specific expectations
