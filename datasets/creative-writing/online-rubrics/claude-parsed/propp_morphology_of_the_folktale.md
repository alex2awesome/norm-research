# Vladimir Propp — Morphology of the Folktale (1928)

Russian formalist analysis identifying invariant narrative units across 100 fairy tales.

## 31 Narrative Functions (Fixed Order)

1. **Absentation** — A family member leaves home
2. **Interdiction** — Hero is warned/prohibited
3. **Violation** — Warning is disregarded
4. **Reconnaissance** — Villain seeks information
5. **Delivery** — Villain learns about victim
6. **Trickery** — Villain attempts to deceive
7. **Complicity** — Victim is taken in by deception
8. **Villainy / Lacking** — Harm is done OR something is lacking
9. **Mediation** — Misfortune made known; hero called
10. **Beginning Counteraction** — Hero decides to act
11. **Departure** — Hero leaves home
12. **First Donor Function** — Hero is tested/approached by donor
13. **Hero's Reaction** — Hero responds to donor
14. **Receipt of Magical Agent** — Hero acquires magical aid
15. **Guidance** — Hero is led to object of search
16. **Struggle** — Hero and villain in direct combat
17. **Branding** — Hero is marked
18. **Victory** — Villain is defeated
19. **Liquidation** — Initial misfortune is resolved
20. **Return** — Hero returns
21. **Pursuit** — Hero is chased
22. **Rescue** — Hero is rescued from pursuit
23. **Unrecognized Arrival** — Hero arrives home or elsewhere unrecognized
24. **Unfounded Claims** — False hero presents unfounded claims
25. **Difficult Task** — Difficult task proposed to hero
26. **Solution** — Task is resolved
27. **Recognition** — Hero is recognized
28. **Exposure** — False hero / villain is exposed
29. **Transfiguration** — Hero is given new appearance
30. **Punishment** — Villain is punished
31. **Wedding** — Hero marries / ascends throne

## 7 Character Spheres (Dramatis Personae)

1. **Villain** — antagonist creating obstacles
2. **Donor** — prepares hero or gives a magical object
3. **Helper** — assists hero in the quest
4. **Princess (Sought-for Person) and her Father** — the prize / one who assigns difficult tasks
5. **Dispatcher** — sends the hero on the quest
6. **Hero** — protagonist who reacts to donor and weds the princess
7. **False Hero** — takes credit for hero's actions, claims princess

## Key Principles

- Functions are **stable, constant elements** independent of who fulfills them
- The **number of functions is limited** (31)
- The **sequence of functions is identical** across tales
- All fairy tales are of **one structural type**

## Application as Evaluative Rubric

A narrative can be analyzed for completeness, sequence integrity, and character-function alignment. Skipping or reordering core functions tends to weaken folktale-style structure.
