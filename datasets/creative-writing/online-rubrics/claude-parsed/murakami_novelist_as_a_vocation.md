# Haruki Murakami — Novelist as a Vocation (2015, English 2022)

Eleven essays — part memoir, part craft manual — by one of the world's most-translated living novelists.

## Originality from Freedom

"If there is indeed something original about my novels, I think it springs from the principle of freedom." Originality emerges not from technique but from refusing constraints — including the expectations of literary tradition, market, and ego.

## The Reading Foundation

Reading widely and constantly is non-negotiable for any aspiring writer. The novelist is first a reader; the catalog of one's reading determines the range of one's writing.

## Observation Over Judgment

"Be observant, but be slow to make judgments about those observations." Take in the detail; resist the impulse to interpret too quickly. Premature judgment ossifies fiction.

## Discipline of Daily Output

Murakami is rigorous: 1,600 words (10 Japanese manuscript pages) per day. Stop when you could continue. Show up tomorrow.

## On Inspiration

The novelist must be receptive to the unbidden moment (Murakami's was the crack of a baseball bat). But inspiration without discipline is nothing — the door must be open every day.

## Long-Form Endurance

Murakami's central craft argument: writing the long novel is an athletic feat. The writer must build physical and mental endurance — running, sleep, routine. Talent without endurance fails the long form.

## On the Originality of the Self

The writer's voice cannot be borrowed or studied. It emerges only through patient excavation of one's own perceptions. Imitation produces competent prose; surrender to one's own oddity produces art.

## On Awards and Reviews

Be indifferent. The work judges itself over time; contemporary judgment is noise.

## Writing Schools

Useful for some, dispensable for others. No technique substitutes for the reader-writer dyad of self with chosen masters.

## The Three Faculties

Murakami names three indispensable faculties for the novelist:
1. **Talent** (innate)
2. **Concentration** (cultivable — sit at the desk for hours)
3. **Endurance** (cultivable — sustain over years)

The first cannot be trained; the second and third can.

## Evaluative Criteria

A novel can be judged by:
- Whether it carries an unmistakable voice
- Whether it sustains its own internal logic over its full length
- Whether observation is honored without premature judgment
- Whether the reader finishes feeling the world has been opened
