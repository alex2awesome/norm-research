# Tessa Barron — What Makes a Great Short Story

1. **Strong Theme** — Central idea must be clear and cohesive. "Everything that happens in that story should be able to tie back to that idea."

2. **Complete Plot** — Beginning, middle, end with character development. Per Robert Barr: develop the plot "letting no incident creep in that does not bear relation to the climax." Plot must be simple, plausible, logical.

3. **Climax-Centered Structure** — Everything leads to a single, powerful conclusion. Climax should feel both surprising and inevitable. Every word advances toward this moment.

4. **Human Dimension** — Even fantastical stories must explore authentic human experiences. Readers connect through recognizable aspects of human condition.

5. **Originality** — Short story readers demand freshness. Clichés and tropes less tolerable than in novels. Original execution essential.

## Bonus: Precision Over Word Count
Perfect length is "exactly how long it needs to be to be good." Focus on eliminating unnecessary words rather than hitting word-count targets.
