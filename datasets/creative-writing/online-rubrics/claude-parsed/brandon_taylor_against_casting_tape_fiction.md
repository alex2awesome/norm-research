# Brandon Taylor — Against Casting Tape Fiction

## The Problem
Contemporary first-person narration stripped of interiority. Writers produce "cool objective" narratives lacking "explanatory power" and depicting characters as passive observers rather than thinking agents.

Root cause: Young writers have "watched more movies, television, and footage" than lived experience, so they "simply do not know how to write thoughts." They mimic cinema's surface-level observation rather than prose's deeper access to consciousness.

## Key Critiques

**Faulty Technique:**
- Phrases like "I could see" or "I felt" break first-person consistency.
- Over-describing physical gestures replaces psychological depth.
- Characters move through arbitrary scenes without dramatic context.

**Passivity:**
Reflects broader cultural passivity. Reality TV normalized "contextless action." Writers internalized arbitrariness, creating characters who don't integrate experiences into meaning.

**Lost Literary Inheritance:**
Abandoning modernism's psychological innovations for something "even more severely positivist than Naturalism."

## Prescriptions

**Essential Questions:**
- Who is your narrator addressing and why?
- What time gap exists between narration and events?
- How has the narrator changed since experiencing these events?

**Craft Principles:**
- Make interiority visible through thought, reflection, and processing.
- Use withholding strategically — let characters reveal themselves through what they do discuss.
- Integrate the narrator's full history, not just forward momentum.
- Make the act of telling itself active and transformative.

**Exemplars:** Knausgaard, Greenwell, Cusk — narrators who "think and process and react."
