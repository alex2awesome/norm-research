# In Medias Res — "Into the Middle of Things"

## Definition
A story-opening technique that drops the reader into the midst of action, with backstory filled in later through dialogue, flashback, or contextual revelation.

## Origin
Horace, *Ars Poetica* (~13 BC). Practiced by Homer (*Iliad*, *Odyssey* both begin in middle).

## Why It Works
- Generates immediate momentum
- Creates an information deficit that pulls the reader forward
- Implicit world-building via context rather than exposition
- Compresses the apparent timeline (much has already happened off-page)
- Bypasses the dreaded "boring opening"

## Craft Techniques for In Medias Res
- Open at a climactic moment, conflict, argument, fight, or revelation
- Trust the reader to catch up
- Withhold backstory until it's earned
- Use flashbacks sparingly
- Let dialogue carry exposition
- Avoid the temptation to over-explain in the second paragraph

## Common Failure Modes
- Action without stakes (we don't care because we don't know who's involved)
- Confusing without rewarding (reader gives up)
- Exposition dumped in chapter 2 to "catch them up"
- Generic action-movie opening with no specificity

## Quality Indicators
- Reader is hooked by paragraph 2
- Curiosity, not confusion
- Backstory revealed in service of present scene
- The opening moment turns out to be load-bearing for the whole story
