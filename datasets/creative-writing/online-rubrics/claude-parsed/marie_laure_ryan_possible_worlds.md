# Marie-Laure Ryan — Possible Worlds, AI, and Narrative Theory (1991) / Narrative as Virtual Reality (2001)

Cognitive narratology grounded in possible-worlds semantics. Provides analytical tools for evaluating storyworld richness and immersion.

## Possible-Worlds Foundation

A narrative constructs a "textual actual world" (TAW) — what is asserted to be true in the story. Around it cluster characters' belief-worlds, wish-worlds, obligation-worlds, fantasy-worlds, etc. Conflict and plot arise from **discrepancies** between these worlds.

## Modal Structure of a Storyworld

Each character has private worlds:
- **Knowledge world (K-world)** — what the character believes is true
- **Wish world (W-world)** — what the character wants to be true
- **Obligation world (O-world)** — what the character believes ought to be true
- **Intention world (I-world)** — what the character plans to do
- **Fantasy world** — alternative imagined realities

## Plot as World-Conflict

Plot = movement of TAW relative to the private worlds of characters. The forward push comes from **modal disequilibrium** — gaps between what is, what is believed, what is wanted, and what ought to be.

## Principle of Minimal Departure

Readers fill gaps in the storyworld by assuming it resembles the real world **except where explicitly contradicted**. The writer controls the minimal-departure budget — when to invoke worldhood, when to spend the suspension of disbelief.

## Immersion (Three Forms)

1. **Spatial immersion** — sense of being in a place
2. **Temporal immersion** — being caught in the unfolding of time (suspense)
3. **Emotional immersion** — caring about characters and their fates

A great narrative produces all three.

## Interactivity vs. Immersion

For traditional fiction, interactivity (the reader's freedom to choose) is in tension with immersion (absorption). The novelist secures immersion by removing reader choice; interactive fiction must work harder to immerse.

## Tellability

A storyworld must be **tellable** — worth narrating. Routine sequences fail. Tellability comes from:
- Modal complexity (rich private-world structures)
- Counterfactual implications (what could have been)
- Surprising configurations of character worlds

## Storyworld Richness

A storyworld is rich to the degree it:
- Sustains references beyond the narrated events
- Permits coherent inferences about unsaid matters
- Has internal logic that could generate further events
- Awakens curiosity beyond the explicit text

## Evaluative Criteria

A narrative can be evaluated on:
- Modal complexity of character private worlds
- Coherence of plot as movement among worlds
- Strength of spatial / temporal / emotional immersion
- Storyworld richness beyond explicit text
- Tellability
- Strategic management of minimal departure
