# The New Yorker — What They Look For in Fiction

## Quality Standards
- **Literary fiction** — character development, nuanced themes, compelling prose over plot-driven narratives
- **Polished, distinctive voice** — carefully controlled tone
- **Original voices and perspectives** — avoid stories too similar to other published works
- **Mastery of language** — flawless grammar, spelling, clarity, tense
- **Polished finished drafts** — submit only your very best work

## What They Want
- Unique storytelling
- Demonstrable mastery of language
- Stories that resonate beyond the page
- Often: literary realism, character interiority, observed detail of class/family/place
- Often interlocking themes of identity, mortality, alienation, intimacy

## What They Don't Publish
- Genre fiction (sci-fi, fantasy, romance, thriller) — with rare exceptions
- Sentimental, MFA-cliché stories
- Plot-twist-driven work without literary craft

## Format
- Word count: 2,000–10,000 words (most stories ~4,000–8,000)
- Cover letter: 1–3 sentences (name, brief credits, title, word count)
- Online portal only

## Implicit Quality Hallmarks (from analyses of published stories)
- Strong opening sentences that imply a whole world
- Compression — no wasted scene or description
- Resonant, often ambiguous endings (not punchlines)
- Subtext — things implied rather than stated
- Specificity of detail — particularized place and class
- A felt sense of moral or psychological stakes
