# Matthew Salesses — 25 Essential Notes on Craft

1. Craft is a set of expectations.
2. Expectations are not universal; they are standardized.
3. Expectations aren't inherently negative — they provide pleasure and structure.
4. Michelle Cliff rejected standard craft conventions to authentically represent her colonial Jamaican experience.
5. Craft is simultaneously more and less complex than typically taught.
6. The Iowa Writers' Workshop promoted craft as non-ideological while spreading individualism.
7. Western fiction is inseparable from individualism.
8. Whose expectations guide craft decisions, and who holds the freedom to break those rules?
9. Craft expectations belong to specific audiences.
10. Milan Kundera offers alternative craft history emphasizing external forces over individual psychology.
11. Iowa Workshop's focus on style/form served to appear apolitical while standardizing literature.
12. Western plot follows Freytag/Aristotle; Eastern uses kishotenketsu (intro, development, twist, reconciliation).
13. Craft reflects culture but can evolve to resist and reshape cultural norms.
14. Understanding craft means understanding how people perceive each other.
15. Chinese fiction traditions valued structural soundness differently than Western critics.
16. Gish Jen blends Western/Eastern: independent and interdependent selves.
17. "Know your audience" fundamentally defines craft.
18. Multiple legitimate crafts exist; teaching craft as singular is a critical failure.
19. African American authors employed masked narrative — writing for white and Black audiences simultaneously.
20. Adoptee/marginalized narratives often feature coincidence and reunion plots.
21. Invisible conventions become visible when broken.
22. Conventions remain debatable (e.g., the pathetic fallacy).
23. Western narrative assumes conflict resolution; alternative worldviews see protagonists as integrated within their world.
24. Cultural differences shape how audiences perceive narrative events.
25. Craft supports particular worldviews; revision enables conscious cultural participation.
