# Pacing — Fiction Craft Principles

## Definition
Pacing = the rate at which narrative events, information, and emotional experiences are delivered. Not just speed; *rhythm* — alternation between tension/release, density/space, action/reflection.

## Scene vs. Summary (the Master Lever)
- **Scene** — events rendered in real time with dialogue, sensory detail, action; slow but immersive
- **Summary** — events condensed into narration; fast but distancing
- All-scene novels = bloated; all-summary novels = emotionally remote
- Quality fiction calibrates per moment

## Tools for Speeding Up
- Short sentences and paragraphs
- Short chapters
- Heavy dialogue (less description)
- Active verbs
- Cut to the next scene mid-action
- Eliminate transitional summary
- Cliffhangers between chapters

## Tools for Slowing Down
- Longer, more complex sentences
- Detailed sensory description
- Interior reflection
- Flashback and contextualization
- Lingering on a single image or moment
- Scene with low overt conflict but rich texture

## Quality Criteria
- **Match pacing to content** — emotional climax slow; chase scene fast
- **Vary the rhythm** — relentless sprint exhausts; unbroken stroll bores
- **Pace shifts must be deliberate** — sloppy transitions break the dream
- **Reading-aloud test** — pacing should feel right to the ear
- **Strategic compression / expansion** — most stories are 90% summary collapsed into 10% scene

## Common Failure Modes
- Saggy middles (Mamet's "second-act problems")
- Endless setup; story doesn't start until page 50
- Climactic scene rushed
- Aftermath dragged out
- Dialogue scenes that should be summarized
- Summary scenes that should be dramatized

## Pacing & Genre
- Thriller — high pace, short chapters, frequent cliffhangers
- Literary — variable, often deliberate slow stretches
- Romance — alternating intimate scene with separation
- Horror — slow build interrupted by quick shocks
- Comedy — escalating absurdity; rhythm of jokes
