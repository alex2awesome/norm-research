# Charles Baxter — Burning Down the House: Essays on Fiction

## Notable Essays & Their Craft Arguments

### "Against Epiphanies"
Critiques the workshop-fashionable Joycean epiphany. Argues many MFA stories arrive at unearned moments of revelation — false closure dressed up as insight. Quality fiction earns its insights or refuses them.

### "Dysfunctional Narratives: Or, 'Mistakes Were Made'"
Argues contemporary fiction often centers around protagonists who *avoid* responsibility. Quality fiction stages real moral choice and consequence.

### "Stillness"
On the importance of moments where nothing apparently "happens" — silence, pause, atmosphere. Quality fiction uses stillness deliberately, not just constant action.

### "Rhyming Action"
Actions in fiction can echo, mirror, refract earlier actions — pattern at the level of plot/event, like rhyme in poetry. Quality fiction has these underground harmonies.

### "Talking Forks: Fiction and the Inner Life of Objects"
Objects in fiction can carry meaning beyond their function. The chair, the cup, the photograph as load-bearing presence.

### "Counterpointed Characterization"
Characters defined by contrast with each other; quality fiction uses character pairings deliberately.

### "On Defamiliarization"
Shklovsky's principle — making the strange familiar and the familiar strange.

### "Maximalist Fiction"
The aesthetic of more — Pynchon, DeLillo, Wallace; the case for excess.

## Quality Criteria (from Baxter's overall stance)
- **Earned insight**, not workshop epiphany
- **Moral seriousness** without moralism
- **Patterning** below conscious notice
- **Objects with weight** beyond plot function
- **Stillness as craft**
- **Character through opposition**
- **Defamiliarized observation** — strangeness in the everyday
