# Robert McKee — Story: Principles of Storytelling

## Core Premise
"Story is about form, not formula." McKee identifies the architectural principles common to compelling narrative across screen, page, and stage.

## Key Principles

### The Story Triangle
- **Archplot** (classical design) — Active protagonist, external conflict, closed ending, linear time, causality.
- **Miniplot** (minimalist) — Subtle, internal, open-ended.
- **Antiplot** (anti-structure) — Coincidence, nonlinear, inconsistent reality.
Most great commercial work sits in archplot; literary work explores mini/anti.

### The Five-Part Structure
1. **Inciting incident** — The event that throws the protagonist's life out of balance.
2. **Progressive complications** — Escalating obstacles.
3. **Crisis** — Decision point with greatest pressure.
4. **Climax** — The decision/action that resolves the conflict.
5. **Resolution** — Aftermath; new equilibrium.

### Substance
- **Want vs. Need** — Conscious desire vs. unconscious desire.
- **Object of Desire** — What the protagonist consciously seeks.
- **Forces of Antagonism** — Sum of all forces opposing the protagonist's will. The story is only as compelling as the antagonism.

### Style
- **Genre conventions** — Audience expectations dictated by the chosen genre; subvert artfully, never ignore.
- **Setting** — Period, duration, location, level of conflict.

### Story Values
Every scene must turn on a story value (life/death, love/hate, freedom/oppression, etc.) and that value must shift positive↔negative.

### Beats, Scenes, Sequences, Acts
- **Beat**: Smallest unit; an exchange of behavior in action/reaction.
- **Scene**: A story event that turns a value charge.
- **Sequence**: 2–5 scenes, climaxing in significant turn.
- **Act**: A series of sequences peaking in a major reversal.

### The Negation of the Negation
The "deepest" stories descend past the simple negative (e.g., hate) to the *contrary of the contrary* (self-hatred), revealing the dark dimension of the value.

### Character & Characterization
- **Characterization**: Sum of observable qualities (job, age, appearance).
- **True Character**: Revealed only by choice under pressure.
- "The deeper the pressure, the deeper the revelation."

### The Principle of Antagonism
A protagonist and her story can be intellectually fascinating and emotionally compelling only to the degree that the forces of antagonism make them so.

### Show, Don't Tell
Dramatize — don't narrate exposition. "If a thing can be shown, it must be shown."

### The Climax — Storytelling's Soul
The whole story exists for the climax. Everything else builds to it.
