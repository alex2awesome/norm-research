# Annie Dillard — The Writing Life

## Core Craft Principles

### Spend It All
"Spend it all, shoot it, play it, lose it, all, right away, every time. Do not hoard what seems good for a later place in the book, or for another book; give it, give it all, give it now." Don't save your best material — use it now and trust more will come.

### Following the Line
Writing is "following a line of words." The work is moment-to-moment: each word leads to the next. The "line" has its own logic that pulls the writer.

### Years, Not Months
A book takes 2–10 years. "Less is so rare as to be statistically insignificant." Fast writing rarely produces work worth keeping.

### Specificity over Generality
"Don't tell the reader that someone was happy or sad. When you do that, the reader has nothing to see. She throws his clothes out the window. Be specific."

### Your Unique Perspective is the Project
"You are the only one of you. Your unique perspective, at this time, in our age, whether it's on Tunis or the trees outside your window, is what matters."

### The Writing Life is Brutal but Necessary
The dedication, absurdity, and daring of writing as a vocation; not for everyone, but those who can do it owe it to the work.

### Cutting is Half the Job
"You write it all, discovering it at the end of the line of words. The line of words is a fiber optic, flexible as wire; it illumines the path just before its fragile tip. You probe with it, delicate as a worm."

### Tools / Daily Practice
- Walk
- Read deeply in your own field
- Write daily
- Make peace with the fact that the room will be lonely

### Discipline as Faith
The writer keeps showing up not because they know it will work but because the act of writing is its own form of devotion to the work emerging.
