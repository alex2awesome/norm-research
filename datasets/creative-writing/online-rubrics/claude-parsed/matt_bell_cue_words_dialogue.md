# Matt Bell — Using Cue Words to Structure Dialogue

## Core Concept
From Robert McKee's screenwriting advice: "the last word or phrase of each speech is the core word that seals meaning and cues a reaction from the other side of the scene."

## The Technique
Position the most important phrase at the **end of a character's line**, prompting the next speaker to respond directly to that element rather than earlier content. Creates natural rhythm and forward momentum.

## Practical Tips

**As a revision tool**, ask two diagnostic questions:
- What cue is Character B responding to in Character A's dialogue?
- Where is that cue located?

**For longer speeches**, characters don't respond to bulk philosophical content or backstory. They pick up the final question or directive — the conversational pivot point.

**For disconnected dialogue**, characters can respond to their own internal cues rather than their partner's — effectively depicts when people don't listen to each other.

## Effect
Makes dialogue "easy to follow, keeps it snappy, and ideally allows the movement of the dialogue to be easily understood."
