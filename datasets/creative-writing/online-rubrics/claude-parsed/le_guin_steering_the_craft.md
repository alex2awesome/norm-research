# Steering the Craft (Ursula K. Le Guin) — Craft Elements & Quality Criteria

Ten chapters address fundamental components of narrative prose, each with examples from the literary canon and an exercise.

## Chapter Topics / Craft Elements
1. **The Sound of Your Writing** — Prose rhythm, musicality, onomatopoeia, alliteration; "dull, choppy, droning" prose stems from poor sound; "flowing, strong" prose has perfect placement and timing of words.
2. **Punctuation and Grammar** — Grammar/punctuation as deliberate craft tools.
3. **Sentence Length and Complex Syntax** — Variety, control of complexity.
4. **Repetition** — Strategic use of intentional repetition for rhythm and emphasis.
5. **Adjective and Adverb** — Lazy modifiers (e.g., "suddenly", "somehow") weaken prose; stronger verbs and nouns create vividness. Le Guin: "find out which are the Styrofoam and which are the heavy gold."
6. **Verbs: Person and Tense** — Choices in tense and POV person change story experience.
7. **Point of View and Voice** — Single-character limited, multi-character limited, involved/detached author.
8. **Changing Point of View** — Transitions, signaling, controlled head-hopping.
9. **Indirect Narration / Crowding and Leaping** — What to include vs. omit; what to dramatize vs. summarize.
10. **The Expository Lump** — Master invisible exposition; integrate background naturally; don't lecture.

## Key Exercises

### Exercise 1: Hearing the Sound of Your Writing
Write 1 paragraph–1 page meant for reading aloud, using onomatopoeia, alliteration, rhythmic effects, and invented words but no rhyme or meter.

### Exercise 2: Eliminate Adjectives & Adverbs
Write 200–350 words of descriptive narrative without adjectives or adverbs (except time-related). Use only verbs, nouns, pronouns, and articles.

### Exercise 3: Master Invisible Exposition
- Dialogue-only scenes that reveal character without lectures
- Settings that reveal absent characters via environmental detail
- Scenes where complex processes unfold through conversation/action

## Underlying Quality Criteria
- Attention and self-discipline (writing as learnable craft, not just talent)
- Vivid sensory rhythm in prose
- Economy: forced word weighing reveals "Styrofoam vs. heavy gold"
- Information should integrate, never interrupt the dream
