# Jorge Luis Borges — On Writing

Compiled from his Paris Review interview ("The Art of Fiction No. 39"), Sur essays, and *Seven Conversations*.

## On Brevity and Form

"The short story can be taken in at a single glance" while novels depend on "padding, on pages which are mere nexuses." Borges famously preferred the story to the novel and is the great defender of compressed fiction. Density should achieve what novelistic length attempts.

## Fiction as Intellectual Hypothesis

"Fiction is based on the examination of an intellectual possibility presented as a narrative hypothesis." A story is a thought experiment dressed as event.

## Refinement Through Tradition

Folk tales are "a much more refined product" than authored verse because they have "been stripped of everything that might be useless or bothersome" through generations of retelling. Communal editing yields perfection.

## Avoiding Contemporary Specificity

Set stories "in somewhat indeterminate places and many years ago" to avoid readers scrutinizing factual accuracy. Abstraction protects artistic integrity.

## On Style

- Use simple words; avoid the word that calls attention to itself
- Distrust the metaphor that surprises only by novelty — use only metaphors that connect things genuinely linked
- "I would like to write in such a way that the reader thinks the words are inevitable"
- Do not be original at the cost of clarity

## Against Schools

"I no longer believe in literary schools now; I believe in the individual." Authenticity over fashion.

## Writing as Play

Creative work "doesn't feel it to be a chore; one feels it to be a form of amusement." The writer who works grimly produces dead text.

## Recurring Structural Devices

- The labyrinth (literal and metaphorical)
- The infinite library
- Doubling and the doppelgänger
- The unreliable scholar / fictional bibliography
- The story-within-a-story
- The dream that contains the dreamer

## Timelessness

A truly timeless book "would be just as admirable if it had been published a hundred years before or if it were published a hundred years later."

## Evaluative Criteria

A story can be judged by:
- Density (does it achieve novel-richness in compressed form?)
- Inevitability of word choice
- Whether its metaphors connect genuinely related things
- Whether it presents a clear intellectual hypothesis
- Whether it transcends its historical moment
