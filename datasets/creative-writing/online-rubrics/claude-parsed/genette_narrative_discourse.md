# Gérard Genette — Narrative Discourse (1972/1980)

The most systematic narratological taxonomy. Five categories for analyzing how a story is told.

## 1. ORDER (Anachrony)

The relation between the chronological order of story events and the order of their narration.

- **Analepsis** — flashback; narration of an event after later events have been told
- **Prolepsis** — flashforward; narration of an event before its chronological place
- **Achrony** — events without identifiable temporal location
- **Reach** — how far back/forward the anachrony extends
- **Extent** — how long the anachrony lasts

## 2. DURATION

The relation between story-time and discourse-time.

- **Ellipsis** — story-time elapses with no discourse-time (gap)
- **Summary** — discourse-time shorter than story-time
- **Scene** — discourse-time roughly equal to story-time (often dialogue)
- **Stretch** — discourse-time longer than story-time (slow motion)
- **Descriptive Pause** — discourse-time elapses with no story-time

## 3. FREQUENCY

The relation between how often an event occurs and how often it is narrated.

- **Singulative** — narrating once what happened once
- **Multiple-Singulative** — narrating n times what happened n times
- **Repetitive** — narrating multiple times what happened once
- **Iterative** — narrating once what happened multiple times

## 4. MOOD

How the narrative is presented; regulates information flow.

### Distance (modes of speech presentation)
- **Narrated speech** — fully transposed
- **Transposed speech** — indirect discourse
- **Reported speech** — direct discourse / quoted

### Focalization (perspective)
- **Zero focalization** — omniscient; narrator knows more than any character
- **Internal focalization** — narrator knows what one character knows (fixed, variable, or multiple)
- **External focalization** — narrator knows less than the character; only externals are reported

## 5. VOICE

Who narrates and from where.

### Time of Narration
- **Subsequent** — past-tense narration after events
- **Prior** — predictive/prophetic
- **Simultaneous** — present-tense
- **Interpolated** — between actions (epistolary, journals)

### Narrative Level
- **Extradiegetic** — narrator outside the story-world
- **Intradiegetic** — narrator inside the story-world
- **Metadiegetic** — narrator inside an embedded story

### Person
- **Heterodiegetic** — narrator absent from the story they tell
- **Homodiegetic** — narrator present as a character
- **Autodiegetic** — narrator is the protagonist

## Evaluative Use

Any narrative can be exhaustively analyzed across these five axes — and craft choices judged for their coherence, effect, and necessity.
