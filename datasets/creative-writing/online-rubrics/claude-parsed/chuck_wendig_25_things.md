# Chuck Wendig — 250 Things You Should Know About Writing

Wendig is a novelist (Star Wars: Aftermath, Wanderers) and prolific blogger at terribleminds.com. The book is 11 lists of 25 things each on writing topics. Tone is profane, funny, brutally honest.

## The 11 Lists (Topics Covered)
1. **Being a Writer** — habits, mindset, daily practice
2. **Writing a Novel** — long-form structure, planning, drafting
3. **Writing a Screenplay** — visual storytelling, dialogue
4. **Writing a Sentence** — line-level craft
5. **Storytelling** — fundamentals of narrative
6. **Character** — making people feel real
7. **Plot** — structure, conflict, momentum
8. **Dialogue** — voice, subtext, naturalism
9. **Description** — sensory, restrained, charged
10. **Editing/Revising** — kill darlings, structural revision
11. **Getting Published** — query, submission, agents

## Sample Wendig Principles (paraphrased from terribleminds essays)
- "You are a storyteller. Your job is to tell stories."
- Every scene must do at least three things (advance plot, reveal character, set tone)
- Conflict on every page; subtext under every line
- Avoid passive characters
- Show characters making *choices*
- Characters should want things, fear things, lose things
- Voice is the line-level music of you
- "Kill your darlings" includes structural choices, not just lines
- Beware the temptation to be clever; aim for true and surprising
- No backstory dumps in chapter 1
- Endings must answer the question raised at the start
- Subvert tropes only after mastering them

## Wendig on the Process
- First drafts are made of trash; that's fine
- Revision is the real work
- Read constantly
- Write daily (but don't moralize about it)
- The internet is both a tool and a black hole — manage it

## Why Useful for Quality Evaluation
Wendig's lists distill working-novelist craft into testable predicates. Many overlap with formal craft books but in shorter, more memorable form — useful as quick-check rubric.
