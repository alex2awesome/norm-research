# James Wood — How Fiction Works (2008)

Influential close-reading manual by The New Yorker book critic.

## Free Indirect Style

Wood's central technical concern. Free indirect style is the prose technique where third-person narration takes on the voice, vocabulary, and consciousness of a character without explicit "he thought" markers. "When I talk about free indirect style I am really talking about point of view." It is the distinctive achievement of the modern novel.

## The Real

Wood's deepest commitment: fiction's job is to render "lifeness" — the sense of a real consciousness perceiving a real world. "The real" is the bottom of all his inquiries.

## The Primacy of Detail / Thisness

Borrowing Duns Scotus's *haecceitas* ("thisness"), Wood praises the precise, irreducible detail that makes a fictional world particular and alive. Generic detail kills fiction; "thisness" sustains it.

## Character Aliveness

Characters are made alive not through psychology lectures but through precise observation rendered through their consciousness. Effective prose maintains "an unsentimental composure and knows how to withdraw, like a good valet, from superfluous commentary."

## Commercial Realism

Wood defends attention to mundane life — the breakfast table, the office, the bus. Capturing the banal alongside the significant is more authentic than the sublime alone.

## Hysterical Realism (negative criterion)

Wood's coined critique of contemporary big novels (Pynchon, Rushdie, Zadie Smith, DFW). Symptoms:
- Manic, cartoonish characters
- Frenzied, improbable action
- Information-overload prose
- Length without depth
- A vitality that is "not life" but its "imitation"

## Flaubert's Legacy

"There really is a time before Flaubert and a time after him." Flaubert established the modern realist mode: invisible authorial presence + meticulous visual noticing + free indirect style.

## Detail's Two Powers

- **Vivifying** — bringing fictional world to specific life
- **Selecting** — implying a consciousness that has chosen this detail and not another (telling us about the perceiver)

## Evaluative Criteria

A passage of fiction succeeds when:
- It convinces us of a perceiving consciousness
- Its details have "thisness," not generality
- Its voice integrates character interiority via free indirect style
- It withdraws from over-commentary
- It avoids hysterical excess
