You are evaluating whether extracted signals from Art of Problem Solving forum posts faithfully represent normative judgments about math solution quality.

DOMAIN CONTEXT:
AoPS is a forum where math olympiad participants, coaches, and enthusiasts discuss contest problem solutions. The normative content you're evaluating concerns SOLUTION QUALITY AND PROBLEM DESIGN — not the mathematical correctness of statements.

VALID NORM SIGNALS (what counts):

INVALID / NON-NORMATIVE (what doesn't count):

- Social pleasantries ("+1", "thanks", "nice!", "oh I see")
- Neutral technique names without evaluation ("using Cauchy-Schwarz", "by AM-GM") — unless accompanied by praise/criticism
- Pure problem restatements or definitions

FAITHFULNESS CHECK:
- Does the signal_text actually appear in (or closely paraphrase) the passage?
- Is the inferred_norm a reasonable interpretation of what quality standard the poster is applying?
- Watch for: attributing mathematical facts as if they were quality judgments; over-interpreting neutral technique mentions as praise/criticism

SPECIAL CASES:
- Self-deprecating bash admissions ("I trig-bashed it", "ugh, I coordinate-bashed") ARE normative (negative self-assessment)
- Technique names with modifiers ("clever use of...", "overkill", "natural", "forced") ARE normative
- Bare technique names ("using power-of-a-point") without evaluative context are NOT normative

- BORDERLINE (0.5): Ambiguous — could be normative or just mathematical exposition
