Annual Report 2020

EURASIAN PATENT EXAMINATION

The Eurasian Patent Office received 3,377applications for Eurasian patent protection in 2020.

Eurasian Application Statistics

Against the backdrop of a global decline in economic activity caused by the pandemic, the number of Eurasian applications in 2020 was relatively stable, with a year-end decline of only about 3 per cent.

Overall, the Eurasian Patent Office had 60,731 Eurasian application filings since the establishment of the Eurasian Patent Organization in 1996 through 2020.

International applications accounted for the bulk of Eurasian applications in 2020 totaling to 2,486 applications, or 73.6 per cent of all the applications filed.

The year 2020 saw 511 applications filed directly with the Eurasian Patent Office, which accounts for 15.1 per cent of overall Eurasian application filings during the year. National IP offices of the Eurasian Patent Convention (EAPC) Contracting States channeled 380 applications in 2020, or 11.3 per cent of the total amount of Eurasian applications filed.

Similarly to the preceding years, the United States of America proved the largest geographical origin of Eurasian applications, 991 or 29.3 per cent of all the applications filed with the Eurasian Patent Office.

Applicants from the EAPC Contracting States filed 679 applications in 2020.

The largest number of Eurasian applications, which amounts to 453, came from the Russian Federation, the Republic of Belarus accounts for 105 applications, the Republic of Kazakhstan  – 90 applications and the Republic of Azerbaijan  – 18 applications.

EURASIAN PATENT GRANTS

In the reporting year, the Eurasian Patent Register saw 2,735 new entries. The total number of EAPO grants for the period 1996 through 2020 makes 36,888 Eurasian patents.

In the year under review 2,754 Eurasian patents were granted, which exceeds the respective results of 2019 by 2.11 per cent. The grand total for 1996 through 2020 reached 36,882 Eurasian patent grants.

Applicants from the EAPC Contracting States and the Republic of Moldova were granted 531 Eurasian patents in 2020, marking 19.28 per cent of all the patent grants during the year.

Applicants from States not party to the EAPC had 2,223 Eurasian patent grants in 2020. The share of patents granted to applicants from States not party to the EAPC reached 80.72 per cent of all the grants for 2020.

Applicants from four EAPC Contracting States and the Republic of Moldova were granted 98 Eurasian patents in 2020, or 4.65 per cent of the total Eurasian patent grants based on PCT applications. The largest amount of such patents, 88 (or 4.18 per cent), went to Russian Federation applicants.

The largest number of patents went to the holders from the United States of America, 474 Eurasian patents (22.50 per cent); Germany, 190 Eurasian patents (9.02 per cent); Netherlands, 153 Eurasian patents (7.26 per cent); Switzerland, 134 Eurasian patents (6.36 per cent); France, 128 Eurasian patents (6.07 per cent).

As of 31 December 2020, a total of 653 Eurasian patents had been terminated on the territory of the EAPC Contracting States and the Republic of Moldova due to the expiry of twenty-year term under Article 11 of the Eurasian Patent Convention.

As of the same date, the Eurasian patents in force on the territory of the EAPC Contracting States and the Republic of Moldova reached 17,410 patents.

The year 2020 saw 111 Eurasian patents restored after lapse for non-payment in due time of the annual maintenance fee.

In conformity with Rule 16(5) of the Patent Regulations under the EAPC, which provides for additional legal protection for products requiring special permission for application, the validity of 29 Eurasian patents was extended during the period under review, at the request by relevant patent holders, on the territory of the Republic of Armenia (4 patents), the Republic of Belarus (4 patents), the Republic of Kazakhstan (7 patents), and the Russian Federation (17 patents).

In 2020, the national IP offices of the Republic of Belarus, the Republic of Kazakhstan, the Kyrgyz Republic and the Russian Federation contributed information to the Eurasian Patent Office on the registered license agreements based on Eurasian patents. Relevant information about 45 registered license agreements based on 63 Eurasian patents was published in the EAPO Gazette.
