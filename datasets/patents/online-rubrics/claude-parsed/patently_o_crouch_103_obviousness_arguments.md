# Patently-O: Winning 103 Obviousness Arguments After KSR

## The Post-KSR Landscape

KSR International v. Teleflex (2007) loosened the TSM (teaching/suggestion/motivation) test for obviousness. Examiners now combine references using KSR rationales: "obvious to try," "design choice," "known technique applied to known problem," "predictable variations." Practitioners must learn to attack each rationale on its own terms.

## Attack Rationale 1 - "Obvious to Try"

The Federal Circuit has narrowed "obvious to try": the prior art must (a) identify a finite number of identified, predictable solutions, (b) suggest the inventor's chosen solution, and (c) provide a reasonable expectation of success. If the prior art lists five candidates and the inventor chose the third, "obvious to try" should apply only if there was reasonable expectation that the third would work.

## Attack Rationale 2 - Hindsight Bias

Examiner combinations often work only because the examiner knows the answer. Argue that without the inventor's disclosure, a POSITA would not have selected those specific references from the universe of available art. Cite KSR itself: "A factfinder should be aware, of course, of the distortion caused by hindsight bias."

## Attack Rationale 3 - Teaching Away

If a cited reference says "do not use approach X" or "X has been shown to fail," that reference teaches away from a combination using X. Teaching-away arguments are powerful because they directly negate motivation to combine.

## Attack Rationale 4 - Lack of Reasonable Expectation of Success

Even if a POSITA would have been motivated to combine, an obviousness rejection requires reasonable expectation that the combination would work. In unpredictable arts (chemistry, biology), this is a strong defense.

## Attack Rationale 5 - Bodily Incorporation Required

Sometimes references cannot be combined without destroying the function of one. If reference A teaches a high-temperature process and reference B teaches a temperature-sensitive component, combining them is non-trivial. Argue the combination requires more than a POSITA would do without inventive insight.

## Secondary Considerations - Use Them

Commercial success, long-felt need, failure of others, copying by competitors, industry praise, unexpected results, licensing/acquiescence by others - these are objective indicators of non-obviousness. They require nexus to the claimed features. Submit them via 1.132 declarations or in the response itself.

## Declarations Beat Argument

A declaration from an expert in the field carries far more weight than attorney argument. For close cases, spend the money on a declaration that explains why a POSITA would not have combined the references.
