# Patent Allowance Rates: Empirical Baselines

This paper provides the foundational empirical estimate of US patent allowance rates and identifies systematic factors that determine whether an application is granted.

## Headline Findings

Studying 2.15 million USPTO applications filed 1996 to mid-2013:

**Overall Allowance Rate**: **55.8%** of applications emerged as granted patents without using continuation procedures.

**Continuation-Adjusted Rate**: When continuations are counted as creating new applications, the effective grant rate per application is closer to 71% (because applicants who fail can refile). The "first-action allowance probability" varies dramatically by technology.

**Technology-Specific Decline**: Allowance rates dropped substantially over the study period in:
- "Drugs and Medical Instruments"
- "Computers and Communications" (especially post-Bilski/Mayo/Alice)

Other technology classes were more stable.

**Firm-Size Effect**: Applications from small entities had **lower** grant rates than large entities. The authors interpret this as reflecting differences in:
- Prosecution sophistication (large firms hire experienced prosecutors)
- Application quality (large firms invest more in application drafting)
- Repeat-player effects (large firms know examiner tendencies)

## Quality Implications

### Allowance Rate as a Quality Signal

A patent issued in an art unit with a low allowance rate is, all else equal, a stronger quality signal than one issued in a high-allowance unit. The allowance rate normalizes for examiner-pool laxity:

- Software business-method art units (e.g., 3622, 3624) historically had low allowance rates → grants signal stronger patentability
- Mechanical-arts units have historically had high allowance rates → grants signal weaker patentability differential

### Continuation Practice as Quality Marker

The paper's distinction between first-action allowance and continuation-adjusted rates highlights:

- A patent issued via a continuation chain after parent abandonment may signal applicant persistence rather than invention quality
- A patent issued without RCEs or continuations is a stronger signal of underlying patentability

### Firm-Size Differentials and Quality

Lower small-entity grant rates carry quality-relevant implications:

- Small-entity grants represent higher passing-bars (selection effect)
- Large-entity grants may include marginal applications that would have been rejected with less sophisticated prosecution
- Patent quality assessment should account for entity size

## Operative Quality Markers Derived from This Work

**Application-Level Markers**:
- Did the application receive a notice of allowance on first action? (mixed signal: could be high quality OR weak examination)
- How many RCEs were filed? (more RCEs = more examiner pushback)
- Continuation chain length to issuance (longer chains may signal weakness)

**Comparative Markers**:
- Allowance rate of the assigned art unit (lower = stronger grant signal)
- Examiner's individual allowance rate vs. art-unit median (lower = stronger signal)
- Time to issuance vs. art-unit median (faster may signal weak examination; slower may signal high-quality application that needed minimal narrowing)

**Macro Indicators**:
- Patent issued during a period of declining art-unit allowance rates is more rigorously vetted
- Patent issued during a period of rising allowance rates may benefit from policy laxity

## Methodology Notes

The Carley-Hegde-Marco methodology counts each application as either:
- Allowed (issued as patent)
- Abandoned (no further action)
- Pending (still in process)

Continuation-children are treated as distinct applications, allowing computation of true per-application success rates rather than per-invention success rates.

## Synthesis

Carley, Hegde & Marco supply the empirical baselines that contextualize any individual patent grant:

- The 55.8% per-application allowance rate is the prior probability against which any patent's grant must be assessed
- Industry-specific deviations (lower in software/biotech, higher in mechanical) recalibrate the prior
- Continuation patterns and entity-size effects further condition the inference

A patent issued in a low-allowance-rate art unit, by a small entity, on first or second action, with no continuation history, is the highest-quality signal in this empirical frame. The opposite pattern—high-allowance unit, large entity, multiple continuations—is the weakest.
