# 2019 Revised Patent Subject Matter Eligibility Guidance (2019 PEG)

## Overview

The 2019 PEG was issued January 7, 2019, with Examples 37-42; updated October 17, 2019 with Examples 43-46. The USPTO has issued 46 examples illustrating analysis of various fact patterns.

## Two Major Changes from 2014 Guidance

### Change 1: Grouping of Abstract Ideas

Abstract ideas now categorized into three enumerated groupings:
1. **Mathematical concepts** — relationships, formulas, calculations
2. **Certain methods of organizing human activity** — fundamental economic practices, commercial/legal interactions, managing personal behavior
3. **Mental processes** — observations, evaluations, judgments, opinions

Claims that don't fall into one of these groupings should not typically be treated as reciting an abstract idea.

### Change 2: Two-Prong Step 2A Analysis

**Step 2A Prong One**: Does the claim recite a judicial exception?

**Step 2A Prong Two**: Does the claim recite additional elements that integrate the judicial exception into a practical application?

If the answer to Prong Two is YES, the claim is eligible (Step 2B not needed).

## Practical Application Indicators

Examples of integration into a practical application:
- Improvement to functioning of a computer or other technology
- Applied/used in conjunction with a particular machine
- Effecting a transformation of an article to a different state or thing
- Applies the judicial exception in some other meaningful way
- Beyond merely linking the use to a technological environment

## "Mere Instructions to Apply" Indicators (NOT Practical Application)

- Adding the words "apply it" with the judicial exception
- Mere instructions to apply on a generic computer
- Field-of-use limitations
- Generic linking to a technological environment
- Adding insignificant extra-solution activity

## Key Examples (37-46)

**Example 37**: Relocating Icons on a Graphical User Interface — eligible (specific way to organize)

**Example 38**: Simulating an Analog Audio Mixer — eligible

**Example 39**: Method for Training a Neural Network for Facial Detection — eligible (specific application)

**Example 40**: Adaptive Monitoring of Network Traffic — Two claims, one eligible (specific traffic-monitoring threshold), one ineligible

**Example 41**: Cryptographic Communications — eligible

**Example 42**: Method for Transmission of Notifications When Medical Records Are Updated — eligible (improvement to information transmission)

**Example 43-46**: Life sciences focus — diagnostic methods, treatment selections, biotech inventions

## Step 2B: Additional Elements Analysis

When Step 2A is YES (claim is directed to abstract idea):
- Look for elements that go BEYOND well-understood, routine, conventional
- Berkheimer factors apply: courts may treat this as factual question
- Generic computer components are not enough
- "Inventive concept" must be more than just the abstract idea itself

## October 2019 Update Highlights

- More guidance on "mere instructions to apply"
- Practical application is broad — broader than "inventive concept"
- Case-by-case analysis required
- Specification can be powerful (improvements stated explicitly)

## Strategic Drafting Implications

To increase eligibility chances:
- State technical improvements explicitly in specification
- Tie claims to specific technical environments
- Avoid pure mental-process steps
- Include hardware/structural elements
- Quantify improvements where possible
- Consider adding technological problem statements
