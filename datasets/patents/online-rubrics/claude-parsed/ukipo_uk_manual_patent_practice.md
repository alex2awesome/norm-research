# UK IPO Manual of Patent Practice (MoPP)

## Overview

The Manual of Patent Practice (MoPP) is the document which lays out the UKIPO's approach to patent examination. The Manual recites sections of the Patents Act 1977 and sections of the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 relating to patents.

## Recent Updates

The most recent update was on 2 January 2026 with substantive changes to:
- Sections 14, 20
- Supplementary Protection Certificates for Medicinal and Plant Protection Products

The January 2025 update offered a clearer insight into the examination of computer-implemented inventions.

## Structure

The MoPP follows the Patents Act 1977 section by section, with detailed commentary on:

- **Section 1**: Patentability requirements
- **Section 2**: Novelty
- **Section 3**: Inventive step
- **Section 4**: Industrial application
- **Section 14**: Form/content of application
- **Section 17**: Search
- **Section 18**: Examination
- **Section 20**: Time for putting application in order
- **Sections 25-37**: Term, lapse, restoration

## Patentability Requirements (UK Patents Act 1977 Section 1)

A patent shall be granted only if:
1. Invention is new
2. Involves an inventive step
3. Capable of industrial application
4. Not excluded subject matter (Section 1(2)-(3))

## Section 1(2) Excluded Subject Matter (Same as EPC Article 52)

- Discoveries, scientific theories, mathematical methods
- Aesthetic creations
- Schemes, rules, methods for performing mental acts, playing games, doing business
- Programs for computers
- Presentation of information
(All "as such")

## Aerotel/Macrossan Test (UK Software/Business Method)

UK courts apply a four-step test:
1. Properly construe the claim
2. Identify the actual contribution
3. Ask whether it falls solely within the excluded subject matter
4. Check whether the actual contribution is technical in nature

## Computer-Implemented Inventions Examination

The UKIPO Manual provides specific guidance:
- Technical contribution required (not just executable nature of computer programs)
- Distinguishes between technical contributions and business/non-technical results
- Aligned with EPO practice but with UK-specific test

## Insufficiency (Section 14(3))

The specification must:
- Disclose the invention clearly enough and completely enough for it to be performed by a person skilled in the art
- Cover the entire scope of the claim

## Strategic Differences from US Practice

- More restrictive software/business method patent eligibility
- Focus on "technical contribution"
- No best mode requirement (unlike US pre-AIA)
- No information disclosure statement requirement (no duty of candor)
- Cheaper filing fees and examination
