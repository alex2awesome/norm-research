# Patent Reissue and Correction of Errors

## What is a Patent Reissue Application?

A reissue application is filed to correct an error in the patent, where, as a result of the error, the patent is deemed wholly or partly inoperative or invalid.

The error must arise out of an error in conduct made in the preparation and/or prosecution of the application that became the patent.

## Statutory Basis (35 USC 251)

The error must cause the patent to be "deemed wholly or partly inoperative or invalid, by reason of:
- A defective specification or drawing, OR
- By reason of the patentee claiming more or less than he had a right to claim in the patent."

## Common Reasons for Filing Reissue

- Claims are too narrow or broad
- Disclosure contains inaccuracies
- Failure to claim or incorrectly claimed foreign priority
- Failure to refer to or incorrectly referred to prior co-pending applications
- Change of inventorship
- Substantive drawing or specification error
- Failure to adequately claim benefit under 35 USC 120
- Correction of problem with patent oath

## Reissue vs. Certificate of Correction

| Feature | Certificate of Correction | Reissue Application |
|---|---|---|
| Type of errors | Non-substantive (typos, minor) | Substantive |
| Scope changes | No | Yes |
| Mistakes by | USPTO or applicant | Applicant |
| New examination | No | Yes (full re-examination) |
| Expense | Low | High |

## Important Constraints

### Surrender Requirement
The patent owner must surrender the original patent — receiving a "do-over" with full re-examination.

### Two-Year Window for Broadening
A **broadening reissue** application must be applied for within 2 years from the grant of the original patent. Narrowing reissue can occur at any time during the patent term.

### Recapture Rule
Subject matter surrendered during prosecution cannot be recovered in reissue (Doctrine of Recapture).

### Intervening Rights
Defendants who used the broadened scope before reissue may have intervening rights that limit the patentee's remedies.

## Reissue Process

1. Submit reissue application (with all changes shown)
2. Surrender original patent
3. Patent re-examined fully (all rejections may be re-applied)
4. New patent issued (with same expiration date as original)

## Strategic Considerations

- Reissue creates risk that other claims will be invalidated
- All claims, not just amended ones, are subject to re-examination
- Public access to file makes reissue strategy visible to competitors
- Often used to expand claim scope to capture infringers not previously covered
- Consider if continuation application or reexamination is better alternative
