# Patently-O: The Architecture of an Independent Claim

## Three Layers of an Independent Claim

Every well-drafted independent claim has three layers: (1) the preamble, which states what the claim is for; (2) the transition, which sets the scope ("comprising," "consisting of," "consisting essentially of"); and (3) the body, which lists the elements/limitations.

## The Preamble Question - Is It Limiting?

A preamble is generally not limiting unless it (a) breathes life and meaning into the claim, (b) provides antecedent basis for terms in the body, or (c) was relied upon during prosecution to distinguish prior art. Treat every preamble as a potential limitation; do not put marketing language there.

## Transition Choice - Three Levels of Openness

"Comprising" is open: the claim covers anything containing the listed elements plus more. "Consisting of" is closed: only the listed elements. "Consisting essentially of" is semi-closed: listed elements plus things that do not materially affect the basic and novel characteristics. Default to "comprising" unless there is a tactical reason otherwise.

## Element Order in the Body

Elements should appear in an order that supports the function of the invention - typically the order in which they interact. Reciting elements in a haphazard order makes the claim harder to read and harder to argue.

## Antecedent Basis Hygiene

Each noun in a claim should be introduced with "a" or "an" once, then referred to with "the" or "said" thereafter. "The widget" without prior introduction of "a widget" is a 112(b) indefiniteness rejection waiting to happen.

## Active Voice Verbs

Each step in a method claim should use an active verb: "applying," "determining," "transmitting," "decoding." Passive constructions ("the signal is received") make it ambiguous which actor performs the step - a divided-infringement nightmare.

## Single-Actor Drafting

Method claims should be drafted so a single actor performs every step. Akamai v. Limelight made divided infringement harder; the easiest defense is to write claims where one entity does everything.

## The "Whereby" Clause - Use Sparingly

"Whereby" clauses describe results. They are usually held to be non-limiting (Texas Instruments v. ITC). Do not rely on a "whereby" clause to distinguish prior art - move the substance into a positive limitation.

## The Length-of-Claim Heuristic

A claim that takes more than a single page in 12-point single-spaced text is almost always too narrow. A claim that takes less than 4 lines is almost always too broad to survive examination. Aim for somewhere between.
