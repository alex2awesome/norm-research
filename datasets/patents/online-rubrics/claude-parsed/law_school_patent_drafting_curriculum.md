# Law School Patent Drafting and Prosecution Curriculum

## Overview

Patent law schools across the US provide structured curricula for patent drafting and prosecution. Programs typically follow three levels:
- Introductory (basic IP Survey)
- Subject-specific (Patents, Copyright, Trademarks)
- Advanced/specialized (Patent Prosecution Practice, Chemical Patent Practice, International IP)

## Patent Claim Drafting Course Topics

### Anatomy of a Claim
- Introductory phrase / preamble
- Transitional phrase ("comprising," "consisting of," etc.)
- Body / elements
- Multiple types: independent, dependent, multiple dependent

### Claim Drafting Choices
- Open vs. closed transitions
- Means-plus-function vs. structural
- Method vs. apparatus vs. system
- Genus vs. species
- Markush groupings
- Negative limitations

## Specification Drafting

The specification encompasses drawings, written description, and claims:
- Title of invention
- Cross-reference to related applications
- Statement regarding federally sponsored research
- Background of the invention
- Brief summary
- Brief description of drawings
- Detailed description
- Claims
- Abstract

## Capstone Projects

Students typically:
- Draft a complete patent application
- Practice claim drafting exercises
- Respond to mock office actions
- Conduct prior art search
- Draft licensing agreements
- Present patent strategy

## Patent Bar Exam Preparation

Most programs prepare students for the USPTO Registration Examination ("Patent Bar"):
- Heavy MPEP focus
- Patent prosecution rules and procedures
- Practice questions on actual patent application elements

## Coursework Topics Beyond Drafting

### Patent Prosecution Strategy
- Office action responses
- Interview practice
- Continuation strategy
- After-final practice
- Appeals (PTAB)

### Patent Law and Policy
- Constitutional/statutory basis
- Enforcement mechanisms
- Patent reform debates
- International harmonization

### Industry-Specific
- Pharma/biotech (Hatch-Waxman, BPCIA, ANDA)
- Software (Alice/Mayo, copyright overlap)
- Mechanical/electrical
- Standard-essential patents (FRAND)

## Practical Skills Emphasized

- Reading patents critically
- Evaluating prior art
- Understanding examiner mindset
- Working with inventors (interviewing for invention disclosure)
- Drafting for litigation defense
- International filing strategy
