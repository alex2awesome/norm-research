# Pharmaceutical Patent Drafting and Hatch-Waxman

## Overview of Hatch-Waxman Framework

The Drug Price Competition and Patent Term Restoration Act of 1984 (Hatch-Waxman Act) created a special regulatory and patent framework for pharmaceuticals.

## Key Patent-Related Provisions

### Patent Term Extensions

A patent term extension adds up to 5 years to a pharmaceutical patent to compensate for time lost during FDA review:
- 50% of the IND (clinical testing) phase + 100% of the NDA (FDA review) phase
- Capped at 5 years total
- Capped at 14 years from FDA approval
- Only ONE patent per approved drug eligible for extension

### Orange Book Listings

NDA sponsors must list in the Orange Book:
- Patents covering the drug substance (active ingredient)
- Patents covering the drug product (formulation)
- Patents on approved methods of use
- Drug delivery system patents reciting the substance/product
- Product-by-process patents where the product is novel

## Types of Patents Filed in Pharma

1. **Composition of matter** (the drug substance/molecule)
2. **Formulation patents** (specific delivery systems)
3. **Method of treatment** patents (specific indications, dosages)
4. **Method of manufacturing** (synthesis routes, intermediates)
5. **Polymorph patents** (specific crystalline forms)
6. **Combination therapy** patents
7. **Salt form** patents
8. **Selection patents** (subgenus from broader genus)
9. **Pediatric exclusivity** patents

## Patent Drafting Considerations

### Composition Claims
- Genus (Markush) for broad protection
- Specific compound for definitiveness
- Pharmaceutically acceptable salts
- Stereoisomers, prodrugs, metabolites

### Method of Treatment Claims
- Specific patient populations
- Specific dosing regimens
- Specific routes of administration
- Specific indications (e.g., "treating breast cancer in HER2+ patients")

## Generic Drug Certification

Under Hatch-Waxman:
- Generic ANDA filers must make Paragraph IV certification challenging Orange Book patents
- Triggers 30-month stay during patent litigation
- 180-day exclusivity for first-filer generic

## Key Patent Drafting Best Practices

- Adequately exemplify the genus for written description
- Provide working examples for unpredictable arts
- Disclose enabling synthesis routes
- Provide pharmacological data to support utility (specific, substantial, credible)
- Consider Markush groups carefully (avoid overly broad scope)
- File continuation applications to capture later-discovered indications

## §112 Issues in Pharma

- **Written description for genus claims**: Must show possession across full scope
- **Enablement (Wands factors)**: Especially demanding in unpredictable arts
- **Amgen v. Sanofi**: Functional genus claims require representative examples + shared quality
- **Utility**: Must be specific, substantial, and credible (no laundry list of speculative uses)
