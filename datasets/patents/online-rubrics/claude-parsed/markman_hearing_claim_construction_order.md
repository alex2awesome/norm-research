# Markman Hearing - Claim Construction Order in Patent Litigation

## What is a Markman Hearing?

A Markman hearing is a proceeding during which a judge interprets the meaning of words and phrases in a patent, ultimately providing what is known as "claim construction." It is held in U.S. District Court for claims dealing with patent infringement.

## Legal Foundation

In Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc. (1996), the Supreme Court held that claim interpretation was a **matter of law** rather than a question of fact for the jury.

This means judges (not juries) decide what claim terms mean.

## Claim Construction Order

After a Markman hearing, the judge issues a claim construction order outlining how disputed terms are to be understood. This order governs:
- All later proceedings
- Trial
- Appeal
- Summary judgment motions

## Evidence Hierarchy in Construction

### Intrinsic Evidence (Highest Priority)
1. **The patent itself** (the claims)
2. **The patent specification**
3. **The patent prosecution history** (file wrapper)

### Extrinsic Evidence (Lower Priority, Disfavored)
- Testimony from the inventor
- Definitions from technical and general dictionaries
- Testimony from experts in the relevant field
- Treatises and learned publications

The Federal Circuit (Phillips v. AWH, 2005) confirmed this hierarchy.

## Construction Standards

### Plain and Ordinary Meaning
Claim terms are given their plain and ordinary meaning to a person of ordinary skill in the art at the time of invention.

### Specification as "Single Best Guide"
The specification is "the single best guide to the meaning of a disputed term."

### Lexicography Exception
A patentee may act as their own lexicographer if they:
- Clearly redefine a term
- Disclaim claim scope
Must be done with reasonable clarity.

## Impact on Litigation

Because claim construction defines the patent's scope, the Markman ruling often determines who wins:
- Favorable interpretation → secures summary judgment or motivates settlement
- Unfavorable interpretation → may eliminate key claims before trial

## Timing

- Most courts conduct Markman hearings before summary judgment
- District courts have discretion on when/how
- Some courts conduct early Markman hearings
- Some include Markman in pretrial proceedings

## Procedure

1. **Joint Claim Construction Statement**: Parties identify disputed terms
2. **Opening Briefs**: Each party submits proposed constructions
3. **Responsive Briefs**: Each party responds to the other's proposals
4. **Hearing**: Oral argument before judge
5. **Order**: Judge issues claim construction order
6. **Appeal**: De novo review at Federal Circuit (often)

## Strategic Considerations

### For Patentees
- Identify potentially disputed terms early
- Provide clear, supportable constructions
- Build constructions on intrinsic evidence
- Avoid creating prosecution history that limits scope

### For Defendants
- Find narrowing constructions in prosecution history
- Identify lexicographic statements in specification
- Argue specification limits scope
- Use prosecution history estoppel arguments

## Phillips v. AWH Tech (2005) Key Holdings

- Specification is the most important guide
- Prosecution history is "intrinsic evidence" with same authority as specification
- Extrinsic evidence (dictionaries) less reliable than intrinsic
- Claims must be read in context of entire specification
- BRI applies during prosecution; ordinary meaning during litigation
