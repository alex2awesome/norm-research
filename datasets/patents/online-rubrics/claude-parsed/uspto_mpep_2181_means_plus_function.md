# MPEP 2181: Means-Plus-Function Claim Limitations

## Overview

MPEP 2181 provides examination guidelines for **35 U.S.C. 112(f)** (and pre-AIA 112, sixth paragraph) "means-plus-function" limitations. These claims receive a narrower interpretation than traditional claim language—limited to the structure disclosed in the specification and equivalents thereof.

## The 3-Prong Analysis

Examiners apply 112(f) when a claim limitation satisfies all three criteria:

**Prong (A): Generic Placeholder or "Means"/"Step"**
The claim uses "means," "step," or a generic substitute term (e.g., "module," "mechanism," "device") that functions as a nonce word without inherent structural meaning. The standard asks whether "words of the claim are understood by persons of ordinary skill in the art to have a sufficiently definite meaning as the name for structure."

**Prong (B): Functional Language**
The term connects to a recited function, typically linked by "for," "configured to," or similar language expressing what the element accomplishes rather than how it achieves results.

**Prong (C): Lack of Sufficient Structure**
The limitation contains no adequate structural details for performing the claimed function. If the claim includes sufficient structure, the presumption that 112(f) applies is rebutted.

## Claim Drafting Requirements & Invocation Rules

**Presumptions:**
- Claims using "means" or "step" *presume* 112(f) applies unless structural modifications rebut this
- Claims *without* "means"/"step" presume 112(f) does *not* apply, unless generic placeholders with functional language overcome this presumption

**To Invoke 112(f):**
- Use "means" or generic placeholder terms
- Couple with functional language ("for," "configured to")
- Avoid adding sufficient structural detail

**To Avoid 112(f):**
- Include specific structural modifiers (e.g., "detent mechanism" versus bare "mechanism")
- Add technical details recognizable to practitioners in the field
- Demonstrate the term has "sufficiently definite" structural meaning

## Specification Requirements

When 112(f) applies, the specification must disclose:

1. **Corresponding Structure:** The specification must identify actual hardware, software, or acts performing the recited function
2. **Clear Association:** The written description must "clearly link or associate" disclosed structure to the claimed function
3. **Algorithm (Software):** For computer-implemented limitations, an explicit algorithm must be disclosed—a general-purpose processor alone is insufficient unless the function is coextensive with basic processor operations (receiving, storing, processing data)

## Key Limitations

- Bare statements that "known techniques can be used" do not suffice
- Structure cannot come solely from incorporated references without specification support
- Multiple functions require disclosure of structure for *each* function
- Implicit or inherent disclosure may satisfy requirements but examiners may require explicit specification amendments for clarity

## Common Nonce Words Triggering 112(f)
"mechanism for," "module for," "device for," "unit for," "component for," "element for," "member for," "apparatus for," "machine for," "system for"
