# 35 USC 102 - Anticipation and Novelty

## What is Anticipation?

A 35 USC 102 rejection ("anticipation rejection") occurs when the USPTO examiner determines that the invention is not novel because all elements of the claimed invention are disclosed in a single prior art reference.

## Single Reference Rule

To reject a claim as anticipated, the disclosure must teach **every element** required by the claim under its broadest reasonable interpretation. Anticipation requires all elements in a claim to be found in a **single piece of prior art**.

## Elements Required for Valid Anticipation

For a valid 102 anticipation rejection:
1. All elements of the claimed invention found in a single prior art reference
2. Prior art must disclose the identical invention "arranged as in the claim"
3. Prior art reference must enable a person of ordinary skill in the art to make and use the invention

## Categories of Prior Art (35 USC 102 - Post-AIA)

A patent application is barred if the claimed invention was:
- **102(a)(1)**: Patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date
- **102(a)(2)**: Described in a U.S. patent or U.S. patent application publication or PCT publication that names another inventor and was filed before the effective filing date

## Grace Period (102(b)(1))

A disclosure made 1 year or less before the effective filing date is not prior art if:
- The disclosure was made by the inventor or joint inventor
- The disclosure was made by another who obtained the subject matter directly or indirectly from the inventor

## Prior Art Exceptions (102(b)(2))

A disclosure under 102(a)(2) is not prior art if:
- It is by another who obtained the subject matter from the inventor
- The subject matter was previously publicly disclosed by the inventor
- Common ownership/joint research agreement (CREATE Act)

## "Inherency" Doctrine

Even if a feature is not explicitly disclosed, it may be **inherent** in the prior art:
- Must necessarily be present (not merely possible)
- Skilled artisan would recognize it
- Inherent disclosure is sufficient for anticipation

## "Enabled" Disclosure Requirement

The prior art must enable the skilled artisan to make and use the invention:
- Mere mention without enablement is insufficient
- Different from §103 (where references can be combined)
- Enabling reference can come from the same source or extrinsic evidence

## How Examiners Handle 102 Rejections

Examiners cite the most relevant prior art reference. The rejection should:
- Identify each claim limitation
- Map each to specific portions of the prior art
- Be specific about where each element is disclosed (column/line, paragraph, figure number)

## Strategic Responses to 102 Rejections

1. **Argue claim language** distinguishing from prior art
2. **Argue absence of element** (not all elements disclosed)
3. **Argue lack of enablement** (reference doesn't enable practice)
4. **Antedate the reference** (swear behind under pre-AIA)
5. **Invoke grace period** (post-AIA inventor disclosures)
6. **Amend claims** to add limitations not in prior art

## Pre-AIA vs Post-AIA Differences

- Pre-AIA: First-to-invent system (effective for applications filed before March 16, 2013)
- Post-AIA: First-inventor-to-file system (March 16, 2013 forward)
- Pre-AIA included subsections (a)-(g)
- Post-AIA simplified to (a) and (b) with grace period exceptions
