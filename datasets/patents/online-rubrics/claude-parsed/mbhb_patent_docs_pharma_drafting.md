# MBHB Patent Docs: Pharmaceutical Patent Drafting Considerations

## Composition Claims - The Core Asset

For a pharmaceutical, the composition-of-matter claim is the keystone. It typically claims a chemical compound or class of compounds. Drafting choices include: claim the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) by structure, by name, by Markush group, or as a salt/polymorph.

## Markush Groupings

A Markush claim recites a closed group of alternatives ("R1 is selected from the group consisting of methyl, ethyl, propyl"). Markush is the standard format for genus claims in chemistry. The group must be sufficiently disclosed to support written description; broad groups need representative species.

## Method of Treatment Claims

In addition to composition claims, file method-of-treatment claims: "A method of treating disease X comprising administering compound Y to a patient in need thereof." These reach pharmacy infringement and inducement liability against marketing.

## Method of Manufacture Claims

Process claims protect the synthesis route. Particularly valuable when the route is novel and difficult to design around. Process claims are subject to special infringement rules under 35 USC 271(g) for products imported into the US.

## Polymorph and Crystal Form Claims

A drug's crystalline form can affect bioavailability. Claim novel polymorphs (Form I, Form II) by powder XRD pattern, melting point, FTIR characteristic peaks. These form the basis of Orange Book listings and Hatch-Waxman litigation.

## Salt Forms

Different pharmaceutically acceptable salts of the same active compound can be separately patentable if novel and non-obvious. Salt selection patents are common follow-ons.

## Dosing Regimen Claims

"A method of treating X comprising administering compound Y at a dose of Z mg per day in a 28-day cycle." These are often patented separately and provide a second wave of protection after composition patents expire.

## Combination Therapy Claims

"A method comprising administering compound A and compound B." Useful when the combination shows synergy or improved tolerability.

## Genus-to-Species Strategy

File a genus application (covering the broad class) early; file species applications (covering the specific lead compound) as the lead emerges. Foreign filing strategy is critical in pharma due to data exclusivity and SPC/PTE considerations.

## Avoid Disparaging the Active

Statements like "compound X has poor bioavailability, but compound Y..." can be used against you in obviousness proceedings if you later seek to broaden coverage to compound X. Stay neutral.

## Supportive Data in the Spec

Pharma patents benefit from in-vitro and in-vivo data in the spec. Examples with measured potency, selectivity, half-life, and toxicity provide both enablement and a foundation for unexpected results arguments.

## Hatch-Waxman and Orange Book

Patents covering an FDA-approved drug must be listed in the FDA's Orange Book. Listed patents trigger the 30-month stay in Hatch-Waxman litigation. Listing decisions affect drafting choices: composition and formulation patents are listable; pure method-of-manufacture patents are not.
