# Harrity Patent Analytics: Patent Quality Metrics for Drafters

## Allowance Rate by Art Unit

Different USPTO art units have dramatically different allowance rates. Some units allow over 80% of applications eventually; others allow under 50%. Knowing the art unit before drafting helps calibrate expectations and inform strategy (e.g., whether to push back hard or amend early).

## First-Action Allowance

A small fraction of applications are allowed on first action - a quality signal for both drafter and case. Aim for clean specs and well-supported claims to maximize this likelihood, even though it remains uncommon (under 10% in most art units).

## Average Number of Office Actions

The mean number of office actions to allowance varies by art unit (typically 1.5 to 3). Drafting choices that reduce this number (clearer claims, narrower initial claim scope) save prosecution costs but may sacrifice claim breadth.

## Examiner Allowance Rate

Within an art unit, individual examiners have very different allowance rates. Knowing the examiner's history (via Patent Bots, LexisNexis PatentAdvisor, or similar tools) informs response strategy: a low-allowance examiner may require appeal; a high-allowance examiner may be persuaded by argument.

## Average Pendency

Pendency from filing to issuance varies from ~12 months (priority track) to ~36+ months (slow art units). Plan continuation strategy and commercial timeline accordingly.

## Forward Citations

Patents that are cited as prior art by many later applications tend to be more valuable. Forward citation count is a lagging quality indicator. Drafting practice that increases citation likelihood: clear technical contribution, accessible writing, appearance in searchable databases.

## Claim Length

Empirical studies (Marco et al. 2019) find that shorter independent claims are correlated with broader scope - and with higher rates of being cited and litigated. The mean independent claim length is around 100-130 words; aim for the lower end without sacrificing definiteness.

## Claim-Word Count vs. Specification-Word Count Ratio

Strong patents tend to have long specs supporting short claims. A spec-to-independent-claim ratio above 100:1 is a quality signal. The opposite (long claims, short spec) often reflects narrow drafting under spec constraints.

## Number of Claims

The USPTO filing fee includes 20 claims with 3 independent. Strong patents typically use the full allotment - more claims provide more scope alternatives and more invalidity-attack targets per dollar.

## Family Size

Patents in larger families (with continuations, divisionals, foreign counterparts) tend to be worth more than standalone patents. Family size is both a cause and an indicator of commercial relevance.

## Maintenance Fee Payment

Patents whose maintenance fees are paid all the way to year 11.5 are commercially relevant - a coarse but useful quality signal. Patents abandoned at the first maintenance deadline (year 3.5) typically were not worth keeping.
