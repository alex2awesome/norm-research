# Wikipedia: Patent Drafting

## Overview

Patent drafting is the preparation of a patent application that complies with patent law and that is well suited to obtain protection for the invention. Effective patent drafting requires understanding both the technology and the legal requirements imposed by patent offices and courts.

## Drafting Goals

A well-drafted patent application:

1. Discloses the invention sufficiently that a person of ordinary skill in the art can make and use it (enablement).
2. Demonstrates the inventor was in possession of the invention as claimed (written description).
3. Defines the invention in claims of appropriate scope, neither too broad (invalid over prior art) nor too narrow (easily designed around).
4. Avoids estoppels that would limit later claim construction or assertion.

## Drafting Process

1. **Invention disclosure interview**: Inventor meets with attorney to explain the invention.
2. **Prior art search**: Identify the closest known references.
3. **Claim drafting**: Draft independent and dependent claims of varying scope.
4. **Specification drafting**: Write specification supporting all claim terms.
5. **Drawings preparation**: Prepare formal patent drawings.
6. **Review and revision**: Multiple drafts; cross-check claims against spec for support.

## Common Drafting Conventions

- **Single sentence claims**: Each claim is a single sentence, however long.
- **Antecedent basis**: First mention of an element uses "a" or "an"; subsequent mentions use "the" or "said."
- **Consistent terminology**: One term per concept throughout the application.
- **Active voice**: Method steps use active verbs.
- **Reference numbers**: Each element in the spec is given a number that ties to the figures.
- **No marketing language**: Avoid superlatives and promotional terms.

## Words to Avoid in Patent Drafting ("Patent Profanity")

- "Essential," "critical," "necessary," "must," "always," "never"
- "The invention is," "the present invention requires"
- "Object of the invention" (creates inferred limitations)
- "Preferred" (may imply non-preferred is excluded)
- "Only" (closes the claim)

## Words That Carry Specific Legal Meaning

- "Comprising": Open transitional phrase.
- "Consisting of": Closed transitional phrase.
- "Consisting essentially of": Semi-closed.
- "Means for": Invokes 35 USC 112(f) in US.
- "About," "approximately," "substantially": Allowed but require spec support to avoid indefiniteness.
- "Plurality": Means "two or more."

## Embodiment Disclosure

Best practice is to disclose multiple embodiments of the invention, including alternative materials, geometries, processes, and ranges. This supports broader claim construction and provides fallback positions during prosecution.

## Claim Hierarchy

A typical strong patent has several independent claims (covering method, system, apparatus, etc.) with dependent claims that narrow on legally meaningful dimensions.

## Continuation Practice

Patent drafting includes planning for continuation applications, which allow the applicant to pursue different claim scopes in subsequent applications based on the same disclosure.
