# USPTO Ex Parte Reexamination

## Overview

Ex parte reexamination is a process whereby the patent owner or a third party requester can have an issued patent reexamined by a Patent Reexamination Specialist to verify that the claimed subject matter is patentable in view of prior art submitted with the request.

## Key Features

- Any person may, at any time during the period of enforceability of a patent, file a request for ex parte reexamination
- Based only on prior art patents or printed publications (not §101, §112)
- Office initially determines if "a substantial new question of patentability" is presented
- If new question presented, reexamination is ordered

## "Substantial New Question of Patentability"

The threshold question:
- Is there a substantial new question?
- Even prior art previously considered can present new question if seen in different light
- Patent owner can contest the new question's substantiality

## Procedure

1. **Request filed** by patent owner or third party
2. **Initial determination** by Central Reexamination Unit (CRU)
3. **If granted**: Reexamination proceeds
4. **Patent owner statement** allowed (optional)
5. **Office actions** issue similar to regular prosecution
6. **Patent owner response** required
7. **Reexamination certificate** issued (cancels, amends, or confirms claims)

## Limited Third-Party Participation

Ex parte reexaminations are initiated by members of the public, but once said members submit their request, they no longer actively participate. The correspondence is strictly between the examiner and the patent owner.

## Cost Comparison

- Ex parte reexamination filing fee: ~$12,000 (USPTO) for standard
- Inter partes review (IPR): ~$19,000-41,500 (with institution fees)
- Post-grant review (PGR): ~$30,000+

## Strategic Use Cases

### When to File Ex Parte Reexamination
- Limited budget for invalidation challenge
- Patent owner doesn't want to broaden claims
- Avoiding adversarial proceeding
- After IPR window has closed
- When prior art is patents/printed publications only

### Limitations
- No discovery
- No oral hearing
- Cannot challenge §101 or §112 issues
- Limited grounds (only §102/§103 based on patents/printed publications)
- Patent owner can amend claims (often used to escape narrow grounds)

## Inter Partes Reexamination (Historical)

Inter partes reexamination existed pre-AIA but was eliminated when the AIA took effect in 2012. It was replaced by:
- Inter Partes Review (IPR)
- Post-Grant Review (PGR)
- Covered Business Method (CBM) review (now expired)

The CRU continues to oversee remaining inter partes reexaminations filed before 2012.

## Reexamination vs. Litigation

Ex parte reexamination can run in parallel with district court litigation:
- Court may stay litigation pending reexamination
- Stay decisions vary by court and circumstances
- USPTO findings can affect litigation
- Statutory estoppel limited (vs. IPR estoppel)

## Practical Considerations

- File reexamination early
- Provide best prior art with detailed explanations
- Consider whether your goal is invalidation or scope reduction
- Patent owner can amend claims, potentially making patent harder to invalidate later
