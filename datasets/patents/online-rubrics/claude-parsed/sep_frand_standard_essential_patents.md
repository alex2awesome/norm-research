# Standard Essential Patents (SEPs) and FRAND Licensing

## What Are SEPs and FRAND?

A **Standard Essential Patent (SEP)** is a patent that protects an invention essential to the implementation of a particular technology standard.

**FRAND** stands for: Fair, Reasonable, And Non-Discriminatory.

## The FRAND Framework

The FRAND framework obligates companies that own SEPs to license their technologies under conditions that are fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory.

The framework seeks to balance:
- SEP owners' interests in recouping R&D investments
- Access to standardized technologies by implementers

## Why FRAND Matters

Industries relying on standardized technologies (telecom, video codecs, WiFi):
- Many companies contribute to standards
- Cumulative royalty rates must be sustainable
- Hold-up problem: SEP owner threatens injunctions to extract supra-FRAND royalties
- Hold-out problem: Implementer refuses to negotiate in good faith

## Determining FRAND Royalties

Courts and arbitrators apply two primary methodologies:

### Top-Down Approach
- Allocates a per-unit royalty based on SEP owner's proportionate share of all essential patents
- Cumulative reasonable royalty divided by patent count
- Often produces lower royalties

### Comparable License Approach
- Uses arm's-length licenses for comparable portfolios
- Excludes licenses affected by hold-up pressure
- Often produces higher royalties

## SEP Drafting Considerations

For patents that may become SEPs:
- Anticipate claim chart mapping to standard
- Use standard's terminology
- Cover both upstream (chipset) and downstream (device) implementations
- Capture mandatory features of the standard
- Avoid optional features (less likely to be essential)

## Licensing Negotiation Best Practices

### For SEP Owners
- Provide claim charts showing essentiality
- Offer FRAND terms early
- Document negotiation in good faith
- Consider portfolio licensing
- Use established arbitration processes

### For Implementers
- Engage in good faith negotiations
- Counter-offer with FRAND terms
- Document negotiation process
- Consider portfolio license discounts
- Use standard development organizations (SDOs)

## SDO Disclosure Requirements

Standards Development Organizations (e.g., ETSI, IEEE, ITU):
- Members must disclose essential patents
- Must commit to license on FRAND terms
- Failure to disclose may render patents unenforceable

## Hold-Up vs. Hold-Out

### Hold-Up
SEP owner seeks above-FRAND royalties by threatening injunction:
- Courts limit injunctive relief for SEPs
- Antitrust scrutiny in major jurisdictions
- Apple v. Motorola, Microsoft v. Motorola precedents

### Hold-Out
Implementer refuses to negotiate in good faith:
- Courts allow injunction if implementer is unwilling licensee
- Huawei v. ZTE (CJEU 2015) - process for negotiation
- Sisvel v. Haier (German precedent)

## Major SEP Cases and Disputes

- **Microsoft v. Motorola**: Top-down methodology, breach of contract
- **Apple v. Samsung**: SEP non-availability for injunctive relief
- **Huawei v. ZTE (CJEU)**: Established negotiation framework in EU
- **Unwired Planet v. Huawei (UK)**: Global FRAND license terms

## SEP Litigation Strategies

### For Patent Owners
- Establish essentiality with claim charts
- File counter-claims against unwilling licensees
- Seek licensing in established jurisdictions (UK, China)
- Use international arbitration

### For Implementers
- Challenge essentiality
- Seek anti-suit injunctions
- Use IPR/PGR to challenge validity
- Engage in good faith negotiations to avoid injunction

## Recent Developments

- Increased use of arbitration for FRAND determination
- China's role as FRAND adjudicator
- Anti-anti-suit injunctions
- USPTO/DOJ/NIST joint policy statement on SEPs
- EU SEP regulation proposal
