# USPTO Patent Quality Chat: Examiner Perspective on Application Quality

## What Examiners Look For First

When opening a new application, examiners typically read in this order: (1) the abstract, (2) the independent claims, (3) the figures, (4) the detailed description as it relates to the independent claims. A clear, accessible abstract and self-contained independent claims set the tone.

## Top Sources of Wasted Examiner Time

(1) Antecedent basis errors that require 112(b) rejections; (2) inconsistent terminology that requires the examiner to figure out what the applicant means; (3) claims that recite features not described in the spec; (4) unnecessarily long claims with restatements of the same concept; (5) figures that do not match the spec's reference numbers.

## Examiners Want Clean Antecedent Basis

Use "a" or "an" for first mention; "the" for subsequent references. Be consistent. Antecedent basis errors are some of the easiest 112(b) rejections to make and avoid.

## Consistent Terminology

If a component is called "the housing" in claim 1, do not call it "the body" in claim 2 or "the case" in the spec. Pick one term per concept and use it everywhere.

## Clear Independent Claims

Independent claims should be self-contained: a reader should be able to understand the invention from the independent claim alone. Avoid claims that require the reader to look up specialized terms in the spec.

## Specification Should Walk Through the Claims

The detailed description should make it clear, paragraph by paragraph, where each claim element is described. The applicant can help the examiner by structuring the spec to mirror the claim sequence.

## Figures Tie It Together

Each element with a reference number in the spec should appear in at least one figure. Each figure should be clearly labeled. Reference numbers should be consistent across figures.

## What Examiners Find Helpful in Office Action Responses

(1) Direct engagement with the cited references - quote the passage, explain why it does not teach the claim element; (2) markup-format amendments per 37 CFR 1.121; (3) brief, focused remarks rather than padded arguments; (4) examiner interview before filing complex amendments.

## What Examiners Find Unhelpful

(1) Arguments that the examiner misunderstood the prior art without specific citations to where in the prior art; (2) accusations of bias or unprofessional conduct; (3) restating arguments from the original spec; (4) repeating the same argument across multiple rejections without tailoring.

## The Goal Is Mutual Understanding

Examiners and applicants are not adversaries. The examination process works best when both sides communicate clearly about what the claim covers and what the prior art teaches. A 15-minute interview often achieves more than three rounds of written correspondence.

## Quality Indicators

The USPTO Patent Quality Chat series consistently emphasizes: clear claims, well-supported amendments, consistent terminology, accurate figures, and engagement with the substance of the office action.
