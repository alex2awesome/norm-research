# EPO Article 84 EPC - Clarity Claim Requirements

## Overview

Article 84 EPC requires that claims shall define the matter for which protection is sought, and they shall be **clear and concise** and be **supported by the description**.

## Purpose and Rationale

Claims must be clear for the sake of legal certainty, as their purpose is to enable the protection conferred by the patent to be determined. The rationale is to ensure that the public is not left in any doubt as to which subject-matter is covered by the patent.

## Key Clarity Requirements

The EPC requires that:
- Claims must be clear (the claim wording cannot be obscure)
- Claims must define the matter for which protection is sought in terms of the **technical features** of the invention
- Clarity must be apparent to the person skilled in the art **from the wording of the claim alone**

Relative or similar terms such as "thin", "wide" or "strong" constitute potentially unclear elements due to context-dependent meanings.

## Support by Description

To meet the requirement of Article 84 EPC that claims be supported by the description:
- The adaptation of the description to amended claims must be performed carefully
- Inconsistencies between claims and description/drawings could render the scope of claims unclear
- Description-claim consistency is mandatory after amendment

## Limitations in Opposition Proceedings

Lack of compliance with Article 84 EPC may not be used as a ground of opposition. However, compliance may be examined when, and to the extent that, an amendment introduces non-compliance with Article 84 EPC.

## Common Article 84 Objections

- Functional features lacking technical specificity
- Result-to-be-achieved language without structure
- Vague references and undefined terms
- Inconsistencies with the description
- Two-part form not respected (Rule 43(1) - prior art portion + characterizing portion)
- Multiple independent claims in same category (Rule 43(2))
