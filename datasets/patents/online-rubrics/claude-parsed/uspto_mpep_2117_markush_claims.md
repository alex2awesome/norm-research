# Markush Claims - Chemical Patent Drafting

## What is a Markush Claim?

A "Markush grouping" is a style of claim drafting by which the applicant recites alternate species of a genus, often used in chemical patent claim drafting. A Markush structure is a representation of a group of chemical compounds that share a common core but differ in one or more variable groups (e.g., R1, R2).

## Key Advantages

Markush structures allow inventors to claim a wide range of structurally related compounds under a single patent claim. A Markush claim conserves writing additional claims, which is financially important because standard filing fees include only twenty total claims.

## Format and Structure

Example format: "A compound of the formula [Core Structure] wherein R1 is selected from the group consisting of [List of Substituents]."

This format provides a flexible framework by allowing the inventor to claim not just one specific compound, but a family of compounds.

## Legal Requirements for Validity

A Markush grouping is ordinarily proper if all the members of the group:
1. Belong to a recognized class (whether physical, chemical, or art recognized)
2. Are disclosed in the specification to possess at least one property in common which is mainly responsible for their function in the claimed invention

To be valid, Markush claims must meet the requirements of 35 U.S.C. § 112, including written description, enablement, and definiteness.

## Important Drafting Considerations

- The term "consisting of" is closed language that limits the claim to only the listed elements
- "Comprising" is open-ended and allows for additional, unlisted elements
- Each substituent in the Markush group should be well-defined, ideally with examples or definitions in the specification
- Overly broad or vague descriptions risk invalidation due to lack of clarity or enablement
- Members must share a "single structural similarity" and "common use"

## Improper Markush Rejections

Examiners may reject a Markush claim as improper if:
- The members do not share a common structural feature
- The members do not share a common use
- The grouping appears to be merely a list of disparate species
