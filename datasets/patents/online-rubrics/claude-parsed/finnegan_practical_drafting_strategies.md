# Finnegan - Practical Drafting Considerations and Strategies

## Core Principle

The specification can rarely be changed during prosecution, which means the applicant only gets one opportunity to explain how the invention works. A carefully drafted specification is particularly important when a federal district judge interprets the patent claims, for the claims must be read in light of the specification.

## Key Drafting Strategies

### 1. Use Consistent Terminology

Use consistent terminology throughout. For example, the terms "pharmaceutical formulation" and "pharmaceutical composition" could easily be used interchangeably, but an EPO Examiner may not allow embodiments relating to the formulation to be combined with embodiments relating to the composition (arguably different things).

### 2. Plan for Future Claim Combinations

Consider filing with multiply dependent claims or include a "numbered embodiments" section to provide basis for combinations in the future.

### 3. Draft With Litigation in Mind

- Draft with an eye toward enforcement
- Consider invalidation proceedings during drafting
- Avoid statements that limit claim scope unintentionally
- Avoid characterizing the prior art in ways that admit prior art

### 4. Avoid Patent Profanity

Common terms that can limit claim scope:
- "the invention is" / "the present invention"
- "must" / "essential" / "critical" / "necessary"
- "always" / "every" / "only"
- "preferred" (when not just identifying preferred embodiment)

### 5. Provide Strong Written Description

- Multiple embodiments
- Examples covering breadth of claims
- Pictures, diagrams, formulas
- Definitions for ambiguous terms

## Standard-Essential Patents (SEPs) Considerations

For standard-essential patents:
- Anticipate claim chart mapping to standard
- Use standard's terminology where possible
- Cover both upstream and downstream implementations
- Capture mandatory features of the standard

## Fintech Patent Strategy

For financial technology patents:
- Focus on technical improvements (not financial improvements)
- Tie methods to specific computer architecture
- Avoid pure financial transactions claims (Alice risk)
- Document network/security/efficiency improvements

## Added Matter Drafting Tips (EPO Particular Concerns)

- Provide explicit basis for every claim feature
- Avoid intermediate generalizations
- Consider numbered embodiments for combinations
- Write originally-filed claims that can be re-used as fallback positions
- Be careful with adjectives — "low-power" may not support claim to "less than 5 watts"
