# MPEP 2163 Guidelines: Written Description Requirement Under 35 USC 112(a)

## Core Standard

The fundamental test asks whether "the specification conveys with reasonable clarity to those skilled in the art that, as of the filing date sought, the inventor was in possession of the invention as now claimed."

An applicant demonstrates possession by describing the claimed invention with all its limitations using "words, structures, figures, diagrams, and formulas that fully set forth the claimed invention."

## Key Principles

**Separate from Enablement**: The written description requirement is "separate and distinct from the enablement requirement" and serves to demonstrate the inventor actually possessed the claimed subject matter.

**Presumption of Adequacy**: There is a presumption that adequate written description exists when the application is filed. The examiner bears the initial burden of presenting evidence why a skilled artisan would not recognize the description supports the claims.

**Not Literal**: The specification need not use identical language (in haec verba). However, "the description need not be in identical words to be sufficient" if a skilled artisan would understand the inventor possessed the invention.

## Possession May Be Shown Through:

- **Actual reduction to practice**: Constructing an embodiment meeting all claim limitations
- **Drawings/chemical formulas**: Detailed structural depictions showing completeness
- **Identifying characteristics**: Structure, physical/chemical properties, functional characteristics with established structure-function correlation, or combinations thereof

## Special Cases

**Single Species/Embodiment Claims**: Must show complete structure, reduction to drawings, or sufficient identifying characteristics distinguishing it from other materials.

**Genus Claims**: Requires disclosure of a "representative number of species" reflecting the genus variation, unless one species alone demonstrates the inventor possessed the broader genus (fact-dependent).

**Biological Materials**: May rely on deposits under 37 CFR 1.801 et seq., but deposits do not substitute for written description—written disclosure must be as complete as possible.

**Means-Plus-Function Claims**: Must disclose adequate corresponding structure, materials, or acts performing the claimed function. Indefinite means-plus-function claims also lack written description.

## Amendment/Priority Scenarios

**New or Amended Claims**: Each limitation must be "expressly, implicitly, or inherently supported in the originally filed disclosure."

**Priority/Benefit Claims**: Subject matter must be disclosed in the manner required by 35 USC 112(a) in the earlier-filed application to receive that filing date.

## Critical Limitations

- Claims omitting elements described as essential to the invention lack adequate description
- Broad genus claims cannot be supported by disclosing only one species in unpredictable arts unless substantial evidence shows the inventor possessed the entire genus
- Functional description alone typically fails absent established structure-function correlation
- Generic disclosure of a broad class does not necessarily support specific compounds or species within it

## Examiner Methodology

When making a written description rejection, the examiner must:

1. Identify specific claim limitations lacking support
2. Establish a prima facie case explaining why skilled artisans would not recognize possession
3. Base findings on substantive law, not procedural guidelines
4. Respond thoroughly to applicant arguments and evidence upon reply
5. Consider the nature of the invention and state of the art

A general assertion of "unpredictability in the art" alone is insufficient to support rejection.
