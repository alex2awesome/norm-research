# Patent Abstract Drafting Requirements (37 CFR 1.72)

## Word Length Requirements

The abstract should be as concise as the disclosure permits, preferably 50 to 150 words. The abstract can have **no more than 150 words** and should be no longer than a single paragraph.

## Physical Format Requirements

- The abstract must be in narrative form
- Generally limited to a single paragraph on a separate sheet
- Must commence on a separate sheet
- Preferably follows the claims
- Heading: "Abstract" or "Abstract of the Disclosure"

## Content Guidelines

The abstract should:
- Describe the disclosure sufficiently to assist readers in deciding whether there is a need to consult the full patent
- NOT contain statements on the alleged merits or value of the claimed invention
- NOT contain statements on speculative application
- NOT repeat information given in the title
- NOT use phrases that can be implied: "The disclosure concerns," "The disclosure describes," "This invention relates to"
- NOT use claim-style phraseology like "means" and "said"

## What to Include

A good abstract should include:
- A statement of what the invention is (technical area)
- The technical features of the invention
- The principal use of the invention
- A short summary of the technical disclosure

## Rejection Risks

The abstract will be rejected if:
- It is not on its own separate page
- It is fewer than 50 words
- It is greater than 150 words
- Contains "alleged merits" language
- Uses prohibited phrases

## Best Practices

- Write the abstract last, after claims are finalized
- Mirror the broadest independent claim language (without claim formality)
- Use clear technical terms searchable by examiners and the public
- Include the field of technology in opening words (helps in searches)
- Avoid trademark designations and trade names

## Function of the Abstract

The abstract is used by:
- Patent searchers and examiners
- The public to evaluate technical disclosure
- Classification authorities
- Database indexers
