# USPTO Post-Grant Review (PGR)

## What is PGR?

Post-grant review (PGR) is an adversarial proceeding before the Patent Trial and Appeals Board (PTAB) of the USPTO, in which a petitioner challenges the patentability of one or more claims in a recently-issued US patent.

PGR was created by the America Invents Act (AIA) and became available in 2012.

## Timing Requirements

A PGR can only be filed within **9 months** from the grant of a patent or issuance of a reissue patent.

## Who Can File

Any person other than the patentee may file a petition for post-grant review. The petition must identify the real party in interest.

## Grounds for Challenge

PGR allows a third party to challenge the validity of a patent under **all statutory grounds** for invalidity:
- Patent ineligible subject matter (§101)
- Novelty (§102)
- Obviousness (§103)
- Written description (§112)
- Enablement (§112)
- Indefiniteness (§112)
- Double patenting

This is broader than Inter Partes Review (IPR), which is limited to §102/§103 based on patents/printed publications.

## Institution Standard

A PGR may be instituted upon a showing that:
"It is more likely than not that at least one claim challenged is unpatentable"
OR
"The petition raises a novel or unsettled legal question that is important to other patents or patent applications"

## Timeline

If instituted, a final determination by the Board issues within:
- 1 year of institution
- Extendable by 6 months for good cause

## Costs

- Initial filing fee: ~$12,000-15,000
- Institution fee: ~$18,000-22,000 (if PGR is instituted)
- Total fees can exceed $30,000-40,000

## Estoppel

PGR creates significant estoppel:
- After final written decision, petitioner cannot challenge in:
  - Civil action (district court litigation)
  - ITC proceeding
  - Subsequent USPTO proceeding
- For grounds that were raised OR REASONABLY COULD HAVE BEEN RAISED

This is broader than IPR estoppel.

## Strategic Use Cases

### Why PGR Instead of IPR
- Patent contains §101 or §112 issues
- Want broader grounds for challenge
- Have prior art that may not qualify for IPR
- Within 9-month window

### Why IPR Instead of PGR
- Beyond 9-month window (IPR available later)
- Estoppel concerns
- Lower costs
- More established practice

## PGR vs. IPR vs. Ex Parte Reexamination

| Feature | PGR | IPR | Ex Parte Reexam |
|---------|-----|-----|-----------------|
| Timing | <9 months from grant | After 9 months | Anytime during enforceability |
| Grounds | All §101, §102, §103, §112 | §102, §103 only | §102, §103 only |
| Prior Art | Any | Patents/Publications | Patents/Publications |
| Discovery | Limited | Limited | None |
| Cost | $30K-100K | $50K-500K | $12K (USPTO) + atty fees |
| Estoppel | Strong | Moderate | None for petitioner |
| Outcome | Final written decision | Final written decision | Reexamination certificate |

## When PGR Was Most Used

- For first-to-file applications (post-AIA)
- Software/business method patents
- Pharma patents with §112 issues
- Bringing eligibility challenges efficiently

## Practical Considerations

- 9-month window is tight; requires preparation in advance
- Institution rate is decision point (gates access to full review)
- Consider parallel district court strategy
- Evaluate estoppel implications carefully
