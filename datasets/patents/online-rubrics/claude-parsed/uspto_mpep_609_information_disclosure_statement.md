# Information Disclosure Statement (IDS) and Duty of Disclosure

## What is an Information Disclosure Statement?

An Information Disclosure Statement (IDS) refers to a submission of relevant background art or information to the USPTO by an applicant for a patent during the patent prosecution process.

## The Duty of Disclosure (37 CFR 1.56)

There is a duty on all patent applicants to disclose relevant art or background information that the applicant is aware of and that may be relevant to the patentability of the applicant's invention.

While there is no duty to perform a search of relevant art, those associated with filing or prosecuting patent applications have a duty to disclose to the USPTO all known prior art or other information that may be "material" in determining patentability.

## Who Has the Duty?

The duty falls upon:
- The inventors
- The applicant or assignee
- Each patent firm/attorney/agent connected with the patent application
- Each individual associated with filing/prosecution

## What Must Be Disclosed?

Information typically includes:
- Other issued patents
- Published patent applications
- Scientific journal articles
- Books
- Magazine articles
- Any other published material relevant to the invention
- Office actions in related/foreign applications
- Litigation involving related patents
- Material from competitor analysis

Material is disclosed irrespective of country or language of publication.

## Materiality Standard

Information is material if a reasonable examiner would consider it important in deciding whether to grant a patent.

## Consequences of Non-Disclosure

If a patent applicant, with deceptive intent, fails to submit material prior art known to the applicant, then any resulting patent may be declared **unenforceable** because of inequitable conduct (Therasense v. Becton Dickinson, Fed. Cir. 2011).

The Therasense standard requires:
- **Materiality**: But-for materiality (PTO would not have allowed claim if it had known)
- **Intent**: Single most reasonable inference must be intent to deceive

## When to File IDS

The duty of candor is an ongoing obligation:
- File initial IDS within 3 months of filing or before first office action
- File supplemental IDS as new prior art is discovered
- Late-filed IDS may require fees and certifications
- Final fee can be paid only with allowable IDS submissions

## IDS Strategic Considerations

- Cite art from related applications (PCT, foreign counterparts)
- Cite art cited in litigation involving similar technology
- Avoid burying examiner with marginally relevant art (recent USPTO concerns)
- Provide concise descriptions of foreign-language documents
