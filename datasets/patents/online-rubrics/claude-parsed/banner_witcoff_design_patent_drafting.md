# Banner & Witcoff: Design Patent Drafting Fundamentals

## Design Patents Protect Ornamental Appearance, Not Function

A design patent protects the ornamental appearance of an article of manufacture (35 USC 171). Functional features are not protectable through design patents - utility patents are the route for functional aspects. The line between ornamental and functional is sometimes contested.

## The Single Claim

A design patent has exactly one claim: "The ornamental design for [article], as shown and described." All scope is conveyed by the figures and the explanatory text.

## The Figures Are Everything

Drawings are the principal means of disclosing the claimed design. Required views typically include: front, rear, top, bottom, left side, right side, and perspective. Each view must be consistent with all others.

## Solid Lines vs. Broken Lines

Solid lines depict claimed design features. Broken lines depict unclaimed environmental features (the rest of the product, or context the design is used in). Strategic use of broken lines is a key drafting decision: more broken lines = broader claim scope.

## Partial Designs

You can claim part of an article by drawing only that part in solid lines and the rest in broken lines. This is powerful for designs where only a specific feature is novel (e.g., the curve of a phone bezel, the shape of a car grille).

## Multiple Embodiments per Application

A single design patent application can include multiple embodiments only if they are patentably indistinct - usually variations of the same design (e.g., the same shape in different colors). Otherwise file separate applications or use a divisional.

## Ordinary Observer Test

Infringement is determined by whether an ordinary observer would deem the accused design substantially the same as the claimed design (Egyptian Goddess v. Swisa, Fed. Cir. 2008, en banc). Drafting choice: include or exclude features in solid lines based on whether they affect the overall visual impression.

## Functionality Defense

Accused infringers often argue the claimed design is dictated by function. Best defense: in the spec or by selection of claimed features, focus on aspects that have alternative functional implementations - making clear the design choices are aesthetic, not functional necessities.

## No Spec Discussion of Function

In contrast to utility patents, design patent specs say very little. The figures do the work. Avoid discussing function in the spec or describing why the design is the way it is - functionality discussion can support a functionality defense.

## Continuation Practice for Design

File design continuations to capture additional broken-line strategies as the product evolves and as competitor knockoffs emerge.

## Foreign Design Protection

The Hague Agreement allows international design filings through a single application. Many countries use registered design (not examined) systems with broader scope considerations than US design patents.
