# WIPO International Patent Drafting Training Program

## Overview

WIPO – in collaboration with FICPI (International Federation of Intellectual Property Attorneys) – offers a comprehensive training program leading to a Certificate upon passing a final examination.

## Program Structure (Four Phases)

### Phase 1 - Drafting Basics
- 10-week distance learning course
- Fundamentals of drafting claims and the specification
- Tutor-led discussions and exercises

### Phase 2 - Geneva Workshop
- 5-day international hands-on workshop at WIPO HQ in Geneva, Switzerland
- Highly qualified instructors
- Focus on technology-relevant patent drafting skills
- Practices in different jurisdictions

### Phase 3 - Mentoring
- Follow-up mentoring during 12 weeks
- Advanced practical exercises
- Designed to address participants' technological fields

### Phase 4 - Final Online Evaluation
- Certificate awarded upon passing

## Key Drafting Topics Covered

- Claim interpretation principles
- Independent and dependent claim structures
- Specification writing for enablement and written description
- Drawing requirements
- Drafting for multiple jurisdictions
- PCT international filing strategy
- Common claim drafting pitfalls
- Mechanical, electrical, chemical, biotech-specific drafting
- AI/software invention drafting

## Eligibility

Open to all eligible individuals interested in improving their drafting skills:
- Patent agents and patent agent candidates
- Inventors with technical backgrounds
- IP law students

## Fee Structure

- Standard registration fee: USD 1000
- Students from developed countries: USD 800 (20% discount)
- Developing countries/transition economies: USD 400 (60% discount)
- Least developed countries: USD 200 (80% discount)

## Common Drafting Pitfalls Taught

1. Ambiguous claim language
2. Missing antecedent basis
3. Improper transitional phrases
4. Insufficient enablement
5. Lack of written description support
6. Improper means-plus-function language
7. Patent profanity (limiting language)
8. Failure to claim multiple invention aspects
9. Inconsistent terminology between specification and claims
10. Failure to disclose best mode (US only)

## Comparative Drafting Approach

The program emphasizes understanding differences between:
- USPTO practice (broadest reasonable interpretation, written description doctrine)
- EPO practice (problem-solution approach, strict added matter rules)
- JPO practice (specific support requirements)
- CNIPA practice (technical solution approach)
- Other regional offices

## Practice-Oriented Methodology

Designed as practice-oriented training so inventors and those providing services to them can acquire technical skills and knowledge to:
- Prepare and file successful patent applications
- Draft all-important claims
- Adapt drafting style across jurisdictions
- Anticipate common rejection patterns
