# MPEP 2106 Patent Subject Matter Eligibility

## Two Criteria for Subject Matter Eligibility

1. **Statutory Category**: Claims must fall within one of four categories—processes, machines, manufactures, or compositions of matter per 35 U.S.C. § 101.

2. **Judicial Exception Requirement**: Claims must not be directed solely to judicial exceptions (abstract ideas, laws of nature, natural phenomena) unless they include "significantly more" than the exception itself.

## Step 1: Statutory Categories

**Process**: A series of acts or steps performed to transform materials into a different state or result.

**Machines**: Concrete devices combining mechanical parts and powers to produce a specific effect.

**Manufactures**: Tangible articles given new form, quality, or properties through human effort.

**Compositions of Matter**: Combinations of two or more substances, whether chemical unions or mechanical mixtures.

**Non-Statutory Subject Matter** includes:
- Intangible information or data lacking physical form
- Software per se without structural limitations
- Transitory signal transmissions

## Step 2A: Judicial Exception Analysis

### Prong One: Does the Claim Recite a Judicial Exception?

Examiners identify whether claims set forth or describe:
- Abstract ideas (enumerated groupings below)
- Laws of nature
- Natural phenomena (products of nature)

Critical distinction: Claims that merely *involve* an exception differ from claims that *recite* one.

### Prong Two: Integration into Practical Application

If Prong One identifies an exception, Prong Two asks whether additional claim elements integrate that exception into a practical application. If yes, the claim proceeds to Pathway B (eligible). If no, Step 2B analysis follows.

## Abstract Idea Groupings

### 1. Mathematical Concepts
Mathematical relationships, formulas/equations, and calculations.

**Examples**:
- Conversion algorithms (binary-coded decimal to binary)
- Numerical formulas and their textual equivalents
- Statistical calculations and computational operations

### 2. Certain Methods of Organizing Human Activity

**Fundamental Economic Principles/Practices**:
- Hedging, insurance, risk mitigation
- Price optimization
- Settlement processes

**Commercial/Legal Interactions**:
- Contracts and agreements
- Advertising and marketing activities
- Business relationships and transactions

**Managing Personal Behavior/Relationships**:
- Social activities (voting, information sharing)
- Educational instruction
- Rules and guidelines for human conduct

### 3. Mental Processes

Concepts performable in the human mind without technological aid, including observations, evaluations, judgments, and opinions.

## Step 2B: Inventive Concept Analysis

When claims are directed to judicial exceptions (Step 2A: YES), Step 2B determines whether additional elements provide "significantly more" than the exception.

**Insufficient Factors**:
- Reciting "useful, concrete, and tangible results"
- Adding generic computer limitations
- Combining multiple exceptions
- Field-of-use restrictions

**Sufficient Factors**:
- Unconventional technical solutions to technological problems
- Meaningful integration improving existing technology
- Non-conventional application of the exception

## Eligibility Pathways

**Pathway A**: Self-evident eligibility for claims clearly within statutory categories without exception concerns.

**Pathway B**: Claims within statutory categories that do not recite judicial exceptions (Step 2A: NO).

**Pathway C**: Claims within statutory categories, directed to exceptions (Step 2A: YES), but containing additional elements amounting to significantly more (Step 2B: YES).

## Superseded Tests

The following approaches no longer control eligibility analysis:
- "Useful, concrete, and tangible result" standard
- "Programmed computer" or "special purpose computer" rationale
- Machine-or-transformation test as standalone analysis
