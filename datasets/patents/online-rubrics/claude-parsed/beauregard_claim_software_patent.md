# Beauregard Claims - Software Patent Drafting

## What is a Beauregard Claim?

A Beauregard claim is a claim to a computer program written in the form of a claim to an article of manufacture: a computer-readable medium on which are encoded, typically, instructions for carrying out a process. Named after the 1995 Federal Circuit decision *In re Beauregard*.

The computer-readable medium that these claims contemplate is typically a floppy disk or CD-ROM, which is why this type of claim is sometimes called a "floppy disk" claim.

## Origin and Legal History

In the past, claims to pure instructions were generally considered not patentable because they were viewed as "printed matter," like a set of instructions written down on paper. In *In re Beauregard*, the Federal Circuit vacated for reconsideration the patent-eligibility of a claim to a computer program encoded in a floppy disk, regarded as an article of manufacture.

## Drafting Guidelines

To be valid, a Beauregard claim must:
- Be directed to a tangible medium
- Contain program instructions or other software-related content
- Be written in a way that complies with 35 U.S.C. § 112
- Specify "non-transitory" computer-readable media to avoid Nuijten signal-based rejection

## Modern Challenges After Alice

Decisions of the Supreme Court leading up to *Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank International* appeared to exclude what amounted to a patent on information from the patent system. In *CyberSource Corp. v. Retail Decisions Inc.*, the Federal Circuit held a method for detecting credit card fraud patent ineligible and then held a corresponding Beauregard claim similarly patent ineligible because it claimed a "mere manipulation or reorganization of data."

If the underlying method claim is not patent-eligible, recasting the claim in Beauregard format will not improve its patent eligibility.

## Best Practice
Always include claims in multiple statutory categories: method, system/apparatus, and computer-readable medium (Beauregard) — to provide flexibility during prosecution and litigation.
