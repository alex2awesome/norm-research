# Request for Continued Examination (RCE) Strategy

## What is an RCE?

A Request for Continued Examination (RCE) is a request by an applicant for continued prosecution after the patent office has issued a "final" rejection or after prosecution "on the merits" has been closed.

An RCE is **not** considered a continuing patent application — rather, prosecution of the pending application is reopened, with the inventor paying an additional filing fee and continuing to argue the case.

## When to File an RCE

An RCE makes sense when:
- The applicant has received a Final Office Action
- The applicant wishes to make further claim amendments and/or arguments
- New evidence is available
- Further dialogue with the examiner could lead to allowance
- New prior art needs to be considered

## Key Strategic Considerations

### Advantages
- Keeps the original application alive, maintaining the priority date
- Avoids the loss of earlier filing benefits
- Provides another chance to engage constructively with the examiner
- Allows submission of new evidence
- Permits more substantial amendments than after-final practice

### When NOT to File an RCE
If two or more RCEs have already been filed, you may have reached the end of the road with a particular examiner. In such cases, a patent appeal may make more sense than filing another RCE.

## Filing Requirements

- Must be filed before the application is abandoned
- Generally before the reply period for a Final Office Action expires (3 months extendable to 6 with fees)
- Must include an amendment, argument, or other response
- Submitting an RCE without a substantive reply may result in dismissal

## Strategic Decision Tree

After Final Office Action, options include:
1. **AFCP 2.0** (After Final Consideration Pilot) - free, limited amendments
2. **Pre-Appeal Brief Conference Request** - 5-page summary, 3 examiners review
3. **Notice of Appeal + Appeal Brief** - PTAB review
4. **RCE** - reopen prosecution, more amendments allowed
5. **Continuation Application** - new application, new claims, new examination

## Cost Considerations

- First RCE: lower fee
- Second and subsequent RCEs: higher fee
- Multiple RCEs may indicate fundamental disagreement with the examiner
- Each RCE delays patent issuance
