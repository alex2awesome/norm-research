# How to Write a Strong Patent Claim: Best Practices

## Key Best Practices

### Focus on Unique Advantages

When drafting claims, focus on the unique advantages and benefits of your invention. Highlighting these aspects can help demonstrate the invention's novelty and non-obviousness.

### Use Clear, Simple Language

Avoid technical jargon or complex sentence structures that might confuse the reader. Each element of the claim should be clearly defined and easy to understand. If your invention involves specialized terminology, define these terms clearly within the claim or in the detailed description.

### Use Positive Language

Avoid using negative limitations or exclusions unless absolutely necessary. Positive language is clearer and less likely to cause confusion. Instead of saying what your invention does not include, specify what it does include.

### Balance Breadth with Specificity

It's important to balance your claims so that they cover a wide range of potential infringements, but they are not so broad that they lose their enforceability.

### Avoid Limiting Language ("Patent Profanity")

Many words should be avoided so as not to unnecessarily limit the scope of claims. Examples of common patent profanity include:
- "necessary"
- "essential"
- "key"
- "every"
- "must"
- "never"
- "only"
- "absolutely"
- "critical"
- "important"
- "preferred"

### Consider Prior Art

Failing to adequately consider existing patents and publications can lead to claims that are not novel or are obvious. This oversight might invalidate your patent or severely limit its scope.

### Maintain Consistency

Consistency in terminology is crucial in patent drafting. Using different terms to refer to the same component or feature within the patent document can cause confusion.

### Use a Layered Claim Structure

Dependent claims offer an extra layer of protection by providing variations of your invention. They inherit all limitations of the claims they depend on, and add further detailed features. If the broadest, independent claim gets invalidated, these narrower claims can still uphold patent protection.

### Get Professional Review

Have your claims reviewed by peers and patent professionals. Fresh eyes can catch inconsistencies, ambiguities, and potential weaknesses that you might have missed.
