# Knobbe Martens Federal Circuit Update: Drafting Implications

## Recurring Federal Circuit Themes

### Specification Lexicography Beats Claim Construction Surprises

The Federal Circuit repeatedly defers to explicit definitions in the specification. **Drafting implication**: For any claim term whose ordinary meaning may not capture your intended scope, define it explicitly. "As used herein, X means Y."

### Disclaimers Must Be Clear and Unmistakable

Statements in the spec or during prosecution can disclaim claim scope only if they are clear and unmistakable. Vague characterizations of prior art are usually not disclaimers. But statements like "the invention requires X" or "in contrast to prior art Y, the invention does not include Y" can be disclaimers. **Drafting implication**: Avoid such statements.

### Method Claims Need Single-Actor Architecture

Direct infringement of a method claim by multiple actors requires direction/control or joint enterprise (Akamai). **Drafting implication**: Draft method claims with a single actor performing all steps. If multiple actors are unavoidable, structure the relationship so one directs the other.

### Functional Language Without Structure Triggers 112(f)

Claim terms like "module configured to," "mechanism for," "system that performs" without structural anchoring may be construed as means-plus-function (Williamson). **Drafting implication**: Use structural terminology - processor, circuit, memory, sensor - and provide concrete corresponding structure in the spec.

### Eligibility Survives With Concrete Technical Improvement

Post-Alice eligibility is more likely when claims recite specific technical mechanisms tied to a concrete improvement in computer functioning (Enfish, McRO, Visual Memory). **Drafting implication**: For software patents, anchor the spec in a specific technical improvement - reduced latency, improved memory utilization, novel data structure - not a business problem.

### Written Description for Genus Claims

For genus claims in unpredictable arts (biotech, chemistry), Amgen v. Sanofi (Sup. Ct. 2023, on enablement) and Ariad Pharmaceuticals v. Eli Lilly (Fed. Cir. 2010, on written description) require disclosure of representative species and a unifying structure. **Drafting implication**: Disclose multiple species; identify common structural features.

### Negative Limitations Need Spec Support

Santarus v. Par (Fed. Cir. 2012) requires that negative claim limitations ("wherein the device is free of X") be supported by an explicit spec disclosure that X is or may be excluded. **Drafting implication**: If you might want to claim absence of a feature, explicitly state in the spec that the feature can be omitted.

### Range Claims Need Range Disclosure

In re Wertheim and progeny require that claimed ranges be supported by spec disclosure of the range or at least endpoints. **Drafting implication**: Disclose multiple ranges and endpoints in the spec.

### Continuation Practice Requires Pendency

To file a continuation, the parent must be pending at the time of filing. **Drafting implication**: Calendar the issue fee deadline of the parent and decide before that whether to file a continuation.

### Inventorship Errors Are Curable but Risky

Inventorship errors can be corrected under 35 USC 256 absent deceptive intent. But errors discovered during litigation can be expensive to cure. **Drafting implication**: Audit inventorship at filing, allowance, and any major amendment.
