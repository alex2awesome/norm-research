# Nolo: Patent Application Basics for Inventors

## What a Utility Patent Covers

A utility patent protects new, useful, and non-obvious processes, machines, manufactures, and compositions of matter. The patent gives the owner the right to exclude others from making, using, selling, offering to sell, or importing the patented invention for 20 years from filing.

## Patentability Requirements

(1) **Patentable subject matter**: Process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter (35 USC 101). Excludes laws of nature, natural phenomena, and abstract ideas.
(2) **Utility**: The invention must have a practical use.
(3) **Novelty**: Not anticipated by a single piece of prior art (35 USC 102).
(4) **Non-obviousness**: Not obvious to a person of ordinary skill in the art (35 USC 103).

## Document the Invention Early

Maintain a dated, signed inventor's notebook. Record the conception of the invention, reductions to practice, and steps taken to develop the invention. Although the AIA moved the US to first-to-file, documentation still helps establish inventorship and supports prior-user-rights defenses.

## Prior Art Search

Before filing, search the USPTO patent database, Google Patents, foreign patent databases (Espacenet, J-PlatPat, KIPRIS), and academic literature. The search informs what is novel and what claim scope is realistic.

## Provisional Applications

A provisional application is a 12-month placeholder. It is not examined and never matures into a patent. The 12 months can be used to refine the invention, raise funding, or test the market. The non-provisional must be filed within 12 months and only gets priority for what is actually disclosed in the provisional.

## Non-Provisional Application Components

- Title
- Abstract
- Background
- Summary
- Brief Description of Drawings
- Detailed Description
- Claims
- Drawings
- Oath/Declaration
- Information Disclosure Statement

## Filing Fees

For small entity (most individuals and small businesses):
- Filing fee: ~$80
- Search fee: ~$370
- Examination fee: ~$430
- Issue fee: ~$600
- Maintenance fees: ~$800 / $1,800 / $3,700 at 3.5/7.5/11.5 years

Large entity fees are double; micro entity is half of small entity.

## Patent Prosecution

After filing, an examiner reviews the application. Most applications receive at least one office action. The applicant responds with arguments and/or amendments. The process continues until allowance or final rejection.

## Hire a Patent Attorney or Agent

Patent law is highly specialized. Patent attorneys (J.D. plus admission to the patent bar) and patent agents (technical degree plus admission to the patent bar) are licensed by the USPTO to draft and prosecute patent applications.

## After the Patent Issues

Maintenance fees must be paid to keep the patent in force. The patent owner can sue for infringement, license the patent, or sell it. Patent rights are territorial; US patents do not protect outside the US.

## International Protection

Within 12 months of US filing, the inventor can file in other countries claiming priority. The PCT route delays the country-by-country decision by 30 months from priority.
