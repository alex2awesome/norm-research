# Empirical Validity of Litigated Patents

Allison & Lemley's 1998 study established the foundational empirical baseline for patent invalidity rates and identified specific patent-characteristics correlated with invalidation.

## Key Empirical Findings

**Invalidation Rate**: Approximately **46% of fully litigated patents are held invalid** in federal court between 1989 and 1996.

**Prior Art Citation Effect**: Courts are **more likely to invalidate patents based on uncited prior art** than on prior art that was disclosed to the PTO during prosecution. This implies:
- Examiners' search-and-cite behavior matters substantially for patent durability
- Robust IDS submissions during prosecution reduce post-grant invalidation risk
- Patents with thin prior-art records are quality-vulnerable

**Validity by Ground**: Among invalidated patents:
- Anticipation (102) and obviousness (103) account for the majority of invalidations
- Indefiniteness, lack of enablement, and inequitable conduct also contribute
- Subject-matter eligibility was a smaller share in 1989-1996 (pre-Bilski/Mayo/Alice)

## Quality Markers Derived from These Findings

### Pre-Grant Markers

**Robust Prior-Art Search**: A patent whose examiner conducted independent search and cited diverse prior art (foreign, NPL, multiple classes) is more likely to survive validity challenge.

**Thorough IDS**: Applicant disclosure of all material art reduces invalidation risk and avoids inequitable conduct issues.

**Substantive Examiner Engagement**: Multiple office actions with substantive 102/103/112 rejections forge a stronger validity record than a single allowance.

### Post-Grant Markers

**Cited-Art Dominance**: A patent invalidated based on cited art is unusual; one invalidated based on uncited art is the norm. Quality assessment should weight whether the universe of art considered during prosecution was comprehensive.

**Continuation Vulnerability**: Continuation patents drafted to capture later-developed competitor products show higher invalidation rates due to written-description and intervening-rights challenges.

**Litigation History**: Patents that survive multiple validity challenges develop "battle-tested" status; patents on first litigation are more likely to fall.

## Industry-Specific Patterns (Supplemental Data)

Subsequent Allison-Lemley work documents:

- Pharmaceutical patents survive validity challenges at higher rates (~70% upheld)
- Software and business-method patents have lower survival rates
- Biotechnology patents face high enablement/written-description challenges
- Mechanical patents are intermediate

## Implications for Patent Drafting

To draft a validity-resilient patent, applicants should:

1. **Submit Comprehensive IDS**: All art known to applicant or named inventors must be disclosed; broad construction of "material" art is safer
2. **Engage Substantively in Prosecution**: Force the examiner to address substantive rejections rather than pursuing quick allowance
3. **Build Multiple Defensible Claim Layers**: Independent and dependent claims at multiple levels of generality preserve fallback positions
4. **Document Reasonable Inventorship**: Inventor-by-inventor contribution records protect against derivation/inventorship challenges
5. **Avoid Functional Claiming Without Structure**: 112(b) and 112(f) issues are common invalidation grounds

## Operative Quality Test

For any issued patent at risk of litigation, ask:

- How comprehensive was the prior-art universe considered during prosecution?
- What fraction of likely invalidating art was actually cited?
- How many substantive office actions did the application receive?
- Are there continuation-related vulnerabilities (intervening rights, written description, OTDP)?
- Are independent claims supported by clear specification structure?
- Is the prosecution history consistent across related applications?

A patent scoring high on each is in the upper tier of the litigated-patent population. The ~46% invalidation rate is the baseline against which any individual patent's validity probability should be assessed.

## Caveat: Selection Effects

Litigated patents are not random; they are selected for litigation by parties who believe (rightly or wrongly) the patent is enforceable. Invalidation rates among litigated patents may be **lower** than among the full universe of issued patents (since weak patents may not be litigated). The 46% invalidation rate is therefore a likely lower bound on systemic invalidity.
