# Jepson Claims - Improvement Patent Format

## What is a Jepson Claim?

A Jepson claim is a method or product claim where one or more limitations are specifically identified as a point of novelty, distinguishable over at least the contents of the preamble. The preamble makes a statement about the state of the prior art, and then claims an improvement over the prior art.

## Structure and Format

A Jepson claim has a specific format:
1. A preamble that identifies conventional/known features of the claimed subject matter
2. A specific transitional phrase (e.g., "wherein the improvement comprises")
3. A recitation of the "new or improved" aspects of the claimed subject matter

Example: "A system for storing information having (...) wherein the improvement comprises:"

## Regulatory Framework (37 C.F.R. 1.75(e))

Improvement patent claims should follow a specific template with three components:
1. A preamble comprising a general description of all the elements or steps of the claimed combination which are conventional or known
2. A phrase such as "wherein the improvement comprises"
3. Those elements, steps, and/or relationships which constitute that portion of the claimed combination which the applicant considers as the new or improved portion

## Critical Prior Art Admission Issue

Drafting a claim in Jepson format is taken as an implied admission that the subject matter of the preamble is the prior art work of another. This facilitates examiners' (or accused infringers') arguments that the improvement is obvious in light of the admitted prior art under 35 U.S.C. § 103(a).

For this reason, this claim style is seldom used in modern U.S. practice.

## When to Use Jepson Claims

One situation where it may be appropriate to use a Jepson claim is when the patentee is attempting to overcome, or prevent, a double patenting rejection. When the patentee employs the Jepson format to avoid a double patenting rejection over the patentee's own work, the use of the Jepson format causes no deleterious effects.

In some circumstances, the examiner may even suggest the use of a Jepson claim to clearly delineate that the patent will only be covering the improvement and not the underlying work of the patentee.
