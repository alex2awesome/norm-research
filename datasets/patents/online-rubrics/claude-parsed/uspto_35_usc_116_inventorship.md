# 35 USC 116 - Joint Inventorship

## Joint Inventors Requirements

When an invention is made by two or more persons jointly, they shall apply for patent jointly and each make the required oath.

## Flexibility in Joint Inventorship

Inventors may apply for a patent jointly even though:
1. They did not physically work together or at the same time
2. Each did not make the same type or amount of contribution
3. Each did not make a contribution to the subject matter of every claim of the patent

## Handling Omitted Inventors

If a joint inventor refuses to join in an application or cannot be found or reached after diligent effort, the application may be made by the other inventor on behalf of himself and the omitted inventor.

The Director may grant a patent to the inventor making the application, subject to the same rights which the omitted inventor would have had.

## Joint Inventor Standard

A person who shares in the **conception** of a claimed invention is a joint inventor of that invention. Each joint inventor must contribute in some significant manner to the conception of the invention.

## Conception vs. Reduction to Practice

- **Conception**: The mental act of formulating the invention; the heart of inventorship
- **Reduction to practice**: Constructing/testing — alone does NOT confer inventorship
- Mere assistance in reducing an invention to practice (without conception) does not make one an inventor

## Common Inventorship Issues

- Adding workers who only followed instructions = NOT inventors
- Suggesting general goal/problem but not solution = NOT inventor
- Contributing to a single dependent claim = IS an inventor (in some circumstances)
- Sharing only in obvious aspects = NOT inventor of the patent

## Correction of Inventorship

35 USC 256 allows correction of inventorship in issued patents:
- Without deceptive intent
- Through application by all parties
- Court order also possible

## Inequitable Conduct Risk

Misjoinder or omission of an inventor with deceptive intent can render a patent unenforceable. Diligence in identifying all inventors is critical.

## Patent Ownership vs. Inventorship

Inventors own a patent equally and can each license it independently absent contractual transfer (35 USC 262). Employers typically own through assignment agreements or hired-to-invent doctrine.
