# Wikipedia: Patent Application

## Overview

A patent application is a request pending at a patent office for the grant of a patent for an invention. It is processed through patent prosecution. To obtain the grant of a patent, a person must file an application meeting the formal requirements of the patent office and pay required fees.

## Required Components (US)

For a US non-provisional utility application:

- **Specification**: Including title, cross-references, technical field, background, summary, brief description of drawings, detailed description, abstract.
- **Claims**: At least one claim required.
- **Drawings**: Required where necessary for understanding (almost always for utility patents).
- **Oath or Declaration**: Inventor's declaration of inventorship.
- **Information Disclosure Statement**: List of known prior art.
- **Application Data Sheet**: Bibliographic information.

## Specification Requirements - 35 USC 112

The specification must:

- (a) **Enablement**: Enable a POSITA to make and use the invention.
- (a) **Written description**: Show possession of the invention as claimed.
- (a) **Best mode**: Disclose the best mode contemplated by the inventor (no longer a basis for invalidity post-AIA but still required).
- (b) **Definiteness**: Particularly point out and distinctly claim the invention.

## Provisional vs. Non-Provisional

A **provisional application** is a less formal filing that establishes a priority date for 12 months. It is not examined and never matures into a patent. Inventors typically file provisional first to establish priority, then file non-provisional within 12 months.

A **non-provisional application** is the formal application that is examined and may mature into a patent. The 20-year patent term runs from the non-provisional filing date.

## Patent Prosecution

After filing, the application is examined by a patent examiner who determines whether the claims meet patentability requirements. The examiner issues office actions (typically rejecting some or all claims), and the applicant responds with arguments and/or amendments. This back-and-forth continues until the application is allowed or finally rejected.

## International Filing - PCT

The Patent Cooperation Treaty allows an applicant to file a single international application that has effect in 150+ member countries. The PCT delays the requirement to file in individual countries by 30 months from the priority date, providing time for market evaluation.

## Foreign Filing Strategy

Within 12 months of a US filing (Paris Convention), an applicant can file in foreign countries claiming priority back to the US filing. Common strategies: PCT filing within 12 months, then national phase entries; or direct filings in priority foreign countries.

## Costs

A typical US utility patent application costs $10,000-$30,000 in attorney fees plus $400-$1,600 in USPTO fees through allowance. Foreign filings add $5,000-$15,000 per country.

## Timeline

Average US patent prosecution: 22-24 months from filing to allowance, with significant variation by art unit (mechanical: ~18 months; software: ~28 months; biotech: ~36 months).
