# Wikibooks: Patent Drafting Guide

## Chapter 1 - The Drafting Mindset

Patent drafting is engineering with words. The goal is to design a document that (a) the patent office will allow, (b) a court will enforce broadly, and (c) competitors cannot easily design around. These goals sometimes conflict; the drafter chooses the trade-off.

## Chapter 2 - Reading Prior Art

Before drafting, the drafter must understand the closest prior art. A claim drafted without this understanding is likely either too broad (anticipated by art the drafter did not see) or too narrow (incorporating limitations that were not necessary to distinguish the actual prior art).

## Chapter 3 - The Independent Claim

The independent claim is the most important sentence in the patent. Drafting it requires answering:

- What is the minimum combination of elements that distinguishes the invention from prior art?
- What is the broadest accurate way to describe each element?
- In what order do the elements interact?
- Who performs the steps (for method claims)?

## Chapter 4 - Dependent Claims

Dependent claims should narrow on dimensions that are independently meaningful. Examples:

- Restricting a generic element to a particular species ("wherein the polymer is polyethylene").
- Adding an optional feature ("further comprising a temperature sensor").
- Specifying a numerical range ("wherein the temperature is between 50 and 80 degrees C").
- Specifying a particular application or use case.
- Specifying a particular ordering of steps.

## Chapter 5 - Specification Sections

- **Title**: Brief description (under 500 characters).
- **Cross-References**: Priority claims to earlier applications.
- **Field of Invention**: One sentence on the technical area.
- **Background**: Brief context; avoid disparaging prior art.
- **Summary**: Mirror the independent claims at high level.
- **Brief Description of Drawings**: One line per figure.
- **Detailed Description**: The bulk of the spec; walk through figures and embodiments.
- **Claims**: The legally operative section.
- **Abstract**: 150-word summary.

## Chapter 6 - Drawings

Patent drawings must comply with 37 CFR 1.84: black ink, specific paper size, sheet numbering, view labeling, reference numbers. Each element with a reference number in the spec must appear in at least one figure with that number.

## Chapter 7 - Common Claim Types

- **Apparatus claim**: "An apparatus comprising: a first component; a second component coupled to the first component; and a third component configured to..."
- **Method claim**: "A method comprising: a first step; a second step performed after the first step; and a third step that produces..."
- **System claim**: "A system comprising: a server configured to...; a client configured to...; a network connecting the server and the client."
- **Composition claim**: "A composition comprising: 50-70% by weight of A; 20-30% by weight of B; and 5-15% by weight of C."

## Chapter 8 - Drafting Errors to Avoid

- Antecedent basis errors.
- Inconsistent terminology.
- Limitations in the preamble that should be in the body.
- Functional language without supporting structure.
- Negative limitations without spec support.
- Numerical limits not supported by ranges in the spec.
