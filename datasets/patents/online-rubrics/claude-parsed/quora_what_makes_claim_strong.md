# Quora: What Makes a Patent Claim Strong

## Strong Claims Have Two Properties

(1) They survive validity attacks (no anticipation, not obvious, definite, enabled, supported by written description). (2) They cover commercially valuable activity (the kinds of products competitors actually want to make).

## Element Count - Fewer Is Generally Better

Each element is a hurdle the patentee must clear in proving infringement, and each is a target for invalidity attack. A claim with 5 elements is generally stronger than the same invention claimed with 12. But you cannot delete elements that are needed to distinguish prior art.

## Avoid Numerical Limits Unless They Distinguish

"Wherein the temperature is between 50 and 100 degrees Celsius" is a hard limit. If the prior art discloses 49 or 101, the limit was needed; if not, the limit was unnecessary scope-shrinking. Add numerical limits only when they distinguish.

## Functional Language - Useful but Risky

"A processor configured to determine X" is functionally claimed. It is broader (covers any processor that determines X) but invokes 112(f) under Williamson if there is not enough surrounding structure. Pair functional with structural elements.

## Strong Spec Support for Each Element

Every claim element should have at least one paragraph of spec discussion explaining what it is, what it does, what alternatives exist, and how it interacts with neighboring elements. Sparse spec support is a 112 vulnerability.

## Detectability of Infringement

A claim that requires inspecting the source code of a competitor's product is hard to enforce - you cannot get discovery without filing suit, and you cannot file suit without a Rule 11 basis to believe infringement. Claims that read on observable behaviors (network packets, UI flows, product packaging) are more enforceable.

## Single-Actor Drafting

Method claims should describe a single entity performing every step. After Akamai v. Limelight (Fed. Cir. 2015), divided infringement requires either a directing/controlling relationship or joint enterprise. Single-actor claims dodge the issue entirely.

## Robust to Construction Variation

The strongest claims read on the accused product under both broad and narrow constructions of the disputed terms. If your infringement read depends on a single favorable claim construction, you are betting the case on one issue.

## Claim Differentiation Within Family

Different claims in the same patent should differ on legally meaningful dimensions - different scope, different element types, different transitions - not trivial restatements. Wasted dependent claims are wasted opportunity.

## Continuation Discipline

Even the strongest claim does not future-proof a portfolio. Always have a continuation pending so new claims can be drafted as new infringers emerge or as new claim-construction trends develop.

## Foreign Counterparts Strengthen Validity

A patent that has been independently granted in EPO, JPO, and CNIPA is harder to invalidate in the US. The independent grants are evidence of patentability strength.
