# IPWatchdog: Patent Drafting Basics

## The Three Audiences for a Patent Application

A patent specification is read by (1) the examiner, who decides whether to allow it; (2) a future district court judge or jury, who decides claim construction and infringement; and (3) a future PTAB panel, who decides validity. Drafting choices that satisfy one audience may hurt with another. The first draft should be examiner-friendly; later drafts must be litigation-friendly.

## Brick-by-Brick Description

Describe the invention as a sequence of components, each with a reference number that ties to the figures. Walk the reader through (a) what each component is, (b) what it does, (c) what alternatives exist, and (d) how it interacts with neighboring components. The bricks become the building blocks for claims.

## Three Independent Claims, Twenty Total

The USPTO filing fee includes three independent claims and twenty total claims. Use them all. Three independent claims of varying scope plus seventeen dependent claims that systematically narrow give you both negotiating leverage with the examiner and a robust portfolio.

## Method, System, Apparatus - File All Three

For a software-related invention, file parallel sets: a method claim (what the system does), a system/apparatus claim (a processor + memory + instructions), and a computer-readable medium claim (instructions stored on a medium). Each claim type reaches different infringers and survives different invalidity attacks.

## The Drawing-First Workflow

Start with the figures. A typical mechanical invention needs (a) an exploded view, (b) an assembled perspective view, (c) a cross-section, (d) a flowchart of operation. A typical software invention needs (a) a system architecture diagram, (b) a data flow diagram, (c) a flowchart of the key algorithm, (d) a screen mockup if user-facing. Then draft the spec around the figures.

## Claim Differentiation as a Drafting Tool

When claim 2 adds "wherein the widget is metal," that creates a presumption that claim 1 covers non-metal widgets too. Use dependent claims to deliberately broaden the construction of independent claims by negative implication.

## The "Best Mode" Requirement Still Exists

Although the AIA removed best-mode failure as a basis for invalidity, the requirement to disclose the best mode under 35 USC 112(a) still technically exists. More importantly, failing to disclose your best mode means a competitor can patent it on top of your patent. Disclose everything.

## Drafting in Layers

Layer 1: paragraphs that read like a textbook for a POSITA. Layer 2: paragraphs that recite the elements of each claim, almost word-for-word, so every claim term has clear antecedent support. Layer 3: paragraphs that describe alternatives, ranges, and equivalents. All three layers belong in the spec.
