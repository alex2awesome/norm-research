# USPTO Inter Partes Review (IPR)

## Overview

Inter partes review (IPR) is a procedure for challenging the validity of a U.S. patent before the USPTO. Enacted on September 16, 2012 as part of the America Invents Act.

## What IPR Covers

IPR is a trial proceeding conducted at the PTAB to review patentability of one or more claims in a patent **only on a ground that could be raised under §§ 102 or 103**, and only on the basis of patents and printed publications.

## Who Can File

Any party (that is not estopped) other than the patent owner can file an IPR petition.

**Estoppel triggers**:
- Service of complaint alleging infringement >1 year earlier
- Final written decision in a previous IPR/PGR

## Timing

- IPR available **starting 9 months after grant** (if no PGR was filed)
- 1-year time limit from service of infringement complaint

## Institution Standard

An IPR may be instituted upon showing:
"There is a **reasonable likelihood** that the petitioner would prevail with respect to at least one claim challenged."

This is a slightly higher threshold than ex parte reexamination ("substantial new question").

## Procedure

### 1. Petition Phase (3 months)
- Petitioner files IPR petition with prior art and arguments
- Patent owner has 3 months to file Preliminary Response (optional)

### 2. Institution Decision (6 months from filing)
- PTAB decides whether to institute
- Institution decisions are not appealable

### 3. Trial Phase (12 months from institution)
- Patent owner Response (3 months)
- Petitioner Reply
- Patent owner Surreply
- Oral Hearing
- Final Written Decision

### 4. Appeal
- Federal Circuit appeal available

## Advantages of IPR

1. **Faster**: 18 months or less from filing to final disposition
2. **Cheaper than litigation**: Limited discovery
3. **Higher invalidation rates**: ~75% of instituted IPRs result in some claims being invalidated
4. **Specialized adjudicators**: PTAB judges have technical backgrounds
5. **Standard of review**: Preponderance of evidence (district court: clear and convincing)

## Discovery in IPR

Limited discovery available:
- Initial mandatory disclosures
- Routine discovery (cited references, supplemental information)
- Additional discovery (only if "in the interests of justice")
- Depositions of declarants

Much narrower than district court litigation.

## Estoppel Effects

After final written decision, petitioner is estopped from raising in:
- USPTO proceedings
- District court litigation
- ITC investigations

Estoppel applies to any ground "raised or reasonably could have been raised."

Estoppel is strict and is one of the major considerations in IPR strategy.

## Multi-Party IPRs

Joinder allowed under 35 USC 315(c):
- Multiple petitioners can join an existing IPR
- Common after one party files first
- Reduces costs across parties
- Avoids estoppel for joining party

## Recent Trends

- USPTO Director discretion to deny institution (Fintiv factors)
- Parallel litigation considerations
- Sovereign immunity defenses
- Stays of district court litigation pending IPR

## Strategic Considerations

### Filing IPR
- Prepare prior art that wasn't considered during prosecution
- Consider whether claims you'd amend are problematic
- Evaluate parallel litigation timing
- Budget for 1-year+ proceeding

### Defending Against IPR
- Preliminary Response is critical (early dismissal possible)
- Argue strong patent owner interests
- Use Director discretionary denial factors
- Amend claims if possible

## IPR vs. Litigation

Many parties pursue both:
- IPR can stay district court litigation
- Different burden of proof (preponderance vs. clear and convincing)
- IPR limited to specific grounds; litigation broader
- Settlement options different
