# Antecedent Basis in Patent Claims

## What is Antecedent Basis?

Antecedent basis refers to the requirement that all terms in a claim must be properly introduced before being modified or referenced. Claim terms must find clear support or antecedent basis in the specification so that the meaning of the terms may be ascertainable by reference to the specification.

## Basic Drafting Rules

### Article Usage
- Use indefinite article "a" or "an" when introducing an element for the first time
- Use definite article "the" or "said" when referring back to the introduced element
- The definite articles "the," "said," and "such" cannot be used unless the noun has already been introduced in the claim

### Dependent Claims
In a dependent claim, the antecedent basis should be present in that dependent claim or any claim upon which that claim is dependent (since a dependent claim incorporates all of the claims from which it depends, by reference).

## Common Antecedent Basis Issues

Lack of clarity arises when:
- A claim refers to "said lever" or "the lever" with no earlier recitation of a lever
- Two different levers are recited earlier in the claim, and "said lever" is later used ambiguously
- Plural nouns lack number agreement ("said widgets" when only one widget was introduced)

## Important Clarifications

The mere fact that a term has no antecedent basis in the specification disclosure does not necessarily mean the term is indefinite. There is no requirement that the words in the claim must match those used in the specification disclosure.

Inherent components of recited elements have antecedent basis in the recitation of the elements themselves (e.g., "a wheel" inherently has "a hub" and "a rim").

## Best Practices

- Review and reconcile both the specification and claims
- First introduction uses "a"/"an"; later references use "the"/"said"
- Avoid implicit antecedence where ambiguous
- Antecedent problems are typically drafting oversights easily corrected
