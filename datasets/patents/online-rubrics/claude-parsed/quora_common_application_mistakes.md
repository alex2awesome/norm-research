# Quora: Most Common Mistakes in Patent Applications

## Mistake 1 - Treating the Application As a Technical Paper

Patent specifications are legal documents. They have specific drafting conventions (single-sentence claims, antecedent basis, particular transitional phrases) that conflict with technical writing norms. Inventors who write the spec like a journal article create cleanup work for the attorney and often hide the inventive concept under technical detail.

## Mistake 2 - Including Marketing Claims

"Best in class," "industry-leading," "groundbreaking," "revolutionary" - these are not patent terms. They cannot help patentability and may hurt: an opponent will quote them to argue that the invention was so revolutionary it must have been driven by external need (filling a long-felt need is a secondary consideration) - but only if you also win the obviousness fight. Marketing language has no place in a spec.

## Mistake 3 - Claiming Your Goal Instead of Your Mechanism

A common pattern: claim "a system for reducing network latency" without specifying how. This invites 101 (abstract idea) and 112 (enablement) attacks. The mechanism is what is patentable.

## Mistake 4 - Failing to Disclose Alternatives

The single biggest source of design-around opportunities for competitors is over-specification. Every concrete choice in your prototype (material, geometry, ordering, parameter value) should be accompanied by a disclosure of alternatives that would also work.

## Mistake 5 - Inconsistent Terminology

Calling the same thing "the widget," "the device," "the component," and "the unit" across different parts of the spec creates ambiguity. Pick one term per concept and use it everywhere.

## Mistake 6 - Antecedent Basis Errors

The first time a concept appears in a claim, use "a" or "an." On every subsequent reference, use "the." Switching ("a widget" then later "said widget" then later "the widget") is fine but inconsistent. Forgetting introduction creates 112(b) rejections.

## Mistake 7 - Putting Limitations in the Preamble

The preamble is dangerous: it may or may not be limiting. Put your distinguishing features in the body of the claim, where they are unambiguously limitations.

## Mistake 8 - Too Many or Too Few Independent Claims

One independent claim is too few (no fallback). Five or more usually waste filing fees and create internal redundancy. Three is the sweet spot.

## Mistake 9 - Wrong Inventorship

Inventors are people who conceived the claimed subject matter. CEOs, advisors, and contractors who provided guidance but did not contribute to the conception are not inventors. Wrong inventorship can render the patent unenforceable until corrected.

## Mistake 10 - Ignoring International Strategy

Decisions made for the US filing (claim style, embodiments disclosed, prior art admissions) ripple into PCT and national-phase filings. Make those decisions with international strategy in mind.

## Mistake 11 - Not Filing Continuations

Letting the parent issue without a pending continuation forecloses future claim scope. Always have at least one continuation pending while the family is commercially relevant.

## Mistake 12 - Skipping the Disclosure Statement

The IDS (Information Disclosure Statement) lists prior art the applicant knows about. Failing to file or failing to update can lead to inequitable conduct findings, which render the patent unenforceable.
