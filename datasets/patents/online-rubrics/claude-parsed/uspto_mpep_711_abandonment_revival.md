# Patent Application Abandonment and Revival

## What Causes Abandonment

The most common reason for abandonment: applicant has not responded to a USPTO communication by the required deadline.

Specific causes:
- Failure to respond to office action within shortened statutory period
- Failure to pay issue fee within 3 months of Notice of Allowance
- Failure to pay maintenance fee
- Failure to pay extension of time fees
- Express abandonment by applicant

## Categories of Abandonment

1. **Express abandonment**: Applicant explicitly abandons
2. **Constructive abandonment**: Failure to take required action
3. **Failure to file divisional**: After restriction requirement, no divisional pursued

## Revival Standards

If the delay was **unintentional**, a petition may be filed to revive an abandoned application.

37 CFR 1.137(a) governs revival based on unintentional delay.

## Two-Year Rule

The USPTO requires additional information when the petition to revive was filed more than **2 years** after the date the application became abandoned.

This is to ensure that the entire delay was "unintentional" — not just the period leading up to the petition.

## Petition Requirements

A petition for revival must include:
- The required reply (response to outstanding office action, fee payment, etc.)
- Petition fee
- Statement that the entire delay was unintentional
- For cases >2 years: explanation of facts and circumstances

## Revival vs. Restoration

- **Revival**: Recovers an application that was abandoned
- **Restoration**: For priority benefit lost due to late filing (under PLT)

## Specific Revival Petitions

### Petition to Revive Under 37 CFR 1.137
- Standard revival petition
- Lower threshold ("unintentional")

### Petition to Revive Under 37 CFR 1.137(b) (Older Standard)
- Required showing of "unavoidable delay"
- More demanding standard
- Largely replaced by unintentional standard

## Common Successful Petitions

- Failed e-mail notifications about office actions
- Personal emergencies
- Office or attorney transitions
- Mail handling errors
- Calendar/docketing system failures

## Common Failed Petitions

- Strategic abandonment then attempt to revive
- Multiple-year unexplained delays
- Failure to provide adequate explanation
- "Knew about it but didn't act" admissions

## Strategic Considerations

- Revive promptly upon discovery
- Provide concise but adequate explanation
- Avoid creating evidence of intentional delay
- Consider if filing a continuation might be better strategy
- For >2 year delays, prepare for additional information requests

## ePetition Filing

The USPTO offers ePetition filing for:
- Petition to Revive Abandoned Patent Application
- Multiple specific revival categories
- Faster processing than paper petitions
