# Transitional Phrases in Patent Claims

## Three Main Transitional Phrases

There are three transition phrases that connect the preamble to the body of a claim:
1. **comprising** (open)
2. **consisting of** (closed)
3. **consisting essentially of** (hybrid)

## "Comprising" - Open-Ended

"Comprising" is an open-ended term allowing for additional steps or components. Effectively shorthand for "including the following elements but not excluding others." The word "comprising" transitioning from the preamble to the body signals that the entire claim is presumptively open-ended.

Equivalent open-ended terms: "including," "containing," "characterized by"

## "Consisting Of" - Closed

"Consisting of" is closed and excludes any element, step, or ingredient not specified in the claim. A claim "consisting of" listed elements infringes only when those exact elements are present (with permissible impurities and conventional adjuncts).

## "Consisting Essentially Of" - Hybrid Approach

The transitional phrase "consisting essentially of" occupies a middle ground. It limits the scope of a claim to the specified materials or steps "and those that do not materially affect the basic and novel characteristic(s)" of the claimed invention.

Mainly used in chemical and metallurgical composition inventions; shorthand for "excluding additional unspecified ingredients which would affect the basic and novel characteristics of the product."

## Examination vs. Litigation

For purposes of searching for and applying prior art, "consisting essentially of" will be construed as equivalent to "comprising" absent a clear indication in the specification or claims of what the basic and novel characteristics are.

When a claim having "consisting essentially of" is involved in litigation, the court will determine the meaning during claim construction based on the available intrinsic evidence.

## Other Modifying Phrases

- "Having" — usually treated as open-ended like "comprising"
- "Composed of" — typically treated as closed
- "Selected from the group consisting of" — Markush group, closed
