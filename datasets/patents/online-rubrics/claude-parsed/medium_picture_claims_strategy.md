# Picture Claims and Why You Should Have One

## What Is a Picture Claim?

A picture claim is a narrow claim that closely tracks a specific commercial embodiment - so close that a literal reading of the claim looks like a verbal photograph of the actual product. Every detail of the actual implementation is in the claim.

## Why Bother With a Narrow Claim?

Counter-intuitively, the narrowest claim in a portfolio is often the most enforceable. It has the lowest risk of invalidation (because it incorporates many limiting details that prior art lacks), the clearest infringement read (because it tracks the real product), and the lowest doctrine-of-equivalents complications.

## Picture Claims Are Insurance, Not Offense

You do not enforce a picture claim against a competitor. You use it as the survivor when the broad independent claims are challenged in IPR or litigation. After your broad claim 1 is invalidated, your picture claim 12 may still stand, and may still cover your own product (proving secondary considerations) and a competitor that knocked you off.

## How to Write One

Take your prototype. Describe every meaningful component, every interface, every data field, every numerical range you actually use. Put it all in one claim. Yes, the claim will be three pages long. Yes, the result will read like an instruction manual. That is the point.

## Use Dependent Claims as a Ladder

In a typical 20-claim application, claim 1 is the broad "moonshot" claim, claims 2-10 progressively narrow, and the final dependent claim is essentially the picture claim. You want every step of the ladder so a court can stop at any rung.

## Picture Claims Help Damages, Too

Damages experts use the smallest-saleable-patent-practicing-unit framework. A claim that closely tracks the product makes it easier to argue the claimed invention drives demand for the product, which raises the royalty base.

## The Risk of Only Picture Claims

A portfolio of only picture claims gives competitors a roadmap to design around. You need both: broad claims that scare designers-around, plus picture claims that are unkillable.
