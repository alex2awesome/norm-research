# Writing Your First Patent Application: 9 Things I Wish I Knew

## 1. Start With the Claims, Then Work Backwards

Most first-time inventors write the specification first and bolt on the claims at the end. Reverse that. The claims define what you actually own. Draft claim 1 first, decide which elements are truly essential, then write the spec to support every word in that claim. If a term appears in a claim but is not anchored in the spec, you have a 112(b) problem waiting to happen.

## 2. Your Independent Claim Is Probably Too Narrow

First-timers reflexively describe their commercial product. The result: a claim with 14 elements, half of which are not load-bearing. Strip each independent claim down to the minimum combination that distinguishes over the closest prior art. Anything that recites "wherein the user clicks a button" is almost certainly an unnecessary limitation.

## 3. Use Multiple Independent Claims of Different Scope

Do not file with just one independent claim. File three or four: one broad, one of moderate scope, and one narrow but commercially aligned. If the broad claim is killed in prosecution or post-grant review, the narrower ones survive.

## 4. Describe Three Embodiments, Not One

Even if you only built one prototype, describe (a) the prototype, (b) a more general version with components swapped for functional equivalents, and (c) a degenerate or simplified version. This buffers against narrow claim construction later.

## 5. Avoid "Patent Profanity"

Words like "essential," "critical," "must," "always," "the invention is," and "the present invention requires" are landmines. Courts read these as disclaimers of scope. Replace with "in some embodiments," "may include," "one example."

## 6. Draw the Figures Yourself First

You will discover gaps in your own understanding when you try to label every reference number. Aim for one figure per concept. Block diagrams beat photographs for clarity.

## 7. Disclose More Than You Want To Claim

Disclosure is cheap; you cannot add new matter later. If you have alternative materials, alternative orderings, alternative ranges - put them in the spec even if you do not currently claim them. They become fodder for continuations.

## 8. Provisional Applications Are Not a Free Pass

A provisional only gets you a priority date for what is actually disclosed. A two-page sketch labeled "provisional" gives you a priority date for two pages of disclosure. If your non-provisional claims things not in the provisional, you lose priority for those claims.

## 9. Get a Real Search Done Before Filing

Not a Google search. A real prior art search by a professional searcher costs $500-$2,000 and routinely surfaces the closest art. Knowing the closest art before you draft saves you from wasting two years prosecuting a claim that reads on a 1998 reference.
