# WikiHow: How to Write a Patent Application

## Step 1 - Confirm Patentability

Your invention must be (a) patentable subject matter (process, machine, manufacture, composition of matter), (b) new (not anticipated by prior art), (c) useful, and (d) non-obvious to a person of ordinary skill in the art. If your invention is a pure mathematical algorithm, a law of nature, or an abstract idea, it likely fails subject matter eligibility.

## Step 2 - Document the Invention

Write a complete description of the invention before talking to attorneys. Include: what it does, how it works, what makes it different from existing solutions, alternative versions, and best results. Date and witness the document.

## Step 3 - Conduct a Prior Art Search

Search the USPTO patent database, Google Patents, Espacenet, and academic literature. Look for patents and publications in the same technical field. The goal is to find references that disclose anything similar.

## Step 4 - Decide Provisional or Non-Provisional

A provisional is cheaper and faster but does not get examined. A non-provisional starts the prosecution clock. Most applicants file provisional first, then convert within 12 months.

## Step 5 - Draft the Specification

Sections in order: Title, Cross-References, Field of Invention, Background, Summary, Brief Description of Drawings, Detailed Description, Claims, Abstract. Each section has a specific purpose.

## Step 6 - Draft the Drawings

Patent drawings have specific format requirements (37 CFR 1.84): black ink, specific paper size, margins, line weights, lettering size. Each element in the spec should be reference-numbered to a corresponding feature in a drawing.

## Step 7 - Draft Claims

Claims are the legal definition of the invention. Independent claims stand alone; dependent claims add limitations to a parent claim. Use specific transitional phrases ("comprising," "consisting of"). Each claim is a single sentence.

## Step 8 - File With the USPTO

File electronically through Patent Center. Pay filing, search, and examination fees (reduced for small entity, micro entity). Include the specification, drawings, claims, abstract, oath/declaration, and assignment if applicable.

## Step 9 - Respond to Office Actions

The USPTO will likely issue an office action with rejections. Respond within the deadline (usually 3 months, extendable to 6). Amend claims and/or argue against the rejection.

## Step 10 - Pay Issue Fee and Maintain

If allowed, pay the issue fee within 3 months. After issuance, pay maintenance fees at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years to keep the patent in force.

## Common Beginner Tips

- Avoid the words "essential" or "critical" in the spec.
- Disclose multiple ways to practice the invention.
- Use figures generously.
- Have a patent attorney review even if you are filing pro se.
