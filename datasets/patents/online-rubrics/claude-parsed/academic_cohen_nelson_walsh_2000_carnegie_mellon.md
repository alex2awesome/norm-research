# Cohen-Nelson-Walsh: When Patents Function as Quality Appropriability

The Carnegie Mellon survey of 1,478 R&D labs is the foundational empirical work on when patents actually capture innovation rents. It supplies criteria for evaluating whether a patent serves its intended economic function.

## Key Empirical Findings

**Patents Are Often Not the Primary Appropriability Mechanism**: Across most industries, firms rank lead-time advantages and secrecy above patents for protecting innovation rents. Only in **pharmaceuticals, medical devices, and (some) chemicals** are patents the dominant appropriability mechanism.

**Strategic Patenting Motives Dominate**: When firms do patent, the leading motives include:
1. Prevention of copying
2. Patent blocking (preventing competitors from patenting related inventions)
3. Use of patents in cross-licensing negotiations
4. Prevention of suits
5. Reputation/financing signaling

**Patent Effectiveness Varies by Innovation Type**:
- Product innovations are protected by patents at higher rates than process innovations
- Process innovations rely more heavily on secrecy
- Discrete-product industries (pharma) > complex-product industries (semiconductors)

## Quality Implications: When a Patent is "Working"

A patent functions as a high-quality appropriability mechanism when:

**The Innovation Type Matches Patent Strength**: Product innovations in discrete-invention fields (pharma compounds, mechanical devices) are well-protected by patents. Process innovations or complex-product innovations are weaker patent matches; these are quality-vulnerable.

**Disclosure Doesn't Defeat Secrecy Value**: For some innovations, the patent disclosure requirement reveals more than the patent's exclusionary value protects. Quality patents in such cases capture genuinely novel information not readily reverse-engineered.

**Lead Time and Complementary Assets Are Aligned**: Firms with manufacturing scale, distribution, and brand may use patents defensively rather than offensively. Quality patents in this context are characterized by:
- Coverage of the firm's actual products (not aspirational coverage)
- Clear scope amenable to licensing or cross-licensing
- Defensive positioning consistent with the firm's overall IP strategy

## Patent-Quality Markers from CNW Framework

**Strategic Coverage Markers**:
- Claims map onto products the firm sells or expects to sell
- Continuation strategy preserves options for capturing competitor designs
- Cross-license potential is recognized in claim drafting

**Anti-Markers** (low-quality strategic patenting):
- Claims drafted to capture only competitor products, not own products (signal of nuisance/troll patents)
- No connection between patent and firm's R&D investments
- Acquired patent portfolio used solely for assertion

## Industry-Specific Patent Function

The CNW survey supports industry-specific quality criteria:

### Pharmaceutical / Biotechnology
- Patents are the primary appropriability mechanism
- Quality requires structural specificity, robust claim layering, and defenses against generics
- Patent quality directly determines commercial value

### Discrete Mechanical Industries
- Patents are an important but not dominant appropriability tool
- Quality requires clear scope and design-around resistance
- Lead time and brand often matter more than patent breadth

### Complex Products (Semiconductors, Software)
- Patents are largely defensive (cross-license currency)
- Quality requires standard-essential or design-essential coverage
- Scope clarity matters more than breadth

### Process Innovations
- Patents weak; secrecy preferred
- Patent quality requires that disclosure be tolerable given trade-secret alternative
- Often filed only when reverse engineering is feasible

## Synthesis: CNW Quality Test

For any patent, ask:

- Does the firm's industry rely on patents as a primary appropriability mechanism?
- Does the patent cover the firm's actual or planned commercial products?
- Is the strategic motive (copying prevention, blocking, cross-license) consistent with claim drafting?
- Does the disclosure preserve trade-secret value where applicable?
- Does the patent fit the firm's broader IP/competitive strategy?

A patent that serves a coherent appropriability function is, in the CNW frame, higher quality than one filed for unclear strategic reasons—even if both meet patentability standards.

## Caveats and Subsequent Work

CNW's findings are consistent with the earlier Levin et al. 1987 (Yale) survey and have been replicated in EU Innovation Survey data, JPO surveys, and Chinese R&D surveys. The cross-national pattern—pharmaceuticals dominant patent users, complex products defensive, process innovation reliant on secrecy—is robust.
