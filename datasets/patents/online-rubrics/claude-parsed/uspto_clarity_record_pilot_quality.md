# USPTO Clarity of the Record Pilot - Patent Quality Assurance

## Overview

The Office of Patent Quality Assurance (OPQA) continuously leverages assessments from multiple sources to gauge patent quality, target areas for improvement, and achieve high-quality work products.

OPQA assesses patent quality at the USPTO by:
- Reviewing work products for statutory compliance, clarity, and best practices
- Defining and standardizing quality metrics
- Providing feedback to Technology Centers

## Clarity of the Record Pilot Program

The Clarity of the Record Pilot is a Patent Quality program that identifies best examiner practices for enhancing the clarity of various aspects of the prosecution record. The Pilot focuses on best practices regarding:

1. **Enhanced documentation of claim interpretation**
2. **More detailed interview summaries**
3. **More precise reasons for allowance**

## Quality Assurance Methodology

### Clarity and Correctness Data Capture (CCDC) Program

Created an improved data capture system that enables all reviewers (OPQA and Technology Center supervisors) to consistently document and access quality review data in one place.

### Master Review Form (MRF)

Captures:
- Traditional inquiries on the correctness of the examiner's determinations on substantive patentability requirements
- New inquiries on the clarity of each determination made in the Office action under review

## Quality Metrics

The USPTO aims to deliver impeccable work product including:
- Appropriate and clear patentability determinations for every pending claim throughout prosecution
- Trend analysis on statutory compliance and clarity assessments
- Identification of underlying causes to reduce rework
- Promotion of efficiency

## Best Practices for Examiner Clarity

Examiners should:
- Document claim interpretation explicitly when terms could be construed multiple ways
- Provide detailed interview summaries capturing all substantive discussions
- Specify reasons for allowance with precision (especially when prior art was overcome)
- Identify specific limitations distinguishing claims over prior art
- Avoid "see relevant section" without citing specific text

## Implications for Drafters

Understanding what examiners are trained to do helps drafters:
- Submit applications with clear claim scope
- Use precise language anticipating examiner interpretations
- Provide claim charts in responses showing how amendments overcome rejections
- Submit precise interview summaries
