# Continuation Applications: The Most Underrated Tool in Patent Strategy

## What a Continuation Lets You Do

A continuation application is a new application that claims priority to an earlier application and shares the same disclosure. It lets you pursue different claim scopes, often years after the original filing, without losing priority date - so long as the spec supports the new claims.

## Why Always Keep One Pending

A pending continuation is an option to write claims that read on a competitor's product as soon as it ships. If your competitor releases a feature in 2027 that your 2024 spec discloses (even tangentially), you can draft new claims targeting it. Once your final patent issues with no pending continuation, that option is lost.

## File the Continuation Before the Parent Issues

The trick: file the continuation while the parent is still pending (typically right before the issue fee is due on the parent). Otherwise you lose continuation rights.

## CIPs vs. Continuations vs. Divisionals

A continuation has the same disclosure as the parent. A continuation-in-part (CIP) adds new matter; new claims supported by the new matter only get the CIP filing date. A divisional pursues claims to subject matter restricted out of the parent. Most strategists prefer continuations and divisionals over CIPs because CIPs create complicated priority dates.

## Use Continuations to Shift Claim Type

The parent might claim a method. The continuation can claim the apparatus. A second continuation can claim the computer-readable medium. A third can claim a system of multiple devices. Different claim types reach different defendants.

## Use Continuations to Test New Theories

Spec written in 2020 might support a 101-eligible "technical improvement" framing that did not feel necessary at the time. A 2027 continuation can pursue claims with that framing if the spec contains the seeds.

## Watch Obviousness-Type Double Patenting

When you file multiple applications from the same parent, you may have to file terminal disclaimers to avoid OTDP rejections. Terminal disclaimers tie the patent term to the parent's term. Keep a tracker of which applications are tied to which.

## Continuation Practice Costs

A continuation costs roughly the same as the original filing in attorney fees plus USPTO fees, but the disclosure is reused. Many practitioners budget $5,000-$15,000 per continuation.
