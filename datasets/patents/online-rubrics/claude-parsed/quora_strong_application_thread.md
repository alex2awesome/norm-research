# Quora: How Do I Write a Strong Patent Application

## Top Practitioner Answers - Synthesis

### Frame the Invention Around the Problem

The strongest patents start with a clear statement of the technical problem and the failure mode of prior solutions. The reader (examiner, judge, jury) should understand by paragraph 5 of the spec what was hard, what was tried, why those attempts failed, and what new insight enables the inventor's solution.

### Identify the "Single Sentence Inventive Concept"

If you cannot fit the inventive concept into a single sentence, you do not yet have a clean invention. Force yourself to write: "The new thing is that ____ enables ____ by ____."

### Spec First, Claims Second, Then Iterate

Many practitioners write a complete spec, draft claims from it, then revise the spec to ensure every claim term has antecedent support and every claim element has at least three sentences of context in the spec.

### Disclose Generalizations, Not Just Implementations

For each implementation choice you made, ask "what is the general principle here?" and "what other choices would also work?" Disclose the general principle and at least two alternatives.

### Watch the "Heart of the Invention" Trap

If the spec repeatedly says "the heart of the invention is X," courts may construe every claim as requiring X. Do not name a single feature as the heart. The whole claim is the invention.

### Anchor Claim Terms in Defined Concepts

If your claim uses an unusual term, define it explicitly: "As used herein, X means Y." Courts respect explicit definitions.

### Use Multiple Independent Claims

A typical strong filing has 3-4 independent claims of different scope and type (method, system, apparatus, computer-readable medium). Each is a separate insurance policy.

### Rectangle Around the Invention

Imagine the universe of all possible products. The invention occupies a small region. Each claim is a rectangle around some part of that region. The broadest claim should cover as much as possible without overlapping prior art. Each narrower claim is a smaller rectangle inside, covering more specific high-value subregions.

### Consider Litigation Survivability

A claim with 12 elements, each cited in a different prior art reference, will likely face an obviousness challenge. A claim with 6 elements where the inventive concept is concentrated in 2-3 specific elements is harder to attack with a multi-reference combination.

### Get Foreign Counterparts in Mind Early

If you plan to file in Europe, draft with European requirements in mind from the start: avoid US-specific drafting tricks (Markush groupings work differently), keep the spec carefully neutral on prior art, and include problem-solution framing that EPO examiners find natural.
