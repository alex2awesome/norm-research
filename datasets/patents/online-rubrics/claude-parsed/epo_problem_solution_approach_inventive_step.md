# EPO Problem-Solution Approach to Inventive Step (Article 56 EPC)

## Overview

Article 56 EPC is the central legal provision relating to inventive step, requiring that an invention, having regard to the state of the art, must not be obvious to a person skilled in the art.

## The Problem-Solution Approach

The problem and solution approach is used almost universally at the EPO in the examination of inventive step. Since 1977, the EPO has broken up the obviousness question into three separate questions:

1. **Identify the closest prior art** — the single reference disclosing the most similar combination of features and addressing the same purpose
2. **Determine the objective technical problem** — in light of the closest prior art, the technical problem the claimed invention addresses and successfully solves
3. **Apply the would/could test** — would (not could) the skilled person have arrived at the claimed invention starting from the closest prior art and faced with the objective technical problem?

## Closest Prior Art Selection

The closest prior art should:
- Be from the same or closely related technical field
- Address a similar problem or purpose
- Have the most relevant technical features in common with the claimed invention
- Require the minimum modifications to arrive at the claimed invention

## Objective Technical Problem

The "objective" technical problem may differ from the problem identified by the applicant in the application. It is reformulated based on:
- The technical effects achieved by the distinguishing features
- The closest prior art (not all prior art)
- Avoiding hindsight by avoiding pointers to the solution

## The Would-Could Test

Central to both EPO and UPC approaches: obvious inventions are those that the unimaginative skilled person *would* (not could) arrive at when faced with the objective technical problem and the prior art.

## Important Limitations

The problem and solution approach is regarded as a tool to be used in the assessment of inventive step, and not as a definition of inventive step that can be used as a substitute for the actual requirement.

An argument of lack of inventive step using the problem and solution approach is only valid if both:
- The problem is known or is obvious in view of the state of the art
- The solution to the problem is obvious in view of the state of the art
