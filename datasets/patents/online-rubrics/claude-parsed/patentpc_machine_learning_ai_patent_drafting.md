# Drafting Patent Claims for AI and Machine Learning Inventions

## Key Challenges

A common challenge for AI and ML patents is rejections based on the abstract idea doctrine. Patent examiners may argue the invention is merely an abstract idea (e.g., mathematical concept, mental process). To overcome this, demonstrate how the invention applies the abstract idea in a practical and technological context.

## Practical Claim Drafting Strategies

### 1. Focus on Technical Specificity

Instead of: "a method for data analysis using machine learning"

Use: "a method for predictive maintenance in industrial machinery, comprising training a neural network on historical sensor data to predict equipment failures"

Specificity helps avoid overlap with prior art and clarifies the practical application.

### 2. Separate Training and Execution Claims

Draft separate independent claims for:
- The training phase (model architecture, training data, loss functions)
- The execution/inference phase (deployment, prediction, output use)

Where the execution phase lacks substance to stand alone, training-phase details can be included in passive clauses.

### 3. Use Multiple ML Models Thoughtfully

Distinguish between different ML models in claims:
- Use ordinals: "first training data" / "second training data," "first output" / "second output"
- Avoid ambiguity about which model performs which function

### 4. Focus on Unique Technical Aspects

To explain how AI inventions cannot be readily analogized to human thinking, focus on unique technical aspects:
- Weighting of nodes in an artificial neural network
- Retraining of an existing ML model
- Deploying a trained ML model to different computing devices
- Specific architectures (CNNs, transformers, GANs)
- Training data preparation (augmentation, sampling)
- Pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation

## Establishing Patent Eligibility

AI inventions are more likely to be patent eligible when they:
- Solve concrete technical problems
- Provide measurable improvements
- Explain how those improvements are achieved
- Draft claims that reflect the technical improvement

The invention must have a real-world application.

## USPTO 2024 AI Guidance Highlights

The 2024 USPTO Subject Matter Eligibility Update on AI clarified:
- Specific applications of AI in computer technology may be eligible
- Improving accuracy of speech recognition: eligible
- Anomaly detection methods tied to specific networks: eligible
- AI-driven medical treatment selection: may be eligible if claims practical application

## Key Pitfalls to Avoid

- Claims that simply recite "using a machine learning model"
- Math-only claims with no technical use
- Claims describing only data manipulation
- Mental processes implemented "on a computer"
- Functional claims without enabling structural disclosure

## Disclosure Requirements (Article 83 EPC / 35 USC 112)

For AI inventions, disclose:
- Model architecture (if novel)
- Training data type and characteristics
- Loss/objective functions
- Hyperparameters or hyperparameter ranges
- Performance metrics
- Sufficient detail to reproduce the technical effect
