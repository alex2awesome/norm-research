# Provisional vs Utility (Nonprovisional) Patent Application Strategy

## Key Differences

### Provisional Patent Applications

A provisional application is a quick and inexpensive way to establish a U.S. filing date.

**Characteristics:**
- Not examined by USPTO
- Cannot mature into a granted patent
- Lower fees and less rigorous formal requirements
- Establishes priority date for later non-provisional
- Becomes abandoned 12 months after filing
- Does not require formal claims (though strongly recommended)

### Nonprovisional (Utility) Patent Applications

A formal patent application examined by USPTO to determine patentability.

**Characteristics:**
- Examined by USPTO
- Can mature into a granted patent
- Required for actual patent rights
- Higher fees and stricter formal requirements
- Examined under all statutory requirements (101, 102, 103, 112)

## Strategic Considerations

### Provisional Applications Are Useful When:
- Need to secure a filing date quickly (before disclosure)
- Working with limited budget early in company life
- Testing market interest before larger investment
- Still refining the invention
- Need 12 months to develop product/seek funding
- Want to label "Patent Pending"
- Establishing priority for later international filings

### Utility Applications Best When:
- Invention is market-ready
- Want to move directly toward patent protection
- Need to enforce rights soon
- Competitors are close to launching similar technology
- Have full disclosure ready to claim

## Critical Requirements

### One-Year Conversion Window
You must file a utility patent application within **12 months** of the filing date of the provisional patent application to receive priority benefit.

### Disclosure Adequacy
You will only get an early filing date for claims that are **supported by the disclosure** in the provisional application. Be as complete as possible. Consider what will ultimately be claimed in the utility application when drafting the provisional.

### Provisional Pitfalls
- A throwaway provisional gives priority for nothing
- Insufficient disclosure = no priority for important claims
- Cannot add new matter to provisional (must file new application)

## Cost Comparison

| Item | Provisional | Utility |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Filing Fee (small entity) | ~$140 | ~$830 |
| Search Fee | None | Required |
| Examination Fee | None | Required |
| Drafting Cost | $1,500-5,000 | $8,000-25,000 |
| Total to Grant | N/A | $15,000-50,000+ |

## Investor Perception

- Provisional filings: support early-stage discussions
- Utility filings: carry greater weight during later funding rounds
- Issued patents: highest signal value for investors

## Common Mistakes

1. Filing a "thin" provisional with no real disclosure
2. Failing to convert within 12 months
3. Adding new matter to provisional (cannot be done)
4. Public disclosure between provisional and non-provisional outside US (loss of foreign rights)
5. Treating provisional as cheap "patent pending" with no follow-through
