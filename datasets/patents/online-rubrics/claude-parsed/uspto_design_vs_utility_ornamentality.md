# USPTO Design Patent vs Utility Patent: Ornamentality

## Basic Distinction

A **utility patent** protects the way an article is used and works.

A **design patent** protects the way an article looks.

The ornamental appearance for an article includes:
- Its shape/configuration
- Surface ornamentation applied to the article
- Or both

## Ornamentality Requirement

Ornamentality is a critical requirement for design patents.

A design consists of the visual ornamental characteristics embodied in, or applied to, an article of manufacture. The subject matter may relate to:
- The configuration or shape of an article
- The surface ornamentation applied to an article
- The combination of configuration and surface ornamentation

## Test for Ornamentality

A design must be **"primarily ornamental"**:
- The claimed design is viewed in its entirety
- Determine whether the overall appearance is dictated by the utilitarian purpose

The test used by courts often evaluates whether alternative designs could achieve the same function:
- If alternatives exist → likely ornamental → patentable
- If no alternatives exist → likely functional → NOT patentable as design

## Key Differences

### Patent Term
- **Utility**: 20 years from US filing date
- **Design**: 15 years from date of grant (filed on or after May 13, 2015)

### Maintenance Fees
- **Utility**: Required (3.5, 7.5, 11.5 years)
- **Design**: NOT required

### Claims
- **Utility**: Multiple claims allowed
- **Design**: Single claim only ("The ornamental design as shown and described")

### Examination
- **Utility**: 18-24 months wait for first action
- **Design**: Often faster (Rocket Docket since 2015)

### Disclosure
- **Utility**: Detailed written description, claims, drawings
- **Design**: Drawings dominant; brief written description

### Cost
- **Utility**: $15,000-50,000+ to grant
- **Design**: $1,500-3,000 to grant

## Dual Protection

Both design and utility patents may be obtained on an article if invention resides both in its utility and ornamental appearance.

## Design Patent Specific Requirements

### Drawings (Critical)
- Must show all sides of the article (top, bottom, left, right, front, back, perspective)
- Surface lines define the design
- Broken lines = unclaimed environment
- Solid lines = claimed design

### Single Claim
"The ornamental design for [article] as shown and described."

### Title
Must identify the article of manufacture (not abstract design)

## Functionality Doctrine

If a design is found to be functional:
- Cannot be protected by design patent
- May be protectable by utility patent if novel/non-obvious
- Trade dress may not protect either

## Common Rejections for Design Patents

- Non-statutory subject matter (e.g., naturally occurring forms)
- Lack of ornamentality (purely functional)
- Anticipation by prior art design
- Obviousness over combinations of references
- Insufficient disclosure (drawings not showing all views)
- Multiple embodiments shown (must be single design)
