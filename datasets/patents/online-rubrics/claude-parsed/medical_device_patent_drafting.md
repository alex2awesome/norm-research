# Medical Device Patent Drafting

## Overview

Medical device patents present a unique drafting and prosecution challenge, as the technology spans mechanical, electrical, software, and biological elements.

## Required Components

A typical filing includes:
- Title
- Abstract
- Background of the invention
- Detailed description
- Claims
- Supporting illustrations

## Multi-Format Claim Strategy

A strong medical device patent uses a mix of independent and dependent claims spanning:

- **Apparatus claims** — protect the physical device
- **Method claims** — safeguard how the device is used
- **System claims** — tie the device into broader workflows or infrastructure
- **Method of treatment claims** — how the device treats patients (limited applicability)
- **Method of manufacturing claims** — how the device is made

Each claim type provides a different angle of protection and theory of infringement.

## Patent Examination Process

Medical device patent applications typically face:
- 18-24 month wait times before initial examination
- Office action highlighting deficiencies or citing prior art
- Multiple rounds of prosecution typical
- Expedited examination available for additional fees

## Key Drafting Considerations

A well-drafted claim:
- Survives examination
- Withstands invalidity challenges
- Reads cleanly on competitor products
- Supports multiple infringement theories (direct, induced, contributory)

A poorly drafted claim:
- Invites office actions
- Creates narrowing amendments
- Faces claim construction rulings reducing commercial value

## Specific Considerations for Medical Devices

### Method-of-Treatment Limitations

Method-of-treatment claims face:
- §101 patent-eligibility scrutiny under Mayo
- Practitioner immunity (35 USC 287(c)) — physicians aren't liable for using patented surgical/medical procedures
- Different rules in EPO (excluded under Article 53(c))

### Mixed Hardware/Software Claims

- Specific structural elements paired with software functions
- Avoid "module" / "means" without enabling structure
- Consider 112(f) implications

### Regulatory Coordination

- Patents often align with FDA 510(k) or PMA submissions
- Class I, II, III device classification affects prosecution timing
- Consider trade secrets vs. patent for trade dress and software

## Common Rejection Issues

- Anticipation by prior art catheters/devices with minor structural differences
- Obviousness combinations of mechanical features
- §112 written description for novel aspects (geometries, materials)
- §101 for diagnostic methods (Mayo scrutiny)

## Trade Secret vs. Patent Strategy

- Patents: 20-year monopoly, public disclosure, enforceable
- Trade secrets: indefinite duration, no disclosure, lost on independent reverse engineering
- Common hybrid: patent the device, trade secret the manufacturing process
