# MPEP 706: Rejection of Claims Summary

## Overview
Section 706 establishes procedures for patent examiners to reject claims when they determine that the claimed invention lacks patentability. The process requires thorough prior art searching and clear communication of rejection grounds to applicants.

## Types of Rejections

**Prior Art Rejections (706.02)**
The most common rejection ground involves unpatentability under 35 U.S.C. § 102 (lack of novelty) or § 103 (obviousness). Examiners must "cite the best references at his or her command" and clearly explain pertinence when not apparent.

**Non-Prior Art Rejections (706.03)**
These include rejections for:
- Lack of utility (35 U.S.C. § 107)
- Ineligible subject matter (35 U.S.C. § 101)
- Inadequate written description or enablement (35 U.S.C. § 112)
- Double patenting violations
- Improper Markush groupings

Examiners must provide "full development of the reasons" rather than mere conclusions.

## Format Requirements

**Language Standards**
Rejection language must be "unequivocal" and clearly state all grounds. The examiner should designate "the particular part relied on as nearly as practicable."

**Required Elements**
Each rejection must:
- Specify which claims are rejected
- Clearly explain each ground of rejection
- Cite supporting references when applicable
- Indicate how rejections might be overcome when practicable

## Final Rejection Rules

**When Final Rejection Is Proper**
Final rejection may occur on the second or subsequent examination, except when the examiner introduces new grounds not necessitated by claim amendments or information disclosure statements filed with required fees.

**Premature Final Rejections**
Questions about prematureness must be raised while the application is pending before the primary examiner. This is a procedural matter reviewable only by petition, not appeal.

**Requirements for Making Final**
Before finality, a "clear issue should be developed between the examiner and applicant." The examiner must repeat all applicable rejection grounds and clearly state supporting reasons. Final rejections require form paragraphs (7.39-7.42) notifying applicants of appeal rights and options.

## Special Circumstances

**Previously Allowed Claims**
When rejecting a claim previously marked allowable, "great care should be exercised." Only primary examiners may make such rejections, and form paragraph 7.50 must notify applicants of the prior allowability.

**Claims Copied from Patents**
Examiners must determine if corresponding claims from existing patents are unpatentable. If the rejection ground also applies to the patent's claims, the Technology Center Director must approve the Office action.

**Defects in Form**
Examiners should make "constructive" rejections and "offer a definite suggestion for correction" rather than issuing bare objections.

## Uniform Standards

All patentability requirements must be uniformly applied across all art areas. The standard is "preponderance of the evidence"—examiners should reject claims "if, in view of the prior art and evidence of record, it is more likely than not that the claim is unpatentable."
