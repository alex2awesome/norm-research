# Foley Hoag IP & Tech: Post-Grant Considerations in Drafting

## Drafting With IPR in Mind

Inter Partes Review at the PTAB has become the dominant patent invalidity venue. A patent is challenged under 102 and 103 grounds using printed publications. PTAB applies broadest reasonable interpretation (BRI) for unexpired patents, claim-construction-narrower than district court.

## Drafting Implications

### Detailed Spec to Anchor Construction

The PTAB defers to clear spec definitions. A claim term defined in the spec is construed accordingly even under BRI. Use express lexicography for critical terms.

### Claim Differentiation Within Family

Claim differentiation is a less powerful tool at the PTAB. Do not rely on it to broaden one claim by reference to a narrower one.

### Multiple Independent Claims

If the petitioner challenges only some claims of a patent, the unchallenged claims survive even if the challenged ones do not. Multiple independent claims of varying scope create more pieces that can survive.

### Avoid Disparaging Statements

Anything in the spec or prosecution history that disparages an alternative approach can be quoted by the petitioner. Stay neutral.

### Long Spec for Anchoring

A long, detailed spec provides anchors for amendments at the PTAB. The PTAB allows amendments only with full spec support; sparse specs limit amendment options.

## PGR (Post-Grant Review)

PGR is broader than IPR (any ground of invalidity, not just 102/103) but only available within 9 months of grant. Most challenges go via IPR after the PGR window.

## EPR (Ex Parte Reexamination)

Less common but still available. Ex parte means the patentee handles examination directly with the examiner; petitioner has no further role after triggering.

## Reissue

The patentee can file a reissue to broaden or narrow claims within 2 years of issuance (broadening) or any time (narrowing). Useful when post-grant analysis reveals scope errors.

## Prosecution Choices That Affect Post-Grant

### Broad Claims Plus Narrow Backups

If only broad claims issue, an IPR challenge that succeeds wipes out the patent. If a graduated claim ladder issued, narrow backups survive.

### Avoid Excessive Argument

Every argument made during prosecution can be cited at the PTAB. Argue economically.

### File Continuations Pre-IPR

Once IPR is filed, the patentee's options for amendment are limited. Pending continuations allow new claims to be drafted to capture the accused product.

### Document Secondary Considerations

Commercial success, long-felt need, copying, industry praise. Build the file during prosecution and keep records.

## Reexamination Statistics

Modern IPR institution rates are roughly 60%, with ~80% of instituted petitions resulting in cancellation of at least some claims. Drafting must anticipate this challenge.
