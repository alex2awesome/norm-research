# Double Patenting Rejection and Terminal Disclaimer

## Two Types of Double Patenting

### 1. Statutory ("Same Invention") Double Patenting (35 USC 101)
Where the same invention is claimed in two patents/applications. **Cannot be overcome by a terminal disclaimer.**

### 2. Nonstatutory ("Obviousness-Type") Double Patenting (Judicial Doctrine)
Where claims are not patentably distinct from claims in another commonly owned patent/application. **Can be overcome by a terminal disclaimer.**

## When Double Patenting Applies

Obviousness-type double patenting occurs when applications have:
- The same inventive entity
- At least one common inventor
- A common applicant
- A common owner/assignee
- Or are owned by parties to a joint research agreement

## Public Policy Rationale

The nonstatutory-type rejection is grounded in public policy and primarily intended to prevent prolongation of the patent term by prohibiting claims in a second patent not patentably distinct from claims in a first patent.

Also prevents harassment by multiple assignees of patents covering essentially the same invention.

## Terminal Disclaimer as a Solution

A patent applicant or patent owner may obviate nonstatutory double patenting by filing a terminal disclaimer meeting the requirements of 37 CFR 1.321(c) or (d).

By filing a terminal disclaimer, the patent applicant agrees that:
- The later-filed patent (if granted) expires when the first patent does
- The patent will only be enforceable while both patents are commonly owned

## Two-Way Test

In limited circumstances (where applicant could not have filed both inventions in the same application due to actions of the PTO), a "two-way" obviousness test applies — both patents must be obvious in light of each other for double patenting to apply.

## Recent USPTO Proposals (2024)

The USPTO proposed rules requiring an agreement that the patent with the terminal disclaimer will be enforced only under certain conditions, but this proposal generated significant pushback.

## Strategic Considerations

- Filing a terminal disclaimer can lose patent term (later patent expires earlier)
- Common ownership requirement can affect licensing/assignment
- Restriction requirements protect against double patenting in continuations/divisionals (35 USC 121 safe harbor)
