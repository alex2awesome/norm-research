# Patent It Yourself (David Pressman): Drafting Excerpt

## The Pressman Drafting Approach

David Pressman's "Patent It Yourself" is a longstanding pro se patent guide. The drafting approach emphasizes: thorough invention documentation, careful prior art search, clear and detailed specification, and methodical claim drafting.

## Step 1 - Invention Documentation

Maintain a bound, dated inventor's notebook. Record:
- Date of conception (the moment you fully formed the idea)
- Description of the invention with sketches
- Modifications and improvements over time
- Tests and results
- Witnesses' signatures and dates

## Step 2 - Patentability Evaluation

Before drafting, evaluate:
- Is it patentable subject matter? (process, machine, manufacture, composition)
- Is it useful?
- Is it novel? (not in prior art)
- Is it non-obvious? (not obvious from combinations of prior art)
- Is it commercially viable?

## Step 3 - Prior Art Search

Pressman recommends a "patentability search" before filing - searching patent databases, technical literature, products on the market, and trade shows. The closest art shapes claim strategy.

## Step 4 - Drafting the Specification

The spec should:
- Describe the technical field briefly
- Provide background sufficient to understand the invention
- Summarize the invention at a high level
- Describe the drawings
- Detail the invention with reference to drawings
- Disclose multiple embodiments and alternatives

## Step 5 - Drafting the Claims

Pressman emphasizes:
- Use the structure: preamble, transition phrase, body
- Use "comprising" for open scope
- Use proper antecedent basis
- Recite each element clearly
- Draft 3-4 independent claims of varying scope
- Add dependent claims to provide fallback positions

## Step 6 - Drawings

Drawings should comply with USPTO rules: black ink, specific paper size, specific line weights, all elements labeled with reference numbers. Each figure should focus on a particular view or aspect.

## Step 7 - Filing the Application

File electronically via Patent Center. Include all required components and pay the fees. Confirm receipt and docket all relevant deadlines.

## Step 8 - Responding to Office Actions

Most applications receive office actions. Pressman recommends:
- Read the office action carefully
- Identify each rejection and the grounds
- Pull the cited references and analyze them
- Decide whether to argue, amend, or both
- Respond within the deadline
- Consider an examiner interview for complex issues

## Common Pro Se Pitfalls Pressman Warns About

- Filing too thin a provisional
- Drafting claims in lay English instead of patent format
- Failing to do a thorough prior art search
- Over-disclosing in a way that creates 102/103 admissions
- Missing deadlines
- Failing to file IDS

## Pressman on Hiring Help

Even Pressman, who wrote the book on pro se filing, recommends consulting a patent attorney for complex inventions or for the office action response phase. The cost of professional help often exceeds DIY savings when the application is rejected on technical drafting grounds.
