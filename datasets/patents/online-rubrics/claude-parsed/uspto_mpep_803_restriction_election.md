# Restriction Requirements and Election of Species

## Overview

Election is the designation by applicant of the one of two or more inventions or patentably distinct species, or of the group of patentably indistinct species, that will be prosecuted in the application.

## When Restriction Requirements Occur

When two or more independent and distinct inventions are presented for examination, the examiner may make a restriction requirement if a serious search and/or examination burden exists.

A requirement for restriction is permissible if:
1. There is a patentable difference between the species/inventions as claimed
2. There would be a serious search and/or examination burden on the examiner if restriction is not required

## Two-Step Test for Restriction

1. **Independent or distinct**: Are the inventions independent (no relation) or distinct (related but separately patentable)?
2. **Burden**: Is there a serious examination/search burden?

## Election of Species vs. Restriction Requirement

- **Restriction requirement**: aims to ensure that separate inventions are appropriately protected by separate patent applications.
- **Election of species**: pertains to choosing among multiple embodiments of the same invention for examination.

An Election of Species requirement is a type of restriction requirement where the examiner asks the applicant to choose a single species or a single grouping of patentably indistinct species for examination.

## Response Requirements

In the reply to the restriction requirement, applicant must:
1. Elect one invention/species for examination
2. Optionally traverse with specific reasons why restriction is in error

A traversal is needed to preserve the right to petition.

## Strategic Considerations

- Non-elected claims can be pursued in divisional applications
- 35 USC 121 safe harbor protects divisionals from double patenting if elected as a result of restriction
- Provisional election allows examination to proceed while preserving right to traverse
- Telephone restriction practice can speed up resolution

## PCT/National Stage Differences

- US restriction practice differs from international PCT "unity of invention" standard
- For PCT national stage applications, USPTO applies a unity of invention analysis (MPEP 1893.03)
