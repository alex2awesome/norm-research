# USPTO After Final Consideration Pilot Program 2.0 (AFCP 2.0)

## Program Overview

The goal of AFCP 2.0 was to improve pendency by reducing the number of Requests for Continued Examination (RCE) and encourage increased collaboration between applicant and examiner to advance prosecution.

## Key Features

- No additional fees required (only standard fees like extensions of time)
- Examiner could spend up to 3 hours considering after-final amendments
- Open to amendments meeting AFCP 2.0 requirements
- Could include interview between examiner and applicant
- Allowed limited claim amendments after final rejection

## AFCP 2.0 Requirements

To qualify for AFCP 2.0:
- File a response under 37 CFR 1.116
- Include an amendment to at least one independent claim that does NOT broaden scope
- Submit Form PTO/SB/434 (request for AFCP 2.0 consideration)
- Be willing to participate in interview if necessary

## Program Termination

The USPTO announced AFCP 2.0 ended on December 14, 2024.

## Reasons for Termination

For fiscal year 2022, the USPTO estimates it expended more than $15 million in costs associated with examiners considering AFCP 2.0 submissions. In response to public concerns about proposed fees, the USPTO ended the program rather than implement charges.

## Alternative Options Post-AFCP 2.0

After close of prosecution, applicants can use:

### 1. Standard After-Final Practice
- Proposed amendments that place the application either:
  - In condition for allowance
  - In better form for appeal
- Limited scope of allowable amendments

### 2. Examiner Interviews
- Pre-appeal request interviews available
- Cannot extend time period
- Beneficial for clarifying issues

### 3. Pre-Appeal Brief Conference Request
- 5-page summary of arguments
- Reviewed by panel of examiners
- Cheaper alternative to full appeal

### 4. Request for Continued Examination (RCE)
- Reopens prosecution
- Higher fee
- Allows broader amendments

### 5. Notice of Appeal + Appeal Brief
- PTAB review
- Most expensive option
- Most rigorous review

## Strategic Considerations Post-2024

- Applicants may rely more on RCEs and appeals
- Examiner interviews remain valuable
- Pre-appeal conference requests gain importance
- Consider after-final response strategy at time of final office action
