# Patent Litigation Strategy and Infringement Defenses

## Key Patent Infringement Defenses

Typical defenses include:
- Non-infringement
- Invalidity
- Unenforceability (inequitable conduct)
- Fraud
- Laches
- Estoppel
- Antitrust violations

## Primary Defense Strategies

### 1. Non-Infringement

The accused product or process does not infringe because it does not meet every element of any asserted patent claim. This is the **all-elements rule**.

**Strategies:**
- Identify missing elements
- Argue narrow claim construction
- Distinguish accused product technically
- Use prosecution history to limit claim scope

### 2. Patent Invalidity

Defenses based on patentability requirements:
- **§101**: Subject matter ineligibility (Alice/Mayo)
- **§102**: Anticipation (single reference disclosure)
- **§103**: Obviousness (combination of references)
- **§112**: Insufficient written description, enablement, or indefiniteness
- Improper inventorship

### 3. Prior Use and Public Disclosure

Showing the accused technology was publicly disclosed or in use before the patent was filed.

**35 USC 273 - Prior User Rights:**
- Defense to infringement (not invalidity)
- Internal commercial use 1 year before patent filing
- Limited scope (not transferable, except with business)

### 4. Inequitable Conduct (Unenforceability)

Patent rendered unenforceable due to:
- Material misrepresentation or omission
- During prosecution
- With intent to deceive USPTO

Therasense (Fed. Cir. 2011) tightened the standard:
- "But-for" materiality
- Single most reasonable inference must be intent to deceive

## Less-Known Defenses

### Implied License
Sale of unpatented component creates implied license to use it in patented combination.

### Patent Exhaustion (First Sale Doctrine)
After authorized first sale, patent rights are exhausted as to that item.

### Experimental Use
Limited defense for non-commercial research (very narrow in U.S.).

### Repair vs. Reconstruction
Buyer of patented item may "repair" but not "reconstruct."

### Patent Misuse
Patentee impermissibly extends patent rights (e.g., tying arrangements).

### Laches/Equitable Estoppel
Excessive delay in bringing suit (mostly eliminated by SCA Hygiene).

### Government Use Defense (28 USC 1498)
Government infringement: only remedy is suit against U.S. (not user).

## Litigation Strategy

### Early Case Assessment
- Understand scope of allegations
- Evaluate potential risks
- Preserve relevant evidence
- Identify weak points in plaintiff's case

### Resolution Options
- Direct settlement negotiations
- Mediation/arbitration
- Patent license agreements
- Cross-licensing

### Procedural Strategies

#### Motion Practice
- Motion to dismiss (especially §101)
- Motion for summary judgment (non-infringement, invalidity)
- Motion to transfer venue
- Motion to stay pending IPR

#### Discovery Strategy
- Source code review (software patents)
- Inventor depositions
- Prior art searches and depositions
- Expert testimony preparation

### Parallel Proceedings

#### IPR/PGR Strategy
- File IPR within 1 year of complaint
- Stay district court litigation
- Use IPR estoppel implications

#### ITC Investigation
- Section 337 proceedings
- Faster than district court
- Exclusion orders as remedy

## Damages Defenses

- Apportionment (only the patented feature's value)
- Non-infringing alternatives
- Standard royalty rates (not enhanced)
- Patent marking requirements (35 USC 287)
- Pre-suit damages limitations

## Willful Infringement Considerations

To avoid enhanced damages (up to 3x):
- Good faith belief of non-infringement/invalidity
- Avoid willful blindness
- Document due diligence efforts
- Consider opinion of counsel

## Settlement Considerations

- Cost-benefit of litigation
- Risk of preliminary injunction
- Reputation effects
- Cross-license potential
- Bargaining power dynamics
