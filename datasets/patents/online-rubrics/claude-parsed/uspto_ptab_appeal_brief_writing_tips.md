# USPTO Practice Tips for Writing Effective PTAB Appeal Briefs

## Present Strong Arguments

Present only the strongest arguments. Do not dilute strong arguments by including weaker arguments or arguments that have no bearing on the issues. Do not simply repeat arguments previously presented because those have already been evaluated and deemed unpersuasive by the examiner.

## Strategic Organization

You generally want the PTAB to read your strongest arguments first. Argue the most glaringly incorrect rejection(s) first — you don't have to start with the rejection of Claim 1.

## Argue Claims Separately

The PTAB will not evaluate the patentability of any claims not individually addressed. To increase chances for success:
- Separately argue as many dependent claims as possible
- Place such arguments under separate sub-headings for ease of readability
- Identify which claims rise/fall together
- Group claims thoughtfully

## Support with Evidence

Attorney argument cannot substitute for evidence. Cite evidence to support arguments. Any relied-upon evidence in the appeal brief must have been before the examiner during prosecution for the PTAB to consider it.

## Address Examiner Errors

Dispute factual errors and legal errors made by the examiner, including:
- Claim and reference misinterpretations
- Citations of non-analogous art
- Improper combinations under §103
- Failure to provide reasoned rationale (KSR)
- Failure to address all claim limitations

## Required Sections of Appeal Brief (37 CFR 41.37)

1. Real party in interest
2. Related appeals, interferences, and trials
3. Summary of claimed subject matter
4. Argument
5. Claims appendix
6. Evidence appendix (if applicable)
7. Related proceedings appendix (if applicable)

## Key Tips for Effective Briefs

- Keep arguments concise and focused
- Use claim charts to compare prior art with claim language
- Address each rejection as a separate ground
- Cite controlling precedent
- Avoid emotional or accusatory language
- Maintain professional tone toward examiner
