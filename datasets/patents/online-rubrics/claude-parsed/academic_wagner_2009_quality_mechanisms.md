# Patent Quality as Incentive-Engineering Problem

Wagner reframes patent quality from a doctrinal/examination problem into an incentive-engineering problem. The system's structural incentives drive low-quality patenting; quality reform requires changing the incentives, not just the rules.

## Core Argument

Wagner identifies systemic incentive failures that affirmatively encourage low-quality patents:

**Applicant Incentives**:
- Broad claims maximize litigation reach
- Functional language captures more competitor activity
- Continuation strategies allow late-drafted claims targeting competitor products
- Strategic ambiguity preserves litigation options

**Examiner Incentives**:
- Production quotas favor allowance over substantive rejection
- Time pressure favors superficial review
- Promotion criteria reward count, not quality
- Risk aversion favors allowance (rejection invites RCE/appeal work)

**System Incentives**:
- Validity presumption discourages post-grant challenges
- High litigation cost protects weak patents from challenge
- USPTO funded by user fees, creating allowance-rate-revenue dependency

These incentives interact to produce systematically low-quality patents. Reform must address all three layers.

## Quality Implications

### Drafting-Quality Incentives

Wagner's analysis implies the following quality-relevant criteria for evaluating drafting:

**High-Quality Drafting Markers**:
- Specific, structurally-supported claim language (resists invalidation)
- Avoidance of functional language without 112(f) treatment
- Honest scope: claims tied to actual disclosed embodiments
- Continuation discipline: continuations reflect inventor's actual development, not post-hoc competitor targeting

**Low-Quality Drafting Markers**:
- Functional/result-oriented claim language
- Genus claims unsupported by representative species
- Continuation chains drafted to capture later-developed competitor products
- Strategic ambiguity ("means for X" without defined structure)

### Examination-Quality Incentives

**High-Quality Examination Markers**:
- Substantive 112 rejections (not just 102/103)
- Examiner-driven prior-art search
- Multiple substantive office actions
- Examiner amendments narrowing overbroad terms

**Low-Quality Examination Markers**:
- First-action allowance
- Allowance based solely on applicant-cited art
- Single short office action
- No 112 engagement

### System-Level Quality Markers

Wagner argues quality reform requires:

- Reducing the validity presumption to neutral standards
- Strengthening post-grant review (PGR, IPR)
- Decoupling USPTO funding from allowance rates
- Tying examiner promotion to quality outcomes (downstream invalidation rates)

## The Incentive-Engineering Test

For evaluating any individual patent's quality:

**Drafting Incentive Test**:
- Did the applicant's drafting choices reflect quality (specific, supported, honest scope)?
- Or did they reflect strategic incentives (functional, unsupported, ambiguous)?

**Examination Incentive Test**:
- Did the examiner's behavior reflect quality (substantive search, multiple actions)?
- Or did it reflect production pressure (rapid allowance, minimal engagement)?

**System Incentive Test**:
- Has the patent been tested through post-grant review?
- Has it survived litigation challenge?
- Are there ongoing IPR petitions or inter partes challenges?

## Operative Quality Markers Derived from Wagner

**Strong Quality Signals**:
- Claims drafted with specific structural language
- Examination involved substantive 112 rejections
- Prosecution history shows genuine examiner-applicant negotiation
- Patent has survived post-grant challenge

**Weak Quality Signals**:
- Claims rely on functional/result-oriented language
- First-action allowance without rejection
- Continuation chain targeting competitor products
- Patent has not been tested through any adversarial process

## Reform Proposals (Quality-Relevant)

Wagner's proposed reforms target the incentive layer:

1. **Mandatory Pre-Grant Public Disclosure with Third-Party Submission**: Allowing third parties to submit prior art before grant
2. **Tiered Examination Fees**: Higher fees for more thorough examination
3. **Quality-Adjusted Examiner Metrics**: Tying examiner advancement to downstream patent survival
4. **Reduced Validity Presumption**: Issued patents should not enjoy strong "clear and convincing" presumption
5. **Strengthened Post-Grant Review**: Cheap, fast administrative challenges

## Synthesis

Wagner's framework supports a quality-evaluation principle: **patent quality is the residual of incentive alignment**. When applicant, examiner, and system incentives are aligned with quality, individual patents are high quality. When incentives diverge, even patents that pass examination are systematically low quality.

For evaluating any patent:

1. What incentives drove the drafting choices visible in the claims?
2. What incentives drove the examination choices visible in the prosecution history?
3. What system incentives have been tested (post-grant review, litigation)?
4. Does the patent's content reflect quality choices or strategic shortcuts?

A patent that passes all three incentive tests is reliably high quality. One that passes none is, in expectation, in the lower-quality tail regardless of nominal validity.
