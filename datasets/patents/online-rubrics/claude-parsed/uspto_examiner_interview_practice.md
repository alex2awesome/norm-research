# USPTO Patent Examiner Interview Strategy

## Key Interview Timing & Preparation

Most interviews occur after a non-final office action has been issued, when the application is top of mind with the examiner. The applicant should:
- Submit an agenda and any proposed claim amendments at least 24 hours prior to the interview
- Propose interview dates at least 5 days out (per USPTO automated interview request form)

## Preparation Best Practices

- Carefully review the rejections and prior art cited by the examiner
- Look beyond the specific passages cited by the examiner — make sure arguments/amendments are not foreclosed by other disclosure in the cited prior art
- Research the examiner's statistics and recent cases addressing similar issues
- Especially for §101 rejections, see what types of arguments and amendments other applicants used to persuade the same examiner
- Use Patent Examiner Statistics websites (e.g., Patent Bots, Juristat)

## Effective Interview Conduct

- The ideal interview lasts no more than 30 minutes
- View the interview as a two-way discussion rather than an argument
- Don't just highlight disagreements — provide potential solutions
- Actively listen to the examiner's comments
- Ask clarifying questions
- Be receptive to suggestions

## Key Strategies

- **Multiple Options**: Give the examiner different strategy options (e.g., different proposed claim amendments). This gives the examiner an opportunity to determine which approach would likely work best.
- **Joint Hypothesis**: Find a "yes" path together
- **Respect for Examiner's Constraints**: Examiners have production quotas; offering allowable subject matter helps both sides

## Building Professional Relationships

Being courteous and respectful to the examiner, especially when disagreements arise, is crucial to maintaining a good relationship and reaching a favorable outcome.

## Interview Summary

After the interview:
- The examiner records an Interview Summary in the file wrapper
- Applicant must file an Interview Summary identifying topics discussed (37 CFR 1.133)
- Substance of agreements should be confirmed in writing

## When Not to Request an Interview

- When the issues are purely formal (e.g., drawing objections)
- After Final unless requesting AFCP 2.0
- When the examiner has not yet substantively examined the case
