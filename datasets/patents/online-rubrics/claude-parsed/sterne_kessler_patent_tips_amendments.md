# Sterne Kessler Patent Tips: Claim Amendments

## Amend Surgically, Not Broadly

When responding to a rejection, amend only the claim element that is necessary to overcome the cited art. Each amendment creates prosecution history estoppel that may foreclose later doctrine of equivalents arguments under Festo Corp. v. Shoketsu Kinzoku Kogyo Kabushiki Co. (Fed. Cir. en banc 2003).

## Identify the Reason for Amendment

In your remarks, state explicitly which limitation overcame the rejection and why. Festo creates a presumption of estoppel; the patentee can rebut by showing the equivalent was unforeseeable at the time of amendment, the rationale was tangential, or "some other reason" exists. Articulating the rationale up front preserves rebuttal arguments.

## Avoid "Cosmetic" Amendments

Amending claim 1 by changing "device" to "apparatus" without affecting scope is wasteful. The amendment may still create estoppel and signals lack of strategy.

## Watch the New Matter Bar

Any amendment that adds subject matter not supported by the originally filed disclosure is rejected as new matter (35 USC 132). Before amending, confirm the new language is fully supported in the as-filed spec, not just in the prior art that you are distinguishing over.

## Cancellation vs. Amendment

If a claim cannot be saved by reasonable amendment, cancel it cleanly rather than amending it into incoherence. A canceled claim creates less estoppel than a heavily amended one.

## Claim Differentiation Effects

If you amend claim 1 by adding limitation X, claim 1 now requires X. If a dependent claim 2 already had X, the claim differentiation doctrine may be disrupted. Audit the claim hierarchy after each amendment.

## Add New Claims, Not Just Amend Existing Ones

If you have spec support for an additional inventive embodiment, add new claims rather than amending existing ones. New claims do not have prosecution history attached and can be argued cleanly.

## Group-Wide Amendments

If multiple claims are rejected on the same ground, amend them in parallel with consistent language. Inconsistent amendments invite further rejection.

## Markup vs. Clean Format

Amended claims should be presented in markup (additions underlined, deletions bracketed) per 37 CFR 1.121. After substantial amendments, also include a clean version of the full claim set for clarity.

## Phone Interview Before Major Amendment

If your amendment is non-trivial, call the examiner first. A 15-minute interview often reveals exactly what amendment will allow the case, saving a round of correspondence.
