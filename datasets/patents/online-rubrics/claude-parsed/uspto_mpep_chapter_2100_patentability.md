# MPEP Chapter 2100: Patentability - Table of Contents & Key Requirements

## Overview
Chapter 2100 provides examination guidelines for determining patent eligibility and patentability across four statutory requirements under 35 U.S.C.

## Key Statutory Requirements

### Section 2104-2106: Subject Matter Eligibility (35 U.S.C. § 101)

**Four Categories of Statutory Subject Matter:**
- Processes
- Machines
- Manufactures
- Compositions of matter

**Judicial Exceptions (Step 2A):**
1. Abstract ideas
2. Laws of nature and natural phenomena
3. Products of nature

**Markedly Different Characteristics Test:** Examiners evaluate whether claims possess characteristics "markedly different" from naturally occurring items.

**Integration into Practical Application (Step 2A Prong Two):** Claims must show integration of exceptions into practical, real-world applications rather than mere recitation.

**Significantly More Analysis (Step 2B):** Determining whether claims amount to "significantly more" than judicial exceptions through improvements to computer functionality or other technologies.

### Section 2107: Utility Requirement (35 U.S.C. § 101)

Applicants must demonstrate the invention possesses specific, substantial, and credible utility. Special considerations apply to therapeutic or pharmacological utilities.

### Section 2120-2159: Prior Art & Novelty (35 U.S.C. § 102)

**Post-AIA Standards (Effective March 16, 2013):**
- Patented, described in printed publications, or publicly available before effective filing date
- U.S. patent documents naming another inventor filed before effective filing date

**Grace Period Exceptions:**
- Inventor-originated disclosures (one-year grace period)
- Prior public disclosures by inventor

**Pre-AIA Standards:**
- Knowledge or use by others in the country
- Public use or sale in the country
- Foreign patents or publications
- Prior U.S. applications

### Section 2141-2158: Non-Obviousness (35 U.S.C. § 103)

**Prima Facie Obviousness Requires:**
1. Scope and content of prior art
2. Differences between prior art and claimed invention
3. Level of ordinary skill in the art
4. Motivation to combine references
5. Reasonable expectation of success

**Examiners May Rely On:**
- Implicit disclosures in references
- Scientific theory and common knowledge
- Art-recognized equivalence and suitability
- Homologs and structural analogs

### Section 2161-2190: Specification & Claims (35 U.S.C. § 112)

**Written Description Requirement (§ 112(a)):**
Claims must find adequate support within the specification demonstrating the inventor possessed the full scope of the claimed invention.

**Enablement Requirement (§ 112(a)):**
Specification must enable persons skilled in the art to "make and use" the invention without undue experimentation.

**Clarity Requirement (§ 112(b)):**
Claims must particularly point out and distinctly claim the invention. Rejection occurs when terms are indefinite or claim scope is unclear.

**Common Indefiniteness Issues:**
- Functional limitations without structure
- Relative terminology lacking clarity
- Antecedent basis failures
- Aggregated limitations without meaningful relationships

## Examiner Rejection Standards

| Ground | Burden | Applicant Response |
|---|---|---|
| §101 Eligibility | Prima facie rejection using claim analysis | Demonstrate integration into practical application or "significantly more" limitations |
| §102 Anticipation | Single reference disclosure or admission | Antedate reference, show non-enabling disclosure, or claim narrow distinctions |
| §103 Obviousness | Motivation + reasonable expectation of success | Secondary considerations, unpredictability, or narrow claim scope |
| §112 Definiteness | Terms lack ordinary meaning | Narrow scope, add antecedent basis, provide structural limitations |

## Critical Examination Procedures

**Formulating Rejections:** Examiners must clearly identify which limitation(s) trigger rejection and provide reasoning connecting evidence to conclusions.

**Streamlined Analysis (§ 2106.06):** Eligibility may be self-evident for clearly patentable subject matter; detailed analysis waived.

**Affidavits/Declarations (§ 2155):** Applicants may overcome prior art rejections by showing inventor-originated disclosures or that references lack enablement.

**Claim Interpretation:** Broadest reasonable interpretation applied during prosecution; terms given plain meaning with reference to specifications.
