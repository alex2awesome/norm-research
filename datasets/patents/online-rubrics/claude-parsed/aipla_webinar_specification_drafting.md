# AIPLA Webinar: Specification Drafting Essentials

## The Specification Is the Foundation

Every claim term, every amendment, every continuation depends on the specification. A weak spec cannot be patched later because new matter cannot be added. Spec investment at the outset pays dividends across the patent lifecycle.

## The Three Disclosure Requirements

35 USC 112(a) imposes three disclosure requirements: written description (showing possession), enablement (showing the POSITA can make and use), and best mode (now non-defensive but still required). All three must be satisfied for every claim term.

## Written Description - Show You Had It

The spec must show that the inventor was in possession of the claimed subject matter as of the filing date. For genus claims in unpredictable arts, this means disclosing representative species and unifying structural features (Ariad Pharmaceuticals v. Eli Lilly, Fed. Cir. 2010).

## Enablement - Show How to Make and Use

The spec must enable a POSITA to make and use the invention without undue experimentation. The Wands factors (In re Wands, Fed. Cir. 1988) inform the analysis: nature of the invention, state of the prior art, predictability, breadth of the claims, working examples, etc.

## Best Mode - Disclose Your Best Implementation

Although the AIA removed best mode failure as a basis for invalidity, the requirement still technically applies. More importantly, withholding the best mode allows competitors to patent it on top of your patent.

## Define Terms Affirmatively

If a claim term has a meaning specific to your application, define it in the spec ("As used herein, X means Y"). Express definitions are respected by courts (Phillips v. AWH).

## Multiple Embodiments

Disclose at least three embodiments: the simplest, the prototype, and a commercially-relevant variant. More embodiments support broader claim construction and provide fallbacks during prosecution.

## Avoid "The Invention Is" Statements

Phrasing the spec as "the invention is X" or "the present invention requires Y" can be construed as defining the invention narrowly. SciMed (Fed. Cir. 2001) is the canonical bad example. Use "an embodiment is" or "in some examples."

## Background Section - Brief and Neutral

Long backgrounds invite admissions and disclaimers. Modern practice favors short backgrounds focused on technical context, avoiding characterizations of prior art shortcomings.

## Summary Section - Mirror the Claims

The Summary should track the independent claims at a high level. Avoid summarizing the invention in ways that go beyond what is claimed.

## Drawings As Anchors

Walk the spec through the drawings figure by figure. Each reference number should be defined in text and shown in figure. The spec-figures correspondence is critical for examiner understanding and for claim construction.

## Section Headings Are Required

37 CFR 1.77 specifies the order and headings of spec sections: Title, Cross-References, Statement Regarding Federally Sponsored Research, Background, Brief Summary, Brief Description of Drawings, Detailed Description, Claims, Abstract.

## Length

Modern utility patent specs typically run 30-100 pages. Length is not a virtue in itself; useful disclosure is. Aim for the shortest spec that fully discloses every claim term and reasonable variations.
