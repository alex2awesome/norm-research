# Springer Nature Book Reviewer Guidelines

## Core Responsibilities

Reviewers must ensure "consistent, fair and timely review of material sent for review" and disclose any qualifications concerns. They should provide honest feedback while avoiding "unfair negative comments, personal or unjustified criticism."

## Conflict of Interest Disclosure

Reviewers must reveal potential biases, including financial interests (grants, stocks, consultation fees) and non-financial ones (professional relationships, advisory positions, mentoring connections).

## Institutional and Relationship Restrictions

Reviewers cannot assess works from authors or editors at their current institution, with limited exceptions for very large institutions or niche fields requiring publisher approval. Recent mentoring or collaboration relationships within three years must also be disclosed.

## Confidentiality Obligations

Reviewers must not "reveal details of the Work such as data, information, interpretation, discussion or review reports" without author and publisher permission. They cannot repurpose unreported material for their own research.

## Standards Reference

Springer Nature aligns with the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE), whose peer reviewer guidelines apply to book reviewers as well.
