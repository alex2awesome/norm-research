# EQUATOR Network Reporting Guidelines

## What is the EQUATOR Network?

The EQUATOR Network is a global collaborative initiative launched in June 2008 to improve the value and reliability of published health research by promoting transparent and accurate reporting and wider use of high-quality, robust reporting guidelines.

## Reporting Guidelines and Checklists

A reporting guideline is "A checklist, flow diagram, or structured text to guide authors in reporting a specific type of research, developed using explicit methodology." The EQUATOR Network website is hosted by the University of Oxford and currently includes more than 400 reporting guidelines and extensions.

## Using Checklists in Peer Review

The EQUATOR Network provides specific support for peer reviewers:

- The toolkit includes template instructions to peer reviewers and editable versions of common checklists with additional explanation for reviewers to use.
- Some journals that have implemented reporting guidelines ask a first peer reviewer to check guideline compliance before recruiting additional reviewers, and if the manuscript fails, peer review halts and the article is returned.

## Major Reporting Guidelines (a non-exhaustive list)

- CONSORT — Randomized trials
- PRISMA — Systematic reviews
- STROBE — Observational studies
- ARRIVE — Animal research
- SRQR / COREQ — Qualitative research
- CARE — Case reports
- SPIRIT — Trial protocols
- STARD — Diagnostic accuracy
- TRIPOD — Prediction models
- CHEERS — Health economic evaluations
- AGREE — Practice guidelines
- ENTREQ — Enhancing transparency in qualitative research synthesis

## Using a Reporting Guideline During Peer Review

Reviewers can:
1. Identify the appropriate guideline for the study type.
2. Check the manuscript against the guideline's checklist.
3. Note items that are missing, unclear, or inadequately reported.
4. Recommend the authors complete the checklist for resubmission.
