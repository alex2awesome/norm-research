# What Makes a Great Peer Reviewer? Tips from Nature Research Editors

## What Makes a Helpful Review

1. **Substantiate every statement** — about a methodology weakness or about the significance of a result, with detailed arguments.
2. **Maintain a constructive tone** even when there is a lot to criticise.
3. **Verify technical soundness** — manuscript is technically sound and claims sufficiently supported by the data.
4. **Check for transparency** — enough detail that other groups have, in principle, enough information to repeat the experiments.
5. **Assess methodology appropriateness** — is the methodology appropriate for the question?
6. **Be transparent about expertise limits** — explicitly state the limits of your technical expertise in your report.
7. **Provide contextual significance** — give specific context on the significance of the results and interpretations.

## Practical Guidance

8. **Know when to decline** — acknowledge time / conflicts rather than accepting every request.
9. **Suggest alternatives** — recommend a well-qualified colleague when declining.
10. **Maintain visibility** — keep a clear online record (homepage, Google Scholar, ORCID) so editors can find you.
