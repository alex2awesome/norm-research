# DFG Review Criteria — Reviewer Rubric (Germany)

Germany's principal public research funder. Reviewers across all DFG programmes (Individual Grants, Priority Programmes, Collaborative Research Centres, Research Units) apply a uniform set of standards documented in DFG forms 10.20 (general) and programme-specific notes (e.g. 60.14 for CRCs).

## Four Main Review Criteria

### 1. Quality of the Project
Reviewers assess:
- Scientific quality and originality of the proposed research.
- Theoretical and methodological foundation.
- State of the art — situating the project in current research.
- Innovative potential — significance of expected results for the field.

### 2. Objectives and Work Programme
Reviewers assess:
- Clarity, plausibility, and feasibility of the research objectives.
- Coherence and adequacy of the planned work programme.
- Realism of the timeline and milestones.
- Appropriateness of methods to the questions.
- Quality of preliminary work.

### 3. Applicants' Qualifications
Reviewers assess:
- Track record of the applicant(s) commensurate with career stage.
- Demonstrated expertise relevant to the proposed project.
- For collaborative projects: complementarity of the team.
- For early-career researchers: capacity for independent research.
- DFG explicitly requires assessment **without recourse to non-scientific criteria** such as age, gender, family obligations, origin, or health restrictions.

### 4. Work and Research Environment
Reviewers assess:
- Availability of necessary infrastructure (equipment, data, archives).
- Institutional support and embedding.
- Access to collaborators, students, networks.
- Adequacy of requested resources to the project scope.

## DFG-Specific Reviewer Obligations (from form 10.20)

### Conflict of Interest — Mandatory Recusal
Reviewers must decline if:
- Close scientific cooperation with the applicant in the last 3 years.
- Teacher / student / supervisor relationship.
- First-degree relatives or comparable personal relationship.
- Membership in same institute / department.
- Significant material or personal interest in the outcome.
- Direct competitive relationship with the applicant.

### Confidentiality
- Treat the proposal as confidential.
- Do not pass it to third parties (including students or collaborators) without express DFG permission.
- Do not exploit ideas from the proposal in own work.
- Destroy or return copies after the review.

### Quality and Substantiation
- Provide a written, substantiated review.
- Justify scores with explicit reasoning tied to the four criteria.
- Distinguish between fundamental concerns and minor remarks.
- Avoid statements that could be construed as discriminatory.
- Use standardised scoring (numeric scale defined per programme).

### Timeliness
- Respond to invitations within the requested period.
- Submit the review by the deadline; notify the office promptly if delay is unavoidable.

## Three-Stage Quality Assurance

DFG reviews are evaluated through:
1. **Individual reviewers** — written assessments per the four criteria.
2. **Review boards** — elected discipline-specific committees that synthesise reviews and rank proposals.
3. **Joint Committee** — final funding decision with consideration of programme balance and discipline strategy.

## Independent Quality Check

A separate Quality and Programme Management unit checks every review process for:
- Procedural compliance.
- Applicant eligibility verification.
- Review consistency across panels.
- Ethics-approval verification.
- Detection of potentially discriminatory language in reviews.
