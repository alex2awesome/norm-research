# Cochrane MECIR — Reviewer Rubric for Systematic Reviews

The Methodological Expectations for Cochrane Intervention Reviews (MECIR) are the binding evaluation criteria peer reviewers and editors apply to Cochrane systematic reviews. Each item is classified **Mandatory** (must be met or justified, otherwise grounds for not publishing) or **Highly Desirable**.

## Conduct Standards (C-codes — for the review process)

### Defining the Question (Mandatory)
- C1 PICO clearly specified (Population, Intervention, Comparator, Outcomes).
- C2 Pre-specify outcomes including primary and secondary outcomes.
- C3 Pre-specify subgroup analyses; classify as a priori vs post hoc.

### Searching (Mandatory)
- C4 Search at least CENTRAL, MEDLINE, Embase.
- C5 Justify any restrictions by language, publication year, or status.
- C6 Search trials registers and conference proceedings.
- C7 Search reference lists of included studies and relevant reviews.
- C8 Document search strategies in full.
- C9 Re-run searches close to publication.

### Selecting Studies (Mandatory)
- C10 Two reviewers independently screen.
- C11 Resolve disagreements with a third reviewer.
- C12 Document reasons for exclusion at full-text stage.

### Collecting Data (Mandatory)
- C13 Use a piloted data-extraction form.
- C14 Two reviewers independently extract.
- C15 Contact study authors when data are missing or unclear.

### Risk of Bias (Mandatory)
- C16 Use Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool for randomised trials, ROBINS-I for non-randomised.
- C17 Two reviewers independently assess.
- C18 Justify each judgement with a quote / citation.

### Synthesising Data (Mandatory)
- C19 Pre-specify meta-analytic methods.
- C20 Justify choice of fixed vs random effects model.
- C21 Investigate heterogeneity (I², Tau²).
- C22 Plan and conduct sensitivity analyses.
- C23 Investigate publication bias when ≥10 studies (funnel plot, Egger's test).
- C24 Use GRADE to rate certainty of evidence per outcome.

## Reporting Standards (R-codes — for the manuscript)

- R1 Plain Language Summary written for non-specialists.
- R2 Abstract follows Cochrane structured format.
- R3 Background includes description of condition, intervention, why review needed.
- R4 Methods section is sufficient to allow replication.
- R5 PRISMA flow diagram of study selection.
- R6 Characteristics of included and excluded studies tables.
- R7 Forest plots for each outcome.
- R8 Summary-of-findings table per comparison using GRADE.
- R9 Discussion addresses summary, completeness, certainty, biases, agreements with other reviews.
- R10 Conclusions distinguish implications for practice and for research.

## Reviewer Operational Norms (Mandatory items)

Cochrane peer reviewers (Sign-off and methods/clinical reviewers) must explicitly evaluate:

1. **Question relevance and clarity** — PICO well-formed?
2. **Search comprehensiveness** — were appropriate databases searched without unjustified restrictions?
3. **Bias reduction in study selection and data extraction** — independent duplicate screening?
4. **Validity of synthesis methods** — was meta-analysis appropriate? Heterogeneity adequately investigated?
5. **Risk-of-bias assessment quality** — was RoB 2 applied correctly with substantiated judgements?
6. **GRADE certainty ratings** — are downgrades/upgrades justified per outcome?
7. **Honesty of conclusions** — do conclusions match the certainty of evidence?
8. **Conflicts of interest** — declared by review authors and assessed by reviewer?
9. **Reporting completeness** — does the review meet PRISMA + Cochrane structural requirements?

## Distinctive Norms

- **No conclusion may be stronger than its GRADE certainty.** Reviewers must flag overstated conclusions.
- **Disagreement among study quality assessors is itself a quality signal.** A review reporting 100% agreement should be questioned.
- **Reviewer must check at least one included-study extraction against source paper** as a spot check.
