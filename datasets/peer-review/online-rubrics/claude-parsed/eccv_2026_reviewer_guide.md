# ECCV 2026 Reviewer Guidelines

## Core Review Principles

Each paper that is accepted should be technically sound and make a contribution to the field. Reviewers should look for what is good or stimulating in the paper and what knowledge advance it has made.

Reviewers are encouraged to embrace novel, brave concepts, even if they have not been tested on many datasets. The fact that a proposed method does not exceed state-of-the-art accuracy on an existing benchmark dataset is not grounds for rejection by itself.

## Contribution Types Framework (NEW)

A major new feature of ECCV 2026 is the use of **Contribution Types**. Since computer vision research encompasses a wide range of work—from theoretical foundations to practical systems, from new algorithms to community datasets, and from early-stage ideas to mature deployments—ECCV 2026 has introduced Contribution Types to support fair, nuanced, and context-aware reviewing.

Authors assign one Contribution Type to their paper, which specifies how reviewers should assess it.

Contribution types include (illustrative):
- Theoretical foundations
- New algorithms
- New datasets / benchmarks
- New systems / applications
- Empirical analyses
- Position / opinion papers
- Survey / synthesis

Each type has its own evaluation criteria and weighting of factors.

## Review Quality Standards

Reviewers should be specific and detailed in their reviews. Discussion (more than scores) helps authors, fellow reviewers, and Area Chairs understand the basis for recommendations, including specific feedback on ways authors can improve their papers.

**Minor flaws** that can be easily corrected should not be a reason to reject a paper.

## Important Policies

- **No LLMs for reviews/meta-reviews**: Large language models (LLMs) are NOT allowed to be used to write reviews or meta-reviews, whether run locally or via an API.
- **Strict deadlines**: April 21, 2026 review submission deadline.
- **Confidentiality**: Submissions are proprietary; do not share.

## Conflict of Interest

Conflicts include:
- Same institution
- Recent collaborators (3 years)
- Advisor-advisee
- Family members
- Recent close personal relationships
