# Frontiers Peer Review Guidelines

## Core Principles

Frontiers employs a quality-based acceptance model rather than rejection-rate targets. The organization states: "We don't reject manuscripts based on their perceived potential impact, nor do we set a rejection rate."

The process involves three key decision-makers: handling editors recommend rejection, editors decide acceptance, and chief editors handle acceptance/rejection determinations.

## Manuscript Acceptance Criteria (VALID)

Submissions must meet five standards:
- **V**alid research question and hypothesis
- **A**ppropriate, transparent methodology
- **L**anguage clarity and presentation standards
- **I**n line with editorial and ethical policies
- **D**riven by adequate literature review

## Rejection Grounds

Manuscripts face rejection for: invalid research questions, methodological errors, lack of originality/plagiarism, insufficient language quality, ethical violations, authorship policy breaches, biased references, misleading conclusions, or pseudoscientific inquiry.

## Peer Review Process

**Full reviews** (most article types) involve two phases:

1. **Independent Review**: Reviewers assess manuscripts separately using standardized templates within ten days
2. **Interactive Review**: Authors and reviewers engage in real-time forum discussions to address concerns

**Short reviews** (Editorials, Classifications) proceed directly to interactive phase with potentially only the handling editor.

## Quality Standards for Reviewers/Editors

Participants must:
- Possess relevant expertise
- Maintain objectivity
- Complete thorough assessments
- Respond promptly
- Behave professionally
- Avoid coercive citations

The guidelines emphasize that "Participants in the peer review will be removed from their assignments if they do not adhere to" established standards.

## Single Anonymized Model

Frontiers operates a single anonymized model — reviewers know who the authors are, but reviewer identities are anonymous unless they choose to be acknowledged on publication.
