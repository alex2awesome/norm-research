# ARRIVE 2.0 Guidelines for Reporting Animal Research

## Overview

The ARRIVE guidelines (Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments) are a checklist of recommendations for the full and transparent reporting of research involving animals — maximizing the quality and reliability of published research, and enabling others to better scrutinize, evaluate and reproduce it.

## Background and Development

The NC3Rs developed the ARRIVE guidelines in 2010 to help authors and journals identify the minimum information necessary to report in publications describing in vivo experiments. The guidelines are recommended by more than a thousand journals.

## ARRIVE 2.0 Updates

A Delphi exercise was used to prioritize and divide the items of the guidelines into 2 sets:
- **The "ARRIVE Essential 10"** — the minimum requirement
- **The "Recommended Set"** — describes the research context

## ARRIVE Essential 10 Items

The Essential 10 are the basic minimum that must be included in any manuscript describing animal research. Without this information readers and reviewers cannot assess the reliability of the findings:

1. Study design
2. Sample size
3. Inclusion and exclusion criteria
4. Randomisation
5. Blinding
6. Outcome measures
7. Statistical methods
8. Experimental animals (species, strain, sex, age)
9. Experimental procedures
10. Results

## Recommended Set Items

Complement the Essential 10 with additional context:
- Abstract
- Background
- Objectives
- Ethical statement
- Housing and husbandry
- Animal care and monitoring
- Interpretation/scientific implications
- Generalisability/translation
- Protocol registration
- Data access
- Declaration of interests

## Reviewer Use

Reviewers should use the ARRIVE checklist to ensure animal research papers report all essential information for reproducibility and ethical evaluation.
