# SPIRIT 2025 Statement: Trial Protocol Reporting

## Overview

SPIRIT (Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials) is a guideline for the minimum content of a clinical trial protocol.

## Checklist Versions

- **SPIRIT 2013**: 33-item checklist applies to protocols for all clinical trials, focuses on content rather than format.
- **SPIRIT 2025 (Updated)**: Evidence-based checklist of **34 minimum items** to address in a trial protocol, plus a diagram illustrating the schedule of enrollment, interventions and assessments.

## Purpose

The checklist recommends a full description of what is planned; it does not prescribe how to design or conduct a trial. By providing guidance for key content, the SPIRIT recommendations facilitate the drafting of high-quality protocols.

## Key Content Areas

The checklist covers:
- **Administrative information** — title, registration, protocol version, funding, roles
- **Introduction** — background and rationale, objectives, trial design
- **Methods: Participants, interventions, outcomes** — study setting, eligibility criteria, interventions, outcomes
- **Methods: Assignment of interventions** — allocation, blinding
- **Methods: Data collection, management, analysis** — data collection methods, data management, statistical methods
- **Methods: Monitoring** — data monitoring, harms, auditing
- **Ethics and dissemination** — research ethics approval, protocol amendments, consent, confidentiality, declaration of interests, access to data, ancillary care, dissemination policy
- **Appendices** — informed consent materials, biological specimens

## SPIRIT 2025 Updates

Notable changes:
- New open science section
- Additional emphasis on harms assessment
- Description of interventions and comparators
- New item on patient and public involvement in trial design, conduct and reporting

## Reviewer Use

When peer reviewing trial protocols, reviewers can use the SPIRIT checklist to verify all essential items are addressed.

## Companion Reporting Guideline

SPIRIT (protocols) is the protocol counterpart to CONSORT (results reporting).
