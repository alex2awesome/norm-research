# PubPeer — Post-Publication Peer Review Commenter Rubric

PubPeer is the principal post-publication peer-review platform (founded 2012). Commenters identify concerns in already-published papers; the site has become a primary mechanism by which image manipulation and data fabrication are detected.

## Categories of Concerns Commenters Flag

PubPeer commenters typically raise issues in the following categories — these define the operational rubric of post-publication review:

### Image-Integrity Concerns
- **Western-blot duplication / band reuse** within or across figures.
- **Microscopy image reuse** — same image used to represent different conditions.
- **Inappropriate splicing / band cropping** with no marked boundary.
- **Inappropriate brightness/contrast adjustments** that obscure structure.
- **Pixel-level duplication** detectable by image-forensics tools (Forensically, ImageTwin).
- **Image rotation, flip, or scaling** to disguise reuse.

### Data-Integrity Concerns
- **Statistical impossibilities** — variance equal to zero where it should not be, GRIM-fail means, terminal-digit anomalies.
- **Data inconsistencies** between text, tables, and figures.
- **Implausible precision** — reported values incompatible with the measurement instrument.
- **Outlier patterns** suggesting fabrication or selective reporting.
- **Missing data not flagged**; or **disappearing/changing data** between manuscript versions.

### Methodological Concerns
- **Non-replication** of the published findings by independent groups.
- **Unspecified randomisation** or blinding.
- **Inadequate sample size** undermining the reported effects.
- **Wrong statistical test** for the data type.

### Citation and Authorship Concerns
- **Plagiarism** — text similarity with prior literature.
- **Citation manipulation** — citation cartels, missing relevant prior work.
- **Authorship disputes** — undeclared contributions, ghost authorship.

## Commenter Conduct Norms (PubPeer Site Rules)

- **Use only publicly verifiable facts** (rule formally adopted in 2016).
- **No allegations of misconduct** — describe the observation, do not impute intent.
- **One concern per comment** — avoid combining multiple observations into a single post.
- **Cite sources** — link to specific page, figure, or sentence.
- **Avoid combining multiple observations** in a single post; allows targeted response.
- **Neutral tone** — explanatory, not accusatory.
- **Anonymity is permitted** but commenters are encouraged to substantiate every claim publicly.
- **Authors are notified** automatically and given right of reply.

## Author-Response Norms

- Authors are encouraged to respond on PubPeer rather than ignore concerns.
- Responses should address each numbered point with supporting documentation (raw data, original blots, code).
- Where the commenter's concern is correct, authors should issue a correction, retraction, or notice of concern in the journal.

## Significance for Rubric Design

The PubPeer concern taxonomy (image, data, methods, citation/authorship) is now the de facto post-publication review checklist. Many journal pre-publication peer-review forms have absorbed elements (image-integrity checks, statistical-plausibility checks) into structured reviewer questions. Tools like ImageTwin and Proofig automate parts of the image-duplication detection that PubPeer commenters pioneered.

## Limits Recognised by the Platform

- PubPeer comments are **not** themselves authoritative judgements of misconduct.
- Authors retain right of reply and the journal/institution makes any final misconduct determination.
- The platform deliberately avoids editorial gatekeeping in order to maximise discovery, accepting that some comments will be ill-founded.
