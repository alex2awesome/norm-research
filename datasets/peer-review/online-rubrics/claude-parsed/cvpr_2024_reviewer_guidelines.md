# CVPR 2024 Reviewer Guidelines

## Core Evaluation Principles

Reviewers should assess whether papers are "technically sound and make a contribution to the field." The guidelines emphasize: "Look for what is good or stimulating in the paper, and what knowledge advancement it has made."

Importantly, novelty and performance shouldn't be conflated. Papers needn't exceed existing benchmarks to merit acceptance—"the fact that a proposed method does not exceed the state-of-the-art accuracy on an existing benchmark dataset is not grounds for rejection by itself."

## Review Quality Standards

Reviews must be substantive and detailed. The document discourages "Bullet lists with one short sentence per bullet" and stresses: "Your discussion, more than your score, will help the authors, fellow reviewers, and Area Chairs understand the basis for your recommendation."

Reviewers should be specific about novelty claims, backing assertions with "specific references and an explanation of how closely they are related."

## Ethics Requirements

**Anonymity & Confidentiality:** Reviewers must not actively identify authors or share papers with others. "You should not show the paper to anyone else" except designated helpers.

**Conflicts of Interest:** Avoid reviewing if you share an institution, collaborated recently, or were an advisor/advisee. Contact the Area Chair immediately if conflicts arise.

**Professionalism:** "Write a courteous, informative, incisive, and helpful review that you would be proud to sign with your name."

## Specific Policy Areas

**arXiv Papers:** Reviewers shouldn't demand comparisons to recent preprints or reject solely based on prior arXiv work. However, authors should acknowledge papers that "directly and obviously inspired them."

**Withdrawn Datasets:** Using withdrawn datasets (like DukeMTMC-ReID) warrants "very close scrutiny" but isn't automatic grounds for rejection.

**LLM Use:** Reviewers may use language models to polish wording but must never show submissions to LLMs—doing so constitutes confidentiality violation.

**Supplementary Material:** Should only be consulted when necessary and cannot contain substantially improved results.
