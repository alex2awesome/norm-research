# COPE Ethical Guidelines for Peer Reviewers

## Overview

COPE is the Committee on Publication Ethics, the membership organisation that helps all involved in research publishing uphold integrity of the publication process.

## Key Guidelines for Peer Reviewers

### On Being Approached to Review

- It is courteous to respond to an invitation to peer review within a reasonable time frame, even if you cannot undertake the review.
- Peer reviewers should declare if they do not have the subject expertise required to carry out the review or if they are able to assess only part of the manuscript, outlining clearly the areas for which they have the relevant expertise.
- If you cannot review, it is helpful to make suggestions for alternative reviewers if relevant, based on their expertise and without any influence of personal considerations or any intention of the manuscript receiving a specific outcome.
- Respect the confidentiality of the peer review process; refrain from using information obtained during the peer review process for your own or another's advantage.

### Conducting the Review

- Reviewers should provide an unbiased, constructive critique of the manuscript that is objective and helpful to authors.
- Do not attempt to rewrite the manuscript to your own preferred style if it is basically sound and clear; suggestions for changes that improve clarity are, however, important.
- Bear in mind that the editor requires a fair, honest, and unbiased assessment of the strengths and weaknesses of the manuscript.
- Be specific in your criticisms, and provide evidence with appropriate references to substantiate general statements.
- Remember it is the authors' paper, so do not attempt to rewrite it to your own preferred style if it is basically sound and clear.
- Not derogate or discredit the work; be aware of any prejudices.

### Preparing the Report

- Bear in mind that the editor may forward your comments to the author, so be specific and constructive.
- Review reports should be balanced and considerate; never personal.
- Avoid making statements that may be perceived as personal critiques or aggressive.
- Be aware that authors might use comments to improve their work for resubmission elsewhere.

### Confidentiality

- Treat any manuscripts received as confidential documents.
- Do not retain or copy the manuscript.
- Do not share the manuscript with any colleagues without the explicit permission of the editor.
- Do not contact the authors directly without the permission of the editor.

### Conflicts of Interest

- Reviewers should declare all potential conflicts of interest, seeking advice from the journal if they are unsure whether something constitutes a relevant interest.
- Do not allow conflicts of interest to influence acceptance or rejection.

### Ethics Violations

- If you come across any irregularities with respect to research and publication ethics, let the journal know.
- Do not attempt to investigate on your own.
- Examples include misconduct during research/writing, substantial similarity between the manuscript and concurrent submissions or published articles.

### Responding to Author Replies

- Respond promptly to requests to review revised manuscripts.
- Try to maintain a consistent approach across versions.
- Be available to provide additional advice if requested.

### After the Review

- Continue to maintain confidentiality of the manuscript and review process.
- Do not use information obtained during the review for your own or another's advantage.
