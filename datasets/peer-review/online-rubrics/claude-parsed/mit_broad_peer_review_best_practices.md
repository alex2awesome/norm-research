# MIT Broad Communication Lab: Peer Review Best Practices

## Core Principle
"Peer review is broken. But let's do it as effectively and as conscientiously as possible." The lab emphasizes conducting thorough, professional evaluations despite systemic challenges.

## Six Criteria for Success
A strong peer review should:

1. **Summarize** the manuscript's main claims and assess its field impact
2. **Direct** editors clearly—accept, reject, or revise recommendation
3. **Identify** major concerns with specific locations in the document
4. **List** minor issues (grammar, references, clarity)
5. **Explain** how arguments could strengthen through additional analysis or experiments
6. **Stay reasonable** in scope—avoid suggesting modifications requiring excessive time, cost, or resource commitment

## Standard Structure (1-2 pages)
- Summary with significance assessment
- Clear decision statement
- Major concerns (if applicable)
- Minor concerns (optional)

## Audience Analysis
**Primary audience:** Journal editors need critical scientific feedback and claims evaluation

**Secondary audience:** Authors need suggestions for manuscript improvement

Reviewers typically remain anonymous to authors but known to editors.

## Key Skills
- Format documents consistently
- Cite resolutions for major concerns (not just problems)
- Suggest feasible, in-scope revisions
- Organize comments by manuscript order with page references
- Maintain professional, diplomatic tone
- Avoid bias and conflicts of interest
