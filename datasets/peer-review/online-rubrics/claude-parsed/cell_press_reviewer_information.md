# Cell Press Information for Reviewers

## Conflicts of Interest and Confidentiality

Reviewers should be sensitive to potential conflicts of interest, both real and perceived. It is not appropriate to discuss unpublished manuscripts at laboratory meetings or journal clubs.

## Collaboration and Co-Reviewers

Reviewers can collaborate with trainees (graduate students and post-docs) in manuscript evaluation, though reviewers should keep the number of collaborators to a minimum and include the identities of all individuals involved in the review.

## Timeliness

Cell Press journals consider ten days to be sufficient time to review a manuscript.

## AI and Technology

Reviewers should not upload their peer review report into AI tools, and generative AI or AI-assisted technologies should not be used by reviewers to assist in the scientific review of a paper.

## Commitment to Revisions

For editorial consistency and fairness to authors, reviewers who agree to review one version of a manuscript should commit to reviewing future revisions if necessary.

## General Editorial Criteria

At the editorial level, the editorial assessment involves considering:
- The fit of the study for the journal's scope and interests
- The advance in the context of the field
- The support for the conclusions

## Multi-Journal Submission

Cell Press operates a multi-journal submission system where reviewers may evaluate manuscripts that could fit multiple journals in the portfolio.
