# PNAS Reviewer Guidelines

## Key Evaluation Criteria

PNAS reviewers are expected to help ensure that:
- Research is well designed and executed
- Presentation of methods will permit replication
- Data are unambiguous and properly analyzed
- Conclusions are supported by data

## Core Review Principles

Reviewers should evaluate all submissions fairly, ensuring that assessments are based solely on the content and rigor of the research. Reviewer comments and conclusions should be based on an objective and impartial evaluation of the manuscript, focusing on:
- Scientific merit
- Methodology
- Originality
- Clarity of writing
- Relevance to scope and purpose of PNAS

## Reviewer Responsibilities

Reviewers are responsible for:
- Acting promptly
- Adhering to the instructions for completing a review
- Completing the review within the requested time frame
- Providing comments to the authors that are constructive and courteous in tone
- Contributing to a productive discussion of the manuscript

## Ethical Standards

A reviewer should not take scientific, financial, personal, or other advantage of peer review material and should make every effort to avoid even the appearance of impropriety or misuse of information obtained through the review process.

Material under review is a privileged communication that should not be shared or discussed with anyone outside the designated review process unless necessary and approved by the editor.

## Consultative Peer Review

PNAS uses a consultative peer review model where reviewers and editors discuss the manuscript before final decisions are made.
