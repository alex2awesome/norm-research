# How to Be a Good Reviewer: A Step-by-Step Guide (Sedaghat et al. 2024)

## Overview

This guide addresses a critical gap in scientific training: formal instruction in peer review. The authors note that "the teaching of scientific peer review is rarely a component of formal scientific or clinical training," yet 77% of reviewers want additional training.

## Key Sections

### 1. The Peer Review Process
The manuscript undergoes editorial screening, then assignment to an Associate Editor who recruits typically two reviewers. The reviewer's timely completion significantly impacts overall efficiency.

### 2. Blinding Systems
Three approaches exist: single-blinded (reviewers know authors), double-blinded (mutual anonymity), and open review (identities disclosed). Each carries potential bias risks.

### 3. Why Serve as Reviewer
Top reasons include: participating in the scientific community, reciprocating peer review received, professional expectation, and improving writing skills. Benefits include CME credits and career visibility.

### 4. Accepting Invitations
Potential reviewers must assess three factors: expertise match, conflict of interest (financial, scientific, personal), and deadline feasibility. Declining is better than non-response.

### 5. Manuscript Preparation
Recommended approach: read once for overview, take a break, reread for methodology, then reread for writing quality. Conduct literature search to verify novelty and identify related work.

### 6. Writing the Review

**Structure recommended:**
- Summary paragraph (3-5 sentences demonstrating comprehension)
- Major comments (numbered, most important first)
- Minor comments

**Major comments address:**
- Relevance and journal fit
- Ethical concerns
- Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion quality
- Internal validity (design appropriateness, bias detection)
- External validity (generalizability)

### 7. Critical Assessment Areas

**Methods scrutiny includes:**
- Study design appropriateness
- Data collection clarity ("what," "when," "how")
- Statistical analysis transparency
- Common biases: design, information, selection, confounding, analysis

**Results evaluation:**
- Transparent reporting of group characteristics
- Visual data representation
- Comprehensiveness of analyses

**Discussion assessment:**
- Fair, objective interpretation
- Context within existing literature
- Logic of conclusions
- Thorough limitations discussion

### 8. Tone and Professionalism
Reviews must be collegial and constructive. The guide notes "12% of reviews contained unprofessional comments." Constructive suggestions should accompany critiques; justified opinions prevent appearance of bias.

### 9. Editor Recommendations
Common decision options: Accept, Conditional Acceptance, Minor/Major Revision, Reject, or Reject without review (for out-of-scope manuscripts).

### 10. Confidential Editor Comments
These separate comments explain decision rationale, assess potential impact (advance field, motivate studies, influence clinical care), and raise concerns unsupported by direct evidence.

## Key Takeaway

Quality peer review requires systematic evaluation across multiple dimensions: scientific validity, novelty, methodological rigor, appropriate interpretation, and clear communication—delivered with collegiality and constructiveness.
