# How Not to Be Reviewer #2 (Ashley ML Guajardo)

Four-step rubric for *not* being the stereotyped destructive reviewer.

## Step 1: Verify Your Expertise

Decline reviews when you lack confidence in the methodology or field. You should have "some knowledge of its themes and methods" to competently assess the work's contribution.

## Step 2: Review When Ready

"Reviewing the work of your peers should be pleasurable." Don't accept papers when stressed or overcommitted — your negative state will degrade both review quality and tone.

## Step 3: Practice Constructive Kindness

Replace accusatory language like "authors clearly aren't aware" with supportive phrasing such as "I'd like to see engagement with theorist X." Maintains professionalism and encourages improvement rather than defensiveness.

## Step 4: Be Specific and Actionable

Vague criticism ("analysis problematic") helps no one. Explain *what* is problematic, *what* is missing, and suggest alternatives. Provide complete citations and links when recommending sources so authors can actually find them.

## Underlying Principle

Treat manuscripts as you'd want yours treated — with fairness, specificity, and genuine helpfulness.
