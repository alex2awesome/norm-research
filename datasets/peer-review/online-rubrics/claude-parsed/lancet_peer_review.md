# The Lancet Peer Review Process

## Peer Review Process and Role

Peer reviewers are asked to provide a written evaluation of the manuscript, ask questions of the authors or request clarifications, and then advise editors on whether, based on what is presented to them, it is acceptable for publication, could be acceptable after revision, or should be rejected.

## Key Responsibilities and Criteria

Critical evaluation of the manuscript by external peers helps:
- Add clarity
- Acknowledge and report both uncertainties and limitations
- Place new work in the context of the totality of available evidence
- Prevent over-interpretation

Reviewers are asked to declare any financial and non-financial academic or intellectual conflicts that might have the potential to influence their critique.

## Reviewer Selection

Editors identify and invite independent experts with relevant scientific or clinical expertise to review the manuscript, ensuring their complementary knowledge and skills provide a thorough review. Editors send manuscripts to reviewers with different expertise and from different locations, deliberately seeking reviews that provide an in-depth critique.

All research articles are also sent to a **statistical reviewer**, and when relevant, other experts such as:
- Modellers
- Data scientists
- Health economists
- Patients with lived experience

## Ethical Guidelines

The Lancet journals follow:
- ICMJE Recommendations for the Conduct, Reporting, Editing, and Publication of Scholarly Work in Medical Journals
- COPE (Committee on Publication Ethics) guidelines on ethical peer review

## Decision Categories

After review, editors make decisions:
- Accept
- Acceptable after revision
- Reject
