# ICLR 2026 Reviewer Guide

## General Requirements

Reviewers are asked to provide timely and substantive reviews and follow the reviewing guidelines. Timely, high quality reviews are essential to the peer review process.

## Review Quality and Consequences

Low quality reviews (e.g., placeholder reviews) will be flagged by Area Chairs and Senior Area Chairs, and flagged reviewers will be warned and urged to update the review. Reviewers who do not respond to these warnings will be liable to having their own papers desk rejected.

ICLR will be looking for **3 quality reviews per paper**.

## Timeline (2025-2026 cycle)

- OpenReview profile (September 19, 2025)
- Bidding on papers (September 28-October 4, 2025)
- Writing reviews (October 10 - November 1, 2025)
- Discussion period (November - December)
- Final decisions

## Code of Ethics

All ICLR participants, including reviewers, are required to adhere to the ICLR Code of Ethics.

## LLM Usage Policy

The core of the LLM policy is twofold:
(a) If a reviewer uses an LLM, they must disclose this and are ultimately responsible for the LLM's outputs.
(b) Whether or not reviewers use LLMs, they must not make false or misleading claims, fabricate or falsify data, or misrepresent results.

## Reciprocal Review Policy

Reviewers who are also authors will not see reviews of their own submissions during the rebuttal period until they complete their assigned reviews. If reviews are late, reviewers and their co-authors will lose access to their paper reviews until completion.

## Strict Rules on LLM-Generated Content

Papers (or reviews) using LLMs without disclosure face immediate rejection. ICLR 2026 has implemented one of the strictest policies in the conference world for AI-generated content disclosure.

## Review Form Components

Reviews should include:
- Summary of contributions
- Strengths and weaknesses
- Soundness, presentation, and contribution scores
- Overall recommendation
- Confidence
- Questions for authors
- Limitations and ethics flags
