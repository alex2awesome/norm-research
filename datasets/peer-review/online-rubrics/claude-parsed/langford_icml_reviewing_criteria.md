# John Langford's ICML Reviewing Criteria (hunch.net)

Langford runs the long-running ML theory blog hunch.net and has reviewed for ICML, NIPS/NeurIPS, COLT, etc.

## Core Principles

- Take reviewing seriously: read the paper, consider implications, then decide.
- No categorical reject classes — judge each paper on merit.

## What Counts as a Good Submission

A paper ideally has all three:
1. Analyzes a new model of machine learning.
2. Provides a new algorithm.
3. Demonstrates empirical effectiveness on plausibly real problems.

Two-of-three may still be acceptable; one-of-three is much harder.

## Standards of Rigor

- Avoid enthusiasm for incremental work.
- Prefer "proof-of-possibility over a quantitative improvement."
- Sample-complexity improvements are significant even if only constant-factor.
- Computational improvements should be at least an order of magnitude to count.

## Things Langford Discounts

- Experiments on toy data only — toy and real data behavior diverge.
- New analysis for existing algorithms is less interesting than analysis that drives new algorithms.

## Personal Interests

He acknowledges his existing research interests bias him, but considers this less important than the rigor criteria above.
