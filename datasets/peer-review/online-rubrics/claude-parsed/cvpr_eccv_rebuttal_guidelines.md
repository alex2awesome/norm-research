# CVPR/ECCV Rebuttal Guidelines

## Format and Length

The rebuttal is limited to a one-page PDF file. Responses longer than one page will simply not be reviewed. This includes responses where the margins and formatting are deemed to have been significantly altered.

## Purpose of Rebuttals

The core guiding principle is that the rebuttal should be **thorough, direct, and easy for the Reviewers and Area Chair (RACs) to follow**. The purpose is to clarify and convince. Science is a deliberative process, and rebuttals are simply a stage in that process.

## What to Include

The author rebuttal is meant to provide an opportunity to:
- Rebut factual errors
- Supply additional information requested by the reviewers
- Clarify misunderstandings
- Provide minor additional results explicitly requested

## What NOT to Include

- New contributions (theorems, algorithms, experiments) absent in the original submission and not specifically requested
- Substantial new experiments
- Personal attacks on reviewers
- New related work (other than to address reviewer-flagged gaps)

## Tone and Approach

**Be conversational.** This makes it easier for RACs to follow, and the responses are less likely to be perceived as combative.

If a reviewer is wrong, do not say they're wrong. Instead, say "there seems to be a misunderstanding" or "perhaps the wording was unclear."

## Process for Writing Rebuttals

1. Itemize reviewer comments using a spreadsheet
2. Group common concerns across reviewers
3. Address each concern point-by-point
4. Indicate where in the revised paper changes will be made
5. Acknowledge legitimate criticism gracefully
6. Provide concrete evidence/data where possible

## Anonymity Requirements

The rebuttal must maintain anonymity. It cannot include links to external material such as code, videos, etc.

## Reviewer Expectations During Rebuttal

In their reviews, reviewers may request clarifications, additional illustrations, or small experiments that could be reasonably run within the rebuttal phase with academic resources. Per a passed 2018 PAMI-TC motion, reviewers should not request substantial additional experiments for the rebuttal.

## Reviewer Norms for Reading Rebuttals

Reviewers should:
- Read all rebuttals carefully
- Update their reviews to acknowledge addressed concerns
- Not raise new substantive criticisms in post-rebuttal phase
- Engage in discussion with co-reviewers and AC
