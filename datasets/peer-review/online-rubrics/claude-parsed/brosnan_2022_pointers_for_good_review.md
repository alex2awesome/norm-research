# A Few Pointers for a Good Review (Royal Society — Brosnan)

Senior editor's view of what good reviews look like at Proceedings B.

## Pointers

1. **Focus on improvement** — "The goal is to help the authors make the paper better" rather than simply listing shortcomings.
2. **Highlight strengths** — acknowledge what the authors did well, regardless of recommendation.
3. **Address big-picture issues** — concentrate on scientific content and organization rather than minor stylistic edits.
4. **Maintain consistency** — private comments to editors should align with feedback given to authors; no contradictory messages.
5. **Consider author context** — be flexible about presentation issues when reviewing work from early-career researchers or those without institutional English-language support, provided the science is sound.
6. **Provide rationale** — explain your reasoning so editors can navigate conflicting reviewer opinions.
7. **Mentor emerging researchers** — include postdocs and graduate students in your review process to develop their skills.

## Underlying Norm

Peer review is a collegial service supporting scientific advancement and author development.
