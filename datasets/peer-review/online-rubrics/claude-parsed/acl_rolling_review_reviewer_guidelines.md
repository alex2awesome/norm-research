# ACL Rolling Review Reviewer Guidelines

## Core Responsibilities

ARR reviewers must complete four main tasks:

1. **Allocate Time**: Block calendar time for reading assignments, writing reviews, and participating in discussion periods.

2. **Assignment Check**: Verify expertise match and conflicts of interest. Complete a desk-rejection checklist examining appropriateness, hallucinated references, anonymity violations, style compliance, limitations sections, and ethics concerns.

3. **Write Strong Reviews**: Follow a structured approach:
   - Read papers twice (skim for expectations, then in-depth analysis)
   - Be specific with criticism, providing concrete examples
   - Avoid common reviewer heuristics that introduce bias
   - Ensure scores align with review content
   - Maintain professional, neutral tone

4. **Engage in Discussion**: Acknowledge author responses, participate in reviewer discussions, and update assessments as warranted.

## Key Evaluation Criteria

**Soundness Score**: Must be justified by review text. Low scores require explicit identification of technical flaws.

**Excitement Score**: Reflects personal interest separately from technical quality.

**Overall Recommendation**: Composite assessment balancing soundness, novelty, and potential impact.

## Common Review Mistakes to Avoid

The guidelines identify 17 heuristics that can undermine review quality:

- Dismissing results as unsurprising (H1)
- Confirmation bias toward expected outcomes (H2)
- Penalizing methodological diversity (H8, H10)
- Demanding unnecessary experiments beyond claim scope (H13)
- Conflating acknowledged limitations with weaknesses (H16)

## Common Paper Problems to Flag

Categories include methodology issues (reproducibility, LLM validation, assumption clarity), sourcing concerns (licensing, ethics), results problems (statistical rigor, overclaiming), and general issues (unclear contributions, weak related work).

## Critical Standards

- Papers must be technically sound and appropriately scoped
- Confidentiality is absolute—no submission details shared publicly
- Generative AI use restricted: reviewers cannot use AI to draft reviews or upload confidential materials to non-private services
- Double-blind review requires not deliberately identifying authors pre-reading
- Resubmission reviews should focus on whether previous weaknesses were adequately addressed

The guidelines emphasize that peer review is mutually beneficial, encouraging collegiality and recognition that reviewers are helping advance the field while accessing cutting-edge research first.
