# F1000Research Open Peer Review — Reviewer Rubric

F1000Research operates open, post-publication peer review. Reviews and reviewer identities are published alongside the article. Reviewers select an approval status and answer a structured set of questions per article type.

## The Six Core Review Questions (Standard Research Article)

Each question addressed by the reviewer in narrative form:

1. **Is the work clearly and accurately presented and does it cite the current literature?**
2. **Is the study design appropriate and does the work have academic merit?**
3. **Are sufficient details of methods and analysis provided to allow replication by others?**
4. **If applicable, is the statistical analysis and its interpretation appropriate?**
5. **Are all the source data underlying the results available to ensure full reproducibility?**
6. **Are the conclusions drawn adequately supported by the results?**

## Three Approval Statuses

- **Approved** — The reviewer judges the article scientifically sound and ready for indexing without further revision.
- **Approved with Reservations** — The article has academic merit but requires changes (small or significant). The reviewer must enumerate the required changes.
- **Not Approved** — The article has fundamental flaws that make it unsuitable for indexing in current form. The reviewer must justify with specific concerns.

A paper passes peer review and is indexed (PubMed, Scopus, etc.) when at least:
- two **Approved** statuses, OR
- one **Approved** + two **Approved with Reservations**.

## Reviewer Eligibility Requirements

- PhD/MD/MBBS or equivalent doctorate (with industry/publication-history alternatives in fields where doctorates are less common).
- Demonstrable experience in the same field as the article topic.
- For some subject areas: lead authorship on at least three publications in the past five years.
- Impartiality and disclosure of any competing interests (financial or non-financial).

## Article-Type-Specific Question Sets

F1000Research uses tailored questions for different article types:

- **Method Articles**: emphasise reproducibility of the protocol, validation, applicability.
- **Software Tool Articles**: emphasise software design, code availability, documentation, testing.
- **Data Notes**: emphasise data quality, availability, format, metadata, FAIR compliance.
- **Systematic Reviews**: PRISMA compliance, search comprehensiveness, RoB assessment.
- **Case Reports**: CARE-statement compliance, clinical relevance, informed consent.
- **Study Protocols**: clarity of objectives, soundness of methods, ethical clearance.

## Reviewer Conduct Norms

- **Open identity**: reviewer's name and affiliation are published with the report.
- **Constructive tone**: report should help the author improve the work.
- **Substantive specificity**: criticisms tied to specific sections, sentences, or data points.
- **No coercive citation** of the reviewer's own work unless directly relevant.
- **Declared competing interests**: explicit statement at the head of the report.
- **Independence**: do not review papers from your own institution or close collaborators.

## Report Structure (Recommended)

- Summary of the article (1 paragraph) demonstrating that the reviewer has read and understood the work.
- Substantive comments organised by category: design, methods, analysis, presentation, conclusions.
- Numbered specific points (so the author can respond point-by-point).
- Required vs suggested changes clearly distinguished.
- Approval status with explicit justification.
