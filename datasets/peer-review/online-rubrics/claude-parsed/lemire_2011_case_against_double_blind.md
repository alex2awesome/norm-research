# The Case Against Double-Blind Peer Review (Lemire)

## Arguments Against Double-Blind

- **Ineffective and harsher**: research shows it doesn't significantly improve paper quality, and reviews tend to be harsher. "It is easier to pull the trigger when the enemy wears a mask."
- **Disadvantages outsiders**: data show researchers from outside academia face *lower* acceptance rates under double-blind — the opposite of intended fairness.
- **Undermines research context**: research papers are often only interesting in the context of a researcher's broader trajectory. Double-blind treats papers as isolated artifacts.

## What Lemire Advocates

- Transparency and open accountability.
- Publicly disclose which reviewers rejected which papers and their reasoning.
- Reviewer accountability — identities revealed so authors can assess reviewer competence.
- Mutual transparency to refocus attention on scientific merit and reduce editorial power disparities.

## Implication for Reviewer Norms

If a reviewer believes their identity might be revealed, they will write more carefully and avoid both nastiness and reflexive rejection. Anonymity is not a free pass.
