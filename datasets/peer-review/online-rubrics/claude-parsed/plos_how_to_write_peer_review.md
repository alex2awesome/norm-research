# PLOS Guide: How to Write a Peer Review

## Review Structure

PLOS recommends organizing your report like an "inverted pyramid" with critical information first, followed by details, then supplementary points.

### 1. Summary Section
Begin by restating the manuscript's claims in your own words. This demonstrates your understanding to editors and reveals perspective differences among reviewers. Provide an overview of strengths and weaknesses, concluding with your recommendation regarding acceptance, revision, or rejection.

### 2. Specific Improvements
Divide feedback into major and minor issues. Number each point for clarity and reference specific lines, pages, figures, or tables.

**Major issues** are essential points requiring resolution before publication—focus on what's fundamental to the current study, not future directions.

**Minor issues** won't affect overall conclusions and include: missing references, technical clarifications, data presentation formatting, and grammar/spelling corrections.

### 3. Confidential Comments
Use this section for concerns about ethics, language quality, or competing interests. As PLOS notes, "Do not use this space to critique the manuscript" since editors won't share it with authors.

## Effective Feedback Principles

**Do's:**
- Justify recommendations with concrete evidence
- Be specific about needed improvements
- Maintain professional, respectful tone
- Acknowledge manuscript strengths

**Don'ts:**
- Suggest out-of-scope experiments
- Rewrite the manuscript for authors
- Promote your own research
- Focus excessively on typos
- Submit without proofreading

## Tone Guidelines

Transform vague criticism into constructive feedback. Rather than "The authors appear clueless," say "The study fails to address how findings relate to previous research."

Submit reviews on time to build editor relationships and increase future review invitations.
