# eLife Peer Review Policy

## Core Publishing Model
eLife publishes all peer-reviewed submissions as **Reviewed Preprints** rather than using traditional accept/reject decisions. Each publication includes the article, peer feedback, author response, and an eLife Assessment.

## Assessment Structure
The eLife Assessment comprises two dimensions:

**Significance of Findings:** Ranges from "landmark" to "useful"

**Strength of Evidence:** Ranges from "exceptional" to "inadequate"

The Assessment "summarises the significance of the findings and the strength of evidence" in each article.

## Public Review Components
Public Reviews must:
- "Describe the strengths and weaknesses of the article"
- Indicate whether "claims and conclusions are justified by the data"
- Make explicit disagreements between reviewers and authors

## Selection Criteria
Editors—all active researchers—select submissions for peer review based on criteria detailed in eLife's article "How and why eLife selects papers for peer review."

## Key Policy Features
- Publication fee: $3,000 (waived for those unable to pay)
- All accepted submissions become preprints
- Authors may submit revised versions with updated assessments
- Authors can publish a Version of Record to conclude the review process
- No artificial limits on review volume
- Editors and reviewers consult during assessment writing

## Significance Vocabulary (Landmark to Useful)

The eLife assessment uses a controlled vocabulary:
- Landmark / Fundamental / Important / Valuable / Useful

## Strength of Evidence Vocabulary (Exceptional to Inadequate)

- Exceptional / Compelling / Convincing / Solid / Incomplete / Inadequate
