# Black et al. 1998 — What Makes a Good Reviewer and a Good Review (JAMA)

Empirical study of 420 BMJ submissions in 1997 that produced the *Review Quality Instrument* used by editors and authors. Established the reference set of dimensions a good medical review must address.

## The 7-Item Review Quality Scoring Form

Each dimension scored 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent):

1. The extent to which the reviewer addressed **the importance of the research question**.
2. The reviewer's discussion of **the originality of the question**.
3. The reviewer's assessment of **the strengths and weaknesses of the method**.
4. The reviewer's assessment of **the presentation of the paper** (writing, organisation, illustrations, tables).
5. The reviewer's assessment of **the interpretation of the results**.
6. The extent to which the reviewer **provided constructive comments and substantiated** the comments (with reasoning, evidence, examples).
7. **Global overall quality** of the review.

## Empirically Identified Predictors of High-Quality Reviews

Predictors that survived multivariate analysis:

- **Training in epidemiology or statistics** (only factor significant for both editors' and authors' assessments).
- **Younger age** (up to ~60 years) — for editors' assessments.
- **North American residence** — significant for both editors and authors (effect of language and writing style).
- **Not being on the journal's editorial board** — significant for authors' assessments.

## Process-Quality Norms

- Review quality **rises with time spent on a review up to ~3 hours**, then plateaus. Reviewers who spend less than an hour on a review produce systematically lower-quality output.
- **Turnaround time is independent of quality** — fast review does not imply low quality, and slow review does not imply high quality.
- Identified author / institution characteristics explain only ~8% of review quality variance: **most of what makes a good review depends on reviewer behaviour during the review itself**, not who the reviewer is.

## Operational Norms for Reviewers (derived)

- Reviewers should **address every item on the RQI** in their report.
- Reviewers without statistical training should **ask the editor for a statistical co-reviewer** rather than skip Item 3.
- Reviewers should **schedule at least 2–3 hours** for a review of a substantial empirical paper.
- Reviewers should **substantiate every criticism** (Item 6) — assertion alone is insufficient.
