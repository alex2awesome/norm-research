# Allen et al. 2019: Five Principles of Better Peer Review

Wiley/Learned Publishing synthesis identifying five overarching principles that any peer review process — and any individual review — should satisfy. Each principle decomposes into specific recommendations for reviewers.

## Principle 1 — Content Integrity

A review must engage with the *substance* of the manuscript:

- Comment on the **research question and aims**.
- Comment on the **methods and analyses**, including reproducibility.
- Comment on the **interpretation of findings** and whether claims are warranted.
- Identify **strengths as well as weaknesses**, not only negatives.
- Detect **statistical, factual, or logical errors**.
- Check for **adequate citation** of prior work.

## Principle 2 — Content Ethics

A reviewer is a custodian of research integrity:

- Flag any suspected **research misconduct** (fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, image manipulation).
- Identify **undeclared conflicts of interest**.
- Identify **ethical concerns**: informed consent, animal welfare, sensitive populations, dual-use research.
- Flag **redundant publication** or **salami slicing**.
- Maintain **manuscript confidentiality**; do not appropriate ideas, data, or methods.
- Decline review where the reviewer has a **conflict of interest** that cannot be managed.

## Principle 3 — Fairness

Reviewers and editors must be impartial:

- Judge the manuscript on its **scientific merit, not the identity of the authors** (institutional, gender, geographic, language).
- Apply **the same standards across submissions** — no double standards by reputation.
- Use **constructive, professional language**; avoid sarcasm and personal attack.
- **Avoid demanding citations** to the reviewer's own work unless genuinely necessary (anti-coercive citation).
- Provide **balanced** assessments — strengths and weaknesses in proportion.
- Consider **alternative interpretations** of the data.

## Principle 4 — Usefulness

The review must help authors and editors:

- Comments must be **specific** (line/page references, not blanket criticisms).
- Comments must be **substantiated** by reasoning, evidence, or example.
- Comments must be **constructive** — explain *how* to fix, not only that something is wrong.
- Distinguish **major from minor concerns** and prioritise.
- Provide a **clear recommendation** consistent with the narrative.
- Tailor language for **author readability** (avoid jargon-only critiques).

## Principle 5 — Timeliness

- **Respond promptly** to review invitations (accept or decline within 7 days).
- **Decline** rather than delay if you cannot meet the deadline.
- **Submit reviews on or before the deadline.**
- **Notify the editor** as soon as you anticipate delay.
- **Do not hold up subsequent rounds** — respond to revision rounds at least as quickly as the first round.

## Cross-Cutting Recommendations

- Adopt **structured review forms** that map to these five principles so coverage is enforced.
- Provide **reviewer training** on each principle; train new reviewers.
- Use **transparent peer review** (publishing reviews and identities by mutual agreement) where appropriate to incentivise the principles.
- **Recognise reviewers** with credit (ORCID, Publons/Web of Science Reviewer Recognition).
- Editors should **monitor reviewer quality** using instruments like the RQI.
