# Cochrane Quality Assessment & Risk of Bias

## Quality vs. Risk of Bias

The Cochrane Handbook draws a distinction between assessment of methodological quality and assessment of risk of bias, and recommends a focus on the latter. The key consideration is the extent to which results of included studies should be believed.

## Cochrane Risk of Bias 2.0 (RoB 2) Tool

Recommended for randomized clinical trials. Five domains:

1. **Bias arising from the randomization process**
2. **Bias due to deviations from intended interventions** (effect of assignment / effect of adhering)
3. **Bias due to missing outcome data**
4. **Bias in measurement of the outcome**
5. **Bias in selection of the reported result**

Each domain receives:
- Low risk of bias
- Some concerns
- High risk of bias

## ROBINS-I (for non-randomized studies)

Risk Of Bias In Non-randomised Studies of Interventions covers seven domains:
1. Confounding
2. Selection of participants
3. Classification of interventions
4. Deviations from intended interventions
5. Missing data
6. Measurement of outcomes
7. Selection of the reported result

## GRADE Framework

The assessment of the quality of the body of evidence follows the GRADE framework, which combines considerations of:
- Risk of bias
- Directness (indirectness)
- Heterogeneity (inconsistency)
- Precision (imprecision)
- Publication bias

Quality ratings: High, Moderate, Low, Very Low.

## Reviewer Use

When peer reviewing systematic reviews or original clinical trials, reviewers should check:
- Whether risk of bias was assessed appropriately
- Whether the right tool (RoB 2 or ROBINS-I) was used
- Whether GRADE ratings are justified
- Whether the certainty of evidence is appropriately stated
