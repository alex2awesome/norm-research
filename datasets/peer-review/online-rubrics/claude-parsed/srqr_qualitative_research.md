# SRQR (Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research)

## Overview

SRQR aims to improve the transparency of all aspects of qualitative research by providing clear standards for reporting qualitative research.

## Development

The authors created the SRQR by searching the literature to identify guidelines, reporting standards, and critical appraisal criteria for qualitative research; reviewing reference lists; and contacting experts.

## Structure

The SRQR consists of **21 items**. The SRQR discusses six topics:
1. Title and abstract
2. Introduction
3. Method
4. Results
5. Discussion
6. Other (conflict of interest, funding)

## Key Components

- Title and abstract: concise description of the nature and topic of the study, identifying whether it's qualitative and indicating the approach or data collection methods
- Problem formulation
- Purpose or research question
- Qualitative approach and research paradigm (e.g., ethnography, grounded theory, case study, phenomenology, narrative research)
- Researcher characteristics and reflexivity
- Context
- Sampling strategy
- Ethical issues
- Data collection methods
- Data collection instruments and technologies
- Units of study
- Data processing
- Data analysis
- Techniques to enhance trustworthiness
- Synthesis and interpretation
- Links to empirical data

## Related Guidelines

There are also related reporting guidelines such as the **COREQ (Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research)**, which uses a 32-item checklist focused on three domains: research team, study design, and analysis/findings.

## Reviewer Use

When reviewing qualitative research, peer reviewers should check that all 21 SRQR items are reported, particularly attending to researcher reflexivity and the trustworthiness of analysis.
