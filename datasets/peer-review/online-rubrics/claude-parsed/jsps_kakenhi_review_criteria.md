# JSPS KAKENHI Reviewer Criteria (Japan)

KAKENHI (Grants-in-Aid for Scientific Research) is Japan's principal competitive research grant programme. Approximately 8,000 reviewers selected from each academic field perform peer review under either Comprehensive Review or Two-Stage Document Review depending on grant category.

## Three Core Rating Elements

### 1. Academic Importance of the Research Project
Reviewers evaluate:
- Originality and novelty of the research question.
- Importance of the expected results to the academic field.
- Contribution to the development of the discipline.
- For "Generative Research Fields" categories: extent to which the project pioneers a new area.
- For "Challenging Research" categories: extent of explicit ambition to overturn established understanding.

### 2. Validity of the Research Method
Reviewers evaluate:
- Soundness of the proposed approach.
- Feasibility of the planned procedures.
- Appropriateness of methods to the research question.
- Plausibility of the timeline.
- Adequacy of preliminary results supporting the approach.

### 3. Appropriateness of the Ability to Conduct Research and the Research Environment
Reviewers evaluate:
- Demonstrated track record relevant to the project.
- Availability of facilities, equipment, materials, and resources.
- Composition and complementarity of the research team.
- Institutional embedding adequate to support the work.

## Distinctive JSPS Norms

- **No mechanical aggregation**: "reviewers are not required to give high scores to research proposals that marked high scores in all individual elements." Reviewers exercise discretion to advance unbalanced but significant proposals.
- **Diversity of research is required to be considered**: reviewers should not converge on a single dominant model of "good" research; the system explicitly aims to fund a diverse portfolio.
- **Two-stage document review** for most categories: each proposal is read independently in a first stage, then re-scored in a second stage where reviewers see the distribution of first-stage scores (without revealing other reviewers' identities).
- **Comprehensive review** for the largest categories (S/A, Challenging Research): reviewers conduct document reviews, then deliberate face-to-face in a panel that produces a synthesised assessment.

## Scoring

Each rating element is scored on a 4- or 5-point scale (varies by category). Scores include verbal anchors (e.g., for Scientific Research (S)):
- 4: Should definitely be selected
- 3: Better to select
- 2: Hard to determine
- 1: Difficult to select

## Conflicts of Interest

Reviewers must decline if:
- They are at the same institution as the applicant.
- They have a co-authorship or supervisory relationship in recent years.
- They have a direct competitive interest in the outcome.
