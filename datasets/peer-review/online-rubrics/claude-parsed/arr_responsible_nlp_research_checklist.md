# ARR Responsible NLP Research Checklist — Reviewer-Verifiable Items

Every NLP paper submitted through ACL Rolling Review must complete this checklist; reviewers verify completion and assess each section. The checklist standardises what an NLP review must cover beyond the substantive content.

## Section A — General Requirements
- **A1. Limitations** — "Did you describe the limitations of your work?"
- **A2. Risks** — "Did you discuss any potential risks of your work?"

## Section B — Scientific Artifacts
- **B1. Creator Attribution** — "Did you cite the creators of artifacts you used?"
- **B2. License and Terms** — "Did you discuss the license or terms for use and / or distribution of any artifacts?"
- **B3. Intended Use Compatibility** — "Did you discuss if your use of existing artifact(s) was consistent with their intended use?"
- **B4. Privacy and Offensive Content** — "Did you discuss the steps taken to check whether the data ... contains any information that names or uniquely identifies individual people or offensive content?"
- **B5. Artifact Documentation** — "Did you provide documentation of the artifacts, e.g., coverage of domains, languages, and linguistic phenomena?"
- **B6. Data Statistics** — "Did you report relevant statistics like the number of examples, details of train / test / dev splits?"

## Section C — Computational Experiments
- **C1. Model Parameters and Computational Budget** — "Did you report the number of parameters in the models used, the total computational budget ... and computing infrastructure used?"
- **C2. Experimental Setup** — "Did you discuss the experimental setup, including hyperparameter search and best-found hyperparameter values?"
- **C3. Result Statistics** — "Did you report descriptive statistics about your results (e.g., error bars around results)?"
- **C4. Package Implementation Details** — "If you used existing packages ... did you report the implementation, model, and parameter settings used?"

## Section D — Human Subjects Research
- **D1. Participant Instructions** — "Did you report the full text of instructions given to participants?"
- **D2. Recruitment and Payment** — "Did you report information about how you recruited ... and paid participants?"
- **D3. Consent** — "Did you discuss whether and how consent was obtained from people whose data you're using?"
- **D4. Ethics Board Approval** — "Was the data collection protocol approved (or determined exempt) by an ethics review board?"
- **D5. Annotator Demographics** — "Did you report the basic demographic and geographic characteristics of the annotator population?"

## Section E — AI Assistant Use
- **E1. AI Assistant Disclosure** — "If you used any AI assistants, did you include information about your use?"

## Reviewer Obligations Tied to the Checklist

- Reviewers must check that author Yes/No answers to every applicable item are accurate.
- Reviewers must flag any **No-without-justification** as a concern (not necessarily a rejection ground).
- Reviewers must use the checklist completion to inform the **Soundness**, **Reproducibility**, and **Ethical Concerns** scores in the ARR review form.
- A paper failing essential D-section items (e.g. consent for human-subjects data) is a candidate for ethics escalation.
