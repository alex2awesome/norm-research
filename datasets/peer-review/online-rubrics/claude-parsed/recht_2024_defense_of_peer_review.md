# A Defense of Peer Review (arg min)

Berkeley professor Ben Recht's reluctant defense of pre-publication peer review on his arg min Substack.

## Core Position

"Peer review might not be all we need, but it's all we have." Recht maintains that academia requires *some* evaluation mechanism for credentialing and canonization, even if imperfect.

## Critiques He Levels at the Current System

- "Most scientists don't work hard at paper reviews."
- Reviewers exhibit predictable biases (e.g., older reviewers can be dismissive of work that does not cite them).
- The system "completely fails to improve science and can't even reliably detect fraud."
- Peer review struggles with disruptive or unexpected research.
- Peer review is fundamentally incompatible with scale; if we want it, we have to bring back elitism (smaller pool of trusted reviewers).

## What Recht Implies a "Good" Review Should Do

- Verify that minimum scholarly criteria are met (the marketplace check).
- Provide credentialing signal that the field can use.
- Not pretend to certify correctness or detect fraud.
- Take the work seriously rather than skim and rubber-stamp.

## Implication for Reviewers

A defensible review system relies on individual reviewers actually reading the paper, applying field-appropriate minimum-quality checks, and being honest that this is a quality floor — not a guarantee of truth or importance.
