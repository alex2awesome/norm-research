# Peer Review Is Not the Gold Standard in Science (Lemire)

A senior CS professor's case that peer review is a recent, fallible institution.

## Key Arguments

- Peer review was introduced over a century after the scientific revolution; standardized only after Einstein's most productive era.
- Peer review systems have historically failed to recognize groundbreaking work — Siler et al. (2014): three focal journals rejected many highly cited manuscripts, including the 14 most popular.
- Mainstream journals can suppress legitimate inquiry (lab-leak hypothesis case study).

## What Lemire Proposes Instead

Evidence-based science:
- Open discourse where "anyone can post ideas and results for others to replicate."
- Science as "a progressive and open process" rejecting gatekeeping.
- Validity depends on evidence, not consensus or institutional authority.

## Implication for Reviewers

A reviewer who treats peer review as the canonical truth-test misunderstands the institution. Reviewers should be humble about the institution's track record and explicitly preserve work they don't understand for the open community.
