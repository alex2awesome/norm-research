# How to Make Good Code Reviews Better (Stack Overflow Blog)

Code-review norms that map directly onto peer-review norms — many ML / data-science teams treat the two as the same skill.

## Areas to Cover

- Clarity of title, description, and the *why* of the change.
- Code correctness, test coverage, adherence to standards.
- Flag unclear naming, commented-out code, untested sections, edge cases.
- Review changes in context of the larger system / architecture.
- Maintainability and opportunities to simplify.

## Tone and Communication

- "Ask open-ended questions instead of making strong or opinionated statements."
- Offer alternatives without insisting they're the only solution.
- Assume you might be missing context; ask for clarification.
- Be empathetic; recognize the author's effort.
- Celebrate good solutions with positive feedback.

## Approval / Change-Request Discipline

- Don't approve while open-ended questions remain unanswered.
- Distinguish blocking vs. non-blocking comments.
- Be explicit when approving ("LGTM").
- Be flexible on practice but firm on principles.

## Escalation

- Switch to face-to-face discussion when back-and-forth gets long.
- Account for time zones.

## Nitpicks

- Mark nitpicks with "nit:" prefix.
- Frequent nitpicks signal missing tooling — automate linting.

## New Employees

- Provide extra explanation of alternatives.
- Point to relevant guides.
- Especially positive tone for early contributions.
