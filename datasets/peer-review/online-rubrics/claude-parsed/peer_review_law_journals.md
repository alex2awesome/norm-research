# Peer Review in Law Journals

## Distinct Tradition

The field of law has retained its distinctiveness regarding peer review to this day. Reviews are often conducted without following standardized rules and principles. External and independent evaluation of submissions has only recently become adopted by European law journals; peer review procedures are still poorly defined, investigated, and attuned to the legal science publishing landscape.

## Editorial vs. Peer Review

In US law schools, traditional law reviews are student-edited rather than peer-reviewed. Decisions are made by law students at the journal. This is unique among scholarly publishing.

By contrast, peer-reviewed legal journals (more common outside the US) follow more typical academic conventions.

## Assessment Criteria

The peer review process involves faculty members or practitioners who assess:
- The article's **legal analysis**
- **Argumentation techniques** (use of authority, logical structure)
- Overall **contribution to the field**
- **Originality** of the legal claim
- **Engagement with prior scholarship**
- **Clarity** of exposition
- **Appropriate use of citations** (Bluebook compliance in US)

## Standardization Issues

In most jurisdictions, editorial boards are rather vague about assessment criteria. Compared to STEM peer review, legal scholarship lacks:
- Standardized rubrics
- Reporting checklists
- Ethical guidelines analogous to COPE
- Consensus methodology criteria

## Distinctive Features of Legal Scholarship Review

- Heavy emphasis on **prose quality** — clear writing valued
- Importance of **argumentative structure** — premises must support conclusion
- **Doctrinal accuracy** — fidelity to existing law
- **Theoretical grounding** — engagement with jurisprudence
- **Policy implications** — practical impact valued
- **Comparative analysis** — engagement with other jurisdictions

## Transparency Trend

Whether the legal community retains editorial review, replaces it with peer review, or develops its own model, transparency of manuscript evaluation will play a key role in improving the quality of legal scholarship.
