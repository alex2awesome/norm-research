# Schroter et al. 2008 — Errors Reviewers Should Detect (Implicit Reviewer Rubric)

Schroter et al. inserted 9 major and 5 minor methodological errors into 3 test papers and sent them to 607 BMJ reviewers (randomised to face-to-face training, self-taught package, or control). The errors are themselves the operational rubric of *what reviewers should be looking for*.

## The 9 Major Errors Reviewers Should Detect

1. **Inadequate / inappropriate randomisation** (e.g. alternation, day-of-week, biased allocation).
2. **Missing details of randomisation method** (sequence generation not described).
3. **Missing allocation concealment** (no description of how the random sequence was hidden).
4. **Inappropriate exclusion of participants** post-randomisation (violation of intention-to-treat).
5. **Failure to use intention-to-treat analysis** despite preserving randomisation.
6. **Inappropriate statistical analysis** (wrong test for the data type).
7. **Discrepancy between data shown and conclusion drawn**.
8. **Conclusions not supported by the data** (overgeneralisation, claim beyond evidence).
9. **Inappropriate / overstated subgroup analysis** (post-hoc, multiple comparisons not adjusted).

## The 5 Minor Errors Reviewers Should Detect

1. **Inaccurate reporting of percentages** in the abstract.
2. **Incorrect total in a table.**
3. **Mismatch between text and figure caption.**
4. **Typographical errors in summary statistics.**
5. **Incorrect citation in the reference list.**

## Empirical Reviewer Performance Norms (Calibration Data)

- Mean number of major errors detected at baseline: **2.58 of 9** (~29%).
- Best-performed major-error category: **biased randomisation** — over 60% of rejecting reviewers caught it.
- Worst-performed major-error category: **conclusions not supported by data** — frequently missed.
- Training (face-to-face or self-taught) had only **modest effects** (mean improved to 2.71–3.0 of 9).

## Implied Reviewer Operational Rubric

Drawing the reviewer's attention to each of these 14 items as a checklist would substantially improve detection. The Schroter rubric has since been embedded in BMJ reviewer training material:

### Checklist (verbatim of the reviewer obligation)
- Verify the **randomisation procedure** is described and free of bias.
- Verify **allocation concealment** is described.
- Check that **all randomised participants** are accounted for and analysed in the group to which they were randomised.
- Independently verify **all summary statistics** in tables and abstract by recomputing from the data.
- Check that **conclusions are concordant with the data** in magnitude, direction, and certainty.
- Distinguish **prespecified vs post-hoc subgroup analyses** and discount the latter.
- Spot-check at least one **citation** to ensure it says what the paper claims it says.

## Significance

The 14-error inventory is now the de-facto reference list of "things a competent medical reviewer must catch." It is cited in the BMJ Reviewer Hub training materials, in EQUATOR Network's reviewer-education output, and in many medical-school courses on peer-review training.
