# How to Be a Good Peer Reviewer (Scholarly Kitchen — Wallace)

## Mind the Time

- Respect deadlines; request extensions immediately if needed.
- Respond promptly to notifications rather than delaying.
- Decline reviews you cannot complete rather than missing deadlines.

## Be Intentional

- Approach reviews with genuine desire to improve the work.
- Provide valuable feedback even if the paper isn't ready for publication.
- Focus on evaluating research quality rather than copyediting.

## Read Guidelines and Scope

- Study the journal's reviewer guidelines before submitting.
- Understand the journal's mission and scope.
- Ask for guidelines if they're unavailable.

## Educate and Grow Your Community

- View peer review as peer mentoring to strengthen your field.
- Provide detailed, specific feedback that educates authors.
- Frame rejections constructively to promote growth.

## Say No and Recommend Others

- Decline invitations you cannot handle responsibly.
- Suggest colleagues as alternatives.
- Recommend promising students and postdocs to editors.

## Be Bold and Constructive

- Treat all authors as intellectual peers regardless of credentials.
- Avoid dogmatic, dismissive, or rude statements.
- Apply the "golden rule" to feedback.

## Get Credit

- Register with Publons (now Web of Science Reviewer Recognition) to track your contributions.
- Connect to ORCID.
- Request formal credit records from publishers.
