# A Beginner's Guide to the Peer Review System (Trietsch / Inside Higher Ed)

Penn State entomology PhD's how-to for new reviewers, based on mentorship from a journal editor.

## Accepting Review Requests

- Verify journal legitimacy first.
- Assess your own expertise: "If you lack an education but have several years of professional experience that is directly applicable to the field, chances are good that you can provide valuable input."
- New grad students (first 6 months) should decline.

## What to Evaluate

Focus on the science:
- Methodology validity.
- Appropriate statistical tests.
- Correct data interpretation.
- Missing recent literature.

Grammar corrections aren't required; excessive language errors imply the manuscript wasn't ready.

## Decision Framework

- **Reject** — major reasoning flaws or evidence the authors lack field understanding.
- **Major revisions** — sound science needing experiments or rewrites.
- **Minor revisions** — well-written work needing tweaks (most common).

## Professional Conduct

- Maintain ethical standards whether anonymous or named.
- Detailed explanations with textual examples and constructive solutions, not criticism alone.

## Track Record

- Track reviews on CV / Publons / Web of Science.
- This appears on promotion and tenure applications.

## For Advisors

- Explicitly teach peer review.
- Share sanitized examples of previously reviewed papers.
- Explain your own decision-making methodology.
