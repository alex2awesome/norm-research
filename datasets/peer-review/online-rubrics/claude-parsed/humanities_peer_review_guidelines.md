# Humanities Peer Review Guidelines

## Key Evaluation Criteria

Peer reviewers in humanities assess whether examples and arguments in papers are:
- **Compelling** — do they advance the case?
- **Well-reasoned** — does the argument follow logically?
- **True** — are the empirical/textual claims accurate?

## Core Assessment Areas

### Clarity and Contribution
- Whether it is clear what the authors want to communicate
- Whether the main aims and conclusions are understandable
- Reviews should explain the manuscript's contribution to existing literature
- The manuscript's aim should be defined consistently

### Scholarly Quality and Context
The first task of a peer review report is to identify:
- The manuscript's main argument
- Its methods (interpretive, archival, theoretical)
- How these are situated within current scholarly literature
- The project's primary and secondary scholarly contributions

### Literature Assessment
- Cited studies should be an up-to-date representation of current knowledge
- The overall approach should meet the standards of the field
- Engagement with major debates and competing positions

### Methodology and Sources
- Appropriate use of primary sources
- Proper handling of archival material
- Sound interpretive approach
- Theoretical framework explicitly stated

## Distinctive Characteristics

Peer review in social sciences and humanities differs from STEM:
- Predominantly **double-blinded**
- The review process tends to last **longer**
- Multiple and diverse guidelines exist
- More emphasis on prose and argumentation
- Less reliance on statistical methods
- More tolerance for theoretical/interpretive frameworks
- Stronger focus on engagement with prior scholarship

## Special Considerations

- **Translation accuracy** when foreign-language sources are used
- **Hermeneutic care** in interpretation
- **Historical context** of textual evidence
- **Disciplinary conventions** (citation style, tone, length)
- **Voice and authorial style** — humanities reviewers may comment on prose more extensively

## Recommendation Categories

Typical decisions:
- Accept
- Minor revisions
- Major revisions / Revise & Resubmit
- Reject

Reviewers should provide detailed, paragraph-by-paragraph commentary if appropriate.
