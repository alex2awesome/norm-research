# CARE Case Report Guidelines

## Overview

The CARE guidelines (for CAse REports) were developed by an international group of experts to support an increase in the accuracy, transparency, and usefulness of case reports.

## CARE Checklist Items

The primary items of the checklist:

1. **Title** — The diagnosis or intervention of primary focus, with the words "case report"
2. **Key words** — 2-5 keywords identifying topics
3. **Abstract** — Background, summary, conclusion
4. **Introduction** — Brief background and rationale
5. **Patient information**
   - De-identified patient demographics
   - Primary concerns and symptoms
   - Medical, family, and psychosocial history
   - Relevant past interventions and outcomes
6. **Clinical findings** — Significant physical examination and clinical findings
7. **Timeline** — Historical and current information from this episode of care, organized as a timeline
8. **Diagnostic assessment**
   - Diagnostic methods (PE, lab testing, imaging)
   - Diagnostic challenges
   - Diagnostic reasoning including differential diagnosis
   - Prognostic characteristics
9. **Therapeutic interventions**
   - Type (pharmacologic, surgical, preventive, self-care)
   - Administration (dosage, strength, duration)
   - Changes in intervention with rationale
10. **Follow-up and outcomes**
    - Clinician- and patient-assessed outcomes
    - Important follow-up diagnostic and other test results
    - Intervention adherence and tolerability
    - Adverse and unanticipated events
11. **Discussion**
    - Strengths and limitations
    - Discussion of relevant medical literature
    - Rationale for conclusions
    - Main takeaway lessons
12. **Patient perspective** — When appropriate, the patient's perspective on their experience
13. **Informed consent** — Statement that informed consent was obtained

## Purpose for Reviewers

Medical journals use the CARE guidelines to support "Author Guidelines" and peer review. The checklist serves as a tool to help ensure that case reports are complete, transparent, and meet quality standards for publication.

## Reviewer Use

When reviewing case reports, peer reviewers should systematically check that all 13 items are present and adequately reported.
