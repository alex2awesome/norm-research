# Review Quality Instrument (RQI) — van Rooyen et al. 1999

The RQI is the most widely used standardized instrument for measuring the quality of an *individual peer-review report* (as distinct from manuscript quality). It was developed for the BMJ open-review randomised trials and validated with Cronbach's alpha 0.84.

## The 7 Items of the RQI

Each scored on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = not at all, 5 = to a great extent):

### Five items on coverage of the manuscript

1. **Importance of the research question** — Did the reviewer discuss the importance of the research question?
2. **Originality of the paper** — Did the reviewer discuss the originality of the work?
3. **Strengths and weaknesses of the method** — Did the reviewer comment substantively on the methodology?
4. **Presentation** (writing, organisation, clarity, tables/figures) — Did the reviewer comment on presentation quality?
5. **Interpretation of results** — Did the reviewer evaluate whether the conclusions follow from the data?

### Two items on the quality of the review itself

6. **Constructiveness of the comments** — Were the reviewer's comments constructive (i.e. would they help the authors improve the paper rather than merely criticising)?
7. **Substantiation of the comments** — Were the reviewer's comments substantiated with reasoning, evidence, or specific examples (rather than assertion)?

## Aggregate score

The mean across the 7 items yields a continuous 1–5 quality score for an individual review. Higher = higher-quality review.

## Operational norms inferred

The RQI implicitly makes these norms binding on reviewers:

- A high-quality review **discusses every one of the five substantive dimensions** rather than focusing on one.
- A reviewer who only criticises without proposing how to fix issues is rated lower on **constructiveness**.
- A reviewer who asserts "method is wrong" without supplying *why* or *how* is rated lower on **substantiation**.
- Reviews should comment on **presentation** (a dimension reviewers commonly neglect).

## Subsequent extensions

- van Rooyen et al. 1999 BMJ used the RQI as the outcome measure in the open-peer-review randomised trial (BMJ 318:23-27).
- Schroter et al. (2008) used the RQI to score 600+ reviews in the BMJ peer-review training trial.
- More than two dozen later instruments are derivatives (Bornmann reviews list 24 tools / 23 scales using 1–26 criteria).
