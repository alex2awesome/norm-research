# Taylor & Francis Reviewer Guidelines

## Main Assessment Factors

Reviewers should provide advice on:
- The **originality**, presentation, and **relevance** of the manuscript's subject matter to the readership of the journal
- The **accuracy and validity of the methodology**
- Whether the **conclusions are appropriately supported**

## Ethical Guidelines

Before agreeing to review:
- Reviewers must give unbiased consideration to each manuscript and judge each on its merits, without regard to race, religion, nationality, gender, seniority, or institutional affiliation of the author(s).
- Reviewers must declare any conflict of interest before agreeing to review a manuscript, including any relationship with the author(s) that may bias their review.
- Reviewers must keep the peer review process confidential and must not share information or correspondence about a manuscript with anyone outside of the peer review process without the explicit permission of the editor.
- Reviewers must not use artificial intelligence tools to generate manuscript review reports, including LLM based tools like ChatGPT.

## Peer Review Checklists

Taylor & Francis offers specialized checklists for different manuscript types:
- STEM research articles
- Arts and humanities research articles
- Review articles
- Case studies
- Position/opinion pieces

## Review Quality Standards

Reviews should be:
- **Constructive** — providing actionable suggestions
- **Comprehensive** — covering all major aspects of the paper
- **Evidenced** — claims backed by specific reasoning
- **Appropriately substantial** — not too brief

## Best Practices

Reviewers should:
- Read the article fully before forming judgments
- Note both strengths and weaknesses
- Provide specific page/line references when possible
- Distinguish major from minor concerns
- Focus on the manuscript's content rather than authors
- Respect the journal's scope and mission
