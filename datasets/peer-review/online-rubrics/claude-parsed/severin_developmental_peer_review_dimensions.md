# Severin et al. 2022: Ten Dimensions of the Developmental Function of Peer Review

Empirical NLP analysis of peer-review reports across 740 journals; the authors construct a 10-dimension dictionary of 1,565 terms and use it to score every review on its coverage of each dimension. This is one of the most widely cited modern operationalisations of "what a peer review should cover."

## The 10 Developmental-Function Dimensions

A high-quality review is one that addresses each of:

1. **Impact** — Comments on the impact of findings or any other manuscript content on society.
2. **Relevant Literature** — Assessments of the state-of-the-art and the manuscript references.
3. **Study Methods** — Feedback on materials, methods, and study design.
4. **Statistical Methods** — Comments concerning the statistical content of the study.
5. **Study Conclusions** — Reviewer input on results and conclusions.
6. **Limitations** — Feedback regarding study limitations.
7. **Applicability** — Comments on applicability, generalisability, and reproducibility.
8. **Presentation** — Critique of the presentation of the manuscript and the quality / readability of tables and figures.
9. **Data Availability** — Comments regarding data availability (raw data, code, materials).
10. **Organization and Writing** — Feedback on the organisation, linguistic content, and style.

## Operational Scoring

Each dimension is normalised by its empirical CDF (so a "high" score on Dimension 4 means the review devoted more attention to statistics than the typical review), and dimensions are aggregated by arithmetic mean to give a composite developmental-function score.

## Norms for Reviewers Implicit in the Framework

- A review that **addresses only one or two dimensions** scores low on developmental function regardless of how trenchant the comments are within those dimensions.
- **Data availability** is a dimension explicitly elevated to first-class status — reviewers should comment on whether code and raw data are available, FAIR, and adequate to reproduce the analysis.
- **Limitations** must be discussed independently of methodology — the question is whether the *authors* acknowledged limits, not just whether the design has flaws.
- **Applicability** is treated as separable from internal validity; reviewers should evaluate generalisability explicitly.
- **Statistical methods** are split out from study methods because cross-disciplinary evidence shows they are the most under-reviewed dimension.

## Cross-Disciplinary Observations Used as Calibration

Severin et al. report which dimensions are covered most and least by discipline. Reviewers can use these as benchmarks: e.g., reviewers in the social sciences typically under-cover *Data Availability* and *Statistical Methods*; reviewers in clinical medicine typically under-cover *Applicability*.
