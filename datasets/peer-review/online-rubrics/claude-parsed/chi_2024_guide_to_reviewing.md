# CHI 2024 Guide to Reviewing Papers: Core Framework

## Primary Evaluation Criterion

The fundamental standard is straightforward: **"Does this submission provide a strong contribution to the field of HCI?"** Reviewers must recognize that contributions manifest through diverse pathways, requiring contextual assessment rather than rigid templates.

## Contribution Types

The guide references detailed contribution categories including:
- Empirical studies
- Design contributions
- Theoretical frameworks
- Methodological contributions
- Replications (constructive replication with sufficient information represents legitimate contribution)
- Dataset contributions
- Survey/integration contributions
- Opinion/argument contributions

## Key Principles

- **Original research required**: All CHI papers must advance knowledge in human-computer interaction
- **Varied forms accepted**: Diverse contribution types qualify
- **Replication valued**: Constructive replication legitimate
- **Transparency matters**: Doubt about contributions grows when research reporting lacks transparency

## Review Criteria by Paper Length

Papers should match length to contribution scope:
- **Short papers** (<5,000 words): Brief, focused contributions; don't demand comprehensiveness
- **Long papers** (7,000–8,000 words): Substantial depth and results justified
- **Excessive length** (>12,000 words): Requires author justification; faces additional scrutiny

**Critical standard**: "Papers whose lengths are incommensurate with their contributions will be rejected."

## Review Quality Standards

Reviewers should provide:
- **Detailed feedback**: Approximately one page minimum; short reviews lack constructive value
- **Justified assessments**: Both positive and negative reviews require substantive argumentation
- **Accuracy responsibility**: Reviewers own their content, including citation accuracy
- **Confidentiality protection**: Avoid sharing unpublished work with online tools; LLMs pose confidentiality risks

## Recommendation Categories

- Accept with minor revisions
- Revise and Resubmit
- Reject

## Subcommittee Structure

Reviewers should not judge the paper by how well it fits the subcommittee theme(s). Many papers will not cleanly fit into a particular subcommittee, and reviewers should not penalize authors for this.

## Prior Publication Policy

Previously published material requires "more than 25% of new content material" for republication. Non-archival venues (workshops, posters, late-breaking work) don't count as prior publication.
