# ICMJE Recommendations: Responsibilities in the Peer-Review Process

## Journal and Editor Responsibilities

It is the responsibility of the journal to ensure that systems are in place for selection of appropriate reviewers. It is the responsibility of the editor to ensure that:
- Reviewers have access to all materials that may be relevant to the evaluation of the manuscript, including supplementary material for e-only publication
- Reviewer comments are properly assessed and interpreted in the context of their declared relationships and activities

A peer-reviewed journal is under no obligation to send submitted manuscripts for review, and under no obligation to follow reviewer recommendations, favorable or negative. The editor of a journal is ultimately responsible for the selection of all its content.

## Reviewer Responsibilities

Reviewers should:
- Declare their relationships and activities that might bias their evaluation of a manuscript
- Recuse themselves from the peer-review process if a conflict exists
- Disclose to editors any relationships or activities that could bias their opinions of the manuscript
- Be asked at the time they are asked to critique a manuscript if they have relationships or activities that could complicate their review

## Confidentiality

Manuscripts submitted to journals are privileged communications that are authors' private, confidential property. Reviewers should respect the privileged nature of the manuscripts they are entrusted with.

## Constructive and Timely Review

Peer review should be conducted in a constructive manner, with reviewers providing timely, fair, and unbiased reviews that help authors improve their work.

## Special Considerations

- Reviewers should not attempt to use the materials to advance their own work or to disadvantage the authors
- Reviewers should not contact the authors directly
- Reviewers should report suspected misconduct to the editors

## Recommendations Apply to All Authors, Editors, and Reviewers

ICMJE Recommendations are widely adopted by medical journals worldwide and serve as the foundational standard for ethical conduct in scholarly publishing.
