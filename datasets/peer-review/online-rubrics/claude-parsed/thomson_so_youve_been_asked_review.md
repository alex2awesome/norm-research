# So You've Been Asked to Review a Paper... (Thomson)

A theoretical physicist's (Edinburgh) practical guide for early-career reviewers, drawing on 30–40 manuscripts reviewed.

## Mindset Principles

- **Collaborative, not adversarial** — "we're all (supposed to be) on the same side"; the editor, not the reviewer, makes the final decision.
- **Professionalism matters** — no snark or informality; tone you'd use face-to-face.
- **Constructive criticism** — explain shortcomings and suggest implementable fixes.
- **Confidence in your expertise** — being asked means your opinion has value.
- **You can decline** — without penalty (conflict of interest, time, fit).

## Recommended Review Structure

1. Concise summary of the paper's contribution.
2. Acknowledgment of strengths.
3. Major criticisms and publication barriers.
4. Recommendations / conclusion.
5. Itemized minor corrections.

## Working Practice

- Be brief.
- Revisit drafts after a few days with fresh perspective before submitting.
