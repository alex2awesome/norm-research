# Gatekeeping vs. Editing in Peer Review (Dynamic Ecology)

Brian McGill argues reviewers must distinguish two different roles and ideally do both.

## The Two Roles

**Gatekeeping**: deciding whether work meets publication standards for the venue. Subjective judgment about what's "good enough."

**Editing**: providing constructive feedback to strengthen the manuscript — "big picture advice" like restructuring arguments or suggesting additional analyses, not merely listing typos.

These roles are "fundamentally orthogonal" and can operate independently, though both are typically needed.

## Reviewer Advice

- Tell the editor and authors clearly whether the work is "fundamentally sound or unsound" and give detailed rationale.
- Even when rejecting a paper, identify "3-5 most important things they can do to improve."
- Recognize "diamonds in the rough" and be willing to work through multiple revision rounds when the potential exists.
- Focus on identifying contributions, not just flaws.

## McGill's Concern

The field is "shifting more and more to gatekeeping" — reviewers writing brief rejections rather than offering developmental feedback. He treats reviewers and editors who combine both functions as having "that rare gift."

## Implicit Norms

- Brevity-only reviews are deficient.
- Pure cheerleading is also deficient.
- A complete review separates: (a) is this fundamentally sound?, (b) is it the right venue?, (c) what would make it stronger?
