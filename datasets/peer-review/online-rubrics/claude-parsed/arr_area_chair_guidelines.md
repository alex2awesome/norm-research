# ARR Area Chair Guidelines: Writing Meta-Reviews

## Core Purpose and Principles

Meta-reviews are the most crucial aspect of the review process, where the Area Chair justifies their recommendation to accept or reject a paper. The golden rule is to write meta-reviews in a way that you, as an author, would appreciate.

## Key Components to Include

Each meta-review should summarize the key points that justify your accept/reject recommendation, whether from the reviews themselves or the (anonymized) discussions:

- **Summary of the paper**: Including its strengths and weaknesses, with comments regarding novelty, significance, and relevance/impact.
- **Summary of post-rebuttal discussion**: Did reviewers engage in discussion or not, did their opinion change, did they change their score?
- **Feedback for rejection cases**: Give authors feedback to improve the paper, and suggestions about the next appropriate venue to submit their work.

## Important Dos and Don'ts

You are an area chair because you are an expert in the field; you do not have to simply average across reviewer scores and summarize their comments.

However, meta-reviewers are not reviewers (obviously), so you should avoid using only your opinion. It is not your job to write a fourth review of the paper. Instead, the goal of the meta-review is primarily to **reconcile and adjudicate the reviewers' views**.

## Recommendation Structure

The meta-review should:
1. Summarize the paper's contributions
2. Synthesize reviewer concerns
3. Note how authors addressed concerns in rebuttal
4. Make a recommendation with justification
5. Provide constructive feedback for the authors

## Quality of Reviews Assessment

Area chairs are also responsible for assessing the quality of the reviews themselves, requesting revisions or replacement if reviews are inadequate, terse, or unprofessional.
