# European Peer Review Guide (ESF) — Reviewer Rubric and Process Standards

Compiled by the European Science Foundation Member Organisation Forum together with 30+ European research funders from 23 countries plus the European Commission and the European Research Council. The guide is the de facto pan-European reference for grant peer review.

## The Five Pillars of Good Peer Review

### Pillar 1 — Core Principles
- **Excellence** as the dominant criterion; identify the best research.
- **Transparency** of the process and the criteria.
- **Impartiality** — equal treatment of comparable proposals.
- **Appropriateness** of process to the call (proportional review).
- **Confidentiality** of submissions and reviews.
- **Ethical considerations** (research integrity, COI, harms).

### Pillar 2 — Quality Assurance
- Recruit reviewers of demonstrably appropriate expertise.
- Use multiple independent reviewers; calibrate scoring.
- Provide written guidance and per-call rubrics.
- Monitor reviewer quality (dropout rate, response time, score distribution, narrative quality).
- Provide feedback to reviewers post-decision.

### Pillar 3 — Process Integrity
- Document COI declarations for every reviewer assignment.
- Separate scientific evaluation from administrative compliance check.
- Document the decision rationale.
- Provide an appeals mechanism for procedural failure.
- Audit a sample of decisions ex-post.

### Pillar 4 — Governance
- Clear roles for programme officers, panel chairs, individual reviewers.
- Defined escalation procedures for disagreement.
- Independence of decision panels from funder management.
- Periodic rotation of panel members.

### Pillar 5 — Methodology
- Choice of review mode (remote-only / panel / hybrid) justified by call type.
- Mode of scoring (numeric, ordinal, narrative-only) declared in advance.
- Use of bibliometrics as supportive but not decisive.

## Reviewer Selection Criteria (Common Profile)

The Guide proposes a common profile of expert reviewer attributes:
1. **Subject-matter expertise** at the level of the proposal.
2. **Methodological expertise** appropriate to the design.
3. **Geographic and institutional independence** from the applicants.
4. **Track record of delivering substantive reviews on time.**
5. **Trained on the funder's specific rubric**.
6. **Diversity** of demographic and disciplinary backgrounds across the panel.

## Common Evaluation Criteria for Research Proposals

Recommended scored dimensions (each 1–5 or 1–6 numeric):

1. **Scientific quality / excellence of the proposed research.**
2. **Originality and innovative potential.**
3. **Importance / expected impact** in the field and (where relevant) for society.
4. **Soundness of methodology and feasibility.**
5. **Quality of the principal investigator(s)** and team.
6. **Quality of the host environment and resources.**
7. **Adequacy of budget and timeline** to the proposed work.
8. **Open-science / data-management plan** quality.
9. **Ethical, legal, and societal aspects** addressed.
10. **Synergies and added value** (especially for international or consortium calls).

## Reviewer Conduct Norms

- Sign and uphold a code of conduct including confidentiality, COI, no-use, no-disclosure.
- Provide written justification for every score.
- Distinguish strengths and weaknesses explicitly.
- Avoid unsupported assertions and personal remarks.
- Flag any suspected research misconduct or prior duplicate submission.

## Panel Norms

- Calibration discussion at the start of every panel.
- Each proposal discussed by at least two reviewers + one moderator.
- Final ranking justified in a consensus report.
- Where reviewers diverge by >2 scale points, mandatory adjudication.
