# Tips for Good Practice in Peer Review (Royal Society — Sear)

Ten-tip rubric from the Head of the Department of Population Health at LSHTM.

## Ten Tips

1. Respond promptly to review requests, accept or decline; recommend early-career researchers as alternatives when declining.
2. Consider dual audiences — comments should help editors decide *and* give authors constructive feedback.
3. Prioritize methodology and data assessment; once those are sound, interpretation issues are usually easier to fix.
4. Review the manuscript that was submitted, not the paper you would have written.
5. Verify reproducibility — sufficient information for others to replicate the study and interpret findings.
6. Evaluate presentation quality — clarity, abstract accuracy, whether figures fairly represent results.
7. Suggest improvements to readability without formally editing; flag language quality issues that obscure scientific content.
8. Flag integrity concerns including ethics, data availability, and transparency to editors.
9. Acknowledge competency limits — tell the editor what you cannot properly assess.
10. Review your own report carefully before sending — clarity, professionalism, politeness, no accidental personal details.

## Guiding Principle

"Review others as you want to be reviewed yourself."
