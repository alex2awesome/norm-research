# How to Spot a Statistical Problem: Advice for a Non-Statistical Reviewer

## When Statistical Review Is Needed

Reviewers should request specialist statistical review when they encounter:
- Insufficient detail in methods or results
- Common statistical issues
- Interpretation not based on the presented evidence

If reviewers lack the expertise to assess statistical components, they are encouraged to recommend specialist statistical review.

## Common Statistical Problems

### 1. Sample size and power issues
- No sample size calculation
- Underpowered analyses
- Post-hoc power calculations (often misleading)

### 2. Inappropriate analyses
- Using parametric tests on non-normal data without checking assumptions
- Applying t-tests when ANOVA needed
- Multiple comparisons without correction

### 3. Outcome reporting bias
- Selective reporting of significant outcomes
- Switching primary outcome between protocol and paper
- Composite outcomes hiding negative individual results

### 4. Confounding and adjustment
- Failing to adjust for known confounders
- Over-adjustment (controlling for mediators)
- "Garden of forking paths" — too many decisions

### 5. P-value abuse
- Overemphasis on p < 0.05
- Confusing statistical and clinical significance
- "Trending toward significance" language

### 6. Subgroup analyses
- Cherry-picking subgroups
- Multiple comparisons not corrected
- Drawing causal inferences from observational subgroups

### 7. Missing data
- Ignoring missingness
- Inappropriate imputation
- No sensitivity analysis

### 8. Time-to-event analyses
- Failing to account for censoring
- Incorrect proportional hazards assumptions
- No competing risks analysis

### 9. Diagnostic accuracy
- No appropriate gold standard
- Spectrum bias
- No confidence intervals on sensitivity/specificity

## Reviewer Strategy

1. Check whether **statistical methods are described in sufficient detail**
2. Verify that the **analysis matches the research question**
3. Ensure **assumptions are checked**
4. Check that **uncertainty is quantified** (CI or SE)
5. Look for **selective reporting**
6. Flag for **specialist statistical review** when uncertain
