# How to Read a Manuscript as a Peer Reviewer (PLOS)

## Two Main Jobs of a Reviewer

1. Determine whether the authors' claims are supported.
2. Help the journal editors make their decision.

## Reading Strategy

Identify four key elements:
- What research question is being addressed?
- What methodology did the authors use?
- How does this relate to existing literature?
- What are the main findings and supporting evidence?

Recommended reading order:
1. Abstract and introduction.
2. Figures and tables with results.
3. Conclusions.
4. Complete manuscript from start to finish.

## Section-by-Section Checklist

**Abstract and Introduction**
- Does it explain why the study matters?
- Are connections to existing research clear?

**Figures and Tables**
- Are they clear and readable?
- Do captions accurately describe content?
- Are axes labeled correctly?
- Do they genuinely support the findings?

**Methods**
- Is the research technically sound?
- Are controls and conditions adequate?
- Is there sufficient data for conclusions?
- Are limitations addressed?
- Could another researcher reproduce this?

**Results and Discussion**
- Do results actually support conclusions?
- Do conclusions overstate findings?
- Are study limitations discussed?

**Statistical Analysis**
- Is methodology appropriate? (Note if you lack expertise.)

**Data and Supporting Information**
- Is evidence sufficient for claims?
- Can others recreate the analyses?

## What to Ignore

- Individual typos or minor writing errors (mention only that the paper needs language editing).
- Don't use references to promote your own work.
- Focus on substance over writing quality.

## Confidentiality

"Everything you have access to as a reviewer needs to stay confidential until the work is published."
