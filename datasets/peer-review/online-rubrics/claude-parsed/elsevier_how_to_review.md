# Elsevier's Complete Guide to Conducting a Peer Review

## 1. Before You Begin

Consider these key questions before accepting:

- **Expertise match**: "Does the article match your area of expertise?" Accept only if capable of providing quality feedback.
- **Conflicts of interest**: Disclose any potential issues to the editor when responding.
- **Time commitment**: Ensure you can meet deadlines; reviewing requires substantial work.
- **Knowledge gaps**: Access free tutorials through Elsevier's Researcher Academy, including the "Certified Peer Reviewer course."
- **Policy familiarity**: Review official reviewer responsibilities and guidelines.

**Critical timing**: Respond promptly, even with a decline. Delays harm authors and slow the process.

## 2. Managing Your Review

### Confidentiality Requirements

Treat all materials as confidential; don't share without editor authorization. This applies to manuscripts, reports, and correspondence about submissions.

### Generative AI Restrictions

The guidance states: "Reviewers or editors should not upload the manuscript or any part of it into a Generative AI tool."

Additional prohibitions include:
- Don't use AI on review reports or decision letters
- Don't employ AI for review evaluation or decision-making

## 3. Structuring Your Review

### Original Research Articles

Evaluate each component:

**Title & Abstract**: "Evaluate whether the title accurately and sufficiently describes the content."

**Introduction**: Assess current literature, scientific rationale, and clearly stated objectives.

**Methods**: Verify sufficient detail for replication, appropriate experimental design, patient population description, and adequate statistical reporting.

**Results**: Check presentation appropriateness and whether additional analyses (confidence intervals, sensitivity tests) are needed.

**Discussion**: Ensure interpretation connects to data and that limitations are explicitly emphasized.

**References**: Confirm relevance and currency for the topic.

**Language**: Recommend professional editing if needed.

**Overall assessment**: Determine novelty, importance, and field contribution.

### Review Articles

Evaluate:
- Rationale for writing the review
- Historical development and future directions
- Coverage of recent, relevant research
- Methodological transparency (search strategies, inclusion criteria)
- Appropriate statistical methods if applicable
- Overall structure and clarity
- Language quality

### Sex and Gender Reporting

Reviewers should assess whether studies include sex-disaggregated data and analyze gender appropriately. The guidance recommends following the SAGER guidelines for consistent terminology where "sex" indicates biological classification and "gender" refers to socially constructed roles.

### Research Data & Ethics

Examine data visualizations, figures, and tables. Verify ethical approval documentation when studies involve human or animal subjects.

## 4. Finalizing Your Review

### Best Practices — DO

- Summarize the article briefly, demonstrating comprehension
- Provide main impressions regarding novelty and impact
- Use short, clearly-defined paragraphs referencing specific sections
- Verify journal-specific formatting compliance
- Give specific, actionable suggestions
- Review methodology, statistics, results, and conclusions carefully
- Consider data presentation, reproducibility, and whether conclusions match findings
- Report suspected plagiarism or research misconduct to editors with detail
- Recognize potential unconscious bias in your assessment

### Best Practices — DON'T

- Comment extensively on minor grammar or spelling issues
- Make personal attacks or ad hominem remarks
- Dismiss alternative viewpoints lacking genuine scientific reason
- Share reviews without editor and author agreement
- Suggest citations solely to promote your own work

### Recommendation Types

Select one of four options:
1. **Reject** — provide reasoning
2. **Accept** without revision
3. **Revise (Major or Minor)** — specify required changes and indicate willingness for follow-up review
4. The editor makes final decisions; recommendations guide but don't determine outcomes
