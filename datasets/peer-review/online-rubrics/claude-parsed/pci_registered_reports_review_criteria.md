# PCI Registered Reports — Reviewer Rubric (Stage 1 and Stage 2)

Registered Reports require pre-registration of methods *before* data collection; reviewers evaluate the *protocol* (Stage 1) and later evaluate execution (Stage 2). The rubric is published verbatim by PCI RR and is mirrored by every Registered-Reports-friendly journal (Royal Society Open Science, Nature Human Behaviour, Cortex, etc.).

## Stage 1 Criteria (protocol review, pre-data)

### 1A. Scientific Validity of the Research Question
The extent to which the research question is scientifically justifiable through theory, application, or evidence base. **Subjective judgments about importance or novelty are explicitly excluded.**

### 1B. Logic and Rationale of the Hypotheses
Evaluates coherence and credibility of *a priori* hypotheses. Hypotheses must follow directly from theory and be worthy of investigation. Hypothesis specification is optional (exploratory RRs are allowed) but where present, must be testable.

### 1C. Soundness and Feasibility of the Methodology
Assessment of:
- study procedures and design choices;
- critical design features (validity, blinding, randomisation, controls);
- analysis plans (statistical tests, model specification);
- for hypothesis tests: sampling plans and **power analysis** (or Bayesian equivalent) demonstrating ability to detect or rule out the predicted effect.

### 1D. Methodological Clarity and Detail
Whether the clarity and degree of methodological detail is sufficient to **closely replicate** the proposed study procedures and to **prevent undisclosed analytical flexibility** ("researcher degrees of freedom").

### 1E. Outcome-Neutral Quality Checks
Whether the authors have specified sufficient **outcome-neutral conditions** that must hold for the design to inform the question — for example:
- positive controls (manipulation checks);
- absence of floor / ceiling effects;
- a priori sample-quality criteria;
- successful instrument validation.

## Stage 2 Criteria (post-data review)

### 2A. Data Quality
Determines whether the data meet the prespecified outcome-neutral conditions and are adequate to test the hypotheses or address the research questions.

### 2B. Introduction Consistency
Whether the Introduction, rationale, and stated hypotheses are the **same as the approved Stage 1 submission** (apart from tense changes and corrections of typographical errors).

### 2C. Protocol Adherence
Whether the authors **adhered precisely** to the registered study procedures. Minor deviations require recommender approval; substantial deviations require resubmission.

### 2D. Quality of Unregistered (Exploratory) Analyses
Whether any exploratory analyses *added* beyond the registered plan are:
- **justified** (motivated, not fishing);
- **methodologically sound**;
- **clearly distinguished** from the preregistered confirmatory analyses;
- not used to draw confirmatory inferences.

### 2E. Justification of Conclusions
Whether the authors' conclusions are **justified given the evidence**. Decisions disregard the perceived importance, novelty, or conclusiveness of the results.

## Distinctive Norms for RR Reviewers

- **Outcome-blind reviewing**: reviewers must not let the *direction* of the result influence the recommendation; in-principle acceptance is granted at Stage 1, before any results exist.
- **No "interestingness" rejections**: novelty / significance are not grounds for rejection at either stage.
- **No "I would have done it differently"**: reviewers must distinguish suggestions from required changes; required changes must be tied to validity, not preference.
- **Transparent scoring rubric**: reviewers' Stage 1 scores anchor the in-principle acceptance and are visible to the author and the public.
