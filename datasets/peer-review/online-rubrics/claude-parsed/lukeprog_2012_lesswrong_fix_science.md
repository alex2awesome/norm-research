# How to Fix Science (LessWrong / lukeprog)

Rationalist-community essay on how to repair scientific epistemics. Part of the genre of meta-science writing that influences how reviewers think about quality.

## On Peer Review

The article *briefly* acknowledges peer review as a discovered useful practice: "We've also discovered the usefulness of peer review, control groups, blind and double-blind studies, plus a variety of statistical methods."

It does **not** propose specific reviewer reforms; the comments section contains substantial criticism of peer review (Barry_Cotter, citing gwern's argument that the value of peer review is overstated).

## What lukeprog Targets Instead

1. **Publication bias** — journals should only accept experiments registered in a public database before they began.
2. **Experimenter bias** — train scientists in rationality skills to reduce cognitive errors.
3. **Bad statistics** — replace frequentist methods with Bayesian inference; improve statistical education.

## Implication for Reviewer Norms

LessWrong's stance: a reviewer's job is *not* to ratify pre-registration breaches and is to demand it; reviewers should treat unregistered exploratory results differently from confirmatory tests. Rationality training and skepticism are the limiting reagents, not formality.
