# Goodman et al. 1994 — 34-Item Manuscript Quality Assessment Tool

The Goodman/Berlin/Fletcher index is one of the earliest validated, multi-item rubrics for assessing the quality of a research report (rather than the underlying research). 44 expert assessors evaluated 111 manuscript pairs (before vs after peer review/editing) at the *Annals of Internal Medicine*.

## The 34-Item Manuscript Quality Index

Each item scored 1 (very poor) – 5 (excellent). Items grouped by manuscript section:

### Title and Abstract (3 items)
1. Title is clear, accurate, and informative.
2. Abstract reflects the content of the article.
3. Abstract is structured and includes objective, design, setting, patients, intervention, measurements, results, conclusions.

### Introduction (3 items)
4. Background information is sufficient and well-referenced.
5. Hypothesis or objective is clearly stated.
6. Importance / relevance of the study is established.

### Methods (10 items)
7. Study design is clearly described and appropriate.
8. Study population / sample selection is fully described.
9. Inclusion and exclusion criteria are stated.
10. Sample size is justified (power calculation if appropriate).
11. Setting and timeframe of the study are stated.
12. Intervention or exposure is described in sufficient detail to replicate.
13. Outcome measures are clearly defined.
14. Statistical methods are clearly described.
15. Confidence intervals or precision measures are specified.
16. Methods of dealing with missing data and dropouts are stated.

### Results (8 items)
17. Demographics and baseline characteristics of the sample are reported.
18. Numbers of subjects analysed in each stage are reported (flow of participants).
19. Primary outcomes are reported with appropriate precision.
20. Confidence intervals are given for primary outcomes.
21. Subgroup analyses are clearly identified (a priori vs post hoc).
22. Negative findings are reported.
23. Tables and figures are clear, accurate, and necessary.
24. Adverse events / unexpected findings are reported.

### Discussion (7 items)
25. Findings are summarised accurately.
26. Limitations of the study are discussed.
27. Generalisability of findings is appropriately considered.
28. Findings are placed in context of prior literature.
29. Tone of conclusions is appropriate to the strength of evidence.
30. Conclusions follow from the data.
31. Implications for practice are discussed where appropriate.

### References and Acknowledgements (3 items)
32. Citations are accurate and reflect the cited works.
33. Acknowledgements include funding, conflicts of interest, and contributions.
34. Overall manuscript quality is high.

## Empirical Findings as Reviewer Norms

- **33 of the 34 items improved** in the direction of higher quality after the peer-review and editing process. Only one item showed no change.
- **Largest improvements** were on:
  - Item 26 — discussion of limitations,
  - Item 27 — generalisability,
  - Item 20 — use of confidence intervals,
  - Item 29 — tone of conclusions.
- These four items therefore represent the **highest-yield reviewer interventions** — places where reviewer comments measurably change manuscript quality.

## Operational Norms for Reviewers

- Use the 34-item Goodman index as a structured **pre-flight checklist** before writing the review.
- Devote particular attention to items where reviewer impact is largest (limitations, generalisability, confidence intervals, conclusion tone).
- Score the manuscript on each item rather than offer a single global judgement.
