# How to Read a Paper — Three-Pass Method (Keshav)

A widely-cited recipe for paper reading that doubles as the implicit reviewer-rubric for many CS reviewers.

## The Three Passes

### First Pass (~5–10 minutes)
Goal: high-level picture.
- Read title, abstract, introduction.
- Read section and subsection headings.
- Read conclusion.
- Glance at references; mark ones you've already read.

After this you should be able to answer the **5 Cs**: Category, Context, Correctness, Contributions, Clarity.

### Second Pass (~1 hour)
Goal: grasp content but skip details.
- Examine figures, diagrams, illustrations carefully (axes labeled? error bars? statistically significant?).
- Mark unread relevant references for later.
- Take notes, summarize main thrust with supporting evidence.

If you can't grasp the paper after the second pass, set it aside for later, ask the authors, or come back when you have more background.

### Third Pass (~4–5 hours for a beginner, ~1 for an expert)
Goal: re-implement the paper.
- Identify and challenge every assumption.
- Note every reference for further reading.
- Think about how *you* would present the same idea.

After the third pass you can reconstruct the entire structure from memory, identify strong and weak points, missing citations, and unstated assumptions.

## Reviewer Implication

Reviewers should plan for at least a Pass 2 effort; a Pass 1 review is a desk-rejection or accept-without-reading and is what generates "Reviewer 2" complaints.
