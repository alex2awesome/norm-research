# CONSORT 2025 Statement: Updated Guideline for Reporting Randomised Trials

## Overview

The CONSORT (Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials) Statement is an evidence-based, minimum set of recommendations for reporting the results of randomized trials, outlined in a 30-item checklist and a flow diagram.

## Purpose and Development

The CONSORT statement was designed to improve the quality of reporting and provides a minimum set of items to be included in a report of a randomized trial. CONSORT was first published in 1996, then updated in 2001 and 2010. The CONSORT 2025 statement includes substantive and structural changes for recent methodological advancements.

## Current Version (CONSORT 2025)

The CONSORT 2025 statement consists of:
- A 30-item checklist of essential items that should be included when reporting the results of a randomized trial
- A diagram for documenting the flow of participants through the trial
- Seven new checklist items added; three items revised; one deleted
- A new section on open science integrated into the checklist

## Section Coverage

The CONSORT checklist covers:
1. **Title and abstract** — identification as randomized trial, structured summary
2. **Introduction** — background, objectives
3. **Methods** — trial design, participants, interventions, outcomes, sample size, randomization, blinding, statistical methods
4. **Results** — participant flow, recruitment, baseline data, numbers analyzed, outcomes and estimation, ancillary analyses, harms
5. **Discussion** — limitations, generalizability, interpretation
6. **Other information** — registration, protocol, funding, open science

## Endorsement and Implementation

CONSORT is endorsed by numerous journals worldwide and by prominent editorial organizations, including the World Association of Medical Editors (WAME), International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) and Council of Science Editors (CSE).

## Reviewer Use

Reviewers can use the CONSORT checklist as a structured rubric to verify that all essential items in a randomized trial report are present.
