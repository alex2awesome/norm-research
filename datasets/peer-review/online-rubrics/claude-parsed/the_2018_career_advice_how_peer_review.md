# Career Advice: How to Peer Review a Paper (THE)

Compiled tips from senior academics across UK / global institutions.

## Be Detailed and Clear

- Jon Tennant (Imperial): "It's not Amazon — peer reviews are supposed to be in-depth critical analyses."
- Edd Pitt (Kent): reviews must be "clear, professional, useful" to help authors improve.

## Maintain Openness to New Ideas

- Simon Marginson (UCL IoE): warns against dismissing novel approaches; "New insights are the lifeblood of academic fields."
- Reviewers should appreciate different perspectives and innovative reworking of established concepts.

## Practice Constructive Criticism

- Helen Kara (independent researcher): find genuine praise alongside critiques. Helps authors understand "what they can relax about and where they need to focus."

## Remain Objective

- Edd Pitt: evaluate "whether or not it makes a contribution towards moving the field forward," regardless of personal agreement.
- Sanjeev Krishna (St George's): warns against confirmation bias and hidden conflicts of interest.

## Avoid Unnecessary Nitpicking

- Randy McIntosh (Toronto): don't comment on minor typos unless grammar significantly impairs comprehension.
- Jon Tennant: accept/reject recommendations belong solely to editors.
