# Reviewing for Machine Learning Conferences Explained

A Medium / Towards Data Science walkthrough of how a junior researcher should approach reviewing for venues like NeurIPS / ICML / ICLR.

## Read Strategically by Section

- **Abstract**: note every author claim so you can verify it later in the paper.
- **Introduction**: identify the related works cited and the positioning relative to state-of-the-art.
- **Methods / Results**: check that each abstract claim is supported by a corresponding piece of evidence in the paper.

## Constructive Intent

- View yourself as a peer advancing the field, not a superior judge.
- Provide positive feedback alongside criticism.
- Help authors improve their work rather than merely criticize.

## Be Transparent About Expertise

- Set the confidence score honestly when outside your specialization.
- "An unknowledgeable reviewer with high confidence is a nightmare."

## Required Review Components

- Paper summary (demonstrates that you understood the paper).
- Strengths section.
- Weaknesses section.
- Detailed comments tying back to specific concerns.
- Multiple revision passes (the author recommends 3+ before submission).

## What to Evaluate

- Novelty and clarity of contribution.
- Experimental support for every claim.
- Justification of design choices ("magic numbers" should be questioned).
- Comparison to related work.
- Clarity and completeness of explanations.

## Practical Tips

- Plan ~3 hours for an initial draft plus revision passes.
- Flag every unsupported claim and missing ablation.
- Balance criticism with explicit acknowledgment of contributions.
