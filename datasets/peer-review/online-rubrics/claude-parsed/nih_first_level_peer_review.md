# NIH First-Level Peer Review Criteria and Scoring

## Overall Impact Assessment
The primary focus is determining "the **overall impact** that your proposed project will likely have on the biomedical research field(s) involved."

## Scoring System
- **Scale**: 1-9 numerical rating (1 = exceptional; 9 = poor)
- **Calculation**: Final Overall Impact score = (mean of all panelist scores) × 10
- **Result Range**: 10 (high impact) to 90 (low impact)

## Five Core Review Criteria (legacy)

1. **Significance** — Importance of the problem; will the project drive change?
2. **Investigator(s)** — Are the PIs/collaborators well-suited?
3. **Innovation** — Does it shift current paradigms or use novel methods?
4. **Approach** — Are the strategy, methodology, analyses well-reasoned?
5. **Environment** — Is the institutional environment appropriate?

## Simplified Peer Review Framework (2025+)

For applications due on or after January 25, 2025, NIH uses three factors:
1. **Importance of the Research** (Significance + Innovation)
2. **Rigor and Feasibility** (Approach)
3. **Expertise and Resources** (Investigator + Environment)

## Review Criteria Categories

1. **Scored Review Criteria**: Receive individual numerical scores (1-9 scale) that directly contribute to Overall Impact
2. **Additional Review Criteria**: Do not receive individual scores but influence Overall Impact (e.g., human subjects protection, animal welfare)
3. **Additional Review Considerations**: Do not affect scoring; address programmatic issues like budget justification

## Funding Probability

- Scores 10-30: most likely to be funded
- Scores 31-45: might be funded
- Scores >46: rarely funded

The overall impact score does not represent a mathematical sum or mean of the criterion scores; reviewers weigh different criteria as they see fit.
