# Thoughtful Peer Review (Goldenrod Editorial — K. Wiley)

A developmental editor's view of what makes review reports useful.

## Eight Recommendations

1. **Emphasize the big picture** — identify three major areas that would benefit from revision rather than overwhelming with minor corrections.
2. **Explain feedback thoroughly** — vague comments like "this was confusing" leave authors stuck; specify *why* something is unclear (e.g., chronological jumps without transitions).
3. **Maintain a kind, encouraging tone** — set the report aside and reread imagining the author as a respected colleague.
4. **Acknowledge strengths** — helps the author remain open to suggestions instead of feeling entirely criticized.
5. **Monitor length** — a few pages for articles, longer for books; >10 pages overwhelms and hides main points.
6. **Respect the author's vision** — don't criticize a different approach simply because you'd write differently. Evaluate how successfully the author accomplishes *their* stated mission.
7. **Meet deadlines** — timely reviews matter for authors' careers and publication timelines. If too busy, decline graciously and suggest alternative reviewers.
8. **Embrace diverse forms** — remain open to nontraditional scholarly structures and voices that serve the author's purpose.

## Implicit Norm

A reviewer who substitutes their own preferences for the author's argument has stopped reviewing.
