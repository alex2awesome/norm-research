# NIH Simplified Peer Review Framework (2025) — Reviewer Rubric

Effective for applications submitted on or after January 25, 2025. Reorganises the five regulatory criteria into three Factors. Replaces the prior independent 1–9 scoring of all five criteria with a streamlined two-numeric / one-binary structure.

## Factor 1 — Importance of the Research (numeric, 1–9)

Combines **Significance** and **Innovation**.

### Significance — reviewers evaluate:
- Importance of the proposed research in the context of current scientific challenges.
- Whether the project addresses a critical knowledge gap, solves a critical problem, or generates a meaningful advance.
- Rigor of the prior research that justifies the proposed work.
- Strength of the scientific premise.

### Innovation — reviewers evaluate:
- Use of novel concepts, approaches, methodologies, instrumentation, or interventions.
- Refinement, improvement, or new application of existing approaches in a novel way.
- Note: a project lacking novelty may still be of critical importance and rated highly here on the basis of significance.

## Factor 2 — Rigor and Feasibility (numeric, 1–9)

Encompasses the **Approach** criterion split into two sub-components.

### Rigor — reviewers evaluate:
- Likelihood that the proposed research will produce **unbiased, reproducible, robust** findings.
- Quality of experimental design, including appropriate **controls**.
- Justification of **sample sizes** (power calculations).
- Plans for **analysis, interpretation, and reporting** (including pre-registration where appropriate).
- Plans for considering **relevant biological variables** (sex as a biological variable, age, ancestry).
- For studies involving humans or animals: rigor of intervention, outcome measurement, generalisability, sample diversity.

### Feasibility — reviewers evaluate:
- Soundness and achievability of the methodology with the time, expertise, and resources available.
- Existence of plans to address potential problems and emerging challenges.
- For human-subjects studies: realism of recruitment and retention plans for **diverse populations**.
- For clinical trials: feasibility of timelines, enrollment milestones, and trial logistics.

## Factor 3 — Expertise and Resources (binary)

Evaluated as **Sufficient** or **Gaps Identified** (with required explanation when gaps are identified). Combines **Investigator(s)** and **Environment**.

### Investigator(s) — reviewers evaluate:
- Demonstrated background, training, and expertise of PI(s) and key personnel relative to career stage.
- For multiple-PI applications: quality of leadership coordination plans, governance, and complementarity.

### Environment — reviewers evaluate:
- Whether institutional resources, facilities, and intellectual environment are adequate to ensure successful project execution.
- Subject availability, instrumentation, collaborative support, ongoing studies in related areas.

## Overall Impact Score

After scoring the three Factors, reviewers assign a single **Overall Impact** score (1 = exceptional, 9 = poor) reflecting the likelihood the project will exert sustained, powerful influence on the relevant research field(s). The Overall Impact is *not* a mathematical function of the Factor scores — reviewers integrate them with judgement.

## Additional Required Considerations (not scored, but addressed)

Reviewers must also evaluate and comment on each of:

- **Protections for human subjects** (where applicable).
- **Inclusion of women, minorities, and individuals across the lifespan.**
- **Vertebrate animals.**
- **Biohazards.**
- **Resubmission justification** (response to prior critique).
- **Renewal progress.**
- **Revision adequacy.**
- **Authentication of key biological and chemical resources.**
- **Budget adequacy and justification.**

## Conflict-of-Interest Norms

- Reviewers must declare and recuse for personal, professional, financial, or institutional conflicts.
- Confidentiality of submitted applications and review discussions is a binding obligation; sharing applications with non-reviewers is misconduct.
