# The Dos and Don'ts of Peer Reviewing (Eos / AGU)

A practical reviewer-conduct checklist published in the AGU Editors' Vox column.

## DOs

- Provide an overall summary of the paper's main contribution and journal appropriateness.
- Summarize major revision items needed.
- List minor suggestions separately with specific line numbers.
- Focus on the research itself, not author attributes.
- Use third-person language to avoid sounding accusatory.
- Provide specific examples with citations.
- Offer "actionable feedback" that guides authors constructively.
- Proofread your review for bias before submitting.

## DON'Ts

- Make vague statements without elaboration.
- Use commanding or accusatory language.
- Include gendered or derogatory remarks.
- Assume language proficiency reflects scientific quality.
- Make inflammatory assumptions without evidence.
- Question authors' qualifications.
- Include personal attacks on character.
- Allow unconscious bias to influence recommendations.
- Proceed if conflicts of interest exist without disclosure.

## Implicit Norms

- A complete review separates major-vs-minor items and references specific line numbers.
- Linguistic phrasing is part of review quality (depersonalize, third-person, no accusation).
- Reviewer self-audit for bias is part of the workflow, not optional.
