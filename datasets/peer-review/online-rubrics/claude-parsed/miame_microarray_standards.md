# MIAME: Minimum Information About a Microarray Experiment

## Overview

MIAME describes the minimum information required to ensure that microarray data can be easily interpreted and that results derived from its analysis can be independently verified. The ultimate goal is to establish a standard for recording and reporting microarray-based gene expression data.

## Background

MIAME was published by the FGED Society in 2001 and was the **first published minimum information standard for high-throughput experiments in the life sciences**. Many journals and funding agencies require microarray data to comply with MIAME standards.

## Six Most Critical Elements

The six elements contributing towards MIAME compliance:

1. **Raw data for each assay** (e.g., CEL or GPR files)
2. **Final processed (normalized) data** for the set of assays in the study
3. **Essential sample annotation** including experimental factors and their values
4. **Experimental design** including sample data relationships
5. **Sufficient annotation of array features** (e.g., gene identifiers, sequence)
6. **Essential laboratory and data processing protocols**

## Related Standards

- **MINSEQE** — Minimum Information About a Next-generation Sequencing Experiment
- **MIAME-env** — Environmental genomics extension
- **MIAME-nut** — Nutritional genomics extension
- **MIAME-tox** — Toxogenomics extension
- **MIAPE** — Minimum Information About a Proteomics Experiment
- **MIBBI** — Minimum Information for Biological and Biomedical Investigations (umbrella)

## Reviewer Use

When reviewing genomics/transcriptomics papers, reviewers should verify:
- Raw data deposited in public repository (GEO, ArrayExpress)
- Adequate sample annotation
- Reproducible data processing pipeline
- Consistency of MIAME requirements
