# What Makes a Good or Bad Peer Review

## Characteristics of Bad Reviews

Bad reviews show that the reviewer didn't read the paper at all or throw a general chunk of generic text. A bad peer review is:
- Non-objective
- Non-organized
- Non-instructional or informative
- Fails to identify major flaws
- Fails to communicate criticism so authors can improve

### Specific Examples of Poor Review Language

- "The paper is not good. The authors should have done a better job." — vague, doesn't help improve
- "Figure 3 is not clear" — without explaining why or how to improve
- Insulting or unprofessional language
- Unstructured stream of consciousness listing many concerns without indicating which are most serious
- Reviews that are too short (one or two sentences)
- Reviews that focus only on grammar/typos and miss substantive issues
- Reviews that demand citations of the reviewer's own work
- Reviews that are factually wrong about the paper's claims

## Characteristics of Good Reviews

A good reviewer should be:
- **Polite, honest and clear** to the authors
- Write clearly to be understood by people whose native language is not English
- Review the author's work as if it was their own

### Constructive Language Examples

Rather than: "This problem has been solved by many people years ago"
Better: "This problem has been solved by A. Smith (AI Journal, 1992), with improvements by C. Miller (ECAI, 1999)"

A good peer review:
- Offers a balanced evaluation highlighting both merits and shortcomings
- Pairs constructive criticism with positive feedback
- Refers to specific examples with line numbers when possible
- Distinguishes major from minor concerns
- Suggests concrete improvements, not just criticism
- Makes claims that are verifiable from the manuscript text
- Acknowledges what the authors got right
- Engages with the substance rather than the surface

## Structural Features of Good Reviews

1. **Summary paragraph** demonstrating comprehension
2. **Strengths section** with specific positives
3. **Major concerns** numbered, ordered by importance
4. **Minor concerns** for clarity, typos, etc.
5. **Recommendation** with clear justification
6. **Tone**: respectful, constructive, professional

## "The Good, the Bad, and the Rude"

Some reviewers cross from constructive criticism into rudeness. This is never appropriate, regardless of how flawed the manuscript is. Editors should edit out such comments before sending to authors.
