# Bornmann 2011: Annual Review Synthesis of Peer-Review Quality Criteria

Bornmann's ARIST chapter is the most widely cited modern review-of-reviews of peer-review research. He extracts a *synthetic taxonomy* of evaluation criteria reviewers (and reviewer-of-reviewers) deploy, drawn from dozens of empirical studies.

## Bornmann's Taxonomy of Criteria Used in Peer Review

### A. Quality criteria for the manuscript

1. **Importance / significance of the research problem.**
2. **Originality / novelty.**
3. **Theoretical sophistication / conceptual clarity.**
4. **Soundness of methodology** — design, controls, statistical treatment.
5. **Validity of conclusions** — do the data support the claims?
6. **Reproducibility / verifiability of the procedure.**
7. **Adequacy of literature integration.**
8. **Quality of writing and presentation** (clarity, structure, length).
9. **Ethical conduct** — IRB approval, conflict of interest, attribution, fabrication signals.
10. **Practical applicability / generalisability.**

### B. Quality criteria for the *review report*

(adapted by Bornmann from van Rooyen and others):

11. **Coverage** — does the review address each substantive dimension of the manuscript?
12. **Specificity** — are comments tied to specific passages with line/page references?
13. **Substantiation** — are claims backed by argument, citation, or example?
14. **Constructiveness** — does the review give the author an actionable path forward?
15. **Tone / civility** — is the review free of ad hominem and rhetorical hostility?
16. **Internal consistency** — do the narrative comments cohere with the numeric recommendation?
17. **Timeliness** — was the review delivered in time?
18. **Appropriateness of recommendation** — does the recommendation track the substantive critique?

## Bornmann's Synthesis: Three Functions of Peer Review

1. **Selection** — gatekeeping the literature (allocation of journal space).
2. **Improvement** — developmental shaping of accepted manuscripts.
3. **Allocation of recognition** — symbolic certification of work.

Each function implies different evaluative emphases — selection emphasises (1)–(5), improvement emphasises (11)–(15), allocation emphasises (1) and (10).

## Identified Bias Risks Reviewers Should Self-Check

Bornmann tabulates evidence-supported biases reviewers must guard against:

- **Confirmation / theoretical bias** (Mahoney 1977).
- **Affiliation / institutional prestige bias** (Peters & Ceci 1982; Tomkins et al. 2017).
- **Gender bias** (Wenneras & Wold 1997).
- **Nationality / language bias.**
- **Cronyism** (favouring known authors).
- **Conservatism bias** against high-risk / heterodox work.
- **Confirmation of priority** by senior authors (Matthew effect).

## Procedural Recommendations Implied by the Synthesis

- Use of **structured review forms** mapping to dimensions A1–A10.
- **Independent multiple review** (3+) with explicit per-dimension scoring.
- **Editor adjudication** of substantive disagreement before decision.
- **Training and feedback** to reviewers on RQI-style metrics.
- Where possible, **double-blind** or **open** review to mitigate identified biases.
