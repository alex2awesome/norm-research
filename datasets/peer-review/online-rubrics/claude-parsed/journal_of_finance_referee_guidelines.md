# Journal of Finance Referee Guidelines

## Review Type
Refereeing at The Journal of Finance (JF) is **single-blind** — referees know author identities but authors do not know referee identities.

## Primary Reference
The Journal recommends "Preparing a Referee Report: Guidelines and Perspectives" by Jonathan B. Berk, Campbell R. Harvey, and David Hirshleifer (presented to the Board of Directors of the American Finance Association in January 2015).

## Berk-Harvey-Hirshleifer Framework

### Purpose of Refereeing
- Help editor make a decision
- Help authors improve their work
- Maintain the quality of published research

### Structure of a Referee Report

1. **Brief synopsis** — what the paper does, in your own words
2. **Summary of the contribution** — why it matters
3. **Major issues** — fundamental concerns
4. **Minor issues** — specific suggestions for improvement
5. **Recommendation** to the editor

### Key Criteria

- **Importance**: Does the topic matter?
- **Originality**: What's new?
- **Quality of analysis**: Is the methodology sound?
- **Identification**: Is the empirical strategy credible?
- **Robustness**: Have alternative explanations been ruled out?
- **Theory-data fit**: Do the empirical results match the theoretical predictions?
- **Writing quality**: Is the paper clearly presented?

### Be Constructive

Even if you recommend rejection, your report should help the authors improve their work. Avoid:
- Personal attacks
- Vague criticisms
- Demanding citations of your own work
- Suggesting unrealistically extensive new analyses

## AI Policy for Referees

The use of AI software may be employed in the preparation of referee reports to assist with drafting or editing text or looking up prior references. However, referees remain fully responsible for the content of their reports.

## Confidentiality

The Journal has strict confidentiality policies regarding the peer review process and referee identities. Manuscripts are privileged communications.
