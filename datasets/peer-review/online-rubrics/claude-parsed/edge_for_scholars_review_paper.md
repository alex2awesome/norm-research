# How to Review a Paper (Edge for Scholars / Vanderbilt)

## Initial Assessment: Should You Accept?

- Sufficient expertise?
- Genuine interest in the manuscript?
- Conflicts of interest (relationships, financial)?
- Time availability (typically 10-day turnaround)?

Decline promptly so editors can find alternatives.

## First Reading

Skim for:
- Central research question and relevance.
- Originality and field contribution.
- Conclusions match evidence?
- Figures/tables necessary?
- Major flaws?

## Major Flaws to Watch For

- Conclusion contradicted by evidence.
- Outdated or discredited methodology.
- Overlooked influential factors.
- Weak, ambiguous, or inconsistent data.
- Weak statistical support.
- Redundant data without novelty.
- Poor writing clarity / numerous errors.

## Detailed Section-by-Section Review

Examine each component (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, References, Acknowledgements) separately. A downloadable checklist with section questions is provided.

## Writing Your Review

- **Opening paragraph**: summarize the research, demonstrate comprehension, highlight strengths.
- **Body**: contribution, methodology, data, novelty; concerns clearly.
- **Recommendation**: Accept / Major / Minor Revision / Reject with rationale. Flag fraud or plagiarism separately.

## Professional Standards

- Treat reviewing as a serious professional responsibility.
- Evaluate scientific rigor and clarity, not potential impact.
- Constructive suggestions; avoid excessive criticism.
- Specific line / page references.
- Neutral language — focus on the work, not the authors. Avoid value-laden adjectives ("terrible," "crazy"); say "the work" / "the data" rather than "the authors."
- Revise before submitting.
