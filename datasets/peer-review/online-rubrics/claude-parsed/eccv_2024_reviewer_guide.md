# ECCV 2024 Reviewer Guidelines

## Overview
The guide emphasizes that reviewers are essential to maintaining ECCV's technical quality. Reviewers should seek innovation and meaningful contributions, not just benchmark performance superiority.

## Core Reviewing Principles

**Evaluation Focus:**
The guide states papers should be "technically sound and make a contribution to the field." Reviewers should acknowledge novel approaches even without extensive empirical validation.

**Quality Standards:**
Reviews require substantive depth. The guidance explicitly rejects "bullet lists with one short sentence per bullet" as insufficient. Feedback should guide authors toward improvement.

**Impartiality:**
Reviewers must treat papers fairly regardless of author knowledge, avoid imposing unnecessary citation burdens for concurrent arXiv work, and refrain from rejecting papers solely for missing comparisons to unpublished preprints.

## Ethics Requirements

**Anonymity Protection:**
Reviewers must not actively discover author identities and should maintain confidentiality throughout the process.

**Idea Safeguarding:**
Papers remain proprietary until publication. Reviewers must not share submissions, results, code, or supplementary materials with non-reviewers, nor use reviewed ideas in their own work.

**Conflict Management:**
Conflicts include institutional affiliations, direct collaboration within three years, advisor-advisee relationships, and perceived impartiality concerns. Conflicted reviewers should decline immediately.

**Professional Conduct:**
Reviews should maintain courtesy and constructiveness, avoiding sarcasm or belittling language.

## Writing Quality Reviews

Effective reviews should:
- Allow reflection time before writing
- Provide specific, detailed feedback
- Ground novelty claims in concrete citations and explanations
- Address writing clarity with targeted suggestions
- Use third-person language referencing "the paper" rather than "you"
- Offer constructive improvement suggestions

## Special Policy Areas

**arXiv Papers:**
The guide clarifies that authors aren't obligated to compare against recent preprints. Performance gaps with arXiv work alone don't warrant rejection. However, obvious direct inspiration should be acknowledged.

**Supplementary Material:**
This section supports detailed derivations and extended results. It cannot contain improved method versions or corrected PDFs. Reviewers may request clarifications but shouldn't demand substantial new experiments during rebuttal.

**Withdrawn Datasets:**
Using discontinued datasets (like DukeMTMC-ReID) warrants scrutiny but isn't automatic grounds for rejection. Reviewers should flag such cases to Area Chairs.

**Dataset Contributions:**
If papers claim datasets as contributions, reasonable expectation exists for public release upon publication. Non-public datasets used for training don't disqualify papers.

## LLM Policy

Reviewers may use language models for "polishing" review wording only. However, showing submissions to LLMs constitutes serious misconduct violating confidentiality. Reviewers remain accountable for review accuracy regardless of tool use.

The policy distinguishes between LLM-polished summaries (acceptable if reviewer vouches for accuracy) versus LLM-generated summaries (unacceptable, as they undermine reviewer engagement with the paper).

## Reviewer FAQs

**Acceptance Rates:** No minimum rejection quotas exist; evaluate each paper independently.

**Known Authors:** Impartial review remains obligatory even when author identity is known.

**Withdrawn Dataset Comparisons:** Requesting evaluation on withdrawn datasets violates policy.

**Professional Conferences:** Four-page papers from venues like ICIP or ICASSP don't trigger dual-submission violations, though substantive value assessment remains necessary.
