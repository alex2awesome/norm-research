# NeurIPS 2020 Broader Impact Statement Review Guidelines

## Historical Context

In 2020, NeurIPS required all papers to include a broader impact statement, but this requirement was removed in 2021 in favor of a checklist approach.

## 2020 Review Guidelines

When NeurIPS did require broader impact statements, reviewers were instructed to evaluate them according to specific criteria.

## Author Requirements

Authors were required to include a statement of the potential broader impact of their work, including:
- Ethical aspects
- Future societal consequences
- Both positive and negative outcomes

## Reviewer Questions

Reviewers were asked:

1. **"Have the authors adequately addressed the broader impact of their work, including potential negative ethical and societal implications of their work?"**

2. Whether the submission raised potential **ethical concerns**, including methods, applications, or data that:
   - Create or reinforce unfair bias
   - Have a primary purpose of harm or injury
   - Use sensitive personal data without proper consent
   - Have national security implications

## Authoring Guidance

For authors, papers should discuss potential negative societal impacts if appropriate, and could discuss mitigation strategies such as:
- Gated release of models
- Providing defenses
- Mechanisms for monitoring misuse

Authors were free to write that a broader impact discussion was not applicable if their work was very theoretical or general enough with no particular application foreseen.

## Legacy

The broader impact requirement evolved into the NeurIPS Paper Checklist (specifically items 10 and 11 on Broader Impacts and Safeguards), which is now widely adopted across ML conferences.
