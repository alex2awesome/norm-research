# EMBO Press Referee/Reviewer Guidelines

## Confidentiality and Conflicts of Interest

Referees should treat the review process as being strictly confidential, and manuscripts refereed for The EMBO Journal should not be discussed with anyone not directly involved in the review process. If you have a conflict of interest regarding the manuscript you have been asked to review (e.g., it competes with your own work), you should immediately delete the manuscript file and decline to referee.

## Quality of Reviews

Factually incorrect statements should be avoided, and arguments in favour or disfavour of a given study should be justified. Reviewers are encouraged to be very clear about what revision will be required for a manuscript to become acceptable.

## Reviewer Anonymity and Openness

Referees should, as a rule, not disclose their identities to the authors or to other colleagues since they may be asked to comment on the criticisms of other referees and may then find it difficult to be objective.

## Consulting Colleagues

If colleagues are consulted, they should be identified to the editors. If experts from outside the referee's own laboratory are consulted, referees should check with the editors beforehand to avoid involving anyone who may have been excluded by the editor or the authors.

## Transparent Peer Review Process

EMBO Press publishes the reviewer reports alongside published papers and maintains reviewer anonymity. Reviewers should note that their reports as well as the authors' point-by-point responses will be included in this transparent process document.

## Response Timeline

EMBO Press asks that reviewers respond promptly or inform the journal if they anticipate a significant delay.

## Cross-Referee Commentary

EMBO Press uses a model where reviewers can comment on each others' reviews after submission, which improves consistency and helps editors make decisions.
