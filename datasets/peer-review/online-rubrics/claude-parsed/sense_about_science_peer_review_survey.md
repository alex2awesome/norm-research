# Sense About Science Peer Review Survey 2019

## Overview

Sense about Science partnered with Elsevier to conduct a follow-up to the influential 2009 Peer Review Survey, surveying 3000 researchers to understand how researcher attitudes have changed over a decade.

## Researcher Expectations

Researchers have **high expectations** for peer review in identifying the best papers and original research. However, under half think peer review is currently able to:
- Reliably determine plagiarism or fraud
- Ensure previous work is properly acknowledged

## Satisfaction with Peer Review

- **75% of researchers are satisfied** with peer review (up from 69% in 2009)
- **62%** trusted the output they assessed just a week before taking the survey
- **37%** were not sure they could put their trust in the output quality

## Training and Support Needs

- **56% believe there is a lack of guidance** on how to review
- **68% agree formal training would improve the quality of reviews**
- Many think more could be done to support reviewers

## What Reviewers Look For (Per the Survey)

In assessing paper quality, reviewers prioritize:
- Originality and novelty
- Methodological soundness
- Clarity of writing
- Importance/impact
- Reproducibility
- Statistical validity
- Ethical conduct

## What Researchers Want

The survey reveals researchers want:
- More transparent review processes
- Better recognition for reviewing
- Faster turnaround times
- More consistent reviewer training
- Open peer review options
- Public review reports for accepted papers

## Implications

The survey suggests a tension: peer review is generally trusted but reviewers want more training, and the system relies heavily on volunteer labor without clear quality assurance mechanisms.
