Systematic Review Registries

  • PROSPERO This link opens in a new window
    PROSPERO is an international database of prospectively registered systematic reviews in health and social care. Key features from the review protocol are recorded and maintained as a permanent record in PROSPERO. This will provide a comprehensive listing of systematic reviews registered at inception, and enable comparison of reported review findings with what was planned in the protocol.
  • The Cochrane Collaboration This link opens in a new window
    Included in the Cochrane Collaboration is DARE, CENTRAL and the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews.
  • Campbell Collaboration This link opens in a new window
    From the website: "The International Development Coordinating Group is committed to providing authors with the support necessary to complete Campbell reviews and will provide or arrange substantial technical assistance for reviewers, especially with respect to information retrieval, critical appraisal and statistical methods."
  • Joanna Briggs Institute This link opens in a new window
    This is a subscription-based service. The McGoogan Library does not carry a subscription.
  • Open Science Framework (OSF) This link opens in a new window
    Publish your systematic review protocol through OSF. This is a platform where you can keep your projects, including your files, data, and protocols, in one location. Access can be available to anyone or restricted. We recommend using the Preregistration template when submitting the protocol.

Systematic Review Protocols

A systematic review protocol describes the rationale, hypothesis, and planned methods of the review. It should be prepared before a review is started and used as a guide to carry out the review. Detailed protocols should be made publicly and registered. (PRISMA, 2021).

The protocol registration falls into the first step of the systematic review process: Initiate Your Review.

Items listed below are typically included in a protocol. For a complete list, visit the PRISMA protocol checklist

  • Rationale and objectives
  • Eligibility criteria
  • Information Sources
  • Draft of a search strategy
  • Data management
  • Outcomes and prioritization
  • Data synthesis

PRISMA. (2021). What is a protocol? https://www.prisma-statement.org/protocols

Scoping Review Protocols

Similar to the systematic review protocol, the scoping review protocol is the plan and/or methodology of your review. Develop your protocol at the beginning of the process. The JBI Reviewer's Manual (chapter 11.2), details what should be included in the protocol:

  1. An introduction detailing the definitions, overall objective, explanation of need, eligibility criteria, and the preliminary search
  2. Sample search literature search strategy
  3. Explanation of the search report
  4. Study selection process
  5. Draft of charting table/data extraction and explanation
  6. How results and data will be presented.

Review the JBI Reviewer's Manual for more information on scoping review protocols.

PRISMA Checklist for Scoping Reviews recommends that a scoping review protocol be developed and publicly available.
