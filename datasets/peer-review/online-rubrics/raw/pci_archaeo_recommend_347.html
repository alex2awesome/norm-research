Similarity Network Fusion: Understanding Patterns and their Spatial Significance in Archaeological Datasets

Since its earliest application in the 1970s, network analysis has become increasingly popular in both theoretical and GIS-based archaeology. Yet, applications of material networks remained relatively restricted. This paper describes a specific kind of material network, so called similarity networks, and presents a new network analysis method to approach them: Similarity Network Fusion (SNF).

Most archaeological applications of material networks approach similarity by simply quantifying the number of co-occurrences of certain traits between two nodes, without considering the relative importance of these traits for the whole network. Statistical similarity measures have so far only been applied to a handful of case studies. The similarity network analysis outlined in this paper relies on SNF, a method common in genomic studies. SNF is based on the iterative integration of similarity networks derived from multiple datatypes for the same set of samples, allowing  the application of a wide range of similarity indices. It has proven to be particularly robust to heterogenous and noisy datasets containing a small number of samples, but a large number of measurements, scale differences, and collection biases.

In a case study, I applied SNF to Early Bronze Age, Middle Bronze Age, and Late Iron Age burial sites in Dorset, resorting to data published by the Grave Goods Project. To enhance the understanding of the resulting networks and topological clusters, the network was spatially represented and the clusters were correlated with five further attributes; physiographic areas, the sex of the buried individuals, the ratio of objects per grave, whether isotopic analyses suggest that the buried people were local inhabitants or moved into the area of their burial, or whether there is an association between clusters and a finer chronological subdivision of sites. The network analysis and the topological clustering of the sites revealed at least two possible spatial clusters and two statistically significant correlations between clusters and further attributes of the burial sites. These results clearly suggest the great potential of SNF for analysing archaeological datasets, unveiling patterns within the archaeological record, and understanding the significance of these patterns for the structuration of the past landscape.

اندماج شبكة التشابه: فهم الأنماط وأهميتها المكانية في مجموعات البيانات الأثرية

منذ أول تطبيق له في السبعينيات، أصبح تحليل الشبكات شائعًا بشكل متزايد في كل من علم الآثار النظري والمعتمد على نظم المعلومات الجغرافية. ومع ذلك، ظلت تطبيقات الشبكات المادية مقيدة نسبيًا. تصف هذه الورقة نوعًا معينًا من شبكات المواد، ما يسمى بشبكات التشابه، وتقدم طريقة جديدة لتحليل الشبكات للتعامل معها: اندماج شبكات التشابه (SNF).

تتعامل معظم التطبيقات الأثرية لشبكات المواد مع التشابه ببساطة عن طريق قياس عدد التواجدات المشتركة لسمات معينة بين عقدتين، دون النظر في الأهمية النسبية لهذه السمات للشبكة بأكملها. ولم يتم تطبيق مقاييس التشابه الإحصائي حتى الآن إلا على عدد قليل من دراسات الحالة. يعتمد تحليل شبكة التشابه المبين في هذه الورقة على SNF، وهي طريقة شائعة في الدراسات الجينومية. يعتمد SNF على التكامل التكراري لشبكات التشابه المستمدة من أنواع بيانات متعددة لنفس مجموعة العينات، مما يسمح بتطبيق مجموعة واسعة من مؤشرات التشابه. لقد ثبت أنه قوي بشكل خاص بالنسبة لمجموعات البيانات غير المتجانسة والصاخبة التي تحتوي على عدد صغير من العينات، ولكن عددًا كبيرًا من القياسات، واختلافات الحجم، وتحيزات التجميع.

في دراسة حالة، قمت بتطبيق SNF على مواقع الدفن في العصر البرونزي المبكر، والعصر البرونزي الأوسط، والعصر الحديدي المتأخر في دورست، باللجوء إلى البيانات المنشورة من قبل مشروع Grave Goods Project. لتعزيز فهم الشبكات الناتجة والمجموعات الطوبولوجية، تم تمثيل الشبكة مكانيًا وتم ربط المجموعات بخمس سمات أخرى؛ المناطق الفيزيوغرافية، وجنس الأفراد المدفونين، ونسبة الأشياء لكل قبر، وما إذا كانت التحليلات النظائرية تشير إلى أن الأشخاص المدفونين كانوا من السكان المحليين أو انتقلوا إلى منطقة دفنهم، أو ما إذا كان هناك ارتباط بين المجموعات وتقسيم فرعي زمني أدق من المواقع. كشف تحليل الشبكة والتجميع الطوبولوجي للمواقع عن مجموعتين مكانيتين محتملتين على الأقل واثنين من الارتباطات ذات دلالة إحصائية بين المجموعات والسمات الإضافية لمواقع الدفن. تشير هذه النتائج بوضوح إلى الإمكانات الكبيرة لـ SNF لتحليل مجموعات البيانات الأثرية، وكشف النقاب عن الأنماط داخل السجل الأثري، وفهم أهمية هذه الأنماط في هيكلة المشهد الماضي.

Fusión de redes de similitud: comprensión de los patrones y su importancia espacial en conjuntos de datos arqueológicos

Desde su primera aplicación en la década de 1970, el análisis de redes se ha vuelto cada vez más popular en la arqueología teórica y basada en SIG. Sin embargo, las aplicaciones de las redes materiales siguieron siendo relativamente restringidas. Este artículo describe un tipo específico de red material, las llamadas redes de similitud, y presenta un nuevo método de análisis de redes para abordarlas: Similarity Network Fusion (SNF).

La mayoría de las aplicaciones arqueológicas de redes materiales abordan la similitud simplemente cuantificando el número de co-ocurrencias de ciertos rasgos entre dos nodos, sin considerar la importancia relativa de estos rasgos para toda la red. Hasta ahora, las medidas de similitud estadística sólo se han aplicado a un puñado de estudios de caso. El análisis de redes de similitud descrito en este artículo se basa en SNF, un método común en estudios genómicos. SNF se basa en la integración iterativa de redes de similitud derivadas de múltiples tipos de datos para el mismo conjunto de muestras, lo que permite la aplicación de una amplia gama de índices de similitud. Ha demostrado ser particularmente sólido para conjuntos de datos heterogéneos y ruidosos que contienen una pequeña cantidad de muestras, pero una gran cantidad de mediciones, diferencias de escala y sesgos de recopilación.

En un estudio de caso, apliqué SNF a sitios de enterramiento de la Edad del Bronce Temprano, la Edad del Bronce Medio y la Edad del Hierro Tardía en Dorset, recurriendo a datos publicados por el Grave Goods Project. Para mejorar la comprensión de las redes resultantes y los grupos topológicos, la red se representó espacialmente y los grupos se correlacionaron con cinco atributos adicionales; áreas fisiográficas, el sexo de los individuos enterrados, la proporción de objetos por tumba, si los análisis isotópicos sugieren que las personas enterradas eran habitantes locales o se mudaron al área de su entierro, o si existe una asociación entre grupos y una subdivisión cronológica más fina de sitios. El análisis de la red y la agrupación topológica de los sitios revelaron al menos dos posibles agrupaciones espaciales y dos correlaciones estadísticamente significativas entre las agrupaciones y otros atributos de los sitios de enterramiento. Estos resultados sugieren claramente el gran potencial de SNF para analizar conjuntos de datos arqueológicos, revelar patrones dentro del registro arqueológico y comprender la importancia de estos patrones para la estructuración del paisaje pasado.

Fusion de réseaux de similarité : comprendre les modèles et leur signification spatiale dans les ensembles de données archéologiques

Depuis ses premières applications dans les années 1970, l'analyse de réseau est devenue de plus en plus populaire en archéologie théorique et basée sur les SIG. Pourtant, les applications des réseaux matériels restent relativement limitées. Cet article décrit un type spécifique de réseaux matériels, appelés réseaux de similarité, et présente une nouvelle méthode d'analyse de réseau pour les aborder : Similarity Network Fusion (SNF).

La plupart des applications archéologiques des réseaux matériels abordent la similarité en quantifiant simplement le nombre de cooccurrences de certains traits entre deux nœuds, sans considérer l'importance relative de ces traits pour l'ensemble du réseau. Jusqu’à présent, les mesures de similarité statistique n’ont été appliquées qu’à une poignée d’études de cas. L'analyse du réseau de similarité décrite dans cet article repose sur le SNF, une méthode courante dans les études génomiques. SNF est basé sur l'intégration itérative de réseaux de similarité dérivés de plusieurs types de données pour le même ensemble d'échantillons, permettant l'application d'un large éventail d'indices de similarité. Il s'est avéré particulièrement robuste face aux ensembles de données hétérogènes et bruyants contenant un petit nombre d'échantillons, mais un grand nombre de mesures, des différences d'échelle et des biais de collecte.

Dans une étude de cas, j'ai appliqué le SNF aux sites funéraires de l'âge du bronze ancien, de l'âge du bronze moyen et de la fin de l'âge du fer dans le Dorset, en recourant aux données publiées par le Grave Goods Project. Pour améliorer la compréhension des réseaux et des clusters topologiques résultants, le réseau a été représenté spatialement et les clusters ont été corrélés avec cinq autres attributs ; les zones physiographiques, le sexe des individus enterrés, le ratio d'objets par tombe, si les analyses isotopiques suggèrent que les personnes enterrées étaient des habitants locaux ou se sont déplacées dans la zone de leur sépulture, ou s'il existe une association entre des clusters et une subdivision chronologique plus fine de sites. L'analyse du réseau et le regroupement topologique des sites ont révélé au moins deux groupes spatiaux possibles et deux corrélations statistiquement significatives entre les groupes et d'autres attributs des lieux de sépulture. Ces résultats suggèrent clairement le grand potentiel du SNF pour analyser des ensembles de données archéologiques, dévoiler des modèles au sein des archives archéologiques et comprendre l'importance de ces modèles pour la structuration du paysage passé.

समानता नेटवर्क फ़्यूज़न: पुरातात्विक डेटासेट में पैटर्न और उनके स्थानिक महत्व को समझना

1970 के दशक में अपने शुरुआती अनुप्रयोग के बाद से, नेटवर्क विश्लेषण सैद्धांतिक और जीआईएस-आधारित पुरातत्व दोनों में तेजी से लोकप्रिय हो गया है। फिर भी, सामग्री नेटवर्क के अनुप्रयोग अपेक्षाकृत प्रतिबंधित रहे। यह पेपर एक विशिष्ट प्रकार के सामग्री नेटवर्क, तथाकथित समानता नेटवर्क का वर्णन करता है, और उनसे संपर्क करने के लिए एक नई नेटवर्क विश्लेषण विधि प्रस्तुत करता है: समानता नेटवर्क फ़्यूज़न (एसएनएफ)।

सामग्री नेटवर्क के अधिकांश पुरातात्विक अनुप्रयोग पूरे नेटवर्क के लिए इन लक्षणों के सापेक्ष महत्व पर विचार किए बिना, केवल दो नोड्स के बीच कुछ लक्षणों की सह-घटनाओं की संख्या निर्धारित करके समानता तक पहुंचते हैं। सांख्यिकीय समानता उपाय अब तक केवल कुछ ही केस अध्ययनों पर लागू किए गए हैं। इस पेपर में उल्लिखित समानता नेटवर्क विश्लेषण एसएनएफ पर निर्भर करता है, जो जीनोमिक अध्ययन में एक सामान्य विधि है। एसएनएफ नमूनों के एक ही सेट के लिए कई डेटाटाइप से प्राप्त समानता नेटवर्क के पुनरावृत्त एकीकरण पर आधारित है, जो समानता सूचकांकों की एक विस्तृत श्रृंखला के अनुप्रयोग की अनुमति देता है। यह विशेष रूप से विषम और शोर वाले डेटासेट के लिए मजबूत साबित हुआ है जिसमें कम संख्या में नमूने हैं, लेकिन बड़ी संख्या में माप, पैमाने में अंतर और संग्रह पूर्वाग्रह हैं।

एक केस अध्ययन में, मैंने ग्रेव गुड्स प्रोजेक्ट द्वारा प्रकाशित डेटा का सहारा लेते हुए, डोरसेट में प्रारंभिक कांस्य युग, मध्य कांस्य युग और स्वर्गीय लौह युग के दफन स्थलों पर एसएनएफ लागू किया। परिणामी नेटवर्क और टोपोलॉजिकल क्लस्टर की समझ को बढ़ाने के लिए, नेटवर्क को स्थानिक रूप से दर्शाया गया था और क्लस्टर को पांच और विशेषताओं के साथ सहसंबद्ध किया गया था; भौगोलिक क्षेत्र, दफनाए गए व्यक्तियों का लिंग, प्रति कब्र वस्तुओं का अनुपात, क्या समस्थानिक विश्लेषण से पता चलता है कि दफनाए गए लोग स्थानीय निवासी थे या उनके दफनाने के क्षेत्र में चले गए, या क्या समूहों और एक बेहतर कालानुक्रमिक उपखंड के बीच कोई संबंध है साइटों का. नेटवर्क विश्लेषण और साइटों के टोपोलॉजिकल क्लस्टरिंग से कम से कम दो संभावित स्थानिक समूहों और समूहों और दफन स्थलों की आगे की विशेषताओं के बीच दो सांख्यिकीय रूप से महत्वपूर्ण सहसंबंधों का पता चला। ये परिणाम स्पष्ट रूप से पुरातात्विक डेटासेट का विश्लेषण करने, पुरातात्विक रिकॉर्ड के भीतर पैटर्न का अनावरण करने और पिछले परिदृश्य की संरचना के लिए इन पैटर्न के महत्व को समझने के लिए एसएनएफ की महान क्षमता का सुझाव देते हैं।

類似性ネットワーク フュージョン: 考古学的データセットにおけるパターンとその空間的重要性を理解する

1970 年代に最初に応用されて以来、ネットワーク解析は理論考古学と GIS ベースの考古学の両方でますます普及してきました。しかし、マテリアル ネットワークの応用は依然として比較的制限されていました。このペーパーでは、類似性ネットワークと呼ばれる特定の種類のマテリアル ネットワークについて説明し、それらにアプローチするための新しいネットワーク分析手法である類似性ネットワーク フュージョン (SNF) を紹介します。

マテリアル ネットワークの考古学的応用のほとんどは、ネットワーク全体に対するこれらの特性の相対的な重要性を考慮せず、2 つのノード間で特定の特性の同時発生の数を単純に定量化することによって類似性にアプローチします。統計的類似性の尺度は、これまでのところ少数のケーススタディにのみ適用されています。この論文で概説されている類似性ネットワーク分析は、ゲノム研究で一般的な手法である SNF に依存しています。 SNF は、同じサンプル セットの複数のデータタイプから派生した類似性ネットワークの反復統合に基づいており、広範囲の類似性インデックスの適用が可能になります。これは、サンプル数は少ないものの、多数の測定値、スケールの違い、収集の偏りを含む、異質でノイズの多いデータセットに対して特に堅牢であることが証明されています。

ケーススタディでは、Grave Goods Project によって公開されたデータを利用して、ドーセット州にある前期青銅器時代、中期青銅器時代、後期鉄器時代の埋葬地に SNF を適用しました。結果として得られるネットワークとトポロジー クラスターの理解を高めるために、ネットワークが空間的に表現され、クラスターがさらに 5 つの属性と関連付けられました。地理学上の地域、埋葬された人の性別、墓当たりの物品の割合、同位体分析が埋葬された人々が地元住民であったことを示唆しているのか、それとも埋葬地域に移動したことを示唆しているのか、あるいはクラスターとより細かい年代区分との間に関連性があるのか​​どうかサイトの。ネットワーク分析と遺跡の位相的クラスタリングにより、少なくとも 2 つの空間クラスターの可能性と、クラスターと埋葬地のさらなる属性との間の 2 つの統計的に有意な相関関係が明らかになりました。これらの結果は、考古学データセットを分析し、考古学記録内のパターンを明らかにし、過去の景観の構造におけるこれらのパターンの重要性を理解する上で、SNF が持つ大きな可能性を明らかに示しています。

Fusão de redes de similaridade: compreendendo padrões e seu significado espacial em conjuntos de dados arqueológicos

Desde a sua primeira aplicação na década de 1970, a análise de redes tornou-se cada vez mais popular tanto na arqueologia teórica como na arqueologia baseada em SIG. No entanto, as aplicações de redes materiais permaneceram relativamente restritas. Este artigo descreve um tipo específico de rede material, as chamadas redes de similaridade, e apresenta um novo método de análise de rede para abordá-las: Similarity Network Fusion (SNF).

A maioria das aplicações arqueológicas de redes materiais aborda a similaridade simplesmente quantificando o número de coocorrências de certas características entre dois nós, sem considerar a importância relativa dessas características para toda a rede. Até agora, as medidas de similaridade estatística só foram aplicadas a alguns estudos de caso. A análise de redes de similaridade descrita neste artigo baseia-se no SNF, um método comum em estudos genômicos. SNF é baseado na integração iterativa de redes de similaridade derivadas de múltiplos tipos de dados para o mesmo conjunto de amostras, permitindo a aplicação de uma ampla gama de índices de similaridade. Ele provou ser particularmente robusto para conjuntos de dados heterogêneos e ruidosos contendo um pequeno número de amostras, mas um grande número de medições, diferenças de escala e vieses de coleta.

Em um estudo de caso, apliquei SNF a cemitérios do início da Idade do Bronze, da Idade do Bronze Médio e do final da Idade do Ferro em Dorset, recorrendo a dados publicados pelo Grave Goods Project. Para melhorar a compreensão das redes e clusters topológicos resultantes, a rede foi representada espacialmente e os clusters foram correlacionados com mais cinco atributos; áreas fisiográficas, o sexo dos indivíduos enterrados, a proporção de objetos por sepultura, se as análises isotópicas sugerem que as pessoas enterradas eram habitantes locais ou se mudaram para a área de seu sepultamento, ou se existe uma associação entre agrupamentos e uma subdivisão cronológica mais refinada de sites. Объединение сетей сходства: понимание закономерностей и их пространственного значения в наборах археологических данных

С момента своего первого применения в 1970-х годах сетевой анализ становится все более популярным как в теоретической археологии, так и в археологии на основе ГИС. Большинство археологических применений материальных сетей приближаются к сходству путем простого количественного определения количества совпадений определенных признаков между двумя узлами, не принимая во внимание относительную важность этих признаков для всей сети. Анализ сети сходства, описанный в этой статье, основан на SNF, методе, распространенном в геномных исследованиях. SNF основан на итеративной интеграции сетей сходства, полученных из нескольких типов данных для одного и того же набора выборок, что позволяет применять широкий диапазон индексов сходства. 相似性网络融合：理解考古数据集中的模式及其空间意义

自 20 世纪 70 年代最早应用以来，网络分析在理论考古学和基于 GIS 的考古学中变得越来越流行。然而，物质网络的应用仍然相对受到限制。本文描述了一种特定的材料网络，即相似网络，并提出了一种新的网络分析方法来处理它们：相似网络融合（SNF）。

物质网络的大多数考古应用通过简单地量化两个节点之间某些特征的共现次数来处理相似性，而不考虑这些特征对于整个网络的相对重要性。迄今为止，统计相似性测量仅应用于少数案例研究。本文概述的相似性网络分析依赖于基因组研究中常见的 SNF 方法。 SNF 基于从同一组样本的多种数据类型派生的相似性网络的迭代集成，允许应用广泛的相似性指数。事实证明，它对于包含少量样本但包含大量测量值、尺度差异和收集偏差的异构和噪声数据集特别稳健。

在一个案例研究中，我利用墓葬物品项目发布的数据，将 SNF 应用于多塞特郡的早期青铜时代、中期青铜时代和晚期铁器时代墓地。为了增强对所得网络和拓扑集群的理解，对网络进行了空间表示，并将集群与五个进一步的属性相关联；地理区域、埋葬者的性别、每个坟墓中物品的比例、同位素分析是否表明埋葬者是当地居民还是搬到了他们埋葬的地区，或者簇与更精细的时间顺序细分之间是否存在关联网站。网络分析和遗址的拓扑聚类揭示了至少两个可能的空间聚类以及聚类与墓地的进一步属性之间的两个统计上显着的相关性。这些结果清楚地表明 SNF 在分析考古数据集、揭示考古记录中的模式以及理解这些模式对过去景观结构的重要性方面具有巨大潜力。

Recommendation

This is a fascinating paper for anyone interested in network analysis or the chronology and cultures of the case study, namely the Late prehistoric burial sites in Dorset, for which the author’s approach allowed a new perspective over an already deeply studied area [1]. This paper's implementation of Similarity Network Fusion (SNF) is noteworthy. This method is typically utilized within genetic research but has yet to be employed in Archaeology. SNF has the potential to benefit Archaeology due to its unique capabilities and approach significantly. 

The author exhibits a deep and thorough understanding of previous investigations concerning material and similarity networks while emphasizing the innovative nature of this particular study. The SNF approach intends to improve a lack of the most used (in Archaeology) similarity coefficient, the Brainerd-Robinson, in certain situations, mainly in heterogenous and noisy datasets containing a small number of samples but a large number of measurements, scale differences, and collection biases, among other things. The SNF technique, demonstrated in the case study, effectively incorporates various similarity networks derived from different datatypes into one network. 

As shown during the Dorset case study, the SNF application has a great application in archaeology, even in already available data, allowing us to go further and bring new visions to the existing interpretations. As stated by the author, SNF shows its potential for other applications and fields in archaeology coping with similar datasets, such as archaeobotany or archaeozoology, and seems to complement different multivariate statistical approaches, such as correspondence or cluster analysis.

This paper has been subject to two excellent revisions, which the author mostly accepted. One of the revisions was more technical, improving the article in the metadata part, data availability and clarification, etc. Although the second revision was more conceptual and gave some excellent technical inputs, it focused more on complementary aspects that will allow the paper to reach a wider audience. I vividly recommend its publication.

Reviewed by Matthew Peeples, 16 Jun 2023

I’m reviewing this article as a specialist in archaeological network methods. I have little knowledge of the regional/temporal context that is the focus of the case study.

 

Overall, I found this to be an interesting application of a new (to archaeology) method of dealing with multiplex archaeological similarity data through the Similarity Network Fusion approach. I had only a general familiarity with the SNF approach from the Wang et al. 2014 paper before reading this article but, after digging into the details, I agree with the authors that there is considerable potential for archaeological applications. This is a nice introduction to the approach for an archaeological audience and an interesting set of data to serve as a test case. I have suggestions for a few additions and clarifications but overall, I think this piece will be useful to those interested in archaeological network science.

 

Replication

 

In the process of the review, I re-ran all of the provided Python code to replicate those results. I ran the code in Python 3.8.3 in a Conda environment in RStudio. Once I installed the relevant snfpy package and edited the output locations in the code (as indicated in the header comments), it worked flawlessly on the first attempt. The only comment I have on the code is that it would be useful to add a few additional comments to specify details on certain steps just so it is easy to find a specific function at a glance. There are a few details in the ArcGIS Pro analysis I replicated in Python as I don’t have access to ArcGIS right now (rescaling, defining 25 highest similarity connections, and plotting) but the text was clear enough that could do that.

 

Comments on text and figures

 

I think the text generally reads well and provides a good introduction to similarity networks in archaeology including some of the common ways that people have defined edges and edge weights. It might be worth adding a citation to work by Habiba and colleagues (2018) which provides and overview of the advantages and disadvantages of some of the potential measures for archaeological data including the cosine similarity measure you use here. I would also suggest that you add a sentence or two to the details of your cluster detection and spectral algorithm discussion on page 5 to briefly explain what these methods do in simple terms.

 

In general, I could follow all of the methods you used in the text pretty easily and I think the analytical decisions you made on choosing clustering methods and defining cutoffs on what to display were generally reasonable. In the discussion of the results, you focus primarily on the topological clusters and their spatial distribution and don’t talk about variation in network structures or positions much directly. This is okay but there may be additional interesting factors worth at least mentioning there. For example, I noted in the EBA time period when I recreated your network that cluster 1 represents a dense cluster of nodes all connected with edges in the top 10% of weight whereas cluster 0 represents nodes that are much more weakly connected overall. Things like this are lost a bit when you only visualize the top 25 nodes, but additional non-spatial network graphs with lower thresholds (like the 0.9 quantile or something) might be informative of additional patterns in these data.

 

I was happy to see discussion of the objects, attributes, etc. associated with specific clusters as that is where my thoughts went as I read but I found a few sections of this discussion a bit unclear. For example, on page 15 you state “the networks covering a great variety of aspects of the burial data seem to cluster sites with a ratio of more than one good per site in different clusters than the sites with fewer goods.” Do you mean sites with more than 1 good per individual here? Also in the discussion of the EBA network you talk about one cluster having more than on burial good per grave and the other having consistently less than 3. Those are mutually exclusive sets so this is a bit confusing. I would suggest going back through this entire discussion section and just checking to make sure that the discussion of groups and attributes is consistent with the data and clearly stated. As a disclaimer, I have no personal experience with Similarity Network Fusion (SNF) and have not checked the submitted Python script.

General impressions

Overall, this is a very interesting and thorough study, aiming to introduce archaeologists to methods of Similarity Network Fusion (SNF). Despite some errors and inconsistencies (see below), the manuscript is also well-written and logically structured.

The author demonstrates that he is aware of previous work on material and similarity networks while highlighting how this study extends or differs from them, and is able to explain the basics of relevant approaches in a clear manner. He presents how the data was selected, pre-processed, analysed and visualised, while also specifying which software was used in the process and arguing for choices made at various stages of the research. Although he does not seem to mention them in the manuscript (?), transparency is further supported by sharing the project’s .csv files - containing the first and second datasets - as well as the Python script.

One thing that is lacking is, however, some more information on the case study and (cultural and ritual) contexts of the studied graves. With the longue durée perspective, it is of course difficult to find common traits to describe the area or phenomenon of interest. With reference to the research objectives (stipulated on pp. 2 and 3), knowing a bit more about the area, people and ritual customs they practiced, might prove valuable for guiding the research as well as interpreting findings in their cultural and ritual contexts.

Abstract and introduction

The paper’s title is fitting but, especially if the paper is not to be accompanied by keywords, it could perhaps be more precise, for example specifying what types of patterns are to be understood and/or mentioning the period and area under study.

The abstract is clear and well written. In addition to introducing the topic and highlighting that SNF applications are rare in archaeological network analysis, it summarises the case study, main findings and potential benefits of applying methods of SNF to archaeologically informed similarity networks.

The author has also written a solid introduction that focuses on relevant methods and earlier applications of similarity coefficients for archaeological case studies, while highlighting what is novel about his approach. The main aims of the paper are also specified (end of p. 2). This short section briefly mentions the case study, but whereas network analysis, similarity networks and SNF are treated in more detail, we only return to Dorset and the studied burials in the next part of the paper. It might be useful to already provide a sentence or two on the relevance of the case study in the introduction. There is, however, a logical build up and smooth transition to this (and other) section(s).

Data and Methods

Whereas the introduction explains that the study’s main aim – to further explore the potential of material network analysis, similarity networks and SNF – is approached with a case study, the next section reveals that the case study’s objective is to gain insight into the cultural and ritual structuration of the studied landscape and burial groups, but also the ritual relationship between graves.

The author clearly explains that the case study is based on data collected by the Grave Goods project, how his approach differs from their analyses and why he chose to focus on the given region and time periods. Whereas the selection of the region was largely dependent on the availability of suitable data, the decision to study two datasets was more directly informed by the researcher’s aim to study cultural and ritual structuration and ritual relationship between graves. Here would be a suitable place for adding a bit of contextualisation. What sort of landscape are we in? What do we already know about the region, people living there, how they buried their dead, etc.? And which holes in our knowledge might the applied methodology help us fill?

In addition to describe the content of the two datasets and rationale behind them, the author also gives a rather detailed account of the research steps. The process appears sufficiently transparent, but as mentioned, I have no personal experience with SNF and am not able to judge the relevance of applied tools or appropriateness of the statistical analyses.

That said, the practicalities of the spatial correlation analysis between the topological clusters and five attributes do not appear to have been made explicit. On p. 11, for example, the author rather vaguely states that “there does not seem to be an obvious correlation between the burial clusters and the different sex categories”, but it is not clear how this observation was reached. From visual examination and comparison of the maps, or another method?

Discussion and findings

In the discussion of the Burial Goods per Grave we get some answer to such questions: at least where potential correlation was spotted, the author conducted statistical analysis of variance. While this becomes clearer, this section could benefit from some revisions and polishing, since the argument is not always easy to follow. For example, it is specified (on p. 15) that “A similar significance [to the EBA data] could not be detected for the MBA (𝐹(1,44)=3.23, 𝑝<0.008) and LIA (𝐹(1,21)=0.109,𝑝=0.75) clusters”, yet argued that “the long-running continuity of this correlation is utterly surprising”. This seems contradictory.

In general, the author should, however, be complimented for taking on a cautious and critical approach while discussing the findings. For example pointing out that the regional classifications of National Character Area do not mirror the past, although they seem more accurate than modern political borders (p. 5), or arguing that most of the attributes checked for potential correlation did not result in clearly discernible patterns.

Ultimately, his analyses lead him to conclude that two potential spatial clusters and two statistically significant correlations (objects per grave and chronology) “shed light on inter-site connections of ritual and cultural behaviour and past landscape structuration not yet recognised” (p. 18), but I do not think he specifies how. Following the discussion of the results of the correlation analyses, I would therefore like to invite the author to synthesize the findings and address the research aim stipulated on pp. 2 and 3 more directly. What exactly has this study and approach taught us about cultural and ritual structuration of this landscape and burial groups, or the ritual relationships between the graves? And how can this case study, in turn, contribute to discussions on the potential of material network analysis and the employment of SNF for similarity networks by archaeologists?

Tables and figures

Tables and figures are clearly presented with appropriate legends and captions. The table and figure captions are comprehensive and can be understood on their own term, although it might be worth also mentioning the topological clusters in the captions of Tables 3 and 4. Also, there is some inconsistency in the use of and in listing the colours (Figs. 3, 10), a comma missing between white and grey for Fig. 8, and I would add the before 25 (in Figs. 2-16).

-      Should it be Morgan & Whitelaw (as in the reference list) or Whitlaw (p. 3)?

Finally, the paper is well-written, but in need of some language revisions and polishing to sharpen the argument, especially in the Burial Goods per Grave, Mover or Local and Chronology sections.
