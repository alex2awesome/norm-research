# Mahoney 1977: Reviewer Evaluation Rubric Used in the Confirmatory-Bias Study

Mahoney mailed 75 reviewers (drawn from the editorial board of the *Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis*) one of five matched manuscripts that differed only in whether the reported results were positive, negative, mixed, or merely procedural. Reviewers used the explicit JABA evaluation criteria. The rubric below is the operational evaluation form Mahoney administered.

## Five Manuscript-Quality Dimensions

Reviewers rated each manuscript on five components:

1. **Topic relevance** — Importance and pertinence of the research question to the field.
2. **Methodology** — Soundness of experimental procedures, controls, and design.
3. **Data presentation** — Clarity, accuracy, and adequacy of how findings are reported.
4. **Discussion** — Quality of interpretation, scope of inference, treatment of alternative explanations.
5. **Overall scientific contribution** — Net advance the manuscript would represent for the discipline.

## Component Rating Scale

A 4-point ordinal rating scale was used for each component:
- **Poor**
- **Marginal**
- **Adequate**
- **Good**

For statistical analysis these were re-expressed on a 0/2/4/6 Likert metric (0 = lowest, 6 = highest).

## Summary Recommendation Scale

A separate 4-point editorial recommendation scale:
- **Accept**
- **Accept with minor revisions**
- **Accept with major revisions**
- **Reject**

## Open-ended Referee Form

Reviewers also completed an open-ended referee form supplying narrative justifications for each component rating and an overall recommendation. Reviewers were instructed to use the evaluation criteria explicitly outlined by *JABA*.

## Mahoney's Empirical Findings as De Facto Norms for Reviewers

Mahoney's results have themselves become prescriptive norms cited in modern reviewer guidelines:

- Reviewers should **separate methodological evaluation from outcome evaluation** — Mahoney showed identical methods sections were rated higher when results agreed with reviewers' priors.
- Reviewers should **avoid confirmatory bias**: judging the *truth* of a finding by whether it conforms to the reviewer's theoretical commitments.
- Editors should **mask reviewer identities** and where possible **mask outcome direction** during methodological appraisal.
- Reviewers should explicitly **flag their own theoretical priors** that might bias appraisal.
