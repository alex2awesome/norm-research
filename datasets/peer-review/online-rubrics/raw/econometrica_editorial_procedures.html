Editorial Procedures and Policies

This page describes the current editorial procedures and policies of Econometrica. To submit a paper, please review the journal’s Submission Guidelines.

1. Review Process and Decisions

We do not pre-review papers; only formally submitted manuscripts are considered.

Upon submission, an acknowledgment is sent to the authors. After confirming author membership and checking the manuscript for compliance with submission requirements, the review process begins and a second message is sent assigning a manuscript number (MS#). This number should be cited in all correspondence.

Many submissions are sent to one or more referees, including Associate Editors, but some may be rejected after editorial evaluation without external review. This practice aims to save authors time and conserve refereeing resources when a paper is clearly not a good fit for the journal.

An international board of editors, assisted by numerous referees, strives to provide timely and thoughtful reviews. Statistics on decision times and referee lists appear in our Annual Reports. Authors experiencing unusual delays are encouraged to contact the editorial office.

Manuscripts may be rejected, invited for revision, or accepted. An offer to revise is typically valid for 12 months from the decision date. Extensions may be granted at the discretion of the handling Co-Editor but must be requested within that 12-month period.

Unless specified otherwise by the Co-Editor, decisions are final. Unsolicited revisions of rejected papers will normally be rejected without review.

After acceptance, authors must return corrected page proofs within one year of receiving them in order for the article to proceed to publication.

2. Appeals

Editorial decisions are primarily based on professional judgment regarding a paper’s importance and relevance. These judgments are necessarily subjective, and the editorial process cannot operate if such assessments are open to debate. Accordingly, editorial decisions are normally final.

Referee reports often include specific comments intended to be helpful; however, rejection decisions generally reflect an overall assessment of the paper’s contribution, not a particular technical issue. Disagreement with individual comments is therefore not grounds for appeal.

An appeal may be appropriate only when a clear factual error appears to have materially affected the decision—for example, if a referee claims that “Theorem A is a trivial consequence of the well-known result B,” but result B does not apply because its conditions are not satisfied. The importance or relevance of a paper is a matter of editorial judgment rather than fact, and appeals on such grounds will not be considered.

3. Retractions

The Econometric Society reserves the right to retract a published paper in cases of: (i) breach of the warranties stated in the copyright form; (ii) substantive errors; or (iii) evidence of scientific misconduct or other fraudulent actions.

A committee consisting of the Editors of the three Econometric Society journals and the Society’s President will evaluate and decide on any proposed retraction. More information on retraction procedures is available from the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE).

4. Prior and Concurrent Publication Policy

If an author has submitted elsewhere work that overlaps with or includes parts of the current submission—before or during review at Econometrica—the author must disclose full details to the journal. This applies to all forms of publication other than working paper series or personal/institutional websites, including appearances in proceedings volumes (e.g., AEA Papers & Proceedings, JEEA Papers & Proceedings, IEEE Proceedings).

Determining what constitutes prior publication may be complex and will be decided case by case. Submissions are evaluated relative to the published literature, including any material deemed previously published or under consideration elsewhere.

5. AI Policy

The Econometric Society recognizes the potential of AI tools to help researchers work efficiently. However, the use of these tools comes with caveats that reflect the limitations and risks of this rapidly evolving technology. Authors preparing a manuscript for Econometrica may use AI tools, but remain responsible and accountable for all content of their work. This accountability includes:

  • The careful review and verification of all AI-generated output.
  • All analysis, interpretations, insights, and ideas.
  • The safeguarding of data privacy and intellectual property.

AI software, such as chatbots or other large language models, may not be listed as an author.

6. Similarity Check Policy

To prevent plagiarism, the Econometric Society uses Similarity Check to screen submissions. Submission to Econometrica implies consent to this electronic verification process.

7. Replication Policy

The Econometric Society journals publish empirical, experimental, and simulation-based studies only when the underlying data and code are clearly documented and made available on a non-exclusive basis.

Before acceptance, authors must provide the raw data, code, and sufficient documentation to enable reproducibility checks on all empirical, experimental, and simulation results presented in the manuscript and any approved supplemental appendices.

If programs require long execution times or specialized hardware/software, authors should provide simplified versions and summary output files to facilitate feasible replication. Authors are expected to cooperate with the Data Editor in conducting reproducibility checks on selected parts of their materials, without incurring a subscription cost to the Econometric Society. The extent of these checks must be recorded in the README file.

Requests for exemptions or limitations on data or code availability must be clearly stated at initial submission. Whether a paper proceeds to review is at the Co-Editor’s discretion; exemptions will not be considered later in the process.

By submitting to any Econometric Society journal, authors agree to the Society’s Data and Code Availability Policy, consistent with the Data and Code Availability Standard (DCAS). More information on this policy and the Data Editor can be found here.

 8. Experimental Papers Policy

Effective January 1, 2026, all experimental paper submissions must include detailed experimental instructions or survey transcripts at the time of initial submission. This applies to studies involving laboratory, lab-in-the-field, or online experiments or surveys.

Additionally, authors of studies that generate their own data—such as randomized controlled trials, lab experiments, lab-in-the-field studies, or online experiments and surveys—must pre-register their studies in a recognized trial registry such as the AEA RCT Registry, AsPredicted, or the OSF Registries. Authors seeking an exemption must contact the Editor before submission with a detailed explanation.

Pre-registration must specify the experimental protocol and the outcome variables of interest. It should be included as a separate file with the submission and may be considered during the review process. While the policy does not prohibit changes to the data collection process, any such modifications must be clearly justified in the manuscript.

All experimental submissions must provide sufficient procedural detail—within the paper or an appendix—to enable proper evaluation. The decision of what to include initially is left to the authors. However, the handling Co-Editor may request additional information during the review, which could delay processing. For this reason, we strongly encourage submitting as complete a file as feasible.

If the paper is accepted, authors will be expected to provide additional materials to facilitate replication and follow-up analysis (see Section 7 above). During the review process, all supplementary materials will be password-protected and accessible only to the Co-editors and reviewers, who may use them solely for the purpose of evaluating the manuscript. Examples of required materials at submission and acceptance are provided below. Any limitations in supplying replication materials post-acceptance must be disclosed at the time of initial submission. For rejected manuscripts, supplementary files will be deleted upon the author’s request.

If any supplementary material is not in English, a translation must be provided.

Suggested information to include for review:

  • Description of the participant pool and recruitment procedures.
  • Details on experimental technology: setting, format (e.g., computer-based, manual, online), and location.
  • Methods used to assess participant understanding (e.g., practice rounds, quizzes).
  • Matching procedures, particularly for game-theoretic designs.
  • Payment details: use of tokens or real money, exchange rates, show-up fees, average earnings, lotteries, or grades.
  • Number of participants per session and their prior experience, if relevant.
  • Session timing, including total duration and time spent on instructions.

  • Any use of deception or deliberate instructional inaccuracies.

9. Disclosure Policy

Submissions to Econometrica must comply with the following disclosure requirements:

  • Every submitted article must disclose its sources of financial support.
  • Each author must disclose interested parties from whom he, she, or a close relative or partner has received financial support totaling at least US$10,000 in the past three years. This support can include consulting fees, retainers, grants, or in-kind support such as access to data. If the support involves a non-disclosure agreement, that should be stated along with as much information as the agreement allows. An "interested" party is any individual, group, or organization with a financial, ideological, or political stake related to the article.
  • Each author must disclose any paid or unpaid positions as an officer, director, or board member of relevant non-profit organizations or profit-making entities held by themselves, a close relative, or a partner. A "relevant" organization is one whose policy positions, goals, or financial interests relate to the article.
  • If another party had the right to review the paper before its circulation, each author must disclose this fact.
  • The corresponding author of a paper that reports research involving the collection of data on human subjects must disclose whether Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval was obtained. If IRB approval was not received, the author must explain why.
  • Disclosure statements for all published articles will be posted on the journal’s website.

Authors complete disclosure forms through the online submission system. Each coauthor receives a separate link to complete their own form. The disclosure information is available to referees.

Failure to disclose relevant information may lead to reversal of acceptance or post-publication notices. Violations are referred to the Executive Committee of the Econometric Society for appropriate action.

Disclosure is author-specific and paper-specific.  In cases of uncertainty, authors are encouraged to err on the side of full disclosure. Examples consistent with this policy are available on the NBER website (http://www.nber.org/researchdisclosurepolicy.html).

10. Responsibility Statements

Effective January 2026, all journals of the Econometric Society require accepted papers with multiple authors to include a responsibility statement. This statement does not attribute contributions among authors but only indicates that they accept responsibility for the content of the paper.

The default responsibility statement stipulates that “All authors assume responsibility for all aspects of the paper.” For instance, even if an author primarily contributed to an empirical section while another author contributed to a theoretical section, we would typically expect that they would have sufficiently discussed and comprehended the content to mutually accept responsibility for both sections.

Sometimes a team of authors may assume specialized roles and take responsibility for specific sections. Such authors should provide a statement outlining their respective responsibilities.

11. Copyright and Open Access

Submission of a paper implies agreement that copyright will be transferred to the Econometric Society if the article is accepted for publication. Authors may post accepted versions on personal or institutional websites. No payment is made to authors.

Authors may alternatively choose to publish their article open access (OA) by paying an article publication charge (APC). This option is offered through the Society’s publisher, Wiley, during completion of the copyright form. We encourage authors to check with their institution(s) regarding any existing OA agreements with Wiley. Eligibility under these agreements requires that the submitting author be affiliated with a participating institution. For additional information about OA options, please visit here.

12. Conflict of Interest Policy

The journal’s conflict of interest policy is outlined in the Rules and Procedures of the Econometric Society.

Editors, Co-Editors, Associate Editors, and the Data Editor are not permitted to handle papers authored by their current colleagues, thesis advisors, active co-authors, or individuals for whom they served as main advisor. Additionally, they cannot handle papers by anyone they advised in a less central way if the submission occurs within two years of the person’s graduation or if the paper includes work done during their time as a student. Finally, they will not have access to referee reports or the names of referees for papers written by authors with whom they have at least one of the conflicts listed in this paragraph.

For this policy, a person is considered a “colleague” of an Editor, Co-Editor, Associate Editor, or Data Editor if they are (a) a member of the same permanent department (even if they hold other part-time positions), or (b) a member of a different department at the same university, or (c) a member of a department that the Editor, Co-Editor, Associate Editor, or Data Editor is visiting, except that cases (b) and (c) exclude individuals with whom there is no collegial interaction. An “active co-author” refers to a co-author on a paper currently in progress, under review, or under revision, or on a paper published less than two years ago. Students advised “in a less central way” include those for whom the Editor, Co-Editor, Associate Editor, or Data Editor wrote a letter of recommendation.

13. Accessibility of Editorial Files

Under current practice, referee reports and cover letters remain in the editorial system, and future Editors have access to them for 10 years. Future Co-Editors have access to them for 6 years. This access is limited to Editors and Co-Editors who have no conflict of interest.

All reports and cover letters are treated confidentially. Referee identities and correspondence are never shared with authors, other referees, or Associate Editors.

14. Sharing Editorial Files with Other Journals

When an author requests that Econometrica transfer an editorial file to another Econometric Society journal, we may share the following materials: (i) referee reports; (ii) the decision letter; and, with the consent of individual referees, (iii) referee names and (iv) cover letters.

Recipient journals are expected to honor our conflict-of-interest and confidentiality policies.
