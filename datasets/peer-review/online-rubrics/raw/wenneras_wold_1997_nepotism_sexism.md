# Wennerås & Wold 1997 — Swedish MRC Postdoctoral Fellowship Reviewer Rubric (and bias finding)

The first quantitative analysis of peer-review *scores* (not just outcomes) on a real funding panel — the Swedish Medical Research Council postdoctoral fellowships. The de facto rubric the MRC used (and that Wennerås & Wold reverse-engineered) is itself an artefact of historical interest, and the operational norms inferred from their findings are now standard reviewer-training material.

## The Three-Dimensional Scoring Rubric (Swedish MRC, mid-1990s)

Each applicant was scored on three competence dimensions, each 0.0–4.0:

1. **Scientific competence of the applicant** — track record, publications, originality, intellectual quality.
2. **Quality of the proposed methodology** — rigour, feasibility, adequacy of design, statistics, controls.
3. **Relevance of the research proposal** — importance of the question to the field, applicability.

The product (or in some panels, the average) of the three scores determined the funding rank order.

## The Bias Findings as Prescriptive Norms

Wennerås and Wold's analysis showed that:

- **Female applicants needed 2.5× the impact-factor-weighted publication count** of male applicants to receive the same competence score (a 64-point publication advantage was required to offset the gender effect).
- **Applicants affiliated with a committee member ("nepotism")** received an additional 0.21 competence points unjustified by productivity.
- These two biases were **roughly equal in magnitude**, and they fully accounted for women's lower funding rates.

These findings have been operationalised in modern reviewer guidelines as norms:

### Norms for Reviewers
- **Disregard the gender of the applicant** when assessing competence; if you cannot, recuse.
- **Declare any personal or institutional affiliation** with the applicant; recuse where conflict exists.
- **Use objective bibliometric anchors** (publication count, journal impact, citation count) rather than impressionistic judgement of "scientific competence."
- **Calibrate against demographic baselines** if available (e.g., productivity per year since PhD).

### Norms for Funders
- **Anonymise applications** where feasible.
- Implement **explicit conflict-of-interest exclusions** rather than reliance on reviewer self-management.
- **Audit score distributions by demographic group** as a routine quality-assurance step.
- **Train panel members** on the documented bias (the Wennerås–Wold result is itself the canonical training case).

## Significance

Wennerås & Wold's three-dimensional MRC rubric is the historical antecedent of every modern fellowship-review form. Their identification of a near-2× productivity penalty on female applicants is the empirical anchor for the structural anti-bias measures (anonymisation, COI, panel audit) found in nearly every contemporary funder-reviewer rubric (DFG, NIH, ERC, NHMRC, MRC).
