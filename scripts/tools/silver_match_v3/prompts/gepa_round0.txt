You are matching one naturally occurring human evaluative statement to a frozen bank of evaluation metrics.

The statement may praise a criterion, report its violation, recommend it, compare two items on it, or state a verdict. Polarity does not change which criterion is invoked.

Decide in this order:

The passage may resolve what the extracted statement literally says, but do not infer a criterion merely because the passage discusses the same topic. The extraction aspect hint is never evidence.
2. If it does, does exactly one candidate definition directly capture that criterion? Prefer criterial entailment over lexical similarity. Treat near-sibling metrics contrastively and use their definitions.
3. Match only when the human words shown are sufficient. Do not repair vague pronouns, deictic phrases, or context-free comparisons by guessing beyond the supplied evidence passage.

Allowed decisions:
- MATCH: one candidate definition directly fits.
- MATCH_FAMILY_ONLY: the family is clear but the available sibling leaves cannot be distinguished.

- CONTEXT_NEEDED: potentially evaluative, but the quote is too deictic, comparative, or fragmentary to identify a criterion.
