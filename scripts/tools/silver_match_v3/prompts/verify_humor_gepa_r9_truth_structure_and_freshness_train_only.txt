Humor full-bank resolver GEPA round 9: truth, structure, and freshness owners

This resolver-only refinement was authored by the supervising judge from
additional disagreements in the predeclared TRAIN role. No dev, test, blind,
or resolver outcome was used. It supplements rounds 1--8; exact-leaf precision
and typed abstention remain primary.

1. Separate three truth-related owners by the role truth plays. Use
truthfulness/recognizability-as-humor-engine when a recognizable social,
emotional, or observational truth is explicitly why the joke works (for
example, “funny because it is true”). Use truthfulness/embellishment-bounds
when a joke's real-world premise, hyperbole, or claimed fact is criticized as
false or insufficiently grounded. Use factual-accuracy/evidentiary-support
when the criterion is verifying, sourcing, supporting, or correcting a
public-facing factual assertion. A bare wording or conventional-order
correction does not become evidentiary support merely because it is “right.”

2. Resolve structure leaves from the named structural object. Missing or
unclear setup-to-punch completion, including a premise that simply stops
before any punch, belongs to core setup--punch--tags architecture. A named
final gag, closing punch, button, tag, or stinger belongs to ending/button
landing under round 8. Whole-piece architecture, progression, restructuring,
or cutting material belongs to structural economy/editorial rigor. Explicit
transitions, continuity, edit rhythm, or pacing between jokes/scenes belongs
to editing/transitions/pacing. Do not collapse all four into “structure.”

3. Surprise alone does not prove an earned payoff. General expectation
violation, abrupt redirection, juxtaposition, substitution, or non-sequitur is
owned by incongruity/surprise mechanics when that is the named mechanism.
Unexpected-yet-earned payoff requires evidence that the turn is meaningfully
connected to the setup, justified in hindsight, or made inevitable by the
reveal. If an unforeseen punchline supplies no such connection, keep the
surprise/payoff family unresolved rather than inventing earnedness.

4. Resolve freshness from the source of novelty or staleness. “Hack,” cliché,
stock, or stale because a form has become massively overused belongs to
freshness-vs-cliché, even if the original performer historically preceded the
imitators. Direct copying, recycled bits, imitation, or ownership concerns
belong to originality-vs-derivativeness. A distinctive premise or hook belongs
to premise-and-angle originality; a general new formal choice or surprising
creative connection belongs to general creative novelty. “Fresh take on a
common premise” remains family-only when the evidence does not distinguish a
new premise angle from novel execution.

5. Separate medium adaptation from platform fit. When moving between spoken,
written, audio, visual, or another medium changes whether the comedic device
can be perceived or land, medium/format-specific adaptation owns the
criterion. When the issue is distribution venue, platform rules, market,
audience appropriateness, or operational constraints, platform/format fit or
the bank's more specific compliance leaf owns it. Do not map punctuation in a
spoken-to-written rewrite to generic prose clarity when cross-medium transfer
is the explicit cause.

6. A named mechanism can be exact without an extra adjective, but only when
one card actually owns it. General “delivery” may use the bank's general
expressive-delivery owner under round 7. By contrast, a bare cultural
reference, originality claim, or surprising punchline often remains shared by
several overlapping leaves; use MATCH_FAMILY_ONLY unless the human words
supply the distinguishing object above.

For every resolver decision, quote the evaluative nucleus internally and name
the strongest losing sibling. These rules clarify ownership; they do not
authorize inferring evaluation from neutral content or a mechanism from
generic praise.
