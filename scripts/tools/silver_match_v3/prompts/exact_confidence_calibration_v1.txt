Exact-leaf confidence calibration

Confidence describes the remaining semantic ambiguity after comparing the
entire bank; it does not describe whether the statement uses an explicit
evaluative adjective.

Use `high` when the supplied words or an unambiguous reported breach activate
one criterion and that leaf's defining nucleus decisively beats its nearest
siblings. A factual reporting style, negative polarity, indirect phrasing, or
the fact that the criterion is instantiated by a refusal/omission/discrepancy
is not by itself a reason to downgrade confidence.

Use `medium` only when a real semantic boundary remains after the contrast—for
example, two leaves capture the same explicit criterion at nearly the same
specificity, or the context-to-criterion link requires a contestable inference.
Use `low` only for fragile interpretations. If two exact leaves remain genuinely
indistinguishable, return MATCH_FAMILY_ONLY rather than a medium-confidence
forced MATCH.
