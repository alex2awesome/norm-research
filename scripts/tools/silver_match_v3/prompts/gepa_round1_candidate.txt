You are matching one naturally occurring human evaluative statement to a frozen bank of evaluation metrics. This is a high-recall silver instrument with explicit abstention, not a forced classifier.

What counts as explicit norm evidence

- A human may praise a criterion, report its violation, request a correction, identify an omission, compare items on it, or state a verdict. A concrete directive or breach reveals the norm even if the human does not utter its abstract name. For example, correcting a misspelling invokes grammar; asking to define a symbol invokes notation precision; saying an issue was not studied invokes coverage/scope when the passage makes that omission evaluative.
- Declarative evaluative attributes also count: "novel", "outperforms the baseline", "validates the bound", "unclear", "inconsistent", and similar language are not merely topical descriptions.
- Do not invent a criterion from subject matter alone. A bare fact with no evaluative attribute, prescription, omission, comparison, or violation is NO_EXPLICIT_CRITERION.
- Use the EVIDENCE PASSAGE to interpret the human's actual words and normative force. The extraction aspect hint is never evidence. Polarity never changes metric identity.

How to use the bank

1. Test semantic entailment, not word overlap. A specific manifestation may MATCH a more general metric when the metric definition directly generalizes it. Do not demand the same surface words.
2. Contrast all supplied candidate definitions. MATCH only if the human words distinguish one candidate.
3. If two or more candidate leaves are semantically indistinguishable on the expressed criterion, choose MATCH_FAMILY_ONLY. A candidate echoing the human's exact phrase does not break a tie when sibling definitions cover the same criterion.
4. Use NO_CANDIDATE_FITS only for a clear, discriminating criterion that no supplied definition directly generalizes. Do not use it for bare praise such as "good results".

Typed abstentions

- MATCH: exactly one candidate directly captures the revealed criterion.
- MATCH_FAMILY_ONLY: the construct is clear, but overlapping sibling leaves cannot be distinguished.
- NO_EXPLICIT_CRITERION: genuinely factual/topical text with no evaluative or prescriptive force.
- CONTEXT_NEEDED: potentially evaluative, but even the supplied passage is too deictic or fragmentary to identify the criterion.
- GENERIC_VERDICT: only undifferentiated praise/blame is explicit, such as "good paper", "weak work", "pretty good results", or "simple yet effective", without a discriminating quality dimension.
- NO_CANDIDATE_FITS: a specific criterion is explicit, but absent from the supplied cards.
- NOISE: garbled extraction debris or meaningless language.

Contrastive examples

- Human: “propoed” should be “proposed”. If a grammar/spelling card is supplied, MATCH it; do not reject it because the request is concrete.
- Human: The symbol Delta_t is not defined. A card requiring precise, defined notation can MATCH by direct generalization.
- Human: This is not studied in the paper. If the passage identifies an untested deployment setting and an external-validity/scope card is supplied, MATCH it. If the passage gives no referent, CONTEXT_NEEDED.
- Human: This work proposes a novel algorithm. "Novel" explicitly invokes novelty; it is not automatically descriptive. If several novelty leaves have indistinguishable definitions, use MATCH_FAMILY_ONLY.
- Human: The results are pretty good. This is GENERIC_VERDICT unless the words name a discriminating dimension such as baseline superiority, statistical reliability, or generalization.
- Human: Call this Done instead. If no naming/identifier card is supplied, use NO_CANDIDATE_FITS; do not call a concrete correction NO_EXPLICIT_CRITERION.

Confidence calibration

- high: the decision and, for MATCH, the contrast against siblings are textually clear.
- medium: the outcome is best supported but one plausible boundary remains.
- low: use only when forced to choose among fragile interpretations; prefer the correct typed abstention.
