Humor K50 round-2 refinements from residual candidate-present training errors

These refinements supersede a broader earlier boundary when they conflict. Preserve
typed abstention: the goal is exact leaves without turning bare facts or broad verdicts
into forced matches.

- a265 versus a257: a rule-of-three, repeated examples, or normal-normal-bad sequence
  that builds/heightens an established joke pattern is a265 Escalation within patterns.
  a257 requires that accumulation/list/overcommitment be the formal device and that the
  format itself is twisted or subverted for payoff. A list is not automatically a257.
- a184 versus a118: if a line, section, or digression "adds nothing to the story" or can
  be removed because it breaks whole-piece progression, use a184 structural/editorial
  rigor—even if the suggestion targets one line. Use a118 for overwriting, plain/tight
  language, concise setup/punch, and verbal fat rather than narrative architecture.
- a210 versus a262: a210 is general expressive performance presence—confidence,
  charisma, likability, vocal/physical choices, and delivery that make material land.
  a262 is showmanship that commands attention through a confident opening, high
  charismatic energy, and especially controlling the room/stage. Bare admiration of a
  performer's confidence/charisma is a210; explicit room command/showmanship is a262.
- a37 versus a17/a102: a37 is targeting a defined demographic or audience segment and
  choosing tone/references/content for its appeal, including narrow versus wide appeal.
  a17 is fit to the actual venue, moment, genre expectation, or core-versus-crossover
  strategy. a102 is adaptation to the specific room/event/demographics as an active
  partner. Explicit demographic targeting without a live-room adaptation is a37.
- a155 versus a116: both cover audience background/reference knowledge. When feedback
  merely says a joke depends on remembering a fact, needs context for outsiders, or is
  intelligible only to a niche, and the evidence cannot distinguish reference
  accessibility from a background-knowledge test, use MATCH_FAMILY_ONLY. Choose a155
  only for calibration of inside/cultural references to intended audiences; choose a116
  only when "getting it" is deliberately used as a knowledge/reasoning test.
- a188 versus a79: a79 is current-event timeliness/topicality. a188 explicitly weighs
  timely relevance against evergreen durability, temporal distance, staleness, or
  whether the surprise will still play later.
- a43 versus a244 remains strict: camera/edit/graphics/staging/sound used to set comic
  tone, misdirect, or time a punch is a43; a244 is general production execution,
  cinematography, sound, set/location, or operational quality without that comic-device
  function.
- a280 requires the fair-cued initial reading, concealed alternate interpretation, and
  justified reveal. a114 requires the alternate frame to resolve causally/intelligibly.
  If misdirection/reveal cues are explicit, do not retreat to family-only.
- a111 requires the function of silence/laughter beats/audience reaction space. a171 is
  general rhythm/momentum; a235 is live flow, delivery energy, transitions, mic and room
  read. Do not use family-only when one of these operational objects is named.
- a166 is a novel, precise angle/execution of a premise; a64 is rejection of an overused
  premise, cliché, or hack convention; a213 is copied/recycled/imitative ownership; a54
  is formula, clout, algorithm bait, or manufactured conflict. Select the named failure,
  not an undifferentiated originality family.
- a61 is not a generic verdict when the passage identifies low threat, detachment,
  emotional distance, relief, or muted urgency as the condition permitting laughter.
- a53 is a title/headline/opening that signals satirical tone; a237 is the setup's shared
  normal world/social rule; a92 is the premise and hook. Keep packaging language,
  social-rule setup, and premise framing separate.
- a56 is responsible identity representation and avoidance of slurs/demeaning
  caricatures. a21 is whether the speaker has standing/license to treat sensitive
  material. a55 is facework, solidarity, politeness, and social distance. a213/a64 are
  derivativeness/cliché, not representation ethics.
- a260 is narrative integration of themes, A/B plots, and formal devices into a whole;
  a239 is editing/transitions/continuity and pace. a217 is the basic through-line and
  organized progression.
- a58 is adaptation to a comedic medium/form while preserving voice. a173 is broader
  platform/venue constraint fit. a219 is authentic personal voice/persona itself.

Use NO_EXPLICIT_CRITERION rather than MATCH for descriptive statements that merely say
a person improvised, will add tags, uses a format, or possesses a trait without a human
evaluation/prescription/comparison. Use GENERIC_VERDICT for only funny/unfunny or broad
praise/blame. Keep the JSON reason brief and name the closest losing sibling.
