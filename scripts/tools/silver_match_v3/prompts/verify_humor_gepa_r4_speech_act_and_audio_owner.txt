Humor verifier GEPA round 4: evaluative speech act and exact craft owner

These optimize-only corrections supersede broader rules above when they conflict.

First apply an evaluative-speech-act gate. A statement reveals a criterion only if
the human praises, criticizes, prescribes, compares, or otherwise commits to a
quality condition. A neutral or explanatory report of a performer's recurring
technique, process, or trait is NO_EXPLICIT_CRITERION even when that description
maps perfectly to a metric card. Do not treat words such as “loves to,” a detailed
process description, audience reaction, or mere defense of the performer as praise
unless the human actually marks the technique as good, bad, desirable, or required.

Then assign the criterion to the leaf that owns the actor and mechanism:

- For microphone and sound problems, distinguish performer technique from production
  execution. How the performer holds, covers, positions, or works the microphone is
  performance delivery/mic technique, even when the symptom is poor dynamic range or
  sound. Recording, mixing, level balance, capture, or system/crew execution is
  production craft. The acoustic symptom alone does not decide the leaf; the criticized
  actor and proposed remedy do.
- When the human explicitly identifies a pun, double meaning, portmanteau, or wordplay
  and asks that the same wordplay be clarified or rewritten, pun/wordplay construction
  owns the criterion. References to logic, syntax, sense, premise, or resolution do not
  turn a named-pun repair into general frame-shift/causal intelligibility. Use the
  latter only when the failing mechanism is a non-wordplay setup, reframe, or causal
  resolution.

Before CONFIRM_MATCH, the reason must answer both questions: “What evaluative
commitment did the human make?” and “Which actor/mechanism does the proposal uniquely
own?” If either answer is absent, use the appropriate typed abstention or alternative.
