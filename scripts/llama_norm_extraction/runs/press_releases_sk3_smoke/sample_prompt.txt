You are an expert news editor analyzing how a press release was covered.

# Your task

You will read one news article covering a press release (the press release text is provided first, the news article second, separated by '---') and extract every quality judgment the article's makes about the original press release, then tag each judgment against a fixed list of canonical journalism norms.

# Two levels of structure

LEVEL 1 — PASSAGE: a paragraph or multi-sentence span where the article (and its author/outlet) is making a quality judgment. Each news article covering a press release (the press release text is provided first, the news article second, separated by '---') usually contains 3-10 passages.

CRITICAL: a passage is NEVER just a section label like "Strengths:" or "Weaknesses:" — it MUST include the actual content of the judgment. The header may be at the start but the passage_text MUST extend through the substantive text. A passage_text of less than 50 characters is almost always wrong.

LEVEL 2 — SIGNAL: a specific phrase INSIDE the passage that EVIDENCES a journalism norm (without necessarily BEING the journalism norm). For example, the phrase "the company provided no evidence for its 50% improvement claim" is a complaint evidencing an 'evidence sourcing' norm; the phrase "rival researchers contested the methodology" is observation evidencing a 'balance / counter-source' norm; an article paragraph that adds Parkland-shooting context the PR omitted is an observation evidencing a 'context completeness' norm.

Each signal has:
- signal_text: an exact substring of THE PASSAGE (not the full news article covering a press release (the press release text is provided first, the news article second, separated by '---')), 10-200 chars
- signal_type: exactly one of `complaint`, `praise`, `observation`, `suggestion`
- polarity: exactly one of `positive`, `negative`, `neutral`
- rubric_matches: a list of 0-3 integer ids from the vocabulary below. Empty list `[]` is allowed when the signal does not match any canonical rubric.
