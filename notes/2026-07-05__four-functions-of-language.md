# Four functions of language in judgment domains

*2026-07-05. Framing note: studying taste-based fields (creative writing, humor) alongside
law-like domains, four uses of "the language of judgment" routinely get merged. This note
separates them, explains why they merge, and maps each onto an instrument we already have.
Companion to the anthropological-framing note (2026-07-01).*

## The four functions

1. **Explanation** — language as post-hoc account of an outcome ("we rejected it because…").
2. **Prediction** — language as ex-ante forecast of how people will feel or decide.
3. **Formation** — language as guide for feeling and perception; a communicative, growing
   medium that *teaches* people how to see/feel.
4. **Regulation** — language as rule-making: how one *should* feel, perceive, or (in law)
   behave.

## Two axes disentangle them

**Axis 1 — direction of fit** (Anscombe; Searle's taxonomy of illocutionary acts).
(1) and (2) are word→world: descriptions that succeed by matching outcomes.
(3) and (4) are world→word: the perceiver or behavior is supposed to change to match the
language.

**Axis 2 — within each pair:**
- (1) vs (2): backward-looking (fit a rationale to an observed outcome) vs forward-looking
  (commit before the outcome).
- (3) vs (4): **constitutive vs regulative** (Searle, *Speech Acts* ch. 2). Formation is
  internalized — once taught you *see* differently, no enforcement needed; it creates the
  very capacity it governs. Regulation is an external standard plus sanction — you can
  comply without perceiving differently.

| | word→world (describes) | world→word (shapes) |
|---|---|---|
| **backward / internalized** | (1) explanation | (3) formation (constitutive) |
| **forward / enforced** | (2) prediction | (4) regulation (regulative) |

Test for a taste field: remove the language — does an unshaped feeling remain (regulative),
or is there nothing left to regulate (constitutive)?

## Why they merge anyway (three systematic couplings)

1. **(1) is frequently (3)/(4) in disguise.** Verbal explanations of one's own judgments are
   often post-hoc theories with no access to process (Nisbett & Wilson 1977, "Telling More
   Than We Can Know"). In taste fields, "why I rated it this way" is usually normative
   assertion (4) dressed as causal report (1). Our recovery certificate R is the
   confabulation detector: an explanation that can't regenerate its verdicts isn't one.
2. **(3) and (4) interconvert over time.** Norms taught as rules become perception
   (Bourdieu, habitus; law students *told* proximate cause end up *seeing* through it).
   Classification reshapes the classified — Hacking's looping effects ("Making Up People").
3. **(2) collapses into (4) — in law specifically.** Holmes, "The Path of the Law" (1897):
   law = "prophecies of what the courts will do in fact." Hart, *The Concept of Law*
   (ch. 5–6): this erases the **internal point of view** — participants treat rules as
   reasons, not forecasts; a judge deciding can't be predicting herself. Same sentences, two
   stances: (2) is the external observer's, (4) the internal participant's.

## The law/taste disanalogy (the trap for comparative work)

- **Taste:** Y (the felt preference) exists **upstream** of the language. Explanation,
  prediction, teaching all point at an independently occurring feeling; rules are at best
  summaries of it.
- **Law:** Y is partially **constituted** by category-4 language — outcomes are *supposed*
  to follow the stated rules; rule↔outcome circularity is by design.

Consequence: the identical measurement inverts meaning across domains.
"Stated criteria fail to predict outcomes" = **tacitness** in taste (the Polanyi result, a
discovery) but = **doctrinal indeterminacy** in law (the legal-realist scandal — rule-of-law
values require category 4 to actually govern). Side-by-side study is legitimate — it is the
same articulability question structurally — but must flag the inversion explicitly.

## Measurement signatures in our machinery

| function | instrument |
|---|---|
| (1) explanation | **R** — recovery/reconstruction: can the stated rationale regenerate the verdicts (explanation vs confabulation) |
| (2) prediction | **V** = I(Y;M), AUC/bits — plain predictive validity |
| (3) formation | **decompression grid** (Face-2 rungs): can this language teach a fresh judge to perceive; taste finding = name suffices / checklist hurts → formative language works by *invoking* enculturation, not transmitting content |
| (4) regulation | **executor seat + Goodhart/reactivity** — held FIXED in our design (non-reactive judges, no feedback into the practice); needs an explicit scope statement in the paper, esp. for law-like corpora |

Four functions, four different experiments — decent evidence they don't belong collapsed
into one notion of "the language of judgment."
